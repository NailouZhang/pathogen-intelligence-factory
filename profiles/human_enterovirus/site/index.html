家长对EV-A71 疫苗接种犹豫情况在手足口病(HFMD)病原体谱系变化中的研究:广州的一项横断面研究

Parental EV-A71 vaccine hesitancy amid shifting HFMD pathogens: a cross-sectional study in Guangzhou
作者： Xin-Ai Su, Zhi-Xiong Gao, Jie-Wei Zhong, Cong-Hua Bai, Rui-Li Xie, Lei Luo, Qi-Lin Wu, Zhong JieWei
摘要中文翻译

问题与背景
在EV-A71 疫苗引入后,手足口病(HFMD)的病原体谱系已从EV-A71转移,引发了家长对其持续相关性的疑虑。
设计与对象
本研究旨在调查广州家长对EV-A71 疫苗接种的犹豫情况及其决定因素。我们采用分层多阶段抽样方式,在广州的10个社区卫生服务中心对年龄≤5岁的儿童家长进行了调查。
核心方法
通过探索性因子分析确定潜在变量,随后使用结构方程模型(SEM)分析其对EV-A71 疫苗接种犹豫的直接和间接影响。采用具有聚类稳健标准误差的逻辑回归分析家长的知识、态度和行为(KAP)以及医生推荐行为的影响,特别关注医生推荐热情与家长对EV-A71 疫苗接种信心之间的跨层级交互作用。
主要结果
The prevalence of EV-A71 vaccine hesitancy among parents was 28.95%. Mothers (OR: 0.46[0.28, 0.76]) and parents with a college degree or associate degree (OR: 0.53[0.32, 0.86]) were less hesitant, whereas being a non-healthcare worker (OR: 2.38[1.03, 5.55]) and having a child without a history of HFMD (OR: 3.45[1.31, 9.12]) were more hesitant.
解释与创新
SEM显示,对EV-A71 疫苗接种的信心(β = -0.39)、对服务的信任(β = -0.17)以及风险感知(β = -0.17)均对接种犹豫产生负向直接影响。知识本身没有直接影响,但通过这三个中介变量的总间接影响显著(β = -0.45)。
科研与公卫意义
针对这些态度的沟通和咨询可能有助于家长做出知情的、自费的EV-A71 疫苗接种接种决策。
局限与证据强度
家长信心与医生推荐之间的交互作用具有边缘显著性(β = 2.10,P = 0.08)。本研究为横断面研究,限制了因果推断的能力。

Parental EV-A71 vaccine hesitancy amid shifting HFMD pathogens: a cross-sectional study in Guangzhou

Authors: Xin-Ai Su, Zhi-Xiong Gao, Jie-Wei Zhong, Cong-Hua Bai, Rui-Li Xie, Lei Luo, Qi-Lin Wu, Zhong JieWei
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Following enterovirus A71 (EV‐A71) vaccine introduction, the hand foot mouth disease (HFMD) pathogen spectrum has shifted away from EV‐A71, prompting parental doubts about the vaccine’s continued relevance. This study aimed to investigate the prevalence and determinants of EV‐A71 vaccine hesitancy among parents in Guangzhou. Methods We surveyed parents of children ≤ 5 years at 10 community health centers in Guangzhou using stratified multi-stage sampling. Exploratory factor analysis identified latent variables, followed by structural equation modeling (SEM) to examine their direct and indirect effects on vaccine hesitancy. Logistic regression with cluster-robust standard errors was used to assess the effects of parental knowledge, attitude, and practice (KAP) and physician recommendation practices, specifically examining the cross-level interaction between physician recommendation enthusiasm and parental vaccine confidence. Results The prevalence of EV-A71 vaccine hesitancy among parents was 28.95%. Mothers (OR: 0.46[0.28, 0.76]) and parents with a college degree or associate degree (OR: 0.53[0.32, 0.86]) were less hesitant, whereas being a non‐healthcare worker (OR: 2.38[1.03, 5.55]) and having a child without a history of HFMD (OR: 3.45[1.31, 9.12]) were more hesitant. SEM indicated that vaccine confidence ( β = -0.39), service trust ( β = -0.17), and risk perception ( β = -0.17) exerted negative direct effects on vaccine hesitancy. Knowledge had no direct effect, its total indirect effect via the three mediators was significant( β = -0.45). Furthermore, the interaction between parental confidence and physician recommendation was marginally significant( β = 2.10, P = 0.08). Conclusion Knowledge reduces hesitancy mainly through confidence, trust, and risk perception, not directly. Communication and counseling addressing these attitudes may support informed parental decisions about voluntary, self-paid EV-A71 vaccination.

Infection-specific long-chain fatty acid metabolism as a broad anti-enterovirus target.

Authors: Marcos Florentin, Jeffrey M. Loube, Ekaterina G. Viktorova, Samuel Gabaglio, Elizabeth Tanner, Margaret A. Scull, George A. Belov, Marcos Marcelo Florentín Pavía
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Enteroviruses are arguably the most numerous group of viruses infecting humans. While most enterovirus infections are benign and self-resolving, their sheer number inevitably increases the chances of multiple complications. The diversity of enteroviruses means that the development of vaccines is only economically feasible against a select few, and no direct-acting or host-targeted anti-virals are approved to treat enteroviral infections, largely due to the rapid development of resistance against all experimental drugs. Here, we explored a universal property of enterovirus infection—a massive upregulation of phospholipid synthesis as a target for anti-viral interventions. The increased phospholipid synthesis consumes endogenously and exogenously derived long-chain fatty acids (LCFA). We demonstrate that polyunsaturated LCFAs can have a broad anti-enteroviral effect, affecting multiple steps of the virus life cycle. The anti-viral activity of LCFAs did not strictly depend on the degree of unsaturation or their capacity to induce lipid peroxidation, but significantly correlated with their conformation. This suggests that their incorporation into the phospholipid molecules makes the replication organelle membranes incapable of properly accommodating viral replication machinery. Accordingly, the inhibition of neutral lipid synthesis promoted LCFAs retargeting to the membranes in infected cells and increased their anti-viral potency. We show that this approach is effective against diverse enteroviruses in different cell types, including differentiated primary cells, and that attempts to establish viruses resistant to such treatment were unsuccessful. IMPORTANCE Humans are hosts to more than 300 distinct enteroviruses. Enterovirus infections can induce multiple short-term and long-term complications, including such serious conditions as paralysis, myocarditis, aseptic meningitis, and others. Yet, licensed vaccines are available only against poliovirus and enterovirus A71, and no specific anti-enterovirus drugs are approved for clinical practice. These viruses rapidly develop resistance to either virus- or host-acting experimental compounds. We explored a shared feature of enterovirus infection, a strong upregulation of phospholipid synthesis in infected cells, as a tool to control enterovirus replication. By altering the long-chain fatty acids composition available to infected cells, replication of diverse enteroviruses can be similarly suppressed, and the selection of resistant mutants was not successful. Thus, targeting infection-induced changes in cellular lipid metabolism, rather than individual viral or host factors, may offer a promising approach for controlling enterovirus infections. Clade-specific transmission dynamics of enterovirus D68 and their association with acute flaccid myelitis

摘要中文翻译摘要 人肠道病毒D68(EV-D68)是儿童轻度呼吸道疾病的一个常见原因,但也与急性弛缓性脊髓炎(AFM)相关,这是一种罕见且严重的神经系统疾病,可能导致永久性瘫痪。由于EV-D68的症状与其他常见的儿童疾病重叠,追踪EV-D68的传播较为困难,使得病例报告不可靠。因此,EV-D68的传播负担仍不明确,尤其是在2014年首次广泛认可的暴发之前。系统发育流行病学方法提供了一种替代基于病例的监测的强大手段,可以直接从病原体的遗传序列推断流行病学参数。在这里,我们使用一个出生-死亡-抽样系统发育流行病学模型,分析了1990年代至2023年间在欧洲和北美收集的公开EV-D68序列,发现季节性EV-D68的传播早在2014年引起广泛关注的暴发之前就已经确立。此外,我们还发现急性弛缓性脊髓炎病例负担与特定EV-D68谱系(谱系B)的传播动态有显著关联,而这种关联在谱系A和D中并未观察到。这些发现表明,谱系特异性的生物学差异驱动了AFM的风险,这对监测的优先级和未来神经系统疾病负担评估具有重要意义。

Clade-specific transmission dynamics of enterovirus D68 and their association with acute flaccid myelitis

Authors: David Jorgensen, Noémie Lefrancq, Nicholas C. Grassly, Louis du Plessis, Margarita Pons-Salort, David Jørgensen
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Enterovirus D68 (EV-D68) is a common cause of mild respiratory illness in children but is also linked to acute flaccid myelitis (AFM), a rare and severe neurological condition which can result in permanent paralysis. Tracking EV-D68 transmission is difficult because symptoms overlap with other common childhood illnesses, making case reporting unreliable. As a result, the burden of EV-D68 transmission remains poorly understood, particularly prior to the first widely recognised outbreak in 2014. Phylodynamic approaches offer a powerful alternative to case-based surveillance, inferring epidemiological parameters directly from pathogen genetic sequences. Here we show, using a birth-death-sampling phylodynamic model applied to publicly available EV-D68 sequences from the 1990s to 2023 collected in Europe and North America, that seasonal EV-D68 transmission was well established long before the 2014 outbreak brought the virus to widespread attention. Furthermore, we show that AFM case burden is strongly associated with the transmission dynamics of a specific EV-D68 clade, clade B, an association not seen for clades A and D. These findings suggest that clade-specific biological differences drive AFM risk, with important implications for surveillance prioritisation and the timing of future burden of neurological disease.

Authors: Jiayao Shen, Kaixuan Cai, Weijie Kang, Qiao He, Jian Zhang, Lei Yin
Original abstract (English)
Atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome (aHUS) represents a complement-mediated thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) characterized by microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, and end-organ damage, most frequently affecting the kidneys. Although infection is a well-recognized precipitant, extensive skin involvement is uncommon. Here, we describe a 14-year-old boy who developed aHUS following Rhinovirus/Enterovirus infection. He was found to have anemia, thrombocytopenia, acute kidney injury, hematuria, and proteinuria, and peripheral smear examination demonstrated schistocytes. Complement evaluation showed reduced C3 and factor I concentrations, increased soluble C5b-9, and preserved ADAMTS13 activity. Investigations for Shiga toxin-producing Escherichia coli, systemic autoimmune disease, and alternative infectious etiologies were negative. Even with eculizumab, corticosteroids, therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE), and continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT), his condition progressed to petechiae, ecchymoses, hemorrhagic bullae, and necrotic lesions of the axilla, neck, and back. Since the necrotic areas failed to heal and were accompanied by continued platelet consumption and a heightened risk of infection, surgical debridement and wound closure were undertaken after the platelet count reached 81 × 10^9/L. Histopathologic analysis of the excised tissue confirmed cutaneous thrombotic microangiopathy with microvascular thrombosis. Following the procedure, platelet count, hemoglobin, and complement C3 returned to normal, and the wound healed without complication. At subsequent follow-up, the scars were supple and level, with no sensory deficit or functional limitation. This report expands the range of infectious triggers associated with aHUS and demonstrates that persistent necrotic cutaneous thrombotic microangiopathy may necessitate prompt surgical management in addition to complement inhibition and supportive treatment.

Original abstract (English)
Poliomyelitis is caused by poliovirus, a member of the genus Enterovirus in the family Picornaviridae. Oral and inactivated poliovirus vaccines have substantially reduced disease worldwide; however, limitations include incomplete intestinal immunity after inactivated vaccine and the rare emergence of vaccine-derived strains after oral vaccine, supporting investigation of non-replicating vaccine platforms. Poliomyelitis is classified into three distinct forms: mild disease (abortive poliomyelitis), paralytic poliomyelitis, and aseptic meningitis (nonparalytic poliomyelitis). Severe neurological complications, including irreversible paralysis, have affected thousands of individuals worldwide. Polio is prevented via vaccination, namely with the inactivated and attenuated oral polio vaccine. Although existing vaccines have driven global eradication, IPV provides limited intestinal mucosal immunity, and OPV can rarely be associated with vaccine-derived poliovirus, motivating evaluation of a non-replicating vaccine platform.Abstruct. This study used immunoinformatics to design and preliminarily evaluate a chimeric multi-epitope vaccine candidate targeting the VPI capsid protein of poliovirus type 3. The VPI sequence (NCB accession number ABG78772.1) was used for linear B-cell, MHC-class I, and MHC-class II epitope prediction and safty filtering. Selected epitopes were joined with defined linkers and an N-terminal human beta-defensin adjuvant. The resulting construct was evaluated for physicochemical properties, structural quality, predicted HLA population coverage, rigid-body docking with TLR3, normal-mode behaviour, and simulated immune responses. Codon adaptation and in silico cloning were also performed to evaluate the potential expression feasibility of the vaccine construct. Four B-cell epitopes, twenty-six cytotoxic T-lymphocyte epitopes, twenty helper T-lymphocyte epitopes, including EAAAK, GPGPG, and AAY linkers and β-defensin, were assembled into a 569-amino-acid construct. The construct was predicted to be antigenic, non-allergenic, non-toxic, and to show favourable physicochemical properties, including hydrophilic characteristics. Combined MCH-I and MCH-II epitopes predicted global HLA population coverage of 98.52%. CLustPro identified a top-ranked docked cluster with a weighted score of -1134; this score ranks models within the docking run and is not a binding free energy or dissociation constant. C-ImmSim predicted secondary humoral and cellular responses after booster doses, while codon analysis suggested compatibility with expression in Escherichia coli. Conclusion: The computational findings support the construct as a preliminary candidate for further study. They do not establish immunogenicity, safety, protective efficacy, receptor activation, or expression, all of which require experimental validation and in vitro and in vivo testing before biological conclusions can be drawn.

Question and background
This study aimed to investigate the associations between ambient air pollution and meteorological factors and the risk of hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) in Shenzhen, China, given the disease's prevalence and high transmissibility.
Design and population
A time-series study was conducted in Shenzhen, China, from 2013 to 2022, involving daily reported HFMD cases and environmental data collected from the Shenzhen Center for Disease Control and Prevention and local monitoring centers.
Core methods
A quasi-Poisson regression framework with distributed lag nonlinear models (DLNM) was used to analyze the associations between air pollutants, meteorological conditions, and HFMD incidence, incorporating adjustments for day-of-week, holidays, EV71 vaccination, and the impact of the COVID-19 period.
Main results
Over the study period, 473,695 HFMD cases were reported, with the strongest correlation observed for mean temperature (r = 0.61) and atmospheric pressure (r = −0.57). SO2 and CO showed elevated cumulative risks at high concentrations, with SO2 having a delayed effect and CO an immediate effect. NO2 and O3 were associated with higher HFMD risks at low concentrations, with NO2 presenting immediate lag risk and O3 delayed lag risk. High concentrations of PM2.5 and PM10 showed risk peaks at low levels but protective effects at extremely high levels.
Interpretation and novelty
The study highlights the complex relationships between air pollutants, meteorological factors, and HFMD incidence, emphasizing the need for further research to understand the mechanisms behind these associations.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings provide insights into the environmental factors influencing HFMD risk, which can inform targeted prevention strategies and public health interventions in urban areas with similar environmental conditions.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study acknowledges potential confounding effects, particularly the seemingly protective effect of PM at high concentrations, which may not be genuine but rather influenced by other factors. The evidence is moderate due to the observational nature of the study and potential confounding variables.

As EV-A71 receded, other enteroviruses—most notably coxsackievirus A16 and coxsackievirus A6—moved in to fill the ecological vacancy, becoming the dominant causes of HFMD in many regions. Parents watching their children catch HFMD despite being vaccinated have begun to ask an uncomfortable question: if the vaccine no longer matches the viruses in circulation, is it still worth getting? A new cross-sectional study from Guangzhou, published in BMC Public Health, quantifies just how widespread that doubt has become and probes the psychological machinery behind it. The research team, led by Xin Su and Qilin Wu of the Guangzhou Center for Disease Control and Prevention and colleagues at district-level CDCs across the city, surveyed parents of children aged five years or younger at ten community health centers. Using stratified multi-stage sampling designed to capture a representative slice of Guangzhou’s diverse urban population, the investigators measured parental knowledge, attitudes and practices related to the EV-A71 vaccine, along with physician recommendation behavior. The headline finding is sobering: 28.95 percent of parents surveyed exhibited vaccine hesitancy toward the EV-A71 shot—a voluntary, self-paid vaccine in China’s immunization landscape, which makes parental decision-making particularly consequential. The demographic patterns the team uncovered through logistic regression with cluster-robust standard errors reveal who is most likely to balk. Mothers were markedly less hesitant than fathers, with an odds ratio of 0.46, suggesting that the primary caregivers most often managing children’s health decisions hold more favorable views of the vaccine. Parents holding a college or associate degree were also less hesitant (odds ratio 0.53), consistent with the broader observation that educational attainment often correlates with greater vaccine acceptance. On the other side of the ledger, parents who were not healthcare workers were more than twice as hesitant (odds ratio 2.38), and—perhaps most tellingly—parents whose children had no history of HFMD were over three times as hesitant (odds ratio 3.45). That last finding hints at a paradox of prevention: families spared direct experience of the disease may perceive less need for a vaccine against it. To move beyond simple associations, the researchers employed structural equation modeling (SEM), a statistical framework that allows latent psychological variables—constructs that cannot be directly observed but are inferred from multiple survey items—to be tested in a network of direct and indirect causal pathways. Exploratory factor analysis, preceded by principal component assessment and Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin sampling adequacy checks, first identified the underlying latent dimensions in the data. The SEM then revealed three psychological constructs exerting significant negative direct effects on hesitancy: vaccine confidence (standardized coefficient -0.39), trust in vaccination services (-0.17), and perceived risk of the disease (-0.17). In plain terms, parents who trusted the vaccine’s safety and effectiveness, trusted the health system delivering it, and felt the disease posed a real threat to their child were substantially less likely to hesitate. One of the study’s most technically interesting results concerns knowledge. Parental knowledge about EV-A71 and the vaccine showed no direct effect on hesitancy at all. Yet its total indirect effect, transmitted through the three mediating constructs, was substantial (standardized coefficient -0.45)—actually larger than any single direct effect in the model. This dissociation between direct and indirect pathways carries a crucial message for public health practice: simply handing parents facts does not by itself reduce hesitancy. Facts work only when they reshape confidence, trust and risk perception. A parent who learns that EV-A71 remains capable of causing severe neurological complications may update their risk assessment; a parent who learns how the inactivated vaccine was tested may grow in confidence. Knowledge is the raw material, but attitude change is the mechanism. The team also examined how physician recommendation interacts with parental psychology, using a cross-level interaction term in the regression framework that specifically tested whether the enthusiasm of healthcare providers’ recommendations modifies the effect of parental vaccine confidence. The interaction was marginally significant (coefficient 2.10, P = 0.08), a result the authors interpret cautiously but which suggests that provider communication style may amplify or buffer the effects of confidence on hesitancy. In the context of a voluntary, self-paid vaccine, where no school-entry mandate or national program force exists to drive uptake, the enthusiasm with which a community health center physician recommends the shot may matter considerably—particularly for parents whose confidence is already wavering. Parents who cannot distinguish between HFMD caused by EV-A71 and HFMD caused by coxsackieviruses see only that their vaccinated child still developed blisters, fever and mouth ulcers—fueling the perception that the vaccine does not work. The authors argue that communication and counseling strategies targeting the three mediating constructs—confidence, service trust and risk perception—are more likely to support informed parental decisions than knowledge campaigns alone. For a voluntary vaccine, where uptake depends entirely on parental willingness, the psychological pathway is the practical pathway. Health providers in Guangzhou and beyond may need to move beyond fact sheets and toward conversations that address the specific doubts the pathogen shift has created: explaining that the vaccine targets the most dangerous HFMD pathogen, that breakthrough HFMD caused by other enteroviruses is typically milder, and that EV-A71 remains a threat capable of re-emergence. The broader lesson extends past HFMD. Vaccine hesitancy is not a static phenomenon; it evolves with epidemiological reality. As pathogens shift, so do parental perceptions of relevance, and public health systems must adapt their communication accordingly. The Guangzhou findings offer a quantitative template for understanding how knowledge, confidence, trust and risk perception interact in the minds of parents weighing a voluntary vaccine against a disease whose face has changed. Clade B transmission dynamics linked to acute flaccid myelitis risk

News Brief
A new phylodynamic analysis published in Nature Communications has linked the transmission dynamics of enterovirus D68 (EV-D68) clade B to an increased risk of acute flaccid myelitis (AFM), a rare but severe neurological condition that can cause permanent paralysis. The study, which used genetic sequences from Europe and North America collected between the 1990s and 2023, reconstructed the virus's transmission history without relying on case reports, which are often unreliable due to overlapping symptoms with other respiratory infections. The findings suggest that clade B, rather than the overall EV-D68 circulation, is associated with AFM outbreaks. This clade-specific risk was not observed for clades A or D, indicating biological differences within the virus may influence its potential to cause neurological disease. The research highlights the value of genomic data in understanding epidemiological patterns, especially for EV-D68 before 2014 when its impact was poorly understood. The study's observational nature means the biological mechanisms behind the clade-specific risk are still under investigation, but the results could guide future surveillance and preparedness efforts for AFM.

No comparable association was observed for clades A or D, suggesting that biological differences between clades, rather than the sheer volume of EV-D68 transmission alone, drive the risk of neurological disease. Phylodynamic approaches of this kind infer epidemiological parameters directly from pathogen genetic sequences, offering an alternative to case-based surveillance where clinical reporting is incomplete or confounded.
