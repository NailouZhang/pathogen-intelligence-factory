The Chiral Isomers of Chlorogenic Acid Exert Distinct Antiviral Effects on Coxsackievirus A16 Infection
作者： Ke Zheng, Yi-Ting Jiang, Ying-Zi Cui, Xiao-Li Ji, Yu-Xin Zhang, Xi Zhu, Xing-Yi Ge, Helen Wang, Hai-Lan Yao, Ye Qiu

The Chiral Isomers of Chlorogenic Acid Exert Distinct Antiviral Effects on Coxsackievirus A16 Infection

Authors: Ke Zheng, Yi-Ting Jiang, Ying-Zi Cui, Xiao-Li Ji, Yu-Xin Zhang, Xi Zhu, Xing-Yi Ge, Helen Wang, Hai-Lan Yao, Ye Qiu
Original abstract (English)
Coxsackievirus A16 (CVA16) is one of the major pathogens responsible for hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD), for which no specific antiviral therapy is currently available. Neochlorogenic acid (NCGA) and chlorogenic acid (CGA) are a pair of chiral isomers with highly similar chemical structures. While the antiviral activity of CGA has been extensively investigated, that of NCGA remains largely unexplored. In our study, we found that NCGA exhibited antiviral activity against CVA16, whereas CGA was completely inactive. Notably, NCGA also displayed extremely low cytotoxicity, and its antiviral efficacy was further confirmed in vivo. We further identified RNA-binding motif protein 24 (RBM24) as a host factor involved in the antiviral effect of NCGA against CVA16, as its knockdown attenuated the antiviral activity of NCGA. Collectively, our findings not only identify a novel natural product against CVA16 but also provide clues for the discovery of host factors involved in CVA16 infection.

Seroprevalence of EV-A71 and EV-D68 antibodies in the paediatric and adolescent population, France, 2023 to 2025

Authors: Hélène Chabrolles, Audrey Mirand, Benoît Visseaux, Marie Louchet-Ducoroy, Sonia Burrel, Mathilde Gay, Eline Brémond, Cyril Debuysschere, Mouna Lazrek, Marion Jeannoel
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUNDEnteroviruses (EV)-A71 and EV-D68 are potentially associated with severe clinical conditions, especially in infants and young children. Population susceptibility to these viruses is unknown in France.AIMWe sought to estimate seroprevalence of EV-A71 and EV-D68 neutralising antibodies (nAbs) among children and adolescents living in France.METHODSA stratified sampling approach based on sex, age and geographical area of residence was applied to obtain sera for routine diagnostic from ˂ 18-year-olds in metropolitan France. A total of 501 serum samples collected between 2023 and 2025 from 0-17-year-olds were included in the investigation. Neutralising activity was tested against EV-A71 sub-genogroup C1 and EV-D68 subclade B3 strains isolated in 2016, allowing to determine seropositivity and nAb titre. Data were analysed overall, by sex, age group and geographical area.RESULTSFor EV-A71, the overall estimated seroprevalence was 43.7% (95% CI: 39.3-48.2) while for EV-D68 this reached 96.0% (95% CI: 93.9-97.5). No differences according to sex were observed. Unlike EV-D68, EV-A71 seropositivity rate varied significantly by age group (p = 0.002) and area of residence (p < 0.001). Notably, 23 of 25 children aged ˂ 1 year lacked EV-A71 protective antibodies. Concerning nAb titres, significant differences occurred across age groups both for EV-A71 (p = 0.004) and EV-D68 (p < 0.001). The nAb titres appeared as highest in 1 to under-2-year-olds for EV-A71 and 0 to under-1-year-olds for EV-D68.CONCLUSIONThis seroprevalence study in France reveals a high proportion of newborns and infants susceptible to EV-A71 at potential risk of severe disease, contrasting with the high level of immunity against EV-D68 across all paediatric age groups.

Scientific and public-health significance
The findings highlight the need for targeted surveillance and prevention strategies for EV-A71 in young children, while suggesting that EV-D68 may be less of a concern due to high immunity levels.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its cross-sectional design and reliance on routine diagnostic sera, which may not represent the entire population. The evidence is moderate as it is based on seroprevalence data without longitudinal or causal analysis.

Original abstract (English)
In this Account, we describe our efforts to establish enteroviral 2C as a tractable target for antiviral drug discovery by integrating medicinal chemistry, molecular pharmacology, resistance profiling, structural modeling, and assay development. Rather than relying solely on conventional phenotypic high-throughput screening, our initial studies focused on the rational optimization of reported 2C inhibitors identified from a drug-repurposing campaign. This strategy led to quinoline-based antivirals with substantially improved potency and selectivity against enterovirus D68 (EV-D68), providing early medicinal chemistry insights into ligand recognition by enteroviral 2C and supporting the feasibility of chemically targeting this protein. Building on these findings, we subsequently developed pyrazolopyridine-based inhibitors, which evolved into one of the most potent classes of enteroviral 2C inhibitors reported to date. Mechanistic studies combining differential scanning fluorimetry (DSF), serial viral passage, reverse genetics, and molecular modeling supported direct engagement of a conserved allosteric pocket corresponding to the previously defined (S)-fluoxetine binding site. Structure-guided optimization led to Jun6504, a rationally designed 2C inhibitor with improved metabolic stability, favorable pharmacokinetic properties, and robust in vivo efficacy in a neonatal mouse model of EV-D68-induced paralysis. Collectively, these studies provided strong pharmacological validation of 2C as a therapeutically actionable antiviral target. In parallel, we recognized that the absence of direct, scalable biochemical assays was a major impediment to 2C-targeted antiviral discovery. To address this bottleneck, we developed a fluorescence polarization-based binding assay that monitors ligand engagement of the conserved allosteric pocket in enteroviral 2C proteins. Using Jun14157, a fluorescent probe derived from the pyrazolopyridine inhibitor series, this platform enabled direct, quantitative, and high-throughput characterization of ligand-2C interactions across EV-D68, EV-A71, and CVB3. Coupling this assay with structure-based virtual screening further enabled the identification of new allosteric inhibitor scaffolds and established a generalizable discovery platform for enteroviral 2C inhibitors. Together, these studies define an integrated framework for enteroviral 2C antiviral discovery, spanning hit-to-lead optimization, mechanism of action studies, resistance mapping, pharmacological validation, and assay innovation. Continued advances in inhibitor-bound structural elucidation, broad-spectrum inhibitor design, and next-generation therapeutic strategies should further accelerate the development of clinically useful antivirals targeting enteroviral 2C proteins.

Original abstract (English)
Background: Antigenic cartography is a computational approach that quantitatively visualizes antigenic relationships among viral strains using serological data, including hemagglutination inhibition and virus-neutralization assays. Since its introduction in the early 2000s, it has substantially advanced the study of viral antigenic evolution by transforming complex serological measurements into interpretable antigenic maps, thereby facilitating assessment of antigenic diversity, immune escape, cross-reactivity, and vaccine–virus relationships. Objective: This review examines the historical development, methodological principles, applications, limitations, and emerging directions of antigenic cartography across clinically relevant viruses, with particular emphasis on its relevance to viral surveillance, antigenic drift monitoring and vaccine-strain selection. Methods: The literature was synthesized comparatively across major applications of antigenic cartography, encompassing human seasonal influenza and other viruses, including dengue, lyssaviruses, foot-and-mouth disease virus, Enterovirus A71, norovirus, rotavirus, SARS-CoV-2, and HIV. Particular attention was given to real-world applications, methodological advances, sources of uncertainty, and integration with genomic, structural, evolutionary, and computational approaches. Findings: Influenza remains the most extensively studied viral pathogen, where antigenic cartography has supported recognition of epidemiologically relevant antigenic transitions and informed vaccine decisions. Integration of artificial intelligence, protein language models, evolutionary modelling, structural vaccinology, and real-time genomic–serological surveillance could enable earlier prediction of antigenic drift, identification of emerging vaccine mismatches, and prioritization of candidate vaccine strains. Future platforms should incorporate standardized and automated serological measurements, inter-laboratory calibration, harmonized data standards, geographically representative datasets, experimental validation, and interpretable continuously updated models. Such integration could establish antigenic cartography as a scalable predictive framework for vaccine design and for strengthening preparedness against emerging viral threats.

A recent study published in Virologica Sinica analyzed enterovirus infections in hospitalized children in China over an eight-year period from 2016 to 2023, involving data from 37 hospitals. The study found that enterovirus infections accounted for 1.49% of all hospital admissions, with over 80% of cases occurring in children aged three years or younger. While most infections resulted in mild outcomes, a small number of cases developed severe complications, such as meningitis, encephalitis, and myocarditis, which required longer hospital stays and higher medical costs. The research highlights the seasonal and age-related patterns of enterovirus infections, with hospitalizations concentrated in summer and autumn. The findings emphasize the importance of targeted prevention and surveillance in young children, as well as the need to recognize less common manifestations of enterovirus disease beyond the typical hand, foot, and mouth disease. The study was conducted by a team of researchers and published by KeAi Communications Co., Ltd., with no conflicts of interest declared.

An eight-year multicenter study from China shows that while most hospitalized cases occur in very young children and have favorable outcomes, a small number develop serious complications involving the brain, heart and other organs, leading to substantially greater use of hospital resources. Published in Virologica Sinica, the study analyzed 163,714 children hospitalized between 2016 and 2023 with enterovirus infections across 37 hospitals in China. The cases accounted for 1.49% of all hospital admissions, and more than four in five patients were aged three years or younger, with children age between 28 days to 1 year representing the largest group. Hand, foot and mouth disease and herpangina made up the great majority of diagnoses. “However, we also captured less common manifestations such as meningitis, encephalitis and myocarditis, which provided a broader view of enterovirus disease than surveillance focused mainly on hand, foot and mouth disease,” shares corresponding author Xiangpeng Chen of Beijing Children’s Hospital, Capital Medical University. “Severe complications are uncommon, but when they occur, they can require longer hospitalization and considerably more medical care.” Across all cases, the median hospital stay was five days and the average hospitalization cost was $587.37. “The small number of children with complications such as coxsackievirus myocarditis, coxsackievirus meningitis or echovirus encephalitis had markedly longer stays and higher costs,” says Chen. The study also revealed strong age and seasonal patterns. “Hospitalizations were concentrated among infants and toddlers and were most common in summer and autumn,” Chen says. “Case numbers reduced during the COVID-19 period before rising again in 2023, illustrating how enterovirus circulation can shift over time.” Notably, infants and toddlers carried most of the hospitalization burden. “Our findings support more targeted prevention, surveillance and early clinical recognition in young children, while also reminding us to look beyond the most familiar enterovirus syndromes,” Chen adds.
