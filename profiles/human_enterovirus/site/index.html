非脊髓灰质炎肠道病毒的分子鉴定、量化和系统发育:来自尼日利亚东北部的废水基流行病学研究

Molecular identification, quantification and phylogeny of non-polio enteroviruses: A wastewater-based epidemiology from northeastern Nigeria
作者： Peter Elisha Ghamba, Tasiu Mallam Hamisu, Idris Nasir Abdullahi, Mustapha Ballah Abubakar, David Nadeba Bukbuk, Arshad Ismail, Wayne Howard, Jonathan Featherston, Mishalan Moodley, El-Yuguda Abdul-Dahiru
摘要中文翻译摘要 非脊髓灰质炎肠道病毒(NPEV)是全球范围内具有潜在致命性的重大健康威胁。尽管大多数感染为轻度或无症状,但某些毒株会导致严重并发症,因此需要加强临床和环境监测以有效管理。本研究利用废水基流行病学方法,评估了尼日利亚东北部六个州的NPEV污染程度。我们收集并处理了400份污水样本,使用RD和L20B细胞系及qPCR进行病毒分离和鉴定,并通过Sanger测序进行基因分型。总体患病率为42.3%,其中戈姆贝州的患病率显著最高(55.4%,OR = 2.14,p = 0.0372)。令人惊讶的是,在阿达马瓦州的一个采样点获得了高达4.28 ± 0.3 log10的高病毒拷贝数。所有州的采样点间病毒拷贝数均存在显著差异(p > 0.05)。博尔诺州的NPEV平均拷贝数最高,其次是塔拉巴州,其平均拷贝数与其他州相近。序列分析显示博尔诺州和约贝州分离出的Echovirus E19毒株之间存在遗传多样性。博尔诺州分离出的一株Echovirus E19与尼日利亚伊巴丹之前描述的一株毒株有密切关系。这些发现表明尼日利亚东北部大多数污水样本中存在多样且高水平的NPEV污染。此外,Echovirus E19与十多年前在尼日利亚西南部描述的毒株之间的相似性突显了当地活跃的传播,并强调了在One Health生态位中持续进行NPEV 监测的必要性。

Molecular identification, quantification and phylogeny of non-polio enteroviruses: A wastewater-based epidemiology from northeastern Nigeria

Authors: Peter Elisha Ghamba, Tasiu Mallam Hamisu, Idris Nasir Abdullahi, Mustapha Ballah Abubakar, David Nadeba Bukbuk, Arshad Ismail, Wayne Howard, Jonathan Featherston, Mishalan Moodley, El-Yuguda Abdul-Dahiru
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Non-polio enteroviruses (NPEV) pose a major, potentially deadly global health threat. While most infections are mild or asymptomatic, certain strains cause severe complications, requiring enhanced clinical and environmental surveillance for effective management. This study utilised wastewater-based epidemiology to assess the extent of NPEV contamination across six northeastern states in Nigeria. We collected and processed 400 sewage water samples for virus isolation and identification using RD and L20B cell lines and qPCR, and genotyped them by Sanger sequencing. The overall prevalence of NPEVs was 42.3% and was significantly highest in Gombe state (55.4%, OR = 2.14, p = 0.0372). Remarkably, a high viral copy number of 4.28 ± 0.3 log10 was obtained at a sampling site in Adamawa State. Viral copy numbers differed significantly across sampling sites in all states ( p p > 0.05). Borno State had the highest mean NPEV copy numbers, followed by Taraba State, whose mean copy number was closely related to those of other states. Sequence analyses revealed genetic diversity among Echovirus E19 isolates from Borno and Yobe States. One Echovirus E19 isolate from Borno State clustered closely with another strain previously described in Ibadan, Nigeria. These findings reveal diverse and high levels of NPEV contamination in most sewage samples in northeastern Nigeria. Moreover, the relatedness of Echovirus E19 to one described over a decade ago in southwestern Nigeria highlights active local transmission and underscores the need for continuous NPEV surveillance across One Health niches.

Question and background
The study aimed to assess the prevalence and genetic diversity of non-polio enteroviruses (NPEVs) in wastewater from northeastern Nigeria, addressing the need for enhanced surveillance of these potentially deadly global health threats.
Design and population
The study utilized wastewater-based epidemiology to collect and process 400 sewage water samples from six northeastern Nigerian states, focusing on NPEVs.
Core methods
Samples were processed using RD and L20B cell lines and qPCR for virus isolation and identification, followed by Sanger sequencing for genotyping.
Main results
The overall prevalence of NPEVs was 42.3%, with the highest prevalence in Gombe state (55.4%). A high viral copy number of 4.28 ± 0.3 log10 was observed in Adamawa State, and significant differences in viral copy numbers were noted across sampling sites in all states.
Interpretation and novelty
The findings reveal diverse and high levels of NPEV contamination in most sewage samples in northeastern Nigeria, with one Echovirus E19 isolate from Borno State closely related to a strain previously described in southwestern Nigeria, highlighting active local transmission.
Scientific and public-health significance
These results emphasize the need for continuous NPEV surveillance across One Health niches to manage potential severe complications and enhance clinical and environmental surveillance.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study's design limitations include the potential for sampling bias and the need for larger sample sizes to improve generalizability. The evidence strength is moderate due to these limitations.

Incidence, age distribution and seasonality of hand, foot and mouth disease, pertussis and mumps in children after COVID-19 non-pharmaceutical interventions: a surveillance study in Beijing, 2015–2025

Incidence, age distribution and seasonality of hand, foot and mouth disease, pertussis and mumps in children after COVID-19 non-pharmaceutical interventions: a surveillance study in Beijing, 2015–2025
作者： Xiao Yu, Tianle Liu
摘要中文翻译
查看研究七要素
问题与背景
Non-pharmaceutical interventions (NPIs) during the COVID-19 pandemic reduced transmission of many childhood infections, but post-pandemic changes in age and seasonal distribution have received less attention.
设计与对象
The study analysed surveillance records of hand, foot and mouth disease (HFMD), pertussis and mumps among children aged < 15 years in Haidian District, Beijing, from 2015 to 2025.
核心方法
在实施非药物干预措施之后(2023–2025年)和疫情暴发前(2015–2019年)期间,使用带有人口分母的准泊松回归分析比较了发病率。通过Kolmogorov–Smirnov检验、疫情前趋势预测和年龄特异性发病率比较年龄分布,并使用Watson U2检验分析季节性。
主要结果
Of 23,379 cases (HFMD 21,134; mumps 1,697; pertussis 548), pertussis incidence increased after NPI relaxation (incidence rate ratio [IRR] 9.93, 95% confidence interval 4.82–20.44), with 104 and 187 cases among children aged 6–14 years in 2023 and 2024, compared with none in 2015–2019. HFMD incidence was lower overall (IRR 0.49, 0.35–0.69), although not after adjustment for a linear time trend, and case numbers in the first post-NPI season were within the pre-pandemic range. The median age of HFMD cases rose from 3.55 to 6.33 years, exceeding the 95% prediction intervals of the pre-pandemic trend in 2023 and 2025, and after accounting for population change the ratio of school-age to preschool incidence increased 3.59-fold (2.06–6.28).
解释与创新
作者认为,三种疾病在NPI放松后表现出不同的变化,手足口病显示出年龄分布和季节性的变化,而总病例数没有增加。
科研与公卫意义
监测 应该监测受影响的人群及其时间,而不仅仅是病例数量,因为年龄和季节性分布的变化可能对公共卫生策略具有重要影响。
局限与证据强度
这些变化的原因尚不确定,证据为中等,这主要是由于依赖监测数据和潜在的混杂因素。

Question and background
Non-pharmaceutical interventions (NPIs) during the COVID-19 pandemic reduced transmission of many childhood infections, but post-pandemic changes in age and seasonal distribution have received less attention.
Design and population
The study analysed surveillance records of hand, foot and mouth disease (HFMD), pertussis and mumps among children aged < 15 years in Haidian District, Beijing, from 2015 to 2025.
Core methods
Incidence in the post-NPI (2023–2025) and pre-pandemic (2015–2019) periods was compared using quasi-Poisson regression with population denominators. Age distributions were compared using Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests, pre-pandemic trend projections and age-specific incidence, and seasonality using Watson U2 tests.
Main results
Of 23,379 cases (HFMD 21,134; mumps 1,697; pertussis 548), pertussis incidence increased after NPI relaxation (incidence rate ratio [IRR] 9.93, 95% confidence interval 4.82–20.44), with 104 and 187 cases among children aged 6–14 years in 2023 and 2024, compared with none in 2015–2019. HFMD incidence was lower overall (IRR 0.49, 0.35–0.69), although not after adjustment for a linear time trend, and case numbers in the first post-NPI season were within the pre-pandemic range. The median age of HFMD cases rose from 3.55 to 6.33 years, exceeding the 95% prediction intervals of the pre-pandemic trend in 2023 and 2025, and after accounting for population change the ratio of school-age to preschool incidence increased 3.59-fold (2.06–6.28).
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted that the three diseases changed differently after NPI relaxation, with HFMD showing changes in age distribution and seasonality without an increase in total incidence.
Scientific and public-health significance
Surveillance should monitor who is affected and when, not only case numbers, as changes in age and seasonal distribution may have important implications for public health strategies.
Limitations and evidence strength
The causes of these changes remain undetermined, and the evidence is moderate due to the reliance on surveillance data and potential confounding factors.
