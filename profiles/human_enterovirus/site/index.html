一种用于快速检测肠道病毒 C99 的灵敏且特异的一步 TaqMan RT-qPCR 检测

A Sensitive and Specific One-step TaqMan RT-qPCR Assay for Rapid Detection of Enterovirus C99
作者： Jingyi Song, Wei Zhang, Baoqin Liu, Baisheng Li, Mengmeng Liu, Yao Qin, Yun Zhang
摘要中文翻译摘要 背景 肠道病毒 C99 (EV-C99) 是肠道病毒 C 的一个新兴成员,其地理分布范围不断扩大,并有记录的跨物种 传播。目前的检测方法,包括传统和巢式 RT-PCR,都受到周转时间延长和交叉污染风险增加的限制,从而导致假阳性结果。本研究旨在开发一种快速、灵敏且特异的 EV-C99 检测方法,以进一步支持流行病学调查。方法 设计并优化了针对 EV-C99 VP1 基因保守区域的一步 TaqMan 一步 RT-qPCR 检测方法。针对多种肠道病毒(包括柯萨奇病毒 A8、柯萨奇病毒 A24、埃可病毒 6、脊髓灰质炎病毒、EV-C96 和 EV-D68)评估了该方法的特异性。使用质粒标准品的 10 倍连续稀释(3.66 × 10 5 至 3.66 × 10 - 1 拷贝/μL)确定灵敏度、线性和测定内重复性。随后将该测定应用于 2024 年至 2025 年期间收集的 92 个肠道病毒阳性废水样本,并通过 VP1 的桑格测序验证了阳性结果。结果 开发的检测方法表现出高特异性,仅观察到 EV-C99 的扩增。检测限 (LOD) 为 3.66 × 10 - 1 拷贝/μL,与传统 RT-PCR 相比,灵敏度提高了 100 倍。标准曲线在整个动态范围内表现出良好的线性,批内变异系数 (CV) 范围为 0.64% 至 2.19%。整个检测在大约 2 小时内完成,明显短于传统 RT-PCR 约 4-6 小时的时间。92个废水样本中,有1个(1.09%)EV-C99检测呈阳性,这一结果通过常规RT-PCR和测序得到证实。结论 在本研究中,我们建立了一种快速、灵敏且特异的一步式 TaqMan RT-qPCR 检测方法,用于检测 EV-C99。该方法为EV-C99的实验室诊断和环境监测提供了可靠的工具,有助于改进EV-C99循环的监测。

A Sensitive and Specific One-step TaqMan RT-qPCR Assay for Rapid Detection of Enterovirus C99

Authors: Jingyi Song, Wei Zhang, Baoqin Liu, Baisheng Li, Mengmeng Liu, Yao Qin, Yun Zhang
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Enterovirus C99 (EV-C99) is an emerging member of the Enterovirus C with an expanding geographic distribution and documented cross-species transmission. Current detection methods, including conventional and nested RT-PCR, are limited by prolonged turnaround times and increased risk of cross-contamination which causes false-positive results. This study aimed to develop a rapid, sensitive, and specific assay for EV-C99 detection to further support epidemiological investigations. Methods A one-step TaqMan one-step RT-qPCR assay targeting a conserved region of the VP1 gene of EV-C99 was designed and optimized. Specificity of the method was evaluated against multiple enteroviruses, including Coxsackievirus A8, Coxsackievirus A24, Echovirus 6, Poliovirus, EV-C96, and EV-D68. Sensitivity, linearity, and intra-assay repeatability were determined using 10-fold serial dilutions of a plasmid standard (3.66 × 10 5 to 3.66 × 10 − 1 copies/μL). The assay was subsequently applied to 92 enterovirus-positive wastewater samples collected during 2024–2025, and positive results were validated by Sanger sequencing of VP1. Results The developed assay demonstrated high specificity, with amplification observed exclusively for EV-C99. The limit of detection (LOD) was 3.66 × 10 − 1 copies/μL, representing a 100-fold increase in sensitivity compared with conventional RT-PCR. The standard curve exhibited good linearity across the dynamic range, and intra-assay coefficients of variation (CV) ranged from 0.64% to 2.19%. The total assay completed within approximately 2 hours, significantly shorter than conventional RT-PCR time of about 4–6 hours. Among 92 wastewater samples, one (1.09%) was tested positive for EV-C99, and this result was confirmed by conventional RT-PCR and sequencing. Conclusions In this study, we established a rapid, sensitive, and specific one-step TaqMan RT-qPCR assay for EV-C99 detection. The method provides a reliable tool for laboratory diagnosis and environmental surveillance of EV-C99, facilitating improved monitoring of EV-C99 circulation.

Question and background
The study aimed to develop a rapid antiviral screening assay for bovine enterovirus (BEV) to address the lack of understanding about its pathogenesis and virulence mechanisms, which are critical for disease control in the cattle industry.
Design and population
The study involved generating a full-length infectious cDNA clone of the BEV-F isolate HB19-1 and establishing a reporter virus system using green fluorescent protein (GFP). The research was conducted in a laboratory setting and focused on the BEV isolate HB19-1.
Core methods
Three overlapping fragments of the BEV genome were amplified by RT-PCR and assembled downstream of a cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter. The GFP gene was inserted between the 5’UTR and the N terminus of VP4, followed by a 2A cleavage sequence to create the recombinant rHB19-GFP virus.
Main results
Growth curve analysis showed that rHB19-GFP had slower replication kinetics at early time points compared to the parental HB19-1 virus, but no significant difference in peak viral titers. Additionally, 5-(N-Ethyl-N-isopropyl)amiloride (EIPA) was found to inhibit BEV replication.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted the results as demonstrating the utility of the rHB19-GFP reporter virus for monitoring BEV replication and as a platform for antiviral screening, highlighting the potential of EIPA as an antiviral candidate.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings provide a practical tool for studying BEV biology and for identifying antiviral compounds, which can inform future research and development of targeted therapies for BEV-related diseases in cattle.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by the absence of datasets and the focus on a single BEV isolate, which may restrict generalizability. The evidence is moderate in strength due to the controlled laboratory environment and limited scope.

Authors: Haya Hayek, Justin Z Amarin, Olla Hamdan, Andrew J Spieker, James D Chappell, Leigh M Howard, Najwa Khuri-Bulos, Natasha B Halasa
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Enterovirus D68 (EV-D68) primarily causes respiratory illness in children but is also associated with serious neurological outcomes. Although EV-D68 circulation has historically demonstrated a biennial pattern, with peaks during summer and fall, infections can occur year-round, with unpredictable outbreaks. Notable outbreaks occurred in the U.S. in 2014, in Canada in 2022, and more recently in Senegal in late 2023, leading to increased hospitalizations in affected children. However, data are lacking in the Middle East. This study aims to describe EV-D68 circulation and clinical severity among hospitalized children in Amman, Jordan. Methods Data were obtained from a prospective viral surveillance study conducted at Al-Bashir Hospital, the largest public hospital in Amman, from January 2023 to April 2024. The study population included children under 5 years hospitalized with fever or respiratory symptoms. Nasal and throat swabs were collected and tested for EV-D68 and other respiratory viruses using RT-qPCR. Demographic and clinical information were gathered through parental interviews and medical chart reviews. Results Of the 2,168 children enrolled, we identified 14 (0.6%) cases of EV-D68, with 13 occurring in January 2023 and one in February 2023. Table 1 compares EV-D68–positive children to EV-D68–negative children. The median age of EV-D68–positive children was 5.2 months (IQR, 1.3–15.4), and most were female (64.3%). The majority were previously healthy (78.6%) and born at term (85.7%). The most common presenting symptoms were cough (100.0%), congestion or runny nose (78.6%), and shortness of breath (78.6%). Fever, reported in two cases (14.3%), was significantly more common in children negative for EV-D68 (59.7%). Another virus was co-detected with EV-D68 in 6 cases (42.9%). Children with EV-D68 were more likely to receive supplemental oxygen during hospitalization (64.3% vs 37.2%, p=0.047). Intensive care was required for three EV-D68-positive cases (21.4%). Among them, one child with adenovirus co-detection was intubated and subsequently died. Figure 1 provides a detailed visual breakdown of the clinical characteristics, presenting symptoms, co-detections, and in-hospital outcomes of the 14 EV-D68–positive cases. Conclusion Our findings underscore that EV-D68, while infrequent, can lead to significant clinical severity among young children. As global circulation patterns of EV-D68 remain insufficiently understood, ongoing surveillance is critical. With monoclonal antibodies in development, monitoring EV-D68 circulation and identifying children at higher risk could enable timely preventive interventions, potentially mitigating EV-D68-associated morbidity and improving clinical outcomes.

Ongoing surveillance is critical to better understand EV-D68 circulation patterns globally. Monitoring EV-D68 and identifying children at higher risk could enable timely preventive interventions, especially with the development of monoclonal antibodies, to mitigate associated morbidity and improve clinical outcomes.

Comparison of immunogenicity, safety, and efficacy of EVA71 vaccine in children: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Authors: Muhammad Gilang Dwi Putra, Rodman Tarigan Girsang, Rais Amaral Haq, Teuku Fais Duta, Frengki Prabowo Saputro Wijayanto, Artha Maressa Theodora Simanjuntak, Eddy Fadlyana, Kusnandi Rusmil
Original abstract (English)
INTRODUCTION: Enterovirus 71 (EV-A71) is a principal cause of hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD), potentially leading to severe neurological complications in children. Inactivated EV-A71 vaccines have been introduced. This study compares the immunogenicity, safety, and efficacy of EV-A71 vaccines versus placebo in pediatric populations. RESEARCH DESIGN AND METHODS: Following a PROSPERO-registered protocol, RCTs involving EV-A71 in children were identified via PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane, and ClinicalTrials.gov. Two independent reviewers performed screening, extraction, and RoB assessments (RoB 2.0). RESULTS: Five phase III RCTs involving 36,659 children were included. EV-A71 vaccination significantly increased seropositivity across follow-up periods, including early (RR 5.8), medium-term (RR 3.09), and long-term (RR 2.95) response. Seroconversion was significantly higher in the vaccinated group (pooled RR 13.04, 95% CI 2.80-60.61; p < 0.001). Geometric mean titers, analyzed using the ratio of means approach, were significantly higher in the vaccinated group during early and medium-term follow-up. Vaccine efficacy against EV-A71-associated HFMD exceeded 98% (pooled RR 0.02, 95% CI 0.01-0.09; p = 0.0028; I2 = 50%). Solicited local and systemic adverse events were mild and comparable between groups. CONCLUSION: Inactivated EV-A71 vaccines robust immunogenicity, high clinical efficacy, and an acceptable safety profile in children. Future studies should explore long-term protection, booster schedules, and multivalent formulations against non-EV-A71 serotypes.

Scope and question
The review evaluates the immunogenicity, safety, and efficacy of inactivated EV-A71 vaccines compared to placebo in pediatric populations, focusing on children as the host and hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) as the primary disease outcome.
Evidence base and method
A systematic review and meta-analysis following a PROSPERO-registered protocol identified randomized controlled trials (RCTs) via PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane, and ClinicalTrials.gov, with two independent reviewers conducting screening, data extraction, and risk-of-bias assessments using RoB 2.0.
Consensus and conclusions
Inactivated EV-A71 vaccines demonstrate robust immunogenicity with significantly increased seropositivity and seroconversion in vaccinated children, high clinical efficacy exceeding 98% against EV-A71-associated HFMD, and an acceptable safety profile with mild and comparable adverse events between vaccinated and placebo groups.
Controversies and gaps
The review reports moderate heterogeneity in vaccine efficacy estimates (I2 = 50%) and does not address long-term protection duration, optimal booster schedules, or efficacy against non-EV-A71 serotypes, indicating unresolved questions about durability and breadth of protection.
Research and practice implications
Priority research should investigate long-term protection and booster schedules for EV-A71 vaccines, evaluate multivalent formulations targeting non-EV-A71 serotypes, and assess broader clinical outcomes beyond HFMD to inform vaccination strategies in pediatric populations.
