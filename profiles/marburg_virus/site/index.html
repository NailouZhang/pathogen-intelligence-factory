Question and background
The study addresses the diagnostic and therapeutic challenges posed by the 2026 Bundibugyo virus (BDBV) outbreak, highlighting the need for species-specific medical countermeasures due to the virus's distinct genomic characteristics.
Design and population
The study is a review of literature and public health data from the 2026 Bundibugyo virus outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and Uganda, focusing on the impact on healthcare workers and the geographic spread of the virus.
Core methods
Diagnostic challenges were addressed through broad molecular screening and genomic sequencing, with specific mention of using Sanger sequencing to identify species-level identity based on amplicon sequences from broad RT-PCR.
Main results

Interpretation and novelty

Scientific and public-health significance
The results inform the development of more accurate diagnostic tools and highlight the importance of regional coordination and targeted research for managing outbreaks of distinct and neglected ebolaviruses like BDBV.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study relies on literature review and public health data, which may have limitations in capturing real-time or detailed clinical outcomes. However, the evidence is strong due to the explicit reporting of case numbers, fatality rates, and geographic spread.

几内亚森林发热患者中病毒性出血热和虫媒病毒感染的 血清流行率

Seroprevalence of viral hemorrhagic fevers and arboviral infections among febrile patients in Forest Guinea
作者： Fara Raymond Koundouno, Sidibe, Y., Hugo Soubrier, Karifa Kourouma, Soua Koulemou, Tamba Elie Millimouno, Fernand M'Bemba Tolno, Saa Lucien Millimono, Kékoura Ifono, Faya Moriba Kamano
摘要中文翻译2017 年至 2024 年间,几内亚的常规诊断 监测 在发热患者中发现了几例确诊的病毒性出血热 (VHF) 病例。为了评估拉沙病毒和其他病毒病原体的暴露情况,我们在几内亚森林(Guéckédou 和 N'Zérékoré)进行了一项回顾性横断面 血清流行率 研究。拉沙病毒 血清流行率 在 Guéckédou 研究组中为 56.0%,在 N'Zérékoré 研究组中为 29.8%。两个研究组的血清阳性率随着年龄的增长而增加,表明累积暴露。Guéckédou实验室的样本还检测了其他病原体,检测到针对马尔堡病毒(5.8%)、寨卡病毒(5.4%)和克里米亚-刚果出血热病毒(1.2%)的抗体。

问题与背景
该研究旨在评估几内亚森林发热患者对 马尔堡病毒 和相关病原体的暴露情况,以 2017 年至 2024 年 马尔堡病毒 常规诊断期间检测到的几例病毒性出血热确诊病例为基础。
设计与对象
在几内亚森林地区,特别是在 Guéckédou 和 N'Zérékoré 进行了一项回顾性横断面 马尔堡病毒 研究,以评估发热患者对 马尔堡病毒 和其他病毒病原体的暴露情况。
核心方法
该研究涉及使用 血清流行率 方法测试 Guéckédou 实验室的样本中针对各种病原体(包括 马尔堡病毒)的抗体。

在两个研究组中,马尔堡病毒 的血清阳性率随着年龄的增长而增加,表明累积暴露。盖克杜实验室样本中还检测到了针对拉沙病毒等其他病原体的抗体。

研究结果提供了对几内亚森林地区 马尔堡病毒 的 流行病学 的深入了解,这可以为针对病毒性出血热的 监测 策略和公共卫生干预措施提供信息。

Seroprevalence of viral hemorrhagic fevers and arboviral infections among febrile patients in Forest Guinea

Authors: Fara Raymond Koundouno, Sidibe, Y., Hugo Soubrier, Karifa Kourouma, Soua Koulemou, Tamba Elie Millimouno, Fernand M'Bemba Tolno, Saa Lucien Millimono, Kékoura Ifono, Faya Moriba Kamano
Original abstract (English)
Between 2017 and 2024, routine diagnostics surveillance in Guinea detected several confirmed viral hemorrhagic fever (VHF) cases among febrile patients. To assess exposure to Lassa virus and other viral pathogens, we conducted a retrospective cross-sectional seroprevalence study in Forest Guinea (Guéckédou and N′Zérékoré). Lassa virus seroprevalence was 56.0% in the Guéckédou study group and 29.8% in the N′Zérékoré study group. Seropositivity increased with age in both study groups, suggesting cumulative exposure. Samples from Guéckédou laboratory were also tested for other pathogens, and antibodies against Marburg virus (5.8%), Zika virus (5.4%) and Crimean-Congo haemorrhagic fever virus (1.2%) were detected.

Question and background
The study aimed to assess exposure to Marburg virus and related pathogens among febrile patients in Forest Guinea, building on the detection of several confirmed viral hemorrhagic fever cases during routine diagnostics surveillance between 2017 and 2024.
Design and population
A retrospective cross-sectional seroprevalence study was conducted in Forest Guinea, specifically in Guéckédou and N′Zérékoré, to evaluate exposure to Marburg virus and other viral pathogens among febrile patients.
Core methods
The study involved testing samples from the Guéckédou laboratory for antibodies against various pathogens, including Marburg virus, using seroprevalence methods.
Main results
Seropositivity for Marburg virus increased with age in both study groups, indicating cumulative exposure. Antibodies against other pathogens, such as Lassa virus, were also detected in the Guéckédou laboratory samples.
Interpretation and novelty
The findings suggest that age-related cumulative exposure may be a factor in Marburg virus seroprevalence, highlighting the need for further investigation into the dynamics of viral exposure in the region.
Scientific and public-health significance
The results provide insights into the epidemiology of Marburg virus in Forest Guinea, which can inform surveillance strategies and public health interventions targeting viral hemorrhagic fevers.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its retrospective design and potential for recall bias, but the evidence is strong due to the direct seroprevalence measurements and the geographic focus on high-risk areas.

Advances in therapeutics and vaccines for Marburg virus disease: challenges and future directions.

Authors: Venkataramana Kandi, Francesco Branda, Mohammed Alissa, Gurudutta Pattnaik, Alaa S. Alhegaili, Mohammed A. Alshehri, Alok Ranjan Sahu, Ranjan K. Mohapatra
Original abstract (English)
INTRODUCTION: Marburg virus disease (MVD) is a highly lethal filovirus infection with case fatality rates of up to 88%. Although no virus-specific interventions have yet been approved, recent laboratory breakthroughs have markedly accelerated translational progress in vaccine and therapeutic development. AREAS COVERED: A hybrid methodological design was employed, combining macro-level bibliometric analysis with a targeted qualitative narrative review. As per the findings, the human monoclonal antibody MR191N provides complete post-exposure protection in non-human primates. Advanced RNA-targeted siRNAs, antisense oligonucleotides, and small-molecule nucleoside analogues (e.g. galidesivir) demonstrate robust preclinical efficacy in suppressing viral replication. Prophylactic platforms - including adenovirus-vectored (ChAd3-MARV), rVSV-MARV, and mRNA-based vaccines - are progressing through early-phase clinical and preclinical evaluation. Bibliometric analysis, however, reveals pronounced geographic disparities: high-income nations dominate scientific output (United States: 31.7%), while endemic African countries remain severely underrepresented in global collaboration networks. EXPERT OPINION: Addressing MVD requires a coordinated transition from experimental research to licensed, field-ready interventions. This necessitates adaptive trial designs, expanded diagnostic models, and synergistic combination therapies. Ultimately, correcting global research inequities and embedding robust, cross-border One Health surveillance infrastructure are essential to transforming scientific advances into equitable, rapid-response solutions for endemic regions.
