Original Abstract
<b>Marburg virus disease (MVD)</b> is a rare but highly virulent hemorrhagic fever caused by the <i>Marburg virus</i> (family <i>Filoviridae</i>), with a high case fatality rate and significant public health implications. This research develops a five-compartment Caputo fractional-order mathematical model incorporating quarantine to investigate the transmission dynamics of MVD. The theoretical analysis establishes the existence, uniqueness, positivity, and boundedness of the model solutions, ensuring its mathematical validity. Global sensitivity analysis is performed to identify the most influential epidemiological parameters governing disease spread.To obtain accurate numerical solutions, the model is solved using the Legendre spectral collocation method, and its accuracy is validated through comparisons with classical numerical methods and residual error analysis. A physics-informed neural network (PINN) framework is further developed to integrate the governing fractional differential equations with deep learning, enabling accurate prediction of disease dynamics while preserving the underlying epidemiological structure.The study also formulates an optimal control framework to evaluate the impact of preventive measures, early detection with quarantine, and treatment under multiple intervention scenarios. Cost-effectiveness analysis is carried out using standard economic indicators to determine the most efficient intervention strategy. The results demonstrate that combining preventive measures with quarantine provides the greatest reduction in disease transmission while remaining the most cost-effective strategy. Overall, this research integrates fractional-order mathematical modeling, numerical analysis, machine learning, and optimal control to provide quantitative insights for the effective management and control of Marburg virus disease.

From Zoonotic Spillover to Hemorrhagic Crisis: Molecular Insights and Epidemiology of Ethiopia’s 2025 Marburg Outbreak Amid Preparedness Gaps and Historical Lethality

Authors: Getnet Molla, Zemenu Bitew, Molalegne Bitew
Original Abstract
Ethiopia confirmed its first Marburg virus disease (MVD) outbreak on 14 November 2025 in Jinka (Omo Valley area), where a single spillover from Rousettus aegyptiacus resulted in nine laboratory-confirmed cases and eight deaths (CFR 89%). Transmission occurred in the context of concurrent cholera and large-scale population displacement. Despite these pressures, case confirmation was achieved within 48 hours, >90% of contacts were traced, and same-day L-gene sequencing placed the isolate within the East African lineage associated with the 2024 Rwanda outbreak. This review summarizes key molecular determinants of MARV virulence including GP–NPC1 mediated entry, VP24-driven interferon antagonism, and GP-associated endothelial dysfunction and examines Ethiopia’s contrasting strengths in genomic capacity and PHEOC coordination versus persistent constraints in diagnostics, clinical readiness, and countermeasure access. We outline a feasible seven-day containment framework integrating low-cost molecular testing, remdesivir therapy, and VSV-MARV ring vaccination to prevent further spread in resource-limited settings.
