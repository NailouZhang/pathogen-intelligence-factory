通过结构动力学和免疫模拟分析,针对 马尔堡病毒 自扩增 RNA 疫苗 进行免疫信息学和反向疫苗学设计

Immunoinformatics and reverse vaccinology design of self-amplifying RNA vaccine against marburg virus with structural dynamics and immune simulation analysis
作者： Md. Aftabur Rahman, Lamyea Kabir, Nadia Ahmed, Atish Barua, Mohammad Hasan, Ahmad Abdullah Mahdeen
摘要中文翻译马尔堡病毒病 (MVD) 是一种致命的人类人畜共患疾病,由 马尔堡病毒 (MARV) 引起,马尔堡病毒 (MARV) 是一种单链 RNA 病毒,属于丝状病毒科正马尔堡病毒属。尽管该病毒的发病率和死亡率很高,特别是在非洲,但目前尚无批准的 疫苗 或有效的 抗病毒药物 疗法用于 MARV。认识到其流行潜力以及将其列入世界卫生组织优先病原体蓝图清单中,采用免疫信息学方法设计了新型多表位 saRNA 疫苗 候选药物(MB-saVaxI 和 MB-saVaxII),通过 CD8 + 、 CD4 + 和 B 细胞表位靶向病毒 GP、NP、VP40 和 VP35。这些表位源自靶蛋白序列,并在抗原性、非过敏性、无毒性和保守性验证后整合到 MB-saVaxI 和 MB-saVaxII 中。此外,选定的表位通过各种接头与合适的佐剂缀合,从而能够设计出潜在稳定的 疫苗。此外,设计的MB-saVaxI和MB-saVaxII被纳入标准saRNA模型,其中ORF-2编码目标疫苗肽。此外,生物物理分析表明,所开发的 疫苗 是碱性的(pI < 7)、可溶的(GRAVY < 0)和稳定的(脂肪族指数 < 80)。随后,MB-saVaxI 与 TLR2 (-938.3) 和 TLR4 (-902.4) 的对接,以及 MB-saVaxII 与 TLR3 (-1485.4) 和 TLR7 (-1543.6) 的对接显示出显着的亲和力,并通过 MM-GBSA 和分子动力学模拟验证了详细的氢键相互作用。此外,MB-saVaxI 和 MB-saVaxII 被有效地克隆到大肠杆菌中,免疫模拟表明 疫苗 触发了强大的先天性和适应性免疫,具有持续的记忆反应和 TH 细胞激活,IFN-γ 和 IL-2 的产生证明了这一点。此外,MB-saVaxI 和 MB-saVaxII 的 mRNA 结构在 宿主 的整个表达途径中保持稳定。然而,需要进行广泛的体外和体内研究来最终确定该 疫苗 候选药物的安全性和有效性。

Immunoinformatics and reverse vaccinology design of self-amplifying RNA vaccine against marburg virus with structural dynamics and immune simulation analysis

Authors: Md. Aftabur Rahman, Lamyea Kabir, Nadia Ahmed, Atish Barua, Mohammad Hasan, Ahmad Abdullah Mahdeen
Original abstract (English)
Marburg virus disease (MVD), a deadly zoonotic illness in humans, is caused by the Marburg virus (MARV), a single-stranded RNA virus belonging to the Orthomarburgvirus genus of the Filoviridae family. There are currently no approved vaccines or efficient antiviral therapies for MARV, despite high morbidity and fatality rates of the virus, particularly in Africa. Recognizing its epidemic potential and its inclusion on the WHO list of blueprint priority pathogens, an immunoinformatics approach was used to design novel multi-epitope saRNA vaccine candidates (MB-saVaxI and MB-saVaxII) targeting the viral GP, NP, VP40, and VP35 via CD8 + , CD4 + , and B cell epitopes. These epitopes were derived from target protein sequences and incorporated into MB-saVaxI and MB-saVaxII after validation for antigenicity, non-allergenicity, non-toxicity, and conservation. Additionally, the selected epitopes were conjugated to suitable adjuvants via various linkers, thereby enabling the design of a potentially stable vaccine. Additionally, the designed MB-saVaxI and MB-saVaxII were incorporated into the standard saRNA model, in which ORF-2 encodes the target vaccine peptide. Moreover, biophysical analyses unveiled that the developed vaccines are basic (pI < 7), soluble (GRAVY < 0), and stable (aliphatic index < 80). Subsequently, docking of MB-saVaxI to TLR2 (-938.3) and TLR4 (-902.4), and MB-saVaxII to TLR3 (-1485.4) and TLR7 (-1543.6) demonstrated significant affinity, with detailed hydrogen-bond interactions validated by MM-GBSA and molecular dynamics simulations. Furthermore, MB-saVaxI and MB-saVaxII were effectively cloned in-silico into Escherichia coli, and immune simulations indicated that the vaccines triggered robust innate and adaptive immunity, with a sustained memory response and TH cell activation, as evidenced by IFN-γ and IL-2 production. Additionally, the mRNA structure of MB-saVaxI and MB-saVaxII remained stable throughout the expression pathway in the host. However, extensive in-vitro and in-vivo studies are needed to establish the safety and efficacy of this vaccine candidate conclusively.

Question and background
The study aimed to address the lack of effective vaccines or antiviral therapies for Marburg virus (MARV), a deadly zoonotic illness. The research sought to design novel multi-epitope self-amplifying RNA (saRNA) vaccine candidates targeting the viral GP, NP, VP40, and VP35 via CD8 + , CD4 + , and B cell epitopes.
Design and population
The study involved the design and in-silico cloning of two saRNA vaccine candidates (MB-saVaxI and MB-saVaxII) into the standard saRNA model, incorporating validated epitopes and suitable adjuvants. The study did not involve human or animal participants.
Core methods
The study utilized immunoinformatics approaches to identify and validate epitopes, conjugate them to adjuvants, and incorporate them into the saRNA model. Biophysical analyses were conducted to assess the stability and solubility of the vaccines. Immune simulations were performed to evaluate the potential immune response.
Main results
The study demonstrated significant affinity of the designed vaccines to TLR2, TLR3, TLR4, and TLR7, with detailed hydrogen-bond interactions validated by molecular dynamics simulations. The vaccines triggered robust innate and adaptive immune responses, evidenced by IFN-γ and IL-2 production, and remained stable throughout the expression pathway in the host.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted the findings as novel and promising, indicating the potential for the designed vaccines to elicit strong immune responses. The study highlighted the use of immunoinformatics and reverse vaccinology in the design of saRNA vaccines against MARV.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings could inform the development of effective vaccines against MARV, contributing to the prevention and control of Marburg virus disease. The study's approach may also be applicable to other filoviruses.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study's limitations include the need for extensive in-vitro and in-vivo studies to establish the safety and efficacy of the vaccine candidates conclusively. The evidence strength is moderate due to the in-silico nature of the study.

Filovirus Seroreactivity Beyond Virus-Specific Outbreak Regions in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, 2015-2023

Authors: Sydney Merritt, Jean Paul Kompany, Mukadi, P., Megan Halbrook, Kamy Musene, Michael Beya, Handdy Kalengi, Merly Tambu, John Daniel Kelly, Aquena H. Ball
Original abstract (English)
The ongoing Bundibugyo virus disease (BVD) outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) is the second-largest Ebola disease outbreak in recorded history, and only the second recorded BVD outbreak in the country. While the DRC has recorded 18 filovirus disease outbreaks, the historical frequency and geographic distribution of human filovirus exposures remain poorly understood. Using a multiplexed multi-filovirus immunoassay, we assessed antibody reactivity against antigens of filoviruses linked to human disease, Bundibugyo virus (BDBV), Ebola virus (EBOV), Marburg virus (MARV), and Sudan virus (SUDV), among eleven cohorts from seven provinces between 2015-2023 (n=5,852). Participants with median fluorescence intensity values exceeding established antigen-specific cutoffs were classified as seroreactive. Seroreactivity against glycoprotein (GP) targets of all four viruses were detected in every cohort, demonstrating evidence of filovirus or an antigenically related exposure well beyond known outbreak regions. Seroreactivity estimates varied by cohort site, with 60.8% of participants (n=175/288) in Mongala Province in April 2016 considered seroreactive against BDBV GP. In this cohort, 4.2%, 29.9%, and 34.7% of participants were seroreactive against EBOV, MARV and SUDV GP, respectively yet only 1.7% were seroreactive against all four GP antigens. In adjusted analyses, site was the primary predictor of seroreactivity, with odds for BDBV seroreactivity 9.1-fold (95%CI 7.0-11.9) and 9.5-fold (95%CI 6.9-13.2) higher in Mongala and Tshuapa provinces than in Kwilu. Similarly, the adjusted odds of MARV and SUDV GP seroreactivity were 4.5-fold and 7.1-fold higher in Mongala, and 3.7-fold and 6.1-fold higher in Tshuapa, than Kwilu. Among 280 individuals sampled longitudinally, BDBV GP detected seroconversion was comparable by site, with 12.8% in Kwilu and 10.4% in Mongala having detected seroreactivity three and five years after initial contact, respectively. Overall, these results indicate widespread, geographically heterogeneous filovirus seroreactivity across sampled cohorts. These findings may reflect zoonotic spillover events, leading to undetected human-to-human filovirus circulation, including paucisymptomatic or asymptomatic infections, or exposure to as-yet unidentified antigenically cross-reactive viruses. These data highlight gaps in understanding the historical and geographic distribution of filovirus exposure in the DRC and support further prospective serological, epidemiological, and molecular investigations, especially given the current outbreak.

Advertisement IDWeek 2-26 in Washington, DC (Adobe Stock) A systematic review presented as a poster at IDWeek in Washington, DC, held from October 21 to 24, found that transmission of viral hemorrhagic fevers to health care personnel in high-income countries was rare. Across 35 studies, presenting author Loukas Kakoullis, MD, PhD, MPH, Infectious Disease Fellow at Massachusetts General Hospital & Brigham and Women's Hospital in Boston, Massachusetts, and his fellow researchers reported 1,607 health care personnel exposures and 37 infections, an infection proportion of 2.3%. Among laboratory personnel, 296 exposures were reported, with no infections. The poster, “Healthcare-Associated High-Consequence Viral Hemorrhagic Fever Exposure Events and Outcomes in High-Income Countries: A Systematic Review,” examined occupational exposures and associated infection outcomes. Alongside its findings on transmission, the review identified a need for more consistent descriptions of exposure events to support risk assessment and response. Examining Exposures Before Disease Recognition The researchers described viral hemorrhagic fevers as a risk to health care personnel. Delayed recognition can contribute to exposures, as can failure to isolate patients and inadequate use of personal protective equipment (PPE). The review sought to characterize these exposures in high-income countries and determine the associated infection outcomes. Sign in or sign up Following PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines, the team searched MEDLINE through PubMed, Web of Science, and Embase. Eligible cases involved viral hemorrhagic fever diagnosed after admission that prompted an investigation. The researchers used the World Bank classification to identify high-income countries. Their outcomes included exposures among health care and laboratory personnel, with attention to exposure types, control measures, and subsequent infections. The search identified 7,719 records from databases or registers and 4 references from other sources. After duplicates were removed, 5,890 records remained for screening. The final review included 35 studies. The median exposure time before disease recognition and implementation of viral hemorrhagic fever precautions was 3 days. Source origins included Africa in 15 reports and Asia in 10. Europe accounted for 5, South America for 4, and North America for 1. Reporting locations differed, with 17 reports from Europe, 9 from North America, 8 from Asia, and 1 from South America. Exposure Circumstances Varied Lassa virus was the most frequently reported virus, appearing in 8 studies. Crimean-Congo hemorrhagic fever virus appeared in 7. Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus and Ebola virus each appeared in 6 cases, while Andes virus appeared in 4 cases. Marburg virus appeared in 2, and Hantaan virus and Sabiá virus each appeared in 1. Direct contact with body fluids was reported in 80% of studies, making it the most frequently documented exposure category. Physical contact without body fluids appeared in 69%. These percentages describe the proportion of studies reporting each circumstance, rather than the proportion of individual personnel exposed. PPE use was another prominent finding. Some, but not all, required PPE was used in 49% of studies, while 37% reported no PPE use. Handling laboratory specimens appeared in 43% of studies, and indirect contact with body fluids appeared in 31%. Intubation, whether performed by the exposed person or occurring in their presence, was reported in 23% of studies. Handling contaminated waste also appeared in 23%. Mucosal exposure was reported in 20%, and performing cardiopulmonary resuscitation appeared in 17%. Surgery was reported in 6%, while sharps injuries appeared in 3%. Contact Tracing Was the Most Frequently Reported Intervention Contact tracing was documented in 89% of studies. Monitoring exposed contacts appeared in 83%, and testing contacts appeared in 80%. Isolation wards were used in 57% of studies, while PPE use after case identification was reported in 46%. Specialized sample management was reported in 34% of studies. Specialized waste management was reported in 23%, and quarantine of exposed contacts appeared in 20%. Staff cohorting was reported in 6%. For exposure management, ribavirin prophylaxis was reported in 20% of studies, while ribavirin treatment was reported in 6%. The poster’s central finding was clear: “Transmission of viral hemorrhagic fevers to health care personnel in high-income settings is rare.” However, its call for standardized exposure definitions remains an important part of that conclusion. More consistent reporting would help guide the categorization of exposures and teams' responses, while preserving the distinctions between exposure circumstances and resulting infections. The molecular diagnostic test can detect 16 pathogens, including multiple Ebola viruses, malaria parasites, Marburg virus, West Nile virus and Lassa virus, from a single whole blood sample in approximately one hour, helping healthcare professionals identify suspected cases, guide patient management and strengthen outbreak response. bioMérieux, a world leader in in vitro diagnostics, has announced that the World Health Organization (WHO) has granted Emergency Use Listing (EUL) for the BIOFIRE® Global Fever Special Pathogens Panel, strengthening access to differential diagnostic testing in support of the ongoing Ebola Bundibugyo outbreak affecting the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and neighboring countries. The WHO listing comes as national health authorities, international organizations and local partners continue efforts to contain one of the largest outbreaks ever recorded of Ebola virus. While no specific treatment or licensed vaccine has been developed against the Bundibugyo strain, fast and accurate diagnostic testing exists and remains the first line of public health action to identify suspected cases, guide patient management, support contact tracing and help limit disease transmission. Manufactured by BioFire Defense, LLC, a bioMérieux company, the BIOFIRE® Global Fever Special Pathogens Panel enables healthcare professionals to simultaneously test for 16 pathogens from a single whole blood sample in approximately one hour, including multiple Ebola viruses, malaria parasites, Marburg virus, West Nile virus and Lassa virus. By helping differentiate Ebola from other causes of acute febrile illness with similar symptoms, the panel supports faster clinical decision making and more effective use of limited healthcare resources. The WHO Emergency Use Listing serves as an international reference providing an additional accelerated pathway for health authorities, international organizations and procurement partners to consider the panel as part of the response to the current outbreak and future preparedness efforts. “The WHO Emergency Use Listing is an important milestone for public health preparedness and outbreak response," said Dr. Charles K. Cooper, Executive Vice President, Chief Medical Officer, bioMérieux. "When an outbreak occurs, early and accurate diagnosis can make the difference between isolated cases and wider transmission. Expanding access to timely differential diagnostics helps healthcare professionals act more quickly, allocate resources more effectively and better protect communities." Importantly, the panel includes detection of Bundibugyo ebolavirus, the strain responsible for the current outbreak. This capability was developed years before the emergence of the current epidemic as part of BioFire Defense's long-term commitment to protecting against biothreats and emerging infectious diseases. Supporting health authorities to reinforce diagnostics capabilities Since the beginning of the outbreak, bioMérieux has worked alongside Ministries of Health, public health agencies and local partners to assess diagnostic needs, strengthen laboratory capacity and support deployment of testing resources across affected regions. The company has expanded manufacturing capacity, accelerated deliveries of instruments and tests, and provided training and on-site support to laboratory professionals to help ensure rapid implementation and sustainable diagnostic capabilities. bioMérieux has already delivered instruments and reagents to the Rwanda Biomedical Centre in June, together with training and implementation support for laboratory teams. bioMérieux also supported Uganda preparedness by delivering Instruments and reagents to the Uganda National Health Laboratory Services. In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, bioMérieux is working with the National Institute for Biomedical Research (INRB) and health authorities to provide additional instruments and tests. These contributions are intended to complement existing diagnostic capabilities and support the use of molecular differential testing when further investigation of suspected cases is required. "It requires strong partnerships, trained healthcare professionals, laboratory capacity and sustained surveillance systems. The BIOFIRE® Global Fever Special Pathogens Panel is a molecular diagnostic test manufactured by BioFire Defense, LLC, a bioMérieux company. It enables healthcare professionals to test a single whole blood sample for 16 pathogens in approximately one hour. Which pathogens can the BIOFIRE Global Fever Special Pathogens Panel detect? The panel detects 16 pathogens associated with acute febrile illness, including multiple Ebola viruses, malaria parasites, Marburg virus, West Nile virus and Lassa virus. It includes detection of Bundibugyo ebolavirus, the strain identified in the current outbreak described in this release. Why is WHO Emergency Use Listing important for Ebola outbreak response? WHO Emergency Use Listing provides an international reference and an accelerated pathway for health authorities, international organisations and procurement partners to consider the panel during the current response and in future preparedness planning. How can differential diagnostics support Ebola case management? Several causes of acute febrile illness can present with similar symptoms. Rapid differential testing can help healthcare professionals distinguish Ebola from other infections, guide patient management, support contact tracing and use limited healthcare resources more effectively.
