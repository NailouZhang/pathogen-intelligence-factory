Quantifying the extinction probability of Marburg virus disease outbreaks: A continuous-time Markov chain model integrating a multi-type branching process

Authors: Ester Damiano, Jacob Ismael Irunde, Maranya Makuru Mayengo, Ester Damiano Damiano, Jacob I. Irunde
Original abstract (English)
Understanding the extinction dynamics of Marburg virus disease is crucial, particularly during the early stages of an outbreak when stochastic effects strongly influence disease outcomes. Although deterministic models have provided valuable insights into virus transmission dynamics, they cannot adequately capture random transmission events or quantify extinction probabilities, particularly when outbreaks begin with few infected cases. This study develops a continuous-time Markov chain stochastic model derived from a deterministic multi-host transmission model. The model incorporates a multi-type branching process, in which distinct infectious types are tracked according to their contributions to secondary infections, enabling estimation of disease extinction probabilities during the early outbreak phase. The extinction analysis shows that outbreaks initiated by exposed humans have a higher probability of extinction (21.6%) compared with those initiated by infectious humans (13.7%), whereas outbreaks initiated by infected bats have an extinction probability of only 0.36%, highlighting their role as important reservoirs sustaining virus transmission. Furthermore, the results demonstrate that reducing human-to-human transmission, decreasing viral shedding, and increasing environmental viral decay significantly enhance the likelihood of disease extinction. The proposed framework provides a quantitative tool for assessing Marburg virus disease outbreak risk and valuable insights for designing early interventions for human transmission, environmental contamination, and cross-species interactions.

Climate change, land use alterations, urbanization, population growth, and increased human–animal interactions may reshape pathogen ecology and create new opportunities for viral emergence and transmission. This review examines the pathophysiological mechanisms, epidemiological patterns, and major outbreaks associated with VHFs, with particular emphasis on how environmental change, ecological disruption, and infectious disease dynamics interact. Marburg virus disease is a severe, often lethal hemorrhagic fever caused by Marburg virus, a member of the Filoviridae family first identified in 1967 after simultaneous outbreaks in Marburg and Frankfurt, Germany, and Belgrade, Yugoslavia, linked to laboratory work with African green monkeys imported from Uganda. The pathogen maintains a natural wildlife reservoir in the Egyptian fruit bat, and human infections carry historical case fatality rates ranging between 24 percent and 90 percent depending on the specific viral strain, host factors, and local clinical capabilities. Clinically, the infection typically begins with an abrupt, nonspecific febrile illness characterized by high fever, severe headache, chills, and profound fatigue, before progressing rapidly to severe gastrointestinal manifestations such as intractable vomiting, profuse watery diarrhea, and intense abdominal pain. Advanced stages frequently involve coagulation abnormalities, marked vascular permeability, and overt bleeding, which can precipitate multi-organ failure, circulatory collapse, and shock. Island Pharmaceuticals Limited (ASX:ILA) is a clinical-stage biotechnology company focused on developing broad-acting antiviral therapies for infectious disease threats with limited or no approved treatments. The company's lead programme, Galidesivir, is an adenosine nucleoside analogue that works by blocking viral RNA polymerase replication and has demonstrated meaningful activity against more than 20 RNA viruses in preclinical settings, including filoviruses such as Marburg, Ebola, and Bundibugyo. The Marburg Opportunity and the Animal Rule Pathway Marburg virus represents the only Category A bioterrorism threat that currently has no approved treatment available in the United States Strategic National Stockpile. Its extremely high case fatality rate and history of interest from a bioterrorism perspective have made developing a countermeasure a persistent priority for public health agencies. The FDA's Animal Rule permits approval of medical countermeasures for diseases where human efficacy trials are not feasible or ethical, using controlled animal studies as the primary evidence for efficacy. This approach has successfully produced eight approved bioterrorism countermeasures since 2012, most of which have entered the Strategic National Stockpile under multi-year procurement contracts. Island Pharmaceuticals acquired Galidesivir from BioCryst in July 2025, following earlier development funded by US government research agencies. Within 15 months of that acquisition, the company progressed through manufacturing, regulatory engagement, and the commencement of non-human primate surgeries. The FDA has confirmed the Animal Rule pathway is appropriate for Marburg, provided Orphan Drug Designation, and confirmed that Galidesivir would qualify for a Tropical Disease Priority Review Voucher on approval — a designation with meaningful commercial value based on recent transactions. Near-Term Clinical Milestones and Uganda Programme The imminent dose-response study beginning in early December will evaluate different dosing levels in Marburg-infected primates to identify the optimal therapeutic dose and treatment window. Results are expected within weeks of the observation period closing. A second study assessing the time window for effective treatment initiation is scheduled to follow in early 2027, with the pivotal efficacy study expected to begin around mid-2027. The historical NHP survival data from prior work already shows compelling results, including 94 per cent survival when treatment commenced within 48 hours of infection — a treatment window that is clinically meaningful relative to real-world outbreak scenarios. In parallel with the animal programme, Island Pharmaceuticals has secured approvals from the Ugandan government and international health authorities to evaluate Galidesivir under the WHO's Monitored Emergency Use of Unregistered and Investigational Interventions framework in the context of the ongoing Ebola outbreak in the country. This represents the first opportunity to generate prospective human safety and efficacy data in a real outbreak setting, complementing the controlled animal studies and supporting a more comprehensive regulatory dossier.
