Authors: Ojomo OA, Moges T, Cancedda C, Cotton P, Oluwaseun Ayoola Ojomo, Tayechalem Moges, Corrado Cancedda, Philip Cotton, Cotton P.
Original abstract (English)
The resurgence of viral haemorrhagic fevers across Africa underscores the fragility of global health security and the persistent ethical vulnerabilities facing frontline healthcare workers. Recurrent outbreaks of Ebola virus disease in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and Uganda, alongside Marburg virus disease alerts in several African countries, show how rapidly localised epidemics can become regional and international public health threats. 1 2 These crises reveal that high-consequence infectious disease outbreaks are not isolated national emergencies. They are interconnected global health challenges that expose structural inequities in preparedness, response and health system resilience.3 4

马尔堡病毒病对卢旺达康复者及一线医护人员的心理社会影响:一项横断面调查。

Psychosocial impact of Marburg virus disease on recovered individuals and frontline health workers in Rwanda: a cross-sectional survey.
作者： Ruth Tsigebrhan, Darius Gishoma, Asmeret Andebirhan, Shadrack Ntirenganya, Kara Neil, Egide Munyaneza, Eugene Harerimana, Athanase Nsengiyumva, Anita Kamikazi, Amanda Rurangwa

问题与背景
本研究旨在探讨2024年MVD 暴发后,马尔堡病毒病对卢旺达康复者及一线医护人员的心理社会影响。研究动机源于了解该疾病对受影响人群心理健康后果的必要性。
设计与对象
在卢旺达首都基加利进行了一项横断面调查,该城市在2024年9月24日至10月24日的MVD 暴发期间设有两个治疗中心。所有符合纳入标准的51名MVD康复者和98名一线医护人员均被纳入研究。
核心方法
使用患者健康问卷(PHQ-9)评估抑郁,使用广泛性焦虑障碍量表(GAD-7)评估焦虑,使用DSM-5创伤后应激障碍检查清单(PCL-5)评估创伤后应激障碍(PTSD)。使用改编版埃博拉病毒污名与歧视问卷评估感知污名,使用多维社会支持感知量表(MPSS)评估社会支持。
主要结果

解释与创新
作者得出结论,卢旺达MVD 暴发被控制六个月后,许多一线医护人员仍经历抑郁、焦虑和PTSD。他们强调了研究的新颖性,即关注马尔堡病毒病对康复者和医护人员的心理社会影响。
科研与公卫意义
研究结果表明,应将心理健康和心理社会支持干预措施纳入传染病应对计划,以应对受影响个体和医护人员持续的心理负担。
局限与证据强度
本研究的局限性在于其横断面设计,无法进行因果推断。由于依赖自我报告测量和调查环境中的潜在偏倚,证据强度中等。

Psychosocial impact of Marburg virus disease on recovered individuals and frontline health workers in Rwanda: a cross-sectional survey.

Authors: Ruth Tsigebrhan, Darius Gishoma, Asmeret Andebirhan, Shadrack Ntirenganya, Kara Neil, Egide Munyaneza, Eugene Harerimana, Athanase Nsengiyumva, Anita Kamikazi, Amanda Rurangwa
Original abstract (English)
OBJECTIVES: To examine the psychosocial impact of this disease on recovered individuals and frontline health workers (FHWs) following the 2024 MVD outbreak in Rwanda. DESIGN: a cross-sectional survey. SETTING: Kigali, the capital city of Rwanda, had two treatment centres during the outbreak of the MVD disease from 24 September to 24 October 2024. PARTICIPANTS: All 51 recovered individuals from MVD and 98 FHWs who met the eligibility criteria were involved in the study.Depression was measured using the Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9), anxiety with the Generalised Anxiety Disorder (GAD-7) scale and post-traumatic stress disorder using the PTSD Checklist for DSM-5 (PCL-5). Perceived stigma was measured using the adapted Ebola stigma and discrimination questionnaire and the Multidimensional Scale of Perceived Social Support Scale (MPSS) to assess social support. Wilcoxon rank-sum and Kruskal-Wallis tests examined differences in depression, anxiety and PTSD by sex and profession. Spearman correlation assessed relationships between variables. RESULTS: 115 participants, including 29 recovered people and 86 FHWs. 11.8% of FHWs had moderate to severe depression and 20.7% of recovered people had mild to moderate depression. Moderate to severe anxiety was exhibited by 9.4% of FHWs, and mild anxiety was reported by 17.2% of recovered people. 9.3% of FHWs and one recovered individual had a total PCL score above 33 (PTSD). Among recovered people, there was a significant negative correlation between anxiety and social support (r=-0.36, p value=0.05) and a significant difference in PTSD symptom levels between sexes (p value=0.01). Among FHWs, MVD-related stigma was positively correlated with PTSD symptoms (r=+0.23, p value=0.05). Symptoms of depression (β coef.=-0.19, 95% CI -0.32, -0.05) and anxiety (β coef.=-0.16, 95% CI -0.26, -0.06) decreased with age. Married FHWs had lower levels of depression (β coef.=-2.72, 95% CI -4.96, -0.49) and anxiety (β coef.=-2.35, 95% CI -4.07, -0.62) compared with single individuals. CONCLUSION: Six months post-containment of the MVD outbreak in Rwanda, many FHWs were still experiencing depression, anxiety and PTSD. Contextualised and culturally adapted mental health and psychosocial support interventions are highly recommended for integration into infectious disease response plans.

Machine learning-enhanced stochastic modeling of Marburg virus transmission in human–bat populations

Authors: Syed Modassir Hussain, Bilal Ali, Sidra Jubair, Waqar Ishaq, Dilrabo Akhmedova
Original abstract (English)
A serious threat to global health is the Marburg virus, which affects human beings as well as non-human primates. This study develops a comprehensive stochastic mathematical model to examine Marburg virus transmission dynamics in human and bat populations, incorporating vaccination effects and random environmental factors including immigration, climate change, and behavioral variations. In this work, we employ stochastic differential equations coupled with neural network analysis to capture the complex disease dynamics. First, we prove that the solution exists globally and is positive. Next, we establish the fundamental stochastic reproduction number, which serves as a threshold parameter determining disease extinction ( R 0 s 1 ) in the population. Numerical simulation is performed to investigate the dynamics of Marburg virus disease in the population. Numerical simulation shows that as value of vaccination increases, it leads to decreases in the spread of Marburg virus. This shows that vaccination is very important to control the spread of Marburg virus in the community. A feedforward neural network (FFNN) is employed as a surrogate model and trained using the numerical solution generated by the Euler–Maruyama scheme. The network learns a continuous nonlinear mapping from simulation time to the corresponding compartmental states, thereby providing a smooth approximation of the realized stochastic trajectory. The simulated dataset is randomly divided into training (70%), validation (15%), and testing (15%) subsets. The accuracy of Neural Networks in prediction and modeling is demonstrated by dividing the data into 70% for training, 15% for testing, and 15% for validation. Validating its accuracy, the model attains a mean squared error of 2.39 × 10−4 averaged over the eight compartments and a regression coefficient close to one (R = 0.999). The results of this study offer public health strategists effective tools for reducing or eliminating Marburg virus.

This batch has been earmarked to support Island's approved emergency-use program during the ongoing Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak. Following completion of API manufacture, Island disclosed it will undertake the required finishing, vialing and packaging activities ahead of planned shipment to Uganda for investigational therapeutic use under the World Health Organization's Monitored Emergency Use of Unregistered and Investigational Interventions (MEURI) framework. Treatment under MEURI will occur under a prospective protocol, enabling collection of clinical, safety and virological data from patients treated with Galidesivir. Second Batch to Support Ebola Strategy and Marburg NHP Study A second 5kg GMP API batch is targeted for completion by the end of November 2026. According to the filing, this additional supply is intended to support Island's broader Ebola strategy and to provide material for the company's planned pivotal Marburg non-human primate study under the US Food and Drug Administration's Animal Rule pathway. Manufacturing is being undertaken at PIHS under the company's existing agreement. The broader program also encompasses analytical method validation, reference standard preparation and stability studies, establishing the manufacturing and quality framework required to support Galidesivir's continued development, potential outbreak deployment and future regulatory and government procurement activities. Galidesivir Development Background and Bundibugyo Outbreak Data The filing states that Galidesivir has benefited from more than US$70 million of prior US Government investment and has existing human clinical experience together with historical efficacy data in non-human primate models of Ebola and Marburg virus disease.
