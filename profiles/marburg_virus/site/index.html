从人畜共患 跨物种溢出 到出血性危机:埃塞俄比亚 2025 年马尔堡 暴发 的分子见解和 流行病学 的准备差距和历史致死率

From Zoonotic Spillover to Hemorrhagic Crisis: Molecular Insights and Epidemiology of Ethiopia’s 2025 Marburg Outbreak Amid Preparedness Gaps and Historical Lethality
作者： Getnet Molla, Zemenu Bitew, Molalegne Bitew
摘要中文翻译埃塞俄比亚于 2025 年 11 月 14 日在金卡(奥莫河谷地区)确认了首例 马尔堡病毒病 (MVD) 暴发,其中一只来自埃及红蝠的 跨物种溢出 导致 9 例实验室确诊病例和 8 例死亡(病死率 89%)。传播 发生在霍乱和大规模人口流离失所的背景下。尽管面临这些压力,我们还是在 48 小时内完成了病例确认,追踪了超过 90% 的接触者,并且当天的 L 基因测序将该分离株置于与 2024 年卢旺达 暴发 相关的东非谱系内。本综述总结了 MARV 毒力的关键分子决定因素,包括 GP-NPC1 介导的进入、VP24 驱动的干扰素拮抗作用和 GP 相关的内皮功能障碍,并研究了埃塞俄比亚在基因组能力和公共卫生紧急事件协调方面的对比优势与诊断、临床准备和对策获取方面的持续限制。我们概述了一个可行的 7 天遏制框架,整合了低成本分子检测、瑞德西韦治疗和 VSV-MARV 环 疫苗接种,以防止在资源有限的环境中进一步传播。

范围与问题
该综述审查了埃塞俄比亚的 2025 年 马尔堡病毒病 (MVD) 暴发,重点关注毒力、流行病学模式和公共卫生应对措施的分子决定因素。中心问题围绕了解 跨物种溢出 事件、传播 动态以及在霍乱和人口流离失所同时发生的资源有限环境中遏制措施的有效性。研究人群包括奥莫河谷金卡受影响的社区,病原体是来自东非谱系的马尔堡病毒 (MARV)。
证据基础与方法
该审查基于 2025 年 暴发 的关键分子和流行病学数据的摘要。它包括有关病例确认、接触者追踪和基因组测序的信息。审查方法包括总结MARV毒力的分子决定因素,并比较埃塞俄比亚在公共卫生紧急行动和协调(PHEOC)方面的优势和限制。
共识与结论
审查得出的结论是,埃塞俄比亚展示了基因组能力和公共卫生紧急行动中心协调方面的优势,实现了快速病例确认和高接触者追踪率。然而,诊断、临床准备和采取对策方面始终存在限制。暴发 是由埃及红毛蜂 跨物种溢出 引起的,导致病死率 (CFR) 高达 89%。
争议与缺口
审查强调了埃塞俄比亚对 暴发 的反应中对比的优势和限制。低成本分子检测、瑞德西韦疗法和 VSV-MARV 环 疫苗接种 在预防进一步传播方面的有效性存在证据差距。审查还指出了同时发生的霍乱和大规模人口流离失所带来的挑战。
科研与实践启示
该审查概述了一个可行的 7 天遏制框架,整合了低成本分子检测、瑞德西韦治疗和 VSV-MARV 环 疫苗接种。它强调需要改进诊断、临床准备以及在资源有限的环境中获得对策。该框架旨在防止 MVD 在类似情况下进一步传播。

From Zoonotic Spillover to Hemorrhagic Crisis: Molecular Insights and Epidemiology of Ethiopia’s 2025 Marburg Outbreak Amid Preparedness Gaps and Historical Lethality

Authors: Getnet Molla, Zemenu Bitew, Molalegne Bitew
Original abstract (English)
Ethiopia confirmed its first Marburg virus disease (MVD) outbreak on 14 November 2025 in Jinka (Omo Valley area), where a single spillover from Rousettus aegyptiacus resulted in nine laboratory-confirmed cases and eight deaths (CFR 89%). Transmission occurred in the context of concurrent cholera and large-scale population displacement. Despite these pressures, case confirmation was achieved within 48 hours, >90% of contacts were traced, and same-day L-gene sequencing placed the isolate within the East African lineage associated with the 2024 Rwanda outbreak. This review summarizes key molecular determinants of MARV virulence including GP–NPC1 mediated entry, VP24-driven interferon antagonism, and GP-associated endothelial dysfunction and examines Ethiopia’s contrasting strengths in genomic capacity and PHEOC coordination versus persistent constraints in diagnostics, clinical readiness, and countermeasure access. We outline a feasible seven-day containment framework integrating low-cost molecular testing, remdesivir therapy, and VSV-MARV ring vaccination to prevent further spread in resource-limited settings.

Scope and question
The review examines the 2025 Marburg virus disease (MVD) outbreak in Ethiopia, focusing on molecular determinants of virulence, epidemiological patterns, and public health responses. The central question revolves around understanding the spillover event, transmission dynamics, and the effectiveness of containment measures in a resource-limited setting amid concurrent cholera and population displacement. The study population includes the affected community in Jinka, Omo Valley, and the pathogen is the Marburg virus (MARV) from the East African lineage.
Evidence base and method
The review is based on a summary of key molecular and epidemiological data from the 2025 outbreak. It includes information on case confirmation, contact tracing, and genomic sequencing. The review method involves summarizing molecular determinants of MARV virulence and comparing Ethiopia's strengths and constraints in public health emergency operations and coordination (PHEOC).
Consensus and conclusions
The review concludes that Ethiopia demonstrated strengths in genomic capacity and PHEOC coordination, achieving rapid case confirmation and high contact tracing rates. However, there are persistent constraints in diagnostics, clinical readiness, and access to countermeasures. The outbreak was caused by a spillover from Rousettus aegyptiacus, resulting in a high case fatality rate (CFR) of 89%.
Controversies and gaps
The review highlights the contrasting strengths and constraints in Ethiopia's response to the outbreak. There are evidence gaps in the effectiveness of low-cost molecular testing, remdesivir therapy, and VSV-MARV ring vaccination in preventing further spread. The review also notes the challenges posed by concurrent cholera and large-scale population displacement.
Research and practice implications
The review outlines a feasible seven-day containment framework integrating low-cost molecular testing, remdesivir therapy, and VSV-MARV ring vaccination. It emphasizes the need for improved diagnostics, clinical readiness, and access to countermeasures in resource-limited settings. The framework aims to prevent further spread of MVD in similar contexts.
