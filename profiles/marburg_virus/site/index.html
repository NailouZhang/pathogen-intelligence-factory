Configurationally Locked Ridaifen-B Analogs as Potent Ebola Virus Entry Inhibitors.

Authors: Shaimaa M Aboukhatwa, Carolina Q Sacramento, Kimberly M Morsheimer, Christian A Zelinski, Ryan Bott, Murugesh Venkatesh, Callum D Owen, Robert A. Davey, Irina N. Gaisina, Lijun Rong
Original abstract (English)
In the face of emerging outbreaks, high mortality rates, and limited treatment options, Ebola virus (EBOV) disease poses a global public health threat, underscoring the need for effective therapies. The unique and critical role that EBOV glycoprotein (GP) plays in the viral life cycle makes it an attractive target for antiviral drug discovery. In this study, we report on the design, synthesis, and evaluation of a series of cyclized ridaifen-B-based inhibitors with enhanced antiviral activity and selectivity against EBOV. Several compounds exhibited nanomolar potency inhibiting EBOV-GP-mediated entry and submicromolar potency against Marburg virus entry. Site-directed mutagenesis indicated inhibitor binding at the EBOV GP1/GP2 fusion loop. The compounds displayed favorable in vitro pharmacokinetic parameters, which encouraged testing the in vivo rapid PK for hit compound 41 . This study highlights the potential of 1,2-dihydronaphthalene and tetrahydronaphthalene derivatives as valuable leads for the development of potent antifiloviral agents targeting EBOV among other filoviruses.

From Zoonotic Spillover to Hemorrhagic Crisis: Molecular Insights and Epidemiology of Ethiopia’s 2025 Marburg Outbreak Amid Preparedness Gaps and Historical Lethality

Authors: Getnet Molla, Zemenu Bitew, Molalegne Bitew
Original abstract (English)
Ethiopia confirmed its first Marburg virus disease (MVD) outbreak on 14 November 2025 in Jinka (Omo Valley area), where a single spillover from Rousettus aegyptiacus resulted in nine laboratory-confirmed cases and eight deaths (CFR 89%). Transmission occurred in the context of concurrent cholera and large-scale population displacement. Despite these pressures, case confirmation was achieved within 48 hours, >90% of contacts were traced, and same-day L-gene sequencing placed the isolate within the East African lineage associated with the 2024 Rwanda outbreak. This review summarizes key molecular determinants of MARV virulence including GP–NPC1 mediated entry, VP24-driven interferon antagonism, and GP-associated endothelial dysfunction and examines Ethiopia’s contrasting strengths in genomic capacity and PHEOC coordination versus persistent constraints in diagnostics, clinical readiness, and countermeasure access. We outline a feasible seven-day containment framework integrating low-cost molecular testing, remdesivir therapy, and VSV-MARV ring vaccination to prevent further spread in resource-limited settings.

Authors: Abdolreza Babamahmoodi, F. Babamahmoodi, Majid Marjani
Original abstract (English)
Context: Recent infectious disease emergencies, including SARS-CoV-2, mpox (2022 - 2024), highly pathogenic avian influenza A(H5N1) in US dairy cattle (2024 - 2025), and the Marburg virus in Rwanda (2024), have strained traditional surveillance systems. Digital health has been proposed as a paradigm shift; however, most reviews have focused on diagnostic accuracy while neglecting equity and governance. Evidence Acquisition: We conducted a structured thematic narrative review of peer-reviewed and grey literature published from January 2010 to March 2025, reported in accordance with PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA-ScR. PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science were searched from February 5 to 18, 2025. Search keywords combined three concept blocks: (1) Digital-health technologies (artificial intelligence, machine learning, mHealth, eHealth, telemedicine, electronic health records, wearables, wastewater surveillance, genomic surveillance, natural-language processing); (2) infectious diseases (outbreak, pandemic, epidemic, COVID-19, Ebola, Zika, mpox, influenza, antimicrobial resistance, Marburg); and (3) public-health functions (surveillance, detection, forecasting, contact tracing, governance, equity). Scope and question
The review examines digital health innovations for infectious disease surveillance, focusing on equity gaps and governance within pandemic preparedness. The central question concerns the role of digital health technologies in surveillance, detection, forecasting, contact tracing, governance, and equity.
Evidence base and method
This is a structured thematic narrative review of peer-reviewed and grey literature published from January 2010 to March 2025, following PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA-ScR guidelines. Searches were conducted in PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science from February 5 to 18, 2025, using keywords covering digital-health technologies, infectious diseases, and public-health functions. Records were deduplicated in EndNote X20 and screened by two blinded reviewers (Cohen κ = 0.81). Empirical studies were appraised using the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool 2018, and reviews were appraised using Joanna Briggs Institute checklists.
Consensus and conclusions
Of 1498 records, 142 studies met inclusion criteria, with 48% rated as high quality. The evidence base is regionally skewed, with 41% from high-income countries and 19% from sub-Saharan Africa or South Asia. No technology was universally superior; AI studies were concentrated in high-income countries, while SMS mHealth studies were concentrated in low- and middle-income countries. Across the included studies, digital health is necessary but not sufficient for pandemic preparedness. Equity, sustainability, and accountability are constituents of effectiveness rather than obstacles.
Controversies and gaps
The evidence base is heavily skewed regionally, with significant underrepresentation from sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia. Recurring barriers include algorithmic bias, fragmented interoperability, donor-dependent sustainability, and slow benefit-sharing of pathogen data with LMICs. The binding constraint is increasingly political and institutional will rather than analytic uncertainty.
Research and practice implications
Priorities should include addressing algorithmic bias, improving interoperability, ensuring donor-independent sustainability, and establishing equitable benefit-sharing mechanisms for pathogen data. Governance frameworks must be strengthened to ensure accountability and political commitment.
