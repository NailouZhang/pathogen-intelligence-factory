量化马尔堡病毒病 暴发的灭绝概率:一种整合多类型分支过程的连续时间马尔可夫链模型

Quantifying the extinction probability of Marburg virus disease outbreaks: A continuous-time Markov chain model integrating a multi-type branching process
作者： Ester Damiano, Jacob Ismael Irunde, Maranya Makuru Mayengo, Ester Damiano Damiano, Jacob I. Irunde
摘要中文翻译理解马尔堡病毒病的灭绝动力学至关重要,尤其是在暴发初期阶段,随机效应对疾病结果有显著影响。尽管确定性模型为病毒传播动力学提供了有价值的见解,但它们无法充分捕捉随机传播事件或量化灭绝概率,特别是在暴发初始病例较少时。本研究基于一个确定性多宿主 传播模型,开发了一个连续时间马尔可夫链随机模型。该模型采用多类型分支过程,根据不同感染类型对二次感染的贡献进行追踪,从而能够在暴发初期阶段估计疾病灭绝概率。灭绝分析表明,由暴露人类引发的暴发灭绝概率为21.6%,高于由传染性人类引发的暴发(13.7%),而由感染蝙蝠引发的暴发灭绝概率仅为0.36%,突显了蝙蝠作为重要储存宿主维持病毒传播的作用。此外,研究结果表明,减少人传人传播、降低病毒排毒量以及增加环境病毒衰减显著提高了疾病灭绝的可能性。所提出的框架为评估马尔堡病毒病 暴发风险提供了定量工具,并为设计针对人类传播、环境污染和跨物种互动的早期干预措施提供了有价值的见解。

Quantifying the extinction probability of Marburg virus disease outbreaks: A continuous-time Markov chain model integrating a multi-type branching process

Authors: Ester Damiano, Jacob Ismael Irunde, Maranya Makuru Mayengo, Ester Damiano Damiano, Jacob I. Irunde
Original abstract (English)
Understanding the extinction dynamics of Marburg virus disease is crucial, particularly during the early stages of an outbreak when stochastic effects strongly influence disease outcomes. Although deterministic models have provided valuable insights into virus transmission dynamics, they cannot adequately capture random transmission events or quantify extinction probabilities, particularly when outbreaks begin with few infected cases. This study develops a continuous-time Markov chain stochastic model derived from a deterministic multi-host transmission model. The model incorporates a multi-type branching process, in which distinct infectious types are tracked according to their contributions to secondary infections, enabling estimation of disease extinction probabilities during the early outbreak phase. The extinction analysis shows that outbreaks initiated by exposed humans have a higher probability of extinction (21.6%) compared with those initiated by infectious humans (13.7%), whereas outbreaks initiated by infected bats have an extinction probability of only 0.36%, highlighting their role as important reservoirs sustaining virus transmission. Furthermore, the results demonstrate that reducing human-to-human transmission, decreasing viral shedding, and increasing environmental viral decay significantly enhance the likelihood of disease extinction. The proposed framework provides a quantitative tool for assessing Marburg virus disease outbreak risk and valuable insights for designing early interventions for human transmission, environmental contamination, and cross-species interactions.

Who leads Marburg virus research? Gender, geography and funding in authorship, 2008 to early 2023: a bibliometric baseline before the 2023-2025 outbreaks

Authors: Maxime Descartes Mbogning Fonkou
Original abstract (English)
Between 2023 and 2025, Marburg virus disease reached Equatorial Guinea, Tanzania, Rwanda and Ethiopia for the first time. WHO's 2025 Marburg research priorities make leadership of studies by local scientists a continuing goal. This study describes who led Marburg virus research before these outbreaks. I analysed 684 English-language Web of Science papers on Marburg virus published from 2008 to February 2023 (5,700 authorships). Gender was inferred from first names with Gender-API, papers about African countries were identified from their text, funders were harmonised by country and type, and themes were mapped to the WHO priorities. I used Wilson 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and logistic and negative binomial regression. Women held 35.1% (95% CI 33.9-36.4) of authorships, 41.6% (37.9-45.4) of first-author and 19.6% (16.7-22.9) of last-author positions. Women last authors rose from 15.1% (2008-2012) to 23.2% (2018-2023). Only 14.6% of papers had an author based in Africa. Of 102 papers about an African country, 25.5% (18.0-34.7) had a first author and 23.5% (16.4-32.6) a last author from that country, below benchmarks for infectious-disease research in Africa. Women based in Africa were last author on 2 papers. Of funded papers, 69.0% acknowledged a US funder (24.0% a US defence agency) and 3.2% an African funder. WHO priorities on clinical care, outbreak trials, social science and capacity strengthening each appeared in fewer than 8% of papers. An apparent citation penalty for funded papers disappeared after adjustment (rate ratio 1.07, 0.87-1.33). Before the recent outbreaks, Marburg virus research was led mostly by men in senior positions and by researchers outside the affected countries, and was funded mainly by US agencies. This baseline can track whether recent African-led responses change who leads the field.

Question and background
This study aimed to establish a baseline for Marburg virus research leadership before the 2023-2025 outbreaks, addressing the lack of understanding about gender and geographic representation in the field.
Design and population
The study analyzed 684 English-language Web of Science papers on Marburg virus published from 2008 to February 2023, encompassing 5,700 authorships across various countries and institutions.
Core methods
The analysis used Wilson 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and logistic and negative binomial regression to assess gender and geographic distribution of authorships, with data harmonized by country and funding source.
Main results
Women held 35.1% (95% CI 33.9-36.4) of authorships, 41.6% (37.9-45.4) of first-author positions, and 19.6% (16.7-22.9) of last-author positions. Only 14.6% of papers had an author based in Africa, and 69.0% acknowledged a US funder, with 24.0% linked to a US defense agency.
Interpretation and novelty
The findings reveal a persistent underrepresentation of African researchers and women in leadership roles, highlighting a gap in the inclusivity of Marburg virus research.
Scientific and public-health significance
This baseline provides critical insights for tracking progress in African-led research and addressing disparities in leadership and funding for Marburg virus studies.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its reliance on Web of Science data, which may not capture all relevant research, and the use of inferred gender data, which could introduce bias. The evidence is moderate in strength due to these factors.

问题与背景
鉴于刚果民主共和国目前缺乏针对 BDBV 暴发 的特定 疫苗,该研究旨在确定 EBOV 感染或 疫苗接种 是否会产生针对 BDBV 的交叉反应免疫。

该研究涉及量化不同 EBOV 疫苗 方案的接受者和 EVD 幸存者的 CD4 和 CD8 T 细胞反应并测量 GP 结合抗体。

这些发现表明,现有的 EBOV 疫苗 可能提供一些针对 BDBV 的交叉保护,这对于管理 BDBV 暴发 至关重要。
局限与证据强度
该研究的局限性包括样本量小以及使用不同的 疫苗 方案,这可能会影响研究结果的普遍性。

Cross-reactive antibody and functional T-cell responses to Bundibugyo virus induced by Zaire ebolavirus vaccination and infection

Authors: Aurélie Wiedemann, Ahidjo Ayouba, Édouard Lhomme, Emile Foucat, Solenne Kisrani, Guillaume Thaurignac, Safaa Ben-Farhat, Postebogui Study group, Prevac study team, Martine Peeters
Original abstract (English)
Background There is no licensed specific vaccine against the current Ebola Bundibugyo virus (BDBV) outbeak affecting the Democratic Republic of the Congo. However, clinical evaluations of a licensed Zaire ebolavirus (EBOV) vaccine have just launched. It remains unclear whether EBOV infection or vaccination generate cross-reactive immunity against BDBV. Methods We quantified CD4 and CD8 T-cell magnitude, polyfunctionality, and cytotoxicity after 8-day expansion with EBOV, BDBV, Sudan (SUDV), or Marburg (MARV) GP peptide pools, and measured GP-binding antibodies by multiplex assay, in recipients of Ad26.ZEBOV/MVA-BN-Filo (n=10), single-dose rVSVG-ZEBOV-GP (n=10), or two-dose rVSVG-ZEBOV-GP (n=8) (NCT02876328), and in EVD survivors sampled approximately 3 years after infection (n=10). PeriodDayWeek Company Profile Island Pharmaceuticals Limited is an Australia-based drug repurposing company. The Company is focused on areas of unmet need for antiviral therapeutics to address infectious diseases. Its lead asset is ISLA-101, a drug with a safety profile being repurposed for the prevention and treatment of dengue fever and other mosquito (or vector) borne diseases. ISLA-101 has been shown to have activity against all four strains of dengue virus as well as other flaviviruses such as Zika virus, West Nile virus, and Yellow Fever virus, as well as Chikungunya virus. While ISLA-101 is an oral formulation, the Company is also considering alternative formulations, including a long-acting oral formulation and intravenous formulations for severe dengue infections. The Company operates a single segment, being research and development activities principally in the geographic regions of Australia and the United States. Its subsidiary is Isla Pharmaceuticals Inc.
