From Zoonotic Spillover to Hemorrhagic Crisis: Molecular Insights and Epidemiology of Ethiopia’s 2025 Marburg Outbreak Amid Preparedness Gaps and Historical Lethality

Authors: Getnet Molla, Zemenu Bitew, Molalegne Bitew
Original Abstract
Ethiopia confirmed its first Marburg virus disease (MVD) outbreak on 14 November 2025 in Jinka (Omo Valley area), where a single spillover from Rousettus aegyptiacus resulted in nine laboratory-confirmed cases and eight deaths (CFR 89%). Transmission occurred in the context of concurrent cholera and large-scale population displacement. Despite these pressures, case confirmation was achieved within 48 hours, >90% of contacts were traced, and same-day L-gene sequencing placed the isolate within the East African lineage associated with the 2024 Rwanda outbreak. This review summarizes key molecular determinants of MARV virulence including GP–NPC1 mediated entry, VP24-driven interferon antagonism, and GP-associated endothelial dysfunction and examines Ethiopia’s contrasting strengths in genomic capacity and PHEOC coordination versus persistent constraints in diagnostics, clinical readiness, and countermeasure access. We outline a feasible seven-day containment framework integrating low-cost molecular testing, remdesivir therapy, and VSV-MARV ring vaccination to prevent further spread in resource-limited settings.
