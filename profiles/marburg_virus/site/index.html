配置锁定的 Ridaifen-B 类似物作为有效的埃博拉病毒进入抑制剂。

Configurationally Locked Ridaifen-B Analogs as Potent Ebola Virus Entry Inhibitors.
作者： Shaimaa M Aboukhatwa, Carolina Q Sacramento, Kimberly M Morsheimer, Christian A Zelinski, Ryan Bott, Murugesh Venkatesh, Callum D Owen, Robert A Davey, Irina N Gaisina, Lijun Rong
摘要中文翻译面对新出现的 暴发、高 死亡率 发病率和有限的 治疗 选择,埃博拉病毒 (EBOV) 疾病对全球公共卫生构成威胁,凸显了有效治疗的必要性。埃博拉病毒糖蛋白 (GP) 在病毒生命周期中发挥的独特而关键的作用使其成为 抗病毒药物 药物发现的有吸引力的目标。在这项研究中,我们报告了一系列基于环化 Ridaifen-B 的抑制剂的设计、合成和评估,这些抑制剂具有增强的 抗病毒药物 活性和针对 EBOV 的选择性。几种化合物表现出抑制 EBOV-GP 介导的进入的纳摩尔效力和对抗 马尔堡病毒 进入的亚微摩尔效力。定点诱变表明抑制剂结合在 EBOV GP1/GP2 融合环上。这些化合物显示出良好的体外药代动力学参数,这有助于测试热门化合物 41 的体内快速 PK。这项研究强调了 1,2-二氢萘和四氢萘衍生物作为开发针对 EBOV 和其他 丝状病毒 的有效抗丝病毒药物的宝贵先导的潜力。

问题与背景
由于新出现的 暴发、高 死亡率 发病率和有限的 治疗 选择,埃博拉病毒病仍然是全球公共卫生威胁,因此需要发现有效的 抗病毒药物 疗法。
设计与对象
该研究设计、合成并评估了一系列针对埃博拉病毒糖蛋白(GP)的环化ridaifen-B抑制剂,以评估其抗病毒药物活性和选择性。
核心方法
作者合成了环化的 Ridaifen-B 类似物,并使用 抗病毒药物 测定评估了它们对抗埃博拉病毒 GP 介导的进入的效力,然后进行定点诱变以识别埃博拉病毒 GP1/GP2 融合环的结合位点。
主要结果
几种环化的ridaifen-B类似物表现出纳摩尔抑制埃博拉病毒GP介导的进入的效力和亚微摩尔抑制马尔堡病毒进入的效力,抑制剂结合定位于埃博拉病毒GP1/GP2融合环。
解释与创新
该研究确定 1,2-二氢萘和四氢萘衍生物是抗丝病毒药物开发的有前景的先导化合物,强调了它们作为具有良好药代动力学特性的埃博拉病毒进入抑制剂的潜力。
科研与公卫意义
这些发现支持开发基于ridaifen-B的抑制剂作为埃博拉病毒病的候选疗法,解决了对有效抗病毒药物 治疗的迫切需求。
局限与证据强度
证据仅限于体外和早期药代动力学评估,没有报告体内疗效或安全性数据,需要谨慎解释治疗潜力。

Configurationally Locked Ridaifen-B Analogs as Potent Ebola Virus Entry Inhibitors.

Authors: Shaimaa M Aboukhatwa, Carolina Q Sacramento, Kimberly M Morsheimer, Christian A Zelinski, Ryan Bott, Murugesh Venkatesh, Callum D Owen, Robert A Davey, Irina N Gaisina, Lijun Rong
Original abstract (English)
In the face of emerging outbreaks, high mortality rates, and limited treatment options, Ebola virus (EBOV) disease poses a global public health threat, underscoring the need for effective therapies. The unique and critical role that EBOV glycoprotein (GP) plays in the viral life cycle makes it an attractive target for antiviral drug discovery. In this study, we report on the design, synthesis, and evaluation of a series of cyclized ridaifen-B-based inhibitors with enhanced antiviral activity and selectivity against EBOV. Several compounds exhibited nanomolar potency inhibiting EBOV-GP-mediated entry and submicromolar potency against Marburg virus entry. Site-directed mutagenesis indicated inhibitor binding at the EBOV GP1/GP2 fusion loop. The compounds displayed favorable in vitro pharmacokinetic parameters, which encouraged testing the in vivo rapid PK for hit compound 41. This study highlights the potential of 1,2-dihydronaphthalene and tetrahydronaphthalene derivatives as valuable leads for the development of potent antifiloviral agents targeting EBOV among other filoviruses.

Question and background
Ebola virus disease remains a global public health threat due to emerging outbreaks, high mortality rates, and limited treatment options, necessitating the discovery of effective antiviral therapies.
Design and population
The study designed, synthesized, and evaluated a series of cyclized ridaifen-B-based inhibitors targeting Ebola virus glycoprotein (GP) to assess their antiviral activity and selectivity.
Core methods
The authors synthesized cyclized ridaifen-B analogs and evaluated their potency against Ebola virus GP-mediated entry using antiviral assays, followed by site-directed mutagenesis to identify the binding site at the Ebola virus GP1/GP2 fusion loop.
Main results
Several cyclized ridaifen-B analogs exhibited nanomolar potency inhibiting Ebola virus GP-mediated entry and submicromolar potency against Marburg virus entry, with inhibitor binding localized to the Ebola virus GP1/GP2 fusion loop.
Interpretation and novelty
The study identifies 1,2-dihydronaphthalene and tetrahydronaphthalene derivatives as promising leads for antifiloviral drug development, highlighting their potential as Ebola virus entry inhibitors with favorable pharmacokinetic properties.
Scientific and public-health significance
These findings support the development of ridaifen-B-based inhibitors as candidate therapies for Ebola virus disease, addressing the urgent need for effective antiviral treatments.
Limitations and evidence strength
The evidence is limited to in vitro and early pharmacokinetic assessments, with no reported in vivo efficacy or safety data, warranting cautious interpretation of the therapeutic potential.

Question and background
The study addresses the urgent need for rapid access to organized scientific information during emerging filovirus outbreaks, specifically the Bundibugyo virus emergency declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern. It highlights the challenge for immunologists and researchers to quickly orient themselves in new fields and the inefficiency of traditional literature reviews in accelerating outbreak response.
Design and population
The paper presents an evidence-mapping methodology applied to filovirus research, designed to systematically assemble and categorize published literature. It involves independent screening and mapping by two reviewers to ensure consistency and aims to provide a comprehensive, unbiased resource for the research community.
Core methods
Evidence mapping is performed by identifying relevant publications in filovirus research and categorizing them according to population (e.g., human, non-human primate, mouse), intervention (e.g., vaccination, infection, treatment), and measured outcomes (e.g., antibody response, T cell response, infection outcome). The process includes independent dual-reviewer screening and aims to create a resource that can be updated regularly to remain current.
Main results
The study demonstrates that evidence mapping enables researchers to rapidly identify all published research relevant to their work in a structured format, reducing the time required to gain an overview of a new field. It contrasts this approach with traditional narrative reviews, emphasizing its role in providing unbiased, comprehensive assembly of available research.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpret evidence mapping as a transformative tool for accelerating research responses to emerging infectious disease outbreaks by providing a centralized, organized resource. They highlight its novelty in systematically categorizing filovirus research to guide priorities and opportunities for action during public health emergencies.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings suggest that evidence mapping can inform outbreak response strategies by enabling rapid identification of research gaps and priorities, thereby facilitating the development of vaccines, treatments, and diagnostics. This approach is particularly valuable for emerging filoviruses like Bundibugyo virus, where timely research is critical.
Limitations and evidence strength
The evidence strength is moderate due to the reliance on published literature and the absence of primary experimental data or direct clinical outcomes. The methodology depends on the comprehensiveness and quality of the included studies, and the map's utility is limited by the timeliness of updates and the reviewers' consistency in categorization.

At a glance CDC established its office in Guinea in 2015 to strengthen national public health systems and enhance the country’s capacity to prevent, detect, and respond to infectious disease threats. Working in close partnership with the Ministry of Health (MOH), the National Agency for Health Security (ANSS), and other national and international partners, CDC Guinea supports efforts to build resilient surveillance systems, expand the skilled public health workforce, and advance global health security and epidemic preparedness across all levels of the health system. CDC’s work aims to protect the health of Americans and support public health around the world. Key accomplishments CDC Guinea works to stop outbreaks at their source. Strengthened infectious disease surveillance and outbreak response by supporting the ANSS to implement the 7-1-7 approach and enhance real-time data reporting through the District Health Information System (DHIS2) Expanded workforce capacity through the Field Epidemiology Training Program, training epidemiologists on the One Health approach for professionals from the human, animal, and environmental health sectors Improved laboratory systems by providing technical assistance for biosafety, diagnostic capacity, and quality assurance through the Global Laboratory Leadership Program and national specimen referral policy implementation Strengthened immunization and disease control efforts through the Expanded Program on Immunization, supporting measles, polio, meningitis, and yellow fever surveillance and vaccination campaigns Enhanced national emergency preparedness and response capacity through the Public Health Emergency Management program, strengthening Public Health Emergency Operations Centers and Rapid Intervention Teams across all 38 districts Advanced cross-sectoral collaboration integrating human, animal, and environmental surveillance systems to prevent and control zoonotic diseases such as rabies and anthrax Global health security CDC’s global health security work in Guinea focuses on strengthening the country’s capacity to prevent, detect, and respond to infectious disease threats. In partnership with the MOH and other national and international partners. CDC Guinea supports integrated surveillance systems, laboratory networks, workforce development, emergency management, and immunization programs that collectively enhance national and global health security. Health systems strengthening CDC provides technical and financial support to strengthen Guinea’s Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response system. This includes integrating Event-Based Surveillance into the District Health Information System (DHIS2) and EMPRES-i platforms, improving early warning and rapid response capabilities. Through collaboration with ANSS, CDC has helped operationalize a multi-tiered surveillance structure with Rapid Response Teams (EPARE and ERARE) and Emergency Operations Centers across all levels. These efforts improve data reporting, analysis, and decision-making through the National Health Information System. Workforce development Since 2016, CDC has supported Guinea’s Field Epidemiology Training Program (FETP), which builds critical workforce capacity for outbreak investigation and disease surveillance. To date, more than 400 public health professionals have graduated across the frontline, intermediate, and advanced levels. Over 80% remain active in country, applying their skills in managing public health emergencies such as Ebola, Marburg virus disease, COVID-19, yellow fever, and polio. The FETP follows a learning-through-service model that emphasizes field practice, mentorship, and One Health collaboration across human, animal, and environmental health sectors. Laboratory systems strengthening CDC collaborates with the National Institute of Public Health and National Directorate of Laboratories to strengthen Guinea’s diagnostic capacity. Key achievements include integrating LabBook with DHIS2 for real-time laboratory data management, launching a Global Laboratory Leadership Program in Guinea, and enhancing testing capacity for Ebola, monkeypox, COVID-19, meningitis, rotavirus, influenza, and other priority diseases. These initiatives have improved biosafety/biosecurity, quality assurance, and coordination between national and regional laboratories. Emergency management CDC helped establish Guinea’s first Public Health Emergency Operations Center, now operational in all 38 districts. CDC continues to strengthen emergency preparedness through the Public Health Emergency Management program. This collaboration supports training, simulation exercises, with standardized operating procedures to ensure coordinated response to health emergencies. Immunization CDC supports Guinea’s Expanded Program on Immunization to increase vaccine coverage and strengthen surveillance for measles, polio, yellow fever, and Meningitis A. Through the deployment of FETP-trained epidemiologists and STOP Program officers, CDC enhances campaign planning, monitoring, and data analysis. CDC also provides technical support for the introduction of the rotavirus vaccine and promotes the Second Year of Life (2YL) policy to improve measles immunity and sustain high vaccination coverage. One Health and Zoonotic Disease Control In coordination with the Ministries of Health, Livestock, and Environment, CDC supports Guinea’s One Health platform, which integrates human, animal, and environmental health systems to better prevent and control zoonotic diseases. The program emphasizes collaborative surveillance, policy development, and community engagement. In 2023, CDC and partners helped vaccinate dogs through a nationwide rabies control campaign and continue to support risk communication and behavioral change strategies in communities. Cross-Border Health Security CDC provides technical assistance to enhance regional health security through community-based surveillance, joint outbreak investigations, and coordinated response at key points of entry. These efforts strengthen cross-border data sharing and align Guinea’s preparedness with International Health Regulations. As Guinea continues to respond to reoccurring public health threats such as monkeypox, Lassa fever and whopping cough, the need to strengthen laboratory leadership has become critical to improving national health security. Despite progress in diagnostics and surveillance, gaps in workforce capacity, quality management, and coordination have limited the effectiveness of the laboratory system. To address these challenges, CDC Guinea partnered with regional institutions to implement the Global Laboratory Leadership Program (GLLP), enrolling four Guinean professionals in a specialized university diploma program in Burkina Faso. In February 2026, laboratory professionals and partners gathered at Joseph Ki-Zerbo University in Ouagadougou to celebrate the GLLP graduates. CDC Guinea's laboratory specialists participated in the final evaluation and graduation of the program's second cohort—creating an opportunity to exchange knowledge and bring proven solutions back home. The results were impressive. Of 34 participants, 33 successfully graduated—a 97 percent success rate—and all four Guinean candidates passed their final presentations and evaluations, achieving a 100 percent success rate. Their projects demonstrated practical solutions to strengthen laboratory systems, improve quality, and enhance leadership skills across sectors. Beyond supporting the graduation of four Guinean laboratory professionals, the visit to Burkina Faso provided CDC Guinea with valuable lessons to strengthen laboratory systems in Guinea. Through meetings with academic institutions, laboratory leaders, and implementing partners, representatives from Guinea learned practical ways to implement GLLP, build sustainable mentorship programs, and coordinate laboratory networks. The visit also showcased examples of successful laboratory systems that could be adopted for use in Guinea. Lessons learned from the visit also showed laboratorians from Guinea how to strengthen their emergency response systems through implementing innovative approaches such as the 7-1-7 framework which improves the speed of outbreak detection and response. Building on these lessons, Guinea can now apply best practices to strengthen public health systems across the country.
