配置锁定的 Ridaifen-B 类似物作为有效的埃博拉病毒进入抑制剂。

Configurationally Locked Ridaifen-B Analogs as Potent Ebola Virus Entry Inhibitors.
作者： Shaimaa M Aboukhatwa, Carolina Q Sacramento, Kimberly M Morsheimer, Christian A Zelinski, Ryan Bott, Murugesh Venkatesh, Callum D Owen, Robert A Davey, Irina N Gaisina, Lijun Rong
摘要中文翻译面对新出现的 暴发、高 死亡率 发病率和有限的 治疗 选择,埃博拉病毒 (EBOV) 疾病对全球公共卫生构成威胁,凸显了有效治疗的必要性。埃博拉病毒糖蛋白 (GP) 在病毒生命周期中发挥的独特而关键的作用使其成为 抗病毒药物 药物发现的有吸引力的目标。在这项研究中,我们报告了一系列基于环化 Ridaifen-B 的抑制剂的设计、合成和评估,这些抑制剂具有增强的 抗病毒药物 活性和针对 EBOV 的选择性。几种化合物表现出抑制 EBOV-GP 介导的进入的纳摩尔效力和对抗 马尔堡病毒 进入的亚微摩尔效力。定点诱变表明抑制剂结合在 EBOV GP1/GP2 融合环上。这些化合物显示出良好的体外药代动力学参数,这鼓励测试命中化合物 41 的体内快速 PK。这项研究强调了 1,2-二氢萘和四氢萘衍生物作为开发针对 EBOV 和其他 丝状病毒 的有效抗丝病毒药物的宝贵先导的潜力。
查看研究七要素
问题与背景
由于新出现的 暴发、高 死亡率 发病率和有限的 治疗 选择,埃博拉病毒病仍然是全球公共卫生威胁,因此需要发现有效的 抗病毒药物 疗法。
设计与对象
该研究报告了基于环化 Ridaifen-B 的抑制剂的设计、合成和评估,该抑制剂针对埃博拉病毒糖蛋白(GP)以破坏病毒进入。
核心方法
作者合成了一系列环化的 Ridaifen-B 类似物,并使用定点诱变评估了它们针对埃博拉病毒 GP 介导的进入的 抗病毒药物 活性,以确定 GP1/GP2 融合环的结合位点。
主要结果
几种化合物表现出纳摩尔抑制埃博拉病毒 GP 介导的进入的效力,以及亚微摩尔抑制 马尔堡病毒 进入的效力,并在埃博拉病毒 GP1/GP2 融合环上证实了结合。
解释与创新
该研究确定 1,2-二氢萘和四氢萘衍生物是抗丝状病毒药物的有前景的先导药物,强调了它们作为具有良好药代动力学特性的埃博拉病毒进入抑制剂的潜力。
科研与公卫意义
这些发现支持开发针对埃博拉病毒和潜在其他 丝状病毒 的靶向 抗病毒药物 疗法,解决 暴发 应对中未满足的关键需求。
局限与证据强度
证据仅限于体外和早期药代动力学评估;体内功效和安全性尚未得到证实,需要谨慎解释结果。

Configurationally Locked Ridaifen-B Analogs as Potent Ebola Virus Entry Inhibitors.

Authors: Shaimaa M Aboukhatwa, Carolina Q Sacramento, Kimberly M Morsheimer, Christian A Zelinski, Ryan Bott, Murugesh Venkatesh, Callum D Owen, Robert A Davey, Irina N Gaisina, Lijun Rong
Original abstract (English)
In the face of emerging outbreaks, high mortality rates, and limited treatment options, Ebola virus (EBOV) disease poses a global public health threat, underscoring the need for effective therapies. The unique and critical role that EBOV glycoprotein (GP) plays in the viral life cycle makes it an attractive target for antiviral drug discovery. In this study, we report on the design, synthesis, and evaluation of a series of cyclized ridaifen-B-based inhibitors with enhanced antiviral activity and selectivity against EBOV. Several compounds exhibited nanomolar potency inhibiting EBOV-GP-mediated entry and submicromolar potency against Marburg virus entry. Site-directed mutagenesis indicated inhibitor binding at the EBOV GP1/GP2 fusion loop. The compounds displayed favorable in vitro pharmacokinetic parameters, which encouraged testing the in vivo rapid PK for hit compound 41 . This study highlights the potential of 1,2-dihydronaphthalene and tetrahydronaphthalene derivatives as valuable leads for the development of potent antifiloviral agents targeting EBOV among other filoviruses.

Question and background
Ebola virus disease remains a global public health threat due to emerging outbreaks, high mortality rates, and limited treatment options, necessitating the discovery of effective antiviral therapies.
Design and population
The study reports the design, synthesis, and evaluation of cyclized ridaifen-B-based inhibitors targeting Ebola virus glycoprotein (GP) to disrupt viral entry.
Core methods
The authors synthesized a series of cyclized ridaifen-B analogs and assessed their antiviral activity against Ebola virus GP-mediated entry using site-directed mutagenesis to identify the binding site at the GP1/GP2 fusion loop.
Main results
Several compounds demonstrated nanomolar potency in inhibiting Ebola virus GP-mediated entry and submicromolar potency against Marburg virus entry, with binding confirmed at the Ebola virus GP1/GP2 fusion loop.
Interpretation and novelty
The study identifies 1,2-dihydronaphthalene and tetrahydronaphthalene derivatives as promising leads for antifiloviral agents, highlighting their potential as Ebola virus entry inhibitors with favorable pharmacokinetic properties.
Scientific and public-health significance
These findings support the development of targeted antiviral therapies against Ebola virus and potentially other filoviruses, addressing a critical unmet need in outbreak response.
Limitations and evidence strength
The evidence is limited to in vitro and early pharmacokinetic assessments; in vivo efficacy and safety remain unconfirmed, warranting cautious interpretation of the results.

From Zoonotic Spillover to Hemorrhagic Crisis: Molecular Insights and Epidemiology of Ethiopia’s 2025 Marburg Outbreak Amid Preparedness Gaps and Historical Lethality

Authors: Getnet Molla, Zemenu Bitew, Molalegne Bitew
Original abstract (English)
Ethiopia confirmed its first Marburg virus disease (MVD) outbreak on 14 November 2025 in Jinka (Omo Valley area), where a single spillover from Rousettus aegyptiacus resulted in nine laboratory-confirmed cases and eight deaths (CFR 89%). Transmission occurred in the context of concurrent cholera and large-scale population displacement. Despite these pressures, case confirmation was achieved within 48 hours, >90% of contacts were traced, and same-day L-gene sequencing placed the isolate within the East African lineage associated with the 2024 Rwanda outbreak. This review summarizes key molecular determinants of MARV virulence including GP–NPC1 mediated entry, VP24-driven interferon antagonism, and GP-associated endothelial dysfunction and examines Ethiopia’s contrasting strengths in genomic capacity and PHEOC coordination versus persistent constraints in diagnostics, clinical readiness, and countermeasure access. We outline a feasible seven-day containment framework integrating low-cost molecular testing, remdesivir therapy, and VSV-MARV ring vaccination to prevent further spread in resource-limited settings.

Digital Health for Pandemic Preparedness: A Critical Review of Innovations, Equity Gaps, and Governance in Infectious Disease Surveillance

Authors: Abdolreza Babamahmoodi, F. Babamahmoodi, Majid Marjani
Original abstract (English)
Context: Recent infectious disease emergencies, including SARS-CoV-2, mpox (2022 - 2024), highly pathogenic avian influenza A(H5N1) in US dairy cattle (2024 - 2025), and the Marburg virus in Rwanda (2024), have strained traditional surveillance systems. Digital health has been proposed as a paradigm shift; however, most reviews have focused on diagnostic accuracy while neglecting equity and governance. Evidence Acquisition: We conducted a structured thematic narrative review of peer-reviewed and grey literature published from January 2010 to March 2025, reported in accordance with PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA-ScR. PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science were searched from February 5 to 18, 2025. Search keywords combined three concept blocks: (1) Digital-health technologies (artificial intelligence, machine learning, mHealth, eHealth, telemedicine, electronic health records, wearables, wastewater surveillance, genomic surveillance, natural-language processing); (2) infectious diseases (outbreak, pandemic, epidemic, COVID-19, Ebola, Zika, mpox, influenza, antimicrobial resistance, Marburg); and (3) public-health functions (surveillance, detection, forecasting, contact tracing, governance, equity). Full Boolean syntax is provided. Records were deduplicated in EndNote X20 and screened by 2 blinded reviewers in Rayyan (Cohen κ = 0.81). Empirical studies were appraised using the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool 2018, and reviews were appraised using Joanna Briggs Institute checklists. The synthesis followed Braun and Clarke's reflexive thematic analysis and was interpreted through sociotechnical systems and data justice lenses. Results: Of 1498 records, 142 studies met the inclusion criteria; 48% (69/142) were rated as high quality. The evidence base was heavily skewed regionally (high-income countries [HICs], 41%; sub-Saharan Africa or South Asia, 19%). No technology was universally superior. Artificial intelligence (AI) studies were concentrated in HICs (18/32, 56%), whereas short-message-service (SMS) mHealth studies were concentrated in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs; 15/25, 60%). Recurring barriers included algorithmic bias, fragmented interoperability, donor-dependent sustainability, and slow benefit-sharing of pathogen data with LMICs. Conclusions: Across 142 included studies the synthesis supports a measured conclusion: Digital health is necessary but not sufficient for pandemic preparedness. The patterns most consistently observed in the evidence — regional skew, recurring governance failures, donor-dependent sustainability, and design choices that determine equity outcomes — together indicate that equity, sustainability, and accountability are constituents of effectiveness rather than obstacles to it. The binding constraint is increasingly political and institutional will rather than analytic uncertainty.

Across the included studies, digital health is necessary but not sufficient for pandemic preparedness. Equity, sustainability, and accountability are constituents of effectiveness rather than obstacles.
Controversies and gaps
The evidence base is heavily skewed regionally, with significant underrepresentation from sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia. Recurring barriers include algorithmic bias, fragmented interoperability, donor-dependent sustainability, and slow benefit-sharing of pathogen data with LMICs. The binding constraint is increasingly political and institutional will rather than analytic uncertainty.
Research and practice implications
Priorities should include addressing algorithmic bias, improving interoperability, ensuring donor-independent sustainability, and establishing equitable benefit-sharing mechanisms for pathogen data. Governance frameworks must be strengthened to ensure accountability and political commitment.
