Adaptation of Bayesian decision analysis for clinical trial design to binary outcomes: illustration for Ebola virus disease treatment.

Authors: Drifa Belhadi, Joonhyuk Cho, Pauline Manchon, Denis J.-M. Malvy, France Mentré, Andrew W. Lo, Cédric Laouénan, Cédric Laouenan
Original abstract (English)
When designing trials for high-mortality diseases with limited therapies, the conventional 5% type I error rate (α) may be unsuitable. A higher α could benefit seriously ill patients lacking therapeutic alternatives. Bayesian decision analysis (BDA) integrates multiple health consequences into trial design. We extend BDA for trials with binary outcomes to calculate optimal sample sizes (n) and α in an Ebola outbreak context. We consider fixed, two-arm randomized trials with binary outcomes and two types of clinical trial losses: post-trial loss, for not approving an effective treatment or approving an ineffective one; in-trial loss, for not administrating an effective treatment to control-arm patients or for administrating an ineffective treatment to experimental-arm patients. The model incorporates treatment side effects and disease severity into a single loss function, from which BDA-optimal n and α are derived. Using mortality as the outcome, we modeled various epidemic scenarios by varying target population size, mortality rate, and treatment efficacy. In most cases, BDA-optimal α exceeded the conventional one-sided 2.5% and sample sizes are smaller. Simulations comparing BDA-optimized two-arm trials (fixed/sequential) to conventional designs (two-arm/single-arm, fixed/sequential) across outbreak scenarios show comparable statistical power across designs, except when sample size assumptions are incorrect, or when trials started after the outbreak peak; in these situations, BDA-optimized trials show superior powers. This BDA framework provides a promising approach for designing trials with binary outcomes, particularly in high-mortality disease outbreaks, where declining case numbers and uncertain mortality and efficacy data challenge conventional methods.

Early detection of immunoglobulin A against a vaccine backbone protein enables fast, durable, and objective identification of individuals vaccinated against Ebola virus.

Authors: Olivia Posadas, Irina Maljkovic Berry, Viviane Callier, Stephen B. Kennedy, Mark Kieh, Sarah M. Browne, Olivier Tshiani Mbaya, Krystle Hensley, Lisa E. Hensley, Jens H. Kuhn
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: rVSVΔG-ZEBOV-GP vaccine is recommended for prevention of Ebola virus (EBOV) infection and disease (EVD). However, breakthrough EBOV infections have been observed in vaccinees. Currently available diagnostic and research assays cannot reliably distinguish individuals vaccinated against Ebola virus from those previously infected with the virus. This limitation complicates interpretation of vaccine-effectiveness studies, seroepidemiologic investigations, and estimates of infection or attack rates. The ability to differentiate vaccine-induced immune responses from those resulting from natural infection remains an important unmet public health and research need. METHODS: We developed a multiplex sero-diagnostic assay simultaneously detecting IgA, IgG, and IgM antibodies against vesicular stomatitis Indiana virus nucleoprotein (VSV-N), a vaccine-specific marker absent in natural infection, and EBOV-GP, the vaccine's target antigen. We evaluated longitudinal samples from 30 individuals after vaccination and 30 placebo (saline) recipients, as well as 8 participants from a long-term vaccination follow-up study. FINDINGS: We detected increase in all antibodies against both protein antigens in vaccinated individuals. Critically, the assay reveals that anti-VSV-N IgA responses emerge earlier and more robustly than anti-GP responses in vaccinees, establishing anti-N IgA as a definitive, early, and durable biomarker of rVSVΔG-ZEBOV-GP vaccination with high sensitivity and specificity. INTERPRETATION: The assay enables rapid, accurate identification of vaccine recipients and supports real-time assessment of vaccine effectiveness during outbreaks. Furthermore, the assay's adaptability to the expanding pipeline of VSV-vectored vaccines positions it as a transformative diagnostic platform for accelerating preparedness for future infectious disease threats and outbreak response strategies. Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT The 2026 Ebola disease outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda is caused by Bundibugyo virus, a species for which, unlike Zaire ebolavirus , no licensed vaccine and no approved therapeutic exist. This commentary argues that this absence is not a misfortune of virology but a structurally produced injustice. Bundibugyo virus has been characterised since 2008, has caused lethal outbreaks, and was foreseeably capable of recurrence; more importantly, species‐specific and cross‐protective countermeasures were scientifically tractable more than a decade before, yet none reached licensure or field readiness. A recombinant vesicular stomatitis virus vaccine expressing the Bundibugyo glycoprotein — built on the same platform as the licensed Zaire vaccine — conferred complete protection in nonhuman primates in 2013; thirteen years later, no clinical‐grade material existed when the outbreak began. The gap, we argue, lay not in what science could build but in what the system chose to finish, because preparedness investment concentrated on the species that threatened wealthier and more connected populations. Drawing on the framework of structural injustice and on the authors’ prior field experience of concurrent epidemics in the DRC, we distinguish tragic scarcity from produced scarcity, answer the feasibility objection, and argue that closing the gap is an obligation rather than a charity.

Authors: Hannah L. Wallace, Megan Halbrook, Jean Paul Kompany, Nicole A. Hoff, Sydney Merritt, Patrick Kakoni Mukadi, Michael Beya, Handdy Kalengi, Merly Tambu, Zephi Mossoko
Original abstract (English)
Background: Filoviruses, specifically Ebola virus (EBOV), Bundibugyo virus (BDBV), and Marburg virus (MARV), have been the etiologic agents of multiple outbreaks of severe disease in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). Two EBOV-specific vaccine regimens have been approved for use in outbreak scenarios, including the Ad26.ZEBOV/MVA‐BN‐Filo (Zabdeno/Mvabea) two-dose vaccine, where the MVA-BN-Filo booster dose contains EBOV, MARV, and Sudan (SUDV) virus glycoproteins (GPs) and the nucleoprotein from Tai Forest virus. While long-term immunogenicity of up to two years has been found in clinical trial settings, limited real-world data exists. Methods: Using a multiplexed immunoassay, samples (n=698 at baseline) collected in Mbandaka, Equateur province, DRC, between September 2023 and October 2024 were assessed for seroreactivity to six filovirus antigens (1 BDBV, 2 EBOV, 2 MARV, and 1 SUDV) at three distinct periods: baseline/pre-vaccination, 6-8 weeks post-first dose (Zabdeno), at booster administration (Mvabea), and one-year post-boost dose. Results: Substantial filovirus seroreactivity was found at baseline, with 48.7% (n=340/698) of individuals being seroreactive to at least one virus antigen. High baseline seroreactivity was found against BDBV GP (31.1%, n=217/698), with lower baseline seroreactivity to EBOV GP (15.5%, n=106/698), comparatively. Overall EBOV GP seroreactivity increased to 66.8% (n=376/563) at one year-post vaccine boost and 57.6% (n=250/434) of participants were seroreactive. Despite the booster dose inclusion of SUDV GP and MARV GP, there were no substantial increases in reactivity against either antigen at subsequent timepoints. Overall filovirus seroreactivity increased at all timepoints for the entire cohort, although filovirus reactivity decreased over the study period for those who were seroreactive at baseline. Findings: These data highlight detection of significantly elevated seoreactivity to EBOV GP following first-dose administration of Ad26.ZEBOV, which remained relatively stable one-year post-booster dose administration. Additionally, this study identifies high levels of baseline seroreactivity to BDBV GP despite no previous documented outbreaks in this region of DRC. These data suggest that vaccination with the Ad26.ZEBOV-MVA-BN-Filo vaccine induces stable seroreactivity against EBOV GP, but not to other filovirus antigens.

Introduction Orthoebolavirus restonense (Reston virus; RESTV) is a zoonotic virus that has been detected primarily in non-human primates and swine. Despite its recognized public health importance, the evolutionary dynamics and population structure of RESTV remain poorly understood. Methods In this study, we conducted an in silico analysis of 20 publicly available RESTV whole-genome sequences to build upon previous analyses by updating phylogenetic relationships, evolutionary timescales, and incorporating additional analyses of genetic diversity and population subdivision across host groups and outbreak periods. Authors: Walekhwa, A. W., Mbaka, P., Nannyonga, B. K., Silal, S. P., Bbuye, M., Magoba, B., Nakiire, L., Lamorde, M., Sulaimon, T. A., Kwesiga, B.
Original abstract (English)
Background: The 2026 Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak in Uganda, linked to the ongoing epidemic in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), spread through human mobility across borders and within the country. Existing modelling has provided regional level importation estimates but not district level operational guidance. We developed an exploratory, scenario based decision support model to characterise subnational importation and spread patterns. Methods: We constructed a directed, weighted mobility network using International Organization for Migration (IOM) Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) flows collected from 15 to 24 May 2026 (11,245 observed movements) and the 2024 Uganda census. A stochastic metapopulation SEIR model was simulated over 90 days across 135 Ugandan districts and two DRC provinces. We evaluated three scenario assumptions: containment (R0 = 0.80), moderate (R0 = 1.35), and weak (R0 = 2.76); plus a one-factor-at-a-time analysis of nonpharmaceutical intervention (NPI) compliance. Global sensitivity analysis was performed using Morris elementary-effects screening and total order Sobol indices. Results: The mobility network was sparse (density 0.11), highly unequal (Gini 0.67), and modular (0.50). Kisoro had the highest import risk (in-strength 3,823) and export risk (out-strength 1,350). Under all three scenarios, the model projected similar cumulative case counts (median 20 to 21; pairwise Mann Whitney p < 0.05). Under containment, Kampala was the only district with a non-zero median (20 cases, 95% simulation interval 20 to 22); all other districts had a median of 0. Prediction-interval coverage against the early observed curve was 0.80. Conclusions: Under the containment assumptions of the model, rapid 7 1 7 implementation was associated with substantial containment, but this is scenario-specific and does not imply that NPIs are unnecessary. Border surveillance, nationwide preparedness, and community engagement remain essential. These projections should be treated as exploratory and require prospective field-based validation.

Authors: Kéryanne Gagnon
Original abstract (English)
With the continuous rise of epidemics with potential for global threats, the need for antiviral drug development continues to be essential. Most drugs approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) are direct-acting agents (DAAs), targeting specific viral proteins, thus restricting the range of applications of the antiviral. Due to the constant evolution of viruses, DAAs regularly have to be optimized or modified to maintain their inhibitory effect against emerging resistant variants. An alternative therapeutic approach to DAAs are host-directed agents (HDAs) which instead target host processes to inhibit viral infection. They offer more potential for broad-spectrum use if the targeted host processes are conserved across viral families. They are also more resistant to viral mutations. However, research has to be done to identify such strategic host targets. Previous work done on TMEM16F, a cell membrane Ca2+-activated scramblase, has identified it as an interesting candidate for host-directed antiviral strategies. TMEM16F’s role in phosphatidylserine (PS) translocation from the inner leaflet of the cell membrane to the outer leaflet is essential for microvesicle secretion. However, research has shown TMEM16F can be hijacked by viruses, such as Ebola virus and Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), for viral apoptotic mimicry and for cell fusion, respectively. By activating TMEM16F for PS translocation, membrane-derived viral envelopes may contain this phospholipid, enabling glycoprotein-independent viral entry and dissemination into a host. The extent to which different virus families utilize this scramblase for viral spread and immune evasion remains to be elucidated.
