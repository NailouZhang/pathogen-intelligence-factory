Characterizing population movement patterns during a Sudan Virus Disease outbreak to adjust response efforts, Uganda, 2022.

Authors: Daniel Eurien, Kanan Mehta, Gabriella Lamb, Elvira McIntyre, Rogers Kisame, Geofrey Kisunzu, Harriet Itiakorit, Michael Muhoozi, Joseph Ojwang, Harriet Mayinja
Original abstract (English)
OBJECTIVES: In September 2022, Uganda declared a Sudan virus disease outbreak in Mubende District. The Uganda Ministry of Health sought to characterize population movement patterns to identify areas at heightened transmission risk and guide outbreak response efforts. METHODS: From September to December 2022, response teams adapted the Population Connectivity Across Borders (PopCAB) method to conduct focus group discussions, key informant interviews, and participatory mapping with case-contacts and community members across three districts. Results characterizing local and cross-border travel routes and reasons for travel were applied to adjust response actions. RESULTS: Participants identified 69 locations across seven non-contiguous districts, including healthcare facilities, gold mines, schools, and transportation hubs linked to travel for trade, farming, healthcare, and family visits. Ministry of Health response teams used the results to enhance surveillance, risk communication, and infection prevention measures at priority locations including cross-border areas. Surveillance activities adjusted by the PopCAB results directly detected two cases in Kampala and indirectly identified 26 additional cases in newly targeted districts. CONCLUSIONS: Integrating community-based mobility mapping into contact tracing enabled rapid identification of high-risk areas beyond the outbreak epicenter. Response teams in similar settings can rapidly deploy this approach to generate real-time mobility data, improve resource allocation, and strengthen communicable disease containment efforts.

The welfare and productivity losses associated with the death of an index case of Ebola Bundibugyo Virus Disease reported in Kenya on 6 October 2026

Authors: Joses Muthuri Kirigia
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Introduction: Ebola Bundibugyo virus disease (EBVD) was imported into Kenya from the Democratic Republic of the Congo on 3 October 2026. Economic evidence is lacking to inform Kenya’s Ministry of Health’s advocacy for domestic and external investment to bolster prevention, mitigation, preparedness, and response to EBVD. This study aimed to estimate, from an economy-wide (societal) perspective, the discounted welfare and productivity losses associated with the death of the index case of EBVD in Kenya on 6 October 2026. Methods Drawing on various secondary sources, this study uses the human capital approach (HCA), the willingness-to-pay small (WTP_S) and large (WTP_L) models, and the willingness-to-accept small (WTA_S) and large (WTA_L) models to estimate discounted welfare and productivity losses associated with the death of the index case of EBVD in Kenya. To assess the robustness of the results, the five models were each re-estimated four times, assuming (i) a 5% discount rate, (ii) a 10% discount rate, (iii) Africa’s highest male average life expectancy at birth, 74.55 years in Tunisia, and (iv) the world’s highest male average life expectancy, 84.78 years in Monaco. Results Under the HCA model, the death of the 40-year-old male patient is expected to result in a total welfare loss of Int$120,944 and a productivity loss of Int$40,763. Using the implied WTP approach, the expected welfare loss would be between Int$423,302 and Int$786,133, with a productivity loss of between Int$142,669 and Int$264,957. Applying the implied WTA approach yields a welfare loss of Int$1,269,907 to Int$4,051,608 and a productivity loss of Int$428,007 to Int$1,365,546. Re-estimating the HCA, WTP_S, WTP_L, WTA_S, and WTA_L models with 5% and 10% discount rates reduced the estimated welfare and productivity losses by about 17% and 45%, respectively. Re-running the welfare and productivity loss models with Africa's and the world’s highest male life expectancies increased the welfare loss estimates by about 35% and 54%, respectively. Conclusions The death of the index case of EBVD in Kenya will likely lead to significant welfare and productivity losses. Kenya’s Ministry of Health should continue advocacy with the whole-of-government, the private sector, civil society, and development partners to urgently increase investment to bridge gaps in International Health Regulations core capacities, universal health coverage, and WASH (water, sanitation, and hygiene), thereby mitigating the risk of EBVD becoming a national epidemic.

Original abstract (English)
Ebolavirus VP40 drives viral budding by remodeling the plasma membrane, yet the underlying real-time dynamics remain poorly understood. Authors: Joseph KASIAMA NGI ONKOR, Camile Likotelo Binene, Glad Lufimpu Mampuya
Original abstract (English)
Epidemiological surveillance systems produce decision-making indicators from data that may be affected by under-reporting, misclassification and notification delays. These errors can propagate through the extraction, transformation, loading and multidimensional aggregation stages. This article proposes a mathematical framework that integrates measurement error correction into an ETL–data warehouse–OLAP decision architecture. The central problem is to determine whether the correction of a misclassification error can be performed before or after aggregation without changing the resulting indicator. We show that the Rogan–Gladen estimator commutes with the roll-up operator only when the sensitivity and specificity parameters are homogeneous across the aggregated strata. A numerical instantiation based on a synthetic dataset calibrated on published parameters of the Ebola virus disease epidemic in North Kivu and Ituri (DRC, 2018–2020) illustrates this non-commutation and its consequences for the estimation of incidence and the reproduction number. These results lead to a design rule: when surveillance quality is heterogeneous, correction must be performed at the finest level at which the error parameters can be regarded as homogeneous, before any aggregation, because this is the only strategy that remains unbiased whatever the spatial distribution of cases. A bias–variance analysis (Theorem 4) and a Monte Carlo uncertainty analysis (5,000 replicates, seven scenarios) further show that aggregate-level correction can have a slightly smaller variance but becomes biased when cases concentrate in poorly covered zones, and that the corrected indicators are highly sensitive to the assumed specificity when test positivity is close to the false-positive floor (a misspecification of 0.005 in specificity changes cumulative incidence by more than 100%). No individual-level surveillance data were used: the synthetic data are fully specified by a generation algorithm and a parameter table provided for reproducibility. The proposed framework thus provides a formal basis for designing surveillance decision-support systems that explicitly account for data quality.

Development of Heterocyclic Small Molecule Antiviral Entry Inhibitors

Authors: John Phillip Sloan
Original abstract (English)
There is an urgent need to develop novel antiviral therapeutics to address the growing threat posed by viruses. Small-molecule inhibitors of viral entry are attractive antiviral agents because they block the virus at its earliest step, preventing the virus from entering host cells and causing downstream damage. Some of the viruses of greatest concern for human health, such as human immunodeficiency virus, respiratory syncytial virus, influenza virus, Ebola virus, severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2, and Lassa virus, encode Class 1 fusion proteins which mediate membrane fusion and viral entry. In this dissertation, we highlight recent advances in the discovery and development of small-molecule entry inhibitors reported over the past five years that target viruses encoding Class 1 fusion proteins. Additionally, we report thieno[3,2-b]pyrroles as novel Group 2 influenza A virus entry inhibitors. Lastly, we report the further optimization of the raloxifene‐inspired benzothiophenes scaffold as novel pan-filovirus entry inhibitors.

Cytoplasmic ATR orchestrates PRRSV and EBOV replication organelle assembly via vimentin phosphorylation

Authors: Chenchen Xu, Yuanyuan Xia, Zengguo Cao, Xinyi Xu, Yuehao Xiang, Fangxu Li, Pengcheng Li, Sandra Chiu, Xuejuan Wang
Original abstract (English)
The ataxia telangiectasia-mutated and Rad3-related (ATR) kinase is a well-established master regulator of the DNA damage response and replication stress. In this study, we uncover an unexpected, conserved role of ATR in promoting the replication of two distinct RNA viruses-an arterivirus and a filovirus-through vimentin phosphorylation. Acute pharmacologic inhibition or knockdown of ATR robustly suppress viral replication and sharply reduce the formation of double-membrane vesicles and membrane-less condensates that serve as replication organelles (ROs) for these divergent viral classes. Notably, although ATR is recruited to cytoplasmic ROs throughout the viral life cycle, it acts specifically at the replication stage without affecting viral attachment, entry, or egress. We further show that ATR directly interacts with and phosphorylates vimentin within ROs, driving vimentin rearrangement and delineating RO boundaries. Mechanistically, ATR-dependent phosphorylation of vimentin at Ser459 is indispensable for RO formation during infection by both PRRSV and EBOV. Together, these findings reveal a conserved ATR-vimentin signaling axis that promotes RO biogenesis across diverse RNA viruses, defining a noncanonical function of cytoplasmic ATR in orchestrating the assembly of both membrane-bound and membrane-less replication compartments via vimentin phosphorylation.

Bundibugyo virus disease in outbreak settings: A practical clinical review

Authors: Samriddhi Srivastava, Ananya Kaul, Mudit Singla, Tweheyo Ronald
Original abstract (English)
Bundibugyo virus disease (BVD) is a severe filovirus infection caused by Bundibugyo virus (BDBV), an orthoebolavirus with diagnostic and countermeasure implications distinct from Ebola virus (EBOV). The ongoing outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) has expanded substantially, with 7890 confirmed cases and 3799 deaths reported across 63 health zones in seven provinces as of 23 September 2026. BVD usually presents as a nonspecific febrile and gastrointestinal illness; haemorrhagic manifestations are uncommon at presentation. Early outbreak investigation also showed that EBOV-oriented testing can fail to detect BDBV, making species-appropriate molecular testing essential when BDBV is epidemiologically plausible. Supportive care remains the standard of management, but what can be delivered safely differs between peripheral facilities and dedicated filovirus treatment centres. First-contact facilities should prioritise recognition, separation, protected sampling, treatment of immediately life-threatening or common treatable conditions, and early referral; treatment centres can provide closer monitoring, advanced supportive care, and access to approved research protocols. Insecurity, displacement, laboratory and transport constraints, and delayed presentation continue to limit implementation. No vaccine or specific therapeutic has established clinical efficacy against BDBV in humans; Ervebo is being evaluated within research protocols and the PARTNERS trial is evaluating MBP134 and remdesivir-based regimens. This review synthesises historical and current evidence on recognition, diagnosis, clinical care, infection prevention, referral, and emerging countermeasures in outbreak-affected settings.

The Attenuation Paradox in Bundibugyo Virus Disease: Case Fatality, Ascertainment, and Delayed Outbreak Recognition

Authors: Shamsi Saad Shamsi
Original abstract (English)
Bundibugyo virus has caused three recognised outbreaks, and each was recognised late. The usual reading of that record treats the delay as a failure of investment, diagnostics or governance. This review sets out a partly mechanistic alternative: the low case fatality ratio observed early in a Bundibugyo virus outbreak is itself one of the conditions that produces the delay, and the delay in turn depresses the observed ratio further. The evidence is organised around three comparison tables: the burden, severity and recognition interval of each outbreak; the presenting clinical features set against a matched Ebola virus series; and the point at which species assignment failed in each event. A schematic draws the relationships together. Two findings emerge. The clinical syndrome does not discriminate between species: haemorrhage was documented in roughly a quarter of confirmed patients in both the 2007–08 Bundibugyo series and a 2014 Ebola virus series, so the presenting picture cannot carry the explanatory weight often placed on it. The discriminating failures are instead genomic and diagnostic, and the early case fatality ratio operates as a surveillance trigger that is biased downward precisely when ascertainment is narrowest. The 2026 epidemic, in which the confirmed ratio rose from 12.1% to 44.3% while the virus doubled every 5.5 days, fits this account better than it fits a change in virulence. A fatality ratio from the first weeks of a filovirus outbreak should therefore be read as an index of surveillance maturity rather than of danger, and escalation should be governed by species-inclusive confirmation.

Recent Advances in Experimental Models to Study Endotheliopathy in Viral Hemorrhagic Fevers: Relevance to Lassa Virus

Authors: Danny Noack, Lars Frenzel, Daryna Krenytska, Till Frederik Omansen, Lisa Oestereich
Original abstract (English)
Endothelial cells are a common target of several viral hemorrhagic fevers (VHF), including those caused by Lassa virus (LASV) and Ebola virus, yet whether endothelial infection drives the endotheliopathy underlying vascular leakage, coagulopathy and inflammation in severe VHF remains unclear. Understanding these mechanisms requires experimental approaches that adequately capture the complexity of the vascular microenvironment. Here, we review recent experimental advances in understanding endothelial involvement in VHF pathogenesis, with a focus on LASV and complementary recent evidence from other VHFs. We discuss endothelial cell infection in non-human primates and in vitro, immune-endothelial crosstalk, and the effects of soluble or surface-exposed viral proteins on endothelial barrier integrity. Although experimental evidence for LASV-induced endothelial cell activation and barrier dysfunction remains limited, we propose that direct endothelial infection alone is insufficient to account for increased vascular permeability during Lassa fever. Drawing on mechanistic insights from other VHFs, we discuss potential contributions of immune-endothelial interactions, including IL-6 trans-signaling, and soluble or surface-exposed viral proteins to vascular dysfunction during Lassa fever. Furthermore, we review recent advances in organ-on-chip and organoid technologies for modeling the human vasculature and investigating barrier function in the context of VHFs, considering the practical biosafety constraints of high-containment laboratories. Finally, we highlight experimental and methodological priorities for improving the physiological relevance and standardization of these models to enable future mechanistic studies of VHF-associated endotheliopathy.

Zaire virus was identified in 1976 and has driven most recognised outbreaks; Sudan virus appeared in the same year; Bundibugyo virus was first detected in 2007 and has since caused repeated epidemics; Tai Forest virus has produced a single known human case. That distribution of risk explains why one species became the reference strain for countermeasure development and why the others were treated as footnotes. Concentration of effort delivered results. After the 2014-2016 West African epidemic, licensed vaccines, monoclonal antibody cocktails and improved molecular diagnostics moved from experimental platforms into national and international stockpiles, and case fatality fell where they were deployed. The design logic, however, remained single-species. Antigens were drawn from Zaire ebolavirus glycoprotein, antibody cocktails were selected against Zaire epitopes, and diagnostic primers were validated on Zaire genomes. Cross-protection was assumed rather than measured, on the reasoning that glycoprotein is conserved enough for a vaccine to work across the genus. The 2026 Bundibugyo outbreak tested that reasoning in the field. Sequence divergence between species lowers the sensitivity of assays built around Zaire targets, and the result is a double failure: false reassurance when a Zaire-only test is negative in a patient who has filovirus disease, and missed non-Zaire infections that never enter surveillance counts. Vaccines and therapeutics authorized for one species offer uncertain benefit against another, and the uncertainty cannot be resolved in the middle of an emergency. A broader framework is the remedy, and it has concrete components. For diagnostics, conserved pan-ebolavirus targets should be paired with species-specific assays, so that a negative result on a Zaire platform is not read as the absence of disease and so that the responsible species is identified at the point of care rather than weeks later. For vaccines, single-antigen designs should give way to multivalent, mosaic or conserved T-cell epitope platforms, and to constructs that can be updated quickly when antigenic data change. For therapeutics, epitope conservation across species deserves the same scrutiny that affinity for Zaire glycoprotein now receives. All three strands depend on genomic surveillance that is continuous rather than reactive, because mutations affecting primer binding, antigenicity or antibody epitopes must be found before they compromise a stockpile, and because sequence data are what allow a vaccine or assay to be revised on a timescale shorter than an outbreak. Operational readiness forms the last layer, and it is the one most often reduced to inventory. Stockpiles are necessary but not sufficient: unfamiliarity with clinical protocols and pathogen-specific procedures delays response even when guidelines exist on paper. Simulation-based training, including virtual reality modules written for high-consequence infectious disease, can embed procedural competence into routine practice, covering protective equipment donning and doffing, cross-contamination prevention and assessment of suspected special pathogens. Exercises should extend to pathogen identification, rapid diagnostic deployment and the operational machinery that accelerates vaccine and therapeutic development, since none of these capacities can be improvised during an outbreak. The lesson generalises beyond Bundibugyo virus to the wider list of priority pathogens, and it argues for readiness sustained between outbreaks, spanning clinical, public health and laboratory systems, so that the interval between emergence and effective response keeps shortening. None of this is specific to filoviruses, which is the point: the capacity to recognise, isolate and manage an unfamiliar pathogen is generic, and it can be built before the pathogen has a name. By Franca Ofili The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) said Nigeria has recorded no additional Ebola case, 21 days after confirming an imported infection of the Bundibugyo virus from Uganda. It however said 28 contacts still remained under active surveillance for Ebola disease in the country. The Director-General of the NCDC, Dr Jide Idris, disclosed this in a national health security update issued on Tuesday. Idris said the agency had identified 62 contacts, with 34 completing their individual 21-day monitoring period, while 28 remained under surveillance. He explained that being identified as a contact did not mean an individual had Ebola, stressing that monitoring enabled early detection and prompt response to symptoms. “NCDC is sustaining contact tracing, laboratory testing, surveillance, infection prevention and investigation of reported alerts in collaboration with relevant federal and state authorities,” Idris said. He urged healthcare workers in public and private facilities to remain vigilant, establish patients’ travel and exposure histories, and immediately report suspected Ebola cases to public health authorities. Idris advised anyone developing symptoms after possible exposure to avoid contact with others and call the NCDC toll-free line, 6232, for guidance before visiting a health facility. He said dengue fever had been confirmed in Sokoto and Zamfara States, while Chikungunya was confirmed in Sokoto, prompting intensified surveillance and response activities. The NCDC boss said National Rapid Response Teams, including entomologists from the National Arbovirus Research Centre, Enugu, had been deployed to support affected states. He said the teams were assisting with case identification, laboratory investigations, clinical readiness, community engagement and mosquito control to contain the infections. Idris warned Nigerians against assuming that every fever was malaria, noting that dengue and Chikungunya could cause fever, headaches, body pains and joint pains. He urged people experiencing persistent fever, rash, vomiting or severe joint pains to seek medical assessment, warning that dengue could become life-threatening when warning signs were ignored. He advised households to eliminate stagnant water, cover water containers, clear discarded tyres and protect themselves against mosquito bites, noting that disease-carrying mosquitoes often bite during the daytime. On cerebrospinal meningitis, Idris said the agency was strengthening preparedness as the dry season advanced, with increased risks in parts of the meningitis belt. He urged states to improve disease surveillance, diagnostic services, clinical readiness and vaccination responses, where indicated, to prevent avoidable deaths. He urged Nigerians experiencing sudden fever, severe headache, neck stiffness or confusion to seek urgent medical attention, while advising health facilities to promptly identify and refer suspected cases. The NCDC director-general said diphtheria remained a concern, particularly among children who had missed routine immunisation, adding that the agency was supporting surveillance, laboratory diagnosis and outbreak response. He urged parents and caregivers to check their children’s vaccination status and seek guidance on missed doses, stressing that vaccination and early treatment were essential. Idris said fever accompanied by sore throat, neck swelling, difficulty swallowing or breathing required prompt medical assessment to prevent complications from diphtheria. On Lassa fever, he said Nigeria was approaching the period when cases historically increased, urging states to strengthen surveillance, testing, treatment readiness and infection prevention before the seasonal peak. He called on health facilities to ensure adequate supplies, trained personnel and safe referral arrangements, while protecting frontline workers against infection. He advised households to protect food from rodents, store food in covered containers and maintain clean surroundings, urging people with persistent fever to seek early medical attention. Idris also urged clinicians to consider other possible infections beyond malaria when assessing patients with persistent fever, particularly during the Lassa fever season. Ahead of the festive period, he called on state governments, event organisers and healthcare providers to strengthen health and safety arrangements for concerts, religious gatherings and other mass events. He urged event organisers to provide basic medical assistance, maintain hygiene and sanitation, and establish clear procedures for identifying and reporting suspected infectious diseases. He also advised travellers and participants at gatherings to follow public health advisories, seek medical attention when unwell and avoid crowded events when experiencing symptoms. “State governments have to sustain surveillance, laboratory capacity, treatment readiness and community engagement, while providing frontline workers with adequate funding and operational resources,” Idris advised. He urged public and private health facilities to promptly report suspected infections and implement appropriate measures to protect healthcare personnel. Idris advised Nigerians to ensure children were fully vaccinated, protect themselves against mosquitoes and rodents, seek medical attention early and avoid spreading unverified information. He said Nigerians could report suspected cases or obtain guidance through the NCDC toll-free line, 6232, or their respective state public health authorities. The NCDC boss said the Ebola response remained active, alongside interventions against dengue, Chikungunya and diphtheria, as the country strengthened preparedness for meningitis and Lassa fever. He acknowledged the Federal Government, the Coordinating Minister of Health and Social Welfare, Prof. Muhammad Pate, state governments, frontline health workers, partners and communities for supporting national health security efforts. He emphasised that early detection, disease prevention and sustained preparedness remained critical to protecting Nigerians, adding that everyone had a role in safeguarding public health.
