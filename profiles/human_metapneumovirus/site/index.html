尼泊尔儿童呼吸道感染中人偏肺病毒(HMPV)全基因组测序

Whole Genome Sequencing of Human Metapneumovirus from Pediatric Respiratory Infections in Nepal
作者： Nishan Katuwal, Aastha Shrestha, Sarita Gosain, Aarjya Tara Bajracharya, Sabin Bikram Shahi, Dipesh Tamrakar, Rajeev Shrestha
摘要中文翻译人偏肺病毒(HMPV)是儿童急性呼吸道感染的主要原因之一,但在尼泊尔其基因组仍缺乏充分研究。以往的研究主要依赖于PCR或部分测序,对病毒基因组的理解有限。本研究旨在利用全基因组测序(WGS)对尼泊尔儿童呼吸道感染中的HMPV基因组进行表征,并了解其系统发育基因组学特征。使用Illumina iSeq100对2024年收集的四份存档的HMPV阳性鼻咽拭子样本进行了测序,这些样本来自15至48个月龄的儿童。通过Chan Zuckerberg ID生成共识基因组,使用Nextclade进行谱系分配,并与代表性区域的HMPV基因组进行最大似然系统发育基因组分析。所有四个样本均成功生成了完整的或接近完整的HMPV基因组,平均测序深度范围为9.4x至103.1x,基因组覆盖率为96.6%至98.7%。其中两个基因组属于亚谱系A2.2.2,一个属于谱系B1,一个属于谱系B2,表明多种谱系在共循环。系统发育分析显示所有基因组均聚集在已知的区域谱系中,未发现新谱系的出现。所有儿童均表现出下呼吸道感染,主要为支气管肺炎,而实验室参数在不同个体间有所差异。本研究报道了尼泊尔首个HMPV全基因组测序,并展示了A2.2.2、B1和B2谱系在儿童呼吸道感染中的共循环现象。这些发现为尼泊尔呼吸道病毒的系统发育监测提供了基础,并支持将下一代测序技术整合到病毒监测中。

Whole Genome Sequencing of Human Metapneumovirus from Pediatric Respiratory Infections in Nepal

Authors: Nishan Katuwal, Aastha Shrestha, Sarita Gosain, Aarjya Tara Bajracharya, Sabin Bikram Shahi, Dipesh Tamrakar, Rajeev Shrestha
Original abstract (English)
Human metapneumovirus (HMPV) is a major cause of acute respiratory infections in children, yet its genomic epidemiology remains understudied in Nepal. Previous studies have relied either on PCR or partial sequencing, providing limited insight into the viral genome. This study aimed to characterize HMPV genomes from pediatric respiratory infections using whole-genome sequencing (WGS) and understand their phylogenomics. Four archived HMPV-positive nasopharyngeal swab samples collected in 2024, from children aged 15–48 months, were sequenced in Illumina iSeq100. The consensus genomes were generated using Chan Zuckerberg ID, lineage assignment was performed using Nextclade, and maximum likelihood phylogenomic analysis was conducted with representative regional HMPV genomes. Complete or near-complete HMPV genomes were successfully generated from all four samples, with average sequencing depths ranging from 9.4x to 103.1x and genome coverage from 96.6% to 98.7%. Two genomes were of sublineage A2.2.2, while one each belonged to lineages B1 and B2, demonstrating the co-circulation of multiple lineages. Phylogenetic analysis showed that all genomes clustered within known regional lineages, with no evidence of novel lineage emergence. All children presented with lower respiratory tract infections, predominantly bronchopneumonia, while laboratory parameters varied across subjects. This study reports one of the first whole-genome sequencing of HMPV from Nepal and demonstrates the co-circulation of A2.2.2, B1, and B2 lineages among pediatric respiratory infections. These findings provide a baseline for genomic surveillance of respiratory viruses in Nepal and support the integration of next-generation sequencing to monitor viral evolution and transmission.

一种自动UVC-LED设备对听诊器膜片上细菌、真菌和包膜呼吸道病毒的体外灭活效果

本研究旨在解决听诊器缺乏一致清洁实践的问题,因为听诊器频繁在患者之间传递,可能携带病原体。研究旨在评估一种自动UVC-LED设备在灭活听诊器膜片上的各种微生物方面的有效性。

In vitro efficacy of an automatic UVC-LED device for disinfection of the stethoscope diaphragm against bacteria, fungi and enveloped respiratory viruses

Authors: Davide Amodeo, Anna Carmina De Francesco, Giulia Nuredini, Diana Mihaela Constanda, Caterina Fazzi, Alessandro Manenti, Emanuele Montomoli, Ilaria Manini, Serena Marchi
Original abstract (English)
Abstract The stethoscope moves from patient to patient far more often than it is cleaned, and chemical disinfection is applied inconsistently because it depends on the operator remembering and on a wipe being within reach. We tested a UVC-LED device emitting at 275 nm that runs a single automatic 150-second cycle on the stethoscope diaphragm, against nine reference bacterial and fungal strains and four enveloped respiratory viruses (influenza A and B, human parainfluenza virus 3 and human metapneumovirus) placed onto a quartz carrier. One cycle drove viable counts to at or below the limit of detection across the whole panel, a reduction of roughly 5 log10 that held for the multidrug-resistant yeast Candida auris , the UV-resistant conidia of Aspergillus brasiliensis and the rapidly growing Mycobacterium abscessus ; because the treated samples fell below detection, these are conservative lower bounds set by the inoculum. All four viruses lost their ability to infect cells down to the lowest concentration that could be measured. For influenza viruses we traced that loss to the genome itself: a long-range PA-gene product disappeared, and sequencing coverage collapsed first on the longest segments, while a short diagnostic RT-PCR target barely moved, below 1 log10. Because damage builds up with the length of the target, short-amplicon assays overstate the residual infectious load. A single operator-independent cycle cleared a deliberately broad panel, drug-resistant and UV-resistant organisms included, and abolished viral infectivity through real loss of genome integrity, not surviving nucleic acid. Confirmation under soiled conditions and in clinical use would carry it into routine practice.

The study aimed to address the lack of consistent cleaning practices for stethoscopes, which are frequently moved between patients and may harbor pathogens. Interpretation and novelty
The findings demonstrate that the UVC-LED device effectively inactivates human metapneumovirus by causing real loss of genome integrity, not just degradation of nucleic acids, which is a novel approach to assessing viral inactivation.
Scientific and public-health significance
The results suggest that the UVC-LED device could be a valuable tool for reducing the transmission of human metapneumovirus in clinical settings, particularly in situations where manual cleaning is inconsistent or impractical.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study was conducted under controlled laboratory conditions, and further confirmation under soiled or clinical settings is needed to establish its routine efficacy. The evidence is strong for the tested conditions but may not fully represent real-world scenarios.

Human metapneumovirus: an underestimated cause of severe respiratory infection.

Authors: Catia Cilloniz, Antonia Ho, Chanu Rhee
Original abstract (English)
Since its identification in 2001, human metapneumovirus (hMPV) has been recognized as an important cause of acute respiratory tract infection in all age groups. Although infection is often mild in healthy adults, hMPV can cause severe lower respiratory tract disease, including pneumonia, in young children, older adults, individuals with chronic diseases and immunocompromising conditions, and residents of long-term care facilities. Accumulating evidence demonstrates that hMPV contributes substantially to hospitalization, intensive care unit admission, and mortality in these high-risk populations. Globally, hMPV circulates seasonally, most commonly in late winter and spring, contributing to both community- and healthcare-associated respiratory infections; however, the COVID-19 pandemic disrupted established epidemiology, resulting in shifts in epidemic timing, off-season resurgences, genotype replacement, and increased overlap with other respiratory viruses, while also providing insights into viral interference and multivirus cocirculation. Clinical manifestations of hMPV overlap with other respiratory viruses, including influenza virus and SARS-CoV-2, and selective diagnostic practices contribute to underrecognition and undertesting. Although no specific antiviral therapies or licensed vaccines are currently available, identification of hMPV has important implications for diagnostic accuracy, prognosis, antimicrobial stewardship, and infection prevention, particularly in hospital and long-term care settings. This review summarizes current evidence on the virology, epidemiology, post-COVID-19 trends, clinical manifestations, outcomes, diagnosis, and prevention of hMPV, with a particular focus on adults and other high-risk populations. We also review the current landscape of hMPV vaccine development and its potential implications for clinical practice and public health.

Authors: Sayad Ahad Ali, Shamim Shamim, Nasiruddin Ahmad Farooqui
Original abstract (English)
Introduction: Human Metapneumovirus is a significant respiratory pathogen causing infections across all age groups, particularly in young children, immunocompromised individuals, and the elderly. Despite its clinical relevance, there are currently no approved vaccines or specific antiviral treatments for Human Metapneumovirus, necessitating further research into therapeutic and preventive strategies. Methods: A systematic literature review was conducted using peer-reviewed journals, clinical trials, and recent studies focusing on Human Metapneumovirus infection. Data were extracted and analyzed to evaluate the effectiveness of antiviral therapies, monoclonal antibodies, immunomodulators, and vaccine candidates. Results: Studies indicate that Human Metapneumovirus primarily targets the respiratory epithelium, triggering immune responses that contribute to both viral clearance and disease pathogenesis. Investigational therapies, including fusion inhibitors and protease inhibitors, show promise but require further validation. Immunomodulatory agents and herbal remedies may offer supportive benefits, but their efficacy against Human Metapneumovirus remains unproven. Advances in vaccine development are ongoing, with several candidates in preclinical and early clinical stages. Discussion: HMPV continues to pose a substantial public health burden due to its seasonal outbreaks, clinical similarity to other respiratory viruses, and absence of specific therapies. The virus evades immune responses via multiple mechanisms, contributing to disease severity, particularly in vulnerable populations. Although supportive care is currently the mainstay, emerging antivirals and vaccines may offer future solutions. Improved diagnostic access, public awareness, and targeted research are essential for controlling HMPV infections. Conclusion: Human Metapneumovirus continues to pose a public health challenge due to the lack of targeted treatments and vaccines. Ongoing research into antiviral agents, monoclonal antibodies, and vaccines is critical for effective disease management. Further clinical trials are needed to establish safe and effective therapeutic options.
