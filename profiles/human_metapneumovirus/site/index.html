Authors: Rachel M. Hartman, Sainimere Boladuadua, Catherine Gayle Sutcliffe, Gilberto Alvarez-Colon, Nikki Anderson, Jessica E. Atwell, Danika Bailie, Amanda B. Burrage, Angela P. Campbell, James D. Chappell
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Data on human metapneumovirus (HMPV)-associated acute respiratory infections (ARI) among American Indian/Alaska Native (AI/AN) children are lacking publications. METHODS: We conducted active ARI surveillance among hospitalized and outpatient AI/AN children <5 years of age at select facilities in Navajo Nation, White Mountain Apache Tribal lands, and Alaska (Anchorage and Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta). Children meeting a symptom-based ARI case definition from November 2019 to May 2024 were eligible. Data were collected via questionnaire and chart review. Mid-turbinate nasal swabs were tested for HMPV and other respiratory pathogens by polymerase chain reaction. Incidence rates of HMPV-associated hospitalization were calculated using Poisson regression. Seasonality was described, and risk factors for HMPV-associated hospitalization were assessed using Poisson multivariable modeling with robust standard errors. RESULTS: Overall, 13.0% (218/1683) of children hospitalized with ARI and 7.0% (81/1165) of outpatient children with ARI were HMPV-positive. HMPV generally circulated December through May; seasonality and incidence were disrupted by the coronavirus disease 2019 pandemic. In 2023-2024, incidence rates of HMPV-associated ARI hospitalization per 1000 children <5 years old were 11.3 (95% confidence interval: 8.6-14.8) in the Southwest, 10.3 (7.4-14.4) in the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta, and 0 (0-1.1) in Anchorage. In the Poisson model, age <2 years (adjusted risk ratio: 1.43 [1.08-1.90]) and presenting to care ≥4 days after illness onset (1.38 [1.09-1.75]) were significantly associated with increased risk for HMPV-associated hospitalization compared with HMPV-associated outpatient visits. CONCLUSIONS: This analysis documents the burden of HMPV-associated ARI and hospitalization among AI/AN children <5 years of age as a baseline to inform the use of future pediatric HMPV prevention products.

Spatiotemporal dynamics of human metapneumovirus and potential impact of respiratory syncytial virus interventions in the United States

Authors: Ke Li, Stephanie Perniciaro, Jiye Kwon, Nathan D. Grubaugh, Daniel M. Weinberger, Virginia E. Pitzer
Original abstract (English)
Human metapneumovirus (HMPV) causes acute lower respiratory infections, primarily affecting young children and older adults, with seasonal outbreaks peaking annually in March or April in the United States and other temperate regions in the Northern hemisphere. However, the factors driving HMPV seasonality in the United States remain poorly understood. We analyzed laboratory-confirmed HMPV cases and age-specific emergency department visits across 10 US regions, fitting an age-stratified dynamic transmission model to assess spatiotemporal patterns and investigate the influence of environmental variables and viral interference from RSV on HMPV transmission rates. We found that models incorporating climate variables into the transmission rate, including vapor pressure, precipitation, potential evapotranspiration, and minimum temperature, could not capture the timing of HMPV activity across all regions. Instead, HMPV timing was associated with RSV activity, with the HMPV transmission rate reduced in the presence of RSV. We showed that among the candidate models considered, only the models that included an RSV-associated term could reproduce the biennial pattern of HMPV observed in some regions, characterized by alternating “late-small” and “early-large” epidemics. When the best-fit model including the RSV-associated term was used to predict the potential impact of RSV interventions on HMPV transmission, we found that RSV interventions are unlikely to result in a substantial increase in HMPV activity despite reducing competition from RSV. Our work unravels the spatiotemporal dynamics of HMPV and its interaction with RSV, informing future seasonal forecasting and intervention strategies for HMPV.

A one-step multiplex real-time reverse transcription quantitative PCR assay for rapid and simultaneous detection of human parainfluenza virus, human metapneumovirus, and rhinovirus in patients with acute respiratory tract infection

Authors: Mingwen Zhao, Peng Qiu, Wei Wang, Yong-Hui Li, Jie Yang, Meng-Ting Wu, Pei Zhao
Original abstract (English)
OBJECTIVE: Clinically, acute respiratory tract infections (ARIs) caused by human parainfluenza virus (PIV), human metapneumovirus (MPV), and human rhinoviruses (HRV) are often indistinguishable due to overlapping symptoms. This study aimed to develop a one-step multiplex real-time RT-qPCR assay for simultaneous, rapid, and accurate detection of PIV (types 1-4), MPV (subgroups A and B), and HRV (species A and B). METHODS: A one-step multiplex real-time RT-qPCR assay was established for simultaneous detection of PIV, MPV, and HRV in clinical specimens. Primers and probes targeted conserved genomic regions, and an internal reference gene (B2M) was included for quality assurance. The limit of detection was determined via serial dilution of standard strains. Assay specificity was verified using 14 non-target pathogens, and repeatability was evaluated across various specimen concentrations. Clinical validation involved comparison with a commercial kit, with sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, agreement rate, and Kappa coefficient calculated for assessment. RESULTS: A one-step multiplex real-time RT-qPCR assay was successfully developed for the simultaneous detection of PIV, MPV, and HRV. The assay demonstrated a detection limit of 100 copies/mL for all three targets, expressed as the equivalent concentration in the original specimen before nucleic acid extraction; this LOD was determined under multiplex co-amplification conditions, with all three targets present in the same reaction tube at every concentration level. No cross-reactivity was observed against the 14 specified non-target pathogens under the conditions evaluated, and all eight target strains were detected. Repeatability testing showed coefficients of variation (CV) of ≤ 2% for Ct values at low and medium concentrations. At the individual-target level, the sensitivity of the developed assay was 98.82% for HRV, 98.55% for PIV, and 100.0% for MPV, with specificity of 100.0% for all three targets. Pooled (target-level) sensitivity and specificity were 99.06% and 100.0%, respectively, and the overall agreement with the reference method was 99.24% (260/262). CONCLUSION: The method provides a technical approach for the simultaneous detection of PIV, MPV, and HRV species A and B. Its clinical utility relative to comprehensive respiratory pathogen panels remains to be established.

Authors: Bart G. Jones, Sherri L. Surman, Kristen C. Branum, Kim J. Allison, Toru Takimoto, Christopher Coleclough, Elisabeth E. Adderson, David I. Bernstein, Octavio Ramilo, Leena Yeolekar
Original abstract (English)
Background Respiratory viruses including human parainfluenza virus (hPIV), respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and human metapneumovirus (hMPV), pose serious, potentially lethal threats to humans, particularly infants and young children. While maternal vaccines and monoclonal antibodies are advancing to provide transient protection against these viruses, licensed pediatric vaccines are not yet available. Sendai virus (SeV) is now being developed as an intranasal (I.N.) vaccine candidate for the prevention of serious respiratory viral infections in children. Methods To examine the potential of replication-competent Sendai virus and its recombinants as pediatric respiratory virus vaccines against hPIV, RSV and hMPV, we searched literature databases and evaluated both historical and contemporary published research. Results SeV and SeV recombinant vaccines have been tested pre-clinically in a variety of animal models and in Phase I clinical studies. In pre-clinical models, a single dose of SeV induced rapid and long-lasting B- and T-cell responses in the upper respiratory tract and blood. When recombinant SeV constructs were produced and formulated into cocktail vaccines, they were protective against four different respiratory pathogens simultaneously. Vaccines were successful when partnered with passively transferred antibodies; specifically, when a recombinant SeV-based RSV vaccine was tested in the presence of passively transferred monoclonal or polyclonal PIV-specific or RSV-specific antibodies in a cotton rat model, the vaccine provided durable protective immunity against RSV. In clinical trials, SeV was well-tolerated and uniformly immunogenic in PIV-seropositive 1–2 year old children. Methods have been developed for the manufacture of stable SeV-based vaccine products, compatible with wide global distribution of SeV vaccines for clinical use. Conclusion To date, results highlight SeV as an attractive pediatric vaccine candidate. Continued clinical testing of SeV may provide global relief from serious respiratory viral infections in infants and young children.

Temporal dynamics of respiratory pathogens among adult SARI patients in Shanghai, 2019–2023: a multi-center longitudinal surveillance study

Authors: Qi Qiu, Huilin Shi, Chenyan Jiang, Zheng Teng, Jiajing Liu, Huanyu Wu, Yaxu Zheng, Jian Chen
Original abstract (English)
COVID-19 greatly reshaped the epidemiology of severe acute respiratory infection (SARI), yet long-term shifts in multi-pathogen circulation remain poorly characterized.This study explored temporal patterns of common respiratory pathogens among adult SARI patients before, during, and after the pandemic. A retrospective multi-center observational study was performed across seven sentinel hospitals in Shanghai, China (2019–2023). Hospitalized SARI patients aged ≥ 15 years were tested for 12 respiratory pathogens (22 subtypes) using real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR). We stratified the study period into pre-pandemic (2019), pandemic (2020–2022), and post-pandemic (2023) phases for comparative analysis. In total, 4738 participants were enrolled. The overall pathogen detection rate decreased from 30.26% in the pre-pandemic period (2019) to 16.50% during the pandemic (2020–2022), then rebounded to 20.07% in the post-pandemic period (2023) ( P <0.001). A novel classification of four distinct temporal trends was established: (1) decline-resurgence for influenza virus (IFV), Mycoplasma pneumoniae ( M. pneumoniae ), Bordetella pertussis ( B. pertussis ), and human bocavirus (HBoV); (2) sustained decline for human parainfluenza virus (HPIV), human coronavirus (HCoV), human adenovirus (HAdV), human rhinovirus/enterovirus (HRV/EV), and Legionella pneumophila ( L. pneumophila ); (3) transient peak for human metapneumovirus (HMPV); (4) continuous increase for respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). IFV, HRV/EV, and HCoV consistently ranked top five across all periods. pneumoniae was the dominant pathogen (33.65%) in patients aged 15–44 years. Seasonal peaks of IFV and RSV were substantially reshaped in the post-pandemic period. The co-infection rate was 1.14% (54/4738), with IFV- M. pneumoniae was the most combination (20.37%). Respiratory pathogen circulation patterns changed substantially from 2019 to 2023. Persistent multi-pathogen surveillance is required to guard against single-pathogen outbreaks and concurrent multi-pathogen epidemics.

Incidence, shedding, and transmission of common respiratory viruses: data from a prospective, longitudinal, household cohort study in the Gambia from March 2021-June 2022.
作者： Sheikh Jarju, Rhys Wenlock, Benjamin B Lindsey, Madikoi Danso, Ya Jankey Jagne, Alansana Darboe, Dawda Jobe, Yusupha Jallow, Mamlie Touray, Ebrima A. Ceesay
摘要中文翻译
查看研究七要素
问题与背景
呼吸道病毒是低收入和中等收入国家(LMICs)发病率的主要原因之一,但在社区环境中对其传播、风险因素和感染动态了解甚少。
设计与对象
我们在冈比亚的一个郊区地区开展了一项前瞻性家庭研究。纳入了至少有5名成员、包括至少一名成人和一名儿童的家庭,并进行了为期一年的随访。
核心方法
每周收集上呼吸道拭子样本,无论是否有症状,通过RT-PCR检测SARS-CoV-2、季节性冠状病毒、人类鼻病毒(HRV)、流感A/B、RSV A/B、副流感病毒1-4型、人偏肺病毒和腺病毒。我们估算了传播率、症状比例、病毒持续时间以及家庭次级感染率(SARs),并探讨了这些指标与病毒类型和人口统计学变量之间的关联。
主要结果
From 2 March 2021 to 13 June 2022, 349 participants from 52 households completed 14,421 scheduled and 129 unscheduled visits. Respiratory viruses were detected in 11.1% of visits (n = 1611), representing 1423 infection episodes. HRV was most common, infecting 86.5% of participants (2.4 infections per person-year). Children aged <5 and 5-17 years shed virus for longer than adults (p = 0.0005), with longer shedding durations seen for HRV and SARS-CoV-2. Overall, 88.4% of infections were asymptomatic, with the highest symptomatic proportions for SARS-CoV-2 (16.3%) and RSV (17.7%) (p = 0.03).
解释与创新
在家庭中,呼吸道病毒的传播主要由大量无症状感染驱动,不同病毒种类在传播、排毒和持续性方面存在关键差异。
科研与公卫意义
这些发现可以为社区环境中呼吸道病毒的防控策略和风险评估提供依据,特别是强调无症状感染在家庭传播中的作用。
局限与证据强度
本研究仅在冈比亚的一个郊区地区进行,限制了其普遍适用性。由于样本量大且数据收集全面,证据强度较强。

Authors: Sheikh Jarju, Rhys Wenlock, Benjamin B Lindsey, Madikoi Danso, Ya Jankey Jagne, Alansana Darboe, Dawda Jobe, Yusupha Jallow, Mamlie Touray, Ebrima A. Ceesay
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Respiratory viruses are a major cause of morbidity in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), yet little is known about their incidence, risk factors, and transmission dynamics in community settings. We aimed to compare the epidemiology of multiple respiratory viruses within households in the Gambia. METHODS: We conducted a prospective household cohort study in a peri-urban region of the Gambia. Households with ≥5 members, including at least one adult and one child, were enrolled and followed for one year. Upper respiratory tract swabs were collected weekly, regardless of symptoms, and tested by RT-PCR for SARS-CoV-2, seasonal coronaviruses, human rhinovirus (HRV), influenza A/B, RSV A/B, parainfluenza viruses 1-4, human metapneumovirus, and adenovirus. We estimated incidence, symptomatic fraction, shedding duration, and household secondary attack rates (SARs), and explored associations with virus type and demographic variables. FINDINGS: From 2 March 2021 to 13 June 2022, 349 participants from 52 households completed 14,421 scheduled and 129 unscheduled visits. Respiratory viruses were detected in 11.1% of visits (n = 1611), representing 1423 infection episodes. HRV was most common, infecting 86.5% of participants (2.4 infections per person-year). Children aged <5 and 5-17 years shed virus for longer than adults (p = 0.0005), with longer shedding durations seen for HRV and SARS-CoV-2. Overall, 88.4% of infections were asymptomatic, with the highest symptomatic proportions for SARS-CoV-2 (16.3%) and RSV (17.7%) (p = 0.03). We found evidence for reductions in incidence risk with increasing age for HRV, RSV, seasonal coronaviruses, and parainfluenza viruses, with no difference for SARS-CoV-2. Among 719 index infections, the mean household SARs for all viruses combined was 6.7%. Infection with prolonged shedding (p = 0.002), specific viral species (p < 0.0001) and a lower Ct value were associated with increased transmission. INTERPRETATION: A large burden of asymptomatic infections underlies the circulation of respiratory viruses within households, with key differences in incidence, shedding and transmission across viral species. Beyond Croup: A Phenotype-Based Perspective on Parainfluenza Virus Types in Children

Authors: Andrea Jaramillo-Cerezo, Andrea Parra-Buitrago, Rolando Ulloa‐Gutiérrez, Iván Felipe Gutiérrez-Tobar, Rolando Ulloa-Gutierrez, Rolando Ulloa-Gutiérrez, I. Gutierrez-Tobar
Original abstract (English)
Human parainfluenza viruses (PIVs) caused an estimated 18.8 million acute lower respiratory infection episodes, 725 000 hospitalizations, and 34 400 deaths among children under 5 in 2018, ranking second only to respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). Despite this burden, clinical attention has historically focused on croup, leading to the common perception that parainfluenza is a single disease entity. However, the 4 types differ substantially in taxonomy, tropism, clinical syndrome, and severity. PIV-1, the most common cause of croup, drives biennial epidemics in toddlers, whereas PIV-2 is less frequent but can cause severe disease, particularly in children with underlying conditions. PIV-3 predominates in young infants, causing bronchiolitis and pneumonia clinically like RSV infection, reflecting its early postnatal susceptibility, which may relate to earlier and more frequent viral exposure. PIV-4, historically underrecognized due to culture limitations, usually does not cause croup but is linked to lower respiratory tract disease, hypoxemia, and occasional neurologic complications. PIV infections are most severe in infants, immunocompromised children, and those in low-resource settings. Seasonal patterns vary by type and geography. Advances in multiplex molecular diagnostics now allow rapid type-specific detection, improving clinical interpretation and management. No vaccine or antiviral is licensed. Emerging therapeutics include sialidase-based antivirals with Food and Drug Administration Breakthrough designation; monoclonal antibodies capable of cross-neutralizing PIV-1, PIV-3, RSV, and human metapneumovirus; and messenger RNA vaccines showing early immunogenicity in children. Recognizing the distinct epidemiology, clinical features, and immune responses of each type is critical for informed pediatric care and the development of targeted preventive and therapeutic strategies.

Research and practice implications
The review suggests that advances in molecular diagnostics are critical for accurate identification of hMPV infections. It also emphasizes the need for further research into hMPV-specific therapeutics and vaccines, as well as improved surveillance and clinical management strategies.

RSV Activity and Interference Shape Human Metapneumovirus Timing in US Study - Bioengineer.org

News Brief
A study published on November 17, 2026, by Bioengineer.org, led by Ke Li and colleagues, found that the timing of human metapneumovirus (HMPV) outbreaks in the United States is shaped by viral interference from respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), rather than regional climate conditions. The research team analyzed laboratory-confirmed HMPV cases and age-specific emergency department visits across ten US regions, using age-stratified dynamic transmission modeling.
