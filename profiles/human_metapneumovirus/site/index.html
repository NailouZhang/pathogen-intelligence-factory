作者： Thaissa Mendes Ilis, Gislene Lichs, Luiz Henrique Ferraz Demarchi, Tatiana de Freitas Fiuza Santos, Nádia Bernardinis, Luciana Appolinario, Thauane Silva, Elisa Cavalcante, Alice Sampaio, Lais Ferreira

问题与背景
呼吸道合胞病毒(RSV)是儿童严重急性呼吸道感染(SARI)的主要原因。我们描述了巴西边境州南马托格罗索州 36 个月以下儿童中与 RSV 相关的 SARI,包括季节性、A/B 亚型和临床特征。
设计与对象
这项基于 监测 的回顾性描述性研究使用了 2022-2024 年通报的 RSV 阳性 SARI 病例的 SIVEP-Gripe 数据。Ct ≤ 27 的保存样本的方便子集(313 个分型;占病例的 13.9%)接受了双重 RT-qPCR 分型。分型样本是非随机子集,临床数据存在缺失值。
核心方法
使用准泊松回归评估季节性;使用卡方或 Fisher 精确检验和 Wilcoxon 检验比较临床特征。
主要结果
Among 9758 SARI cases, 2254 (23%) were RSV positive. Peak activity was highest in 2023 (Week 14). Most cases (75%) occurred in infants younger than 12 months. Among cases with information, 79.5% (1502/1889) received ventilatory support (8.4% invasive), 14.2% (294/2076) required ICU admission, and 37 died (case fatality 1.6%). From July 2023, with all samples tested for rhinovirus, adenovirus, and human metapneumovirus, 18.4% of cases had codetection, mostly rhinovirus. Among typed samples, RSV-B accounted for 31/37 in 2022 and 104/137 in 2024, and RSV-A accounted for 131/138 in 2023. Deaths among typed cases (6/170 RSV-A and 0/142 RSV-B) all occurred in 2023.
解释与创新
RSV 是巴西边境州 SARI 住院的常见原因,主要是婴儿。RSV-A 和 RSV-B 共同循环,具有相似的临床特征;无法评估亚型特异性 死亡率,因为分型病例中的所有死亡都发生在 2023 年。
科研与公卫意义
研究结果表明,巴西边境各州需要针对幼儿(尤其是婴儿)制定有针对性的 监测 和 RSV 干预策略。他们还强调了在临床管理中考虑与其他呼吸道病毒合并感染的重要性。
局限与证据强度

Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: RSV is a leading cause of severe acute respiratory infection (SARI) in children. We describe RSV-associated SARI in children up to 36 months, including seasonality, A/B subtyping, and clinical profile, in Mato Grosso do Sul, a Brazilian border state. METHODS: This retrospective, surveillance-based descriptive study used SIVEP-Gripe data on RSV-positive SARI cases notified in 2022-2024. Pathogens associated with childhood febrile illness in West Africa: a systematic review

Authors: Flavia Kaduni Bawa, Mamadu Baldeh, Jethro Adam Herberg, Rakhitha Katangodage, Effua Usuf, Samuel Duodu, Aubrey J. Cunnington, Martin Chamai, Gordon A. Awandare, Francis Dzabeng
Original abstract (English)
Background Up to 80% of children seeking medical attention in Africa present with acute febrile illness. Epidemiological data on the locally circulating pathogens causing fever are essential to inform clinical management and for public health interventions. This systematic review sought to understand the aetiologies of febrile illness among children in West Africa. Methods We included healthcare facility and community-based studies with cross-sectional, case-control, and retrospective cross-sectional design which used laboratory methods to detect pathogens causing fever in children aged 1 month to 15 years. A literature search was conducted in Medline and Embase via OVID, Scopus, ScienceDirect, Connecting Repositories (CoRE), Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ), African Journals Online (AJOL) and World Health Organisation (WHO) Africa Index Medicus, for studies published from 2013 to 2023. Data was extracted on pathogens detected from routine clinical investigations and study-specific laboratory testing. A risk of bias assessment was conducted using a consolidated CASP checklist developed from the case-control and cross-sectional study checklists. Results We included 84 studies from 11 West African countries. 42% of studies investigated bacterial pathogens in blood, 24% in respiratory samples and 19% in urine, stool, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and ear swabs combined, resulting in the detection of 25 different bacterial species. The most common of these were Salmonella enterica, Streptococcus pneumoniae, Staphylococcus aureus, Haemophilus influenzae, and Escherichia coli. Seventeen different viruses were detected. Dengue virus, chikungunya virus and West Nile virus were mostly detected from serological studies. Influenza viruses, rhinoviruses, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), parainfluenza viruses, human metapneumovirus, coronaviruses, adenoviruses, and enteroviruses were commonly isolated from respiratory samples. Plasmodium falciparum was the most abundant parasite of the 5 different parasites/fungal species detected. Only 8% of studies performed comprehensive investigations (concurrent testing across ≥ 3 pathogen classes in the same participant) for a wide range of pathogens. Conclusion This study found that diagnostic testing was predominantly blood-based, with limited investigation of gastrointestinal, zoonotic, and emerging pathogens. These findings reveal important gaps in the existing evidence base for febrile pathogen detection and highlight areas where future primary research with a broader diagnostic scope would provide more complete epidemiological data. This systematic review has been registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) under the registration number: CRD 42023466517. Generative AI-Driven Hybrid Optimized Deep Learning Model for Computational Intelligence Predicting HMPV Virus Infections in Healthcare

Authors: Senthil G. A, Srinidhi Sundaram, K. Revathi, R. Deepa, L. Prinslin, G.A. Senthil
Original abstract (English)
Human Metapneumovirus (HMPV) is a major etiology of acute respiratory illness, especially among high-risk groups like young children, the elderly, and immunocompromised patients. Despite its prevalence, HMPV diagnosis is not always accurate due to nonspecific clinical presentation and lack of routine testing, with many cases going undetected or being detected late. Accurate and timely prediction of HMPV infection is important for improving patient outcomes and optimizing healthcare interventions. This paper introduces an innovative AI-based hybrid deep learning framework that integrates Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), CNN-RNN architectures, and Quantum-Inspired Support Vector Machines (QSVM) for precise HMPV prediction. Our approach resolves fundamental problems in medical AI, such as data insufficiency, class imbalance, interpretability, and real-time processing, by introducing three innovations: (1) GAN-based synthetic data augmentation for realistic patient record generation without destroying clinical relationships, substantially boosting the diversity and robustness of training data; (2) a hybrid CNN-RNN model for spatiotemporal feature extraction from multimodal clinical data sources such as Electronic Health Records (EHRs), time-series vitals, and chest X-rays to enable end-to-end pattern recognition and sequence analysis; and (3) QSVM optimization for improved classification in high-dimensional spaces by exploiting quantum-inspired decision boundaries to realize superior separability and minimize misclassification rates. The suggested model has 96.2% accuracy, 96.1% F1-score, and 0.983 AUC-ROC, 5%–7% higher than traditional approaches and 32% fewer false negatives, hence minimizing the chance of undetected instances. To ensure model robustness and generalizability, rigorous cross-validation was conducted across various hospital networks and patient populations. Clinical three-hospital network validation attests to the strength and scalability of the model, with real-time inference times (<200ms) enabling smooth integration into EHR systems and rapid clinical decision support. Further, attention mechanisms integrated into the CNN-RNN model enhance interpretability by highlighting essential features that influence predictions, 59 thereby closing the loop between AI-derived insights and medical reasoning and increasing clinician confidence. The integration of generative augmentation, hybrid modeling, and quantum-inspired optimization makes our framework a state-of-the-art solution for predictive healthcare, significantly improving the early detection and treatment of HMPV infections.

The research uncovered a notable temporal transition in the age distribution of affected children, marked by a progressive shift from older children toward younger infants over the consecutive surveillance years. The study also characterized co-circulating viral lineages, identified distinct amino acid substitutions within the viral fusion protein in emerging strains, and detailed clinical outcomes across varying levels of hospital care, providing comprehensive epidemiological evidence on pediatric human metapneumovirus activity during the post-pandemic recovery era. Human metapneumovirus was initially identified in 2001 as a widespread respiratory pathogen belonging to the Pneumoviridae family, sharing genetic relationships with respiratory syncytial virus. Worldwide pooled prevalence estimates indicate that the virus accounts for approximately 5.3 percent of respiratory tract infections across all age brackets, reaching its highest documented prevalence of 6.7 percent among children younger than five years and falling to 2.5 percent among adults. The viral genome spans roughly 13,300 base pairs encoding eight genes, with the fusion protein serving as the primary neutralizing antibody target and both the fusion and attachment glycoprotein genes utilized for molecular classification. The pathogen divides into two major genotypes, designated A and B, comprising four circulating sublineages termed A1, A2, B1, and B2. Following extensive disruptions to typical viral seasonality during the COVID-19 pandemic, widespread post-pandemic resurgences were recorded internationally, yet comprehensive clinical and molecular data within Taiwanese healthcare settings remained sparse. To evaluate these dynamics, researchers implemented a hybrid retrospective and prospective cohort protocol collaborating between the National Taiwan University Children’s Hospital, an academic tertiary medical center situated in northern Taiwan, and the Show Chwan Healthcare System, encompassing two community hospitals located in Changhua County within central Taiwan. The investigation enrolled hospitalized children younger than eighteen years diagnosed with acute respiratory illness who underwent molecular testing with either the BioFire FilmArray Respiratory Panel 2.1 or the Pneumonia Panel. Period 1 spanned January 2023 through June 2024 as a retrospective electronic medical record review, while Period 2 operated prospectively from July 2024 through December 2025, gathering nasopharyngeal swabs and structured clinical records after obtaining written informed parental consent. Across the entire cohort, lower respiratory tract illness was classified by attending physicians into bronchitis, bronchiolitis, bronchopneumonia, or pneumonia, while dyspnea was documented by observable nasal flaring or intercostal and subcostal retractions. The public health motivation underlying this investigation stems from the substantial global burden imposed by acute respiratory infections, which represent leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide. While many viral respiratory episodes present with mild symptomatology, infections frequently progress to severe outcomes including hospital admissions, long-term disability, and premature mortality, particularly among clinically vulnerable demographics. Operating across all twelve NUTS-2 provincial territories in the Netherlands, the platform collects weekly self-reported symptom data alongside basic health profiles, healthcare-seeking practices, and social contact behaviors from volunteer participants. A subset of symptomatic participants undergoes laboratory multiplex polymerase chain reaction testing, yielding verified pathogen-specific detections rather than broad community incidence or raw positivity rates.
