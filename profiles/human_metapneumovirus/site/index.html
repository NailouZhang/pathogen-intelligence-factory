来自人类呼吸道合胞病毒的温度敏感性 突变 减弱了活 人偏肺病毒 疫苗 候选物

Live human metapneumovirus vaccine candidates attenuated by temperature sensitivity mutations from human respiratory syncytial virus
作者： Pramila Lamichhane, Lijuan Yang, Celia Santos, Hong-Su Park, Li Ou, Tongqing Zhou, Cyril Le Nouën, Ursula J. Buchholz
摘要中文翻译摘要 人偏肺病毒(HMPV,间质肺病毒属)和呼吸道合胞病毒(HRSV,正肺病毒属)是肺病毒科的儿科呼吸道病原体。我们之前开发了鼻内活 HRSV 疫苗 候选物,通过 HRSV 聚合酶 (L) ORF 中的遗传稳定温度敏感性/减毒 (ts/att) 突变 进行减毒,包括位点“1030s”(L 密码子变化 S1313(AGC-to-TCA)/Y1321K(AAA),以克隆号命名1030)和“Δ1313/I1314L”,包含 L 密码子 1313 的遗传稳定缺失。在儿科 疫苗 研究中,具有“1030s”或 Δ1313/I1314L 的 HRSV 候选者是减毒的、免疫原性的和遗传稳定的。鉴于HRSV和HMPV之间的相似性,我们在HMPV L ORF中引入了相应的修饰,产生密码子1238(Δ1238)缺失的HMPV,对应于HRSV Δ1313/I1314L,以及C1238S(TCA)/Y1246K(AAA)一起替换的HMPV(C1238S/Y1246K,对应于HRSV“ 1030s”),或单独(C1238S,Y1246K),作为实验对照。所有版本均在 32°C 的 Vero 细胞中复制良好。37°C 时,Δ1238、C1238S/Y1246K 和 Y1246K 的复制延迟,峰值滴度比野生型 HMPV 低约 80 倍、200 倍和 25 倍。正如预测的那样,C1238S 对照对温度不敏感(关闭温度 [ T SH ] > 40°C,与野生型 HMPV 相似),而 Y1246K 和 Δ1238 ( T SH = 39°C) 和 C1238S/Y1246K ( T SH = 38°C) 具有 ts 表型。因此,添加 C1238S (TCA) 取代物提高了 Y1246K 的温度敏感性。HMPV ts/att 位点在温度胁迫下遗传稳定。在鼻内接种的仓鼠中,C1238S/Y1246K 和 Δ1238 的复制受到限制,但具有免疫原性并能抵抗 HMPV 攻击。因此,将对应于HRSV ts/att位点的C1238S/Y1246K和Δ1238 突变的HMPV L ORF包含在内,提供了遗传稳定的减毒活HMPV,适合临床开发。重要性 人偏肺病毒 (HMPV) 是幼儿下呼吸道疾病的主要原因,并且没有获得许可的 疫苗。与针对相关人类呼吸道合胞病毒(HRSV)的免疫策略类似,用于鼻内免疫的减毒活HMPV 疫苗最适合幼儿。活鼻内 疫苗 在残留母体抗体存在的情况下感染并诱导免疫,而不会引发增强的呼吸道疾病。减毒 突变 的遗传稳定性代表了 疫苗 设计中的挑战。我们通过将温度敏感性诱导减毒突变引入HMPV聚合酶基因的相应位点,产生了活减毒HMPV 疫苗候选物,该减毒突变最初是为HRSV开发的,基因稳定,并在HRSV 疫苗研究中被证明不会出现逆转。由此产生的 HMPV 疫苗 候选病毒对温度敏感、减毒且在温度胁迫下遗传稳定。在仓鼠模型中,新的 HMPV 候选物具有免疫原性和保护性。这些减毒活 HMPV 候选药物将进入儿科 疫苗 研究。

问题与背景
人偏肺病毒 (HMPV) 是幼儿下呼吸道疾病的主要原因,目前尚无获得许可的 疫苗。鉴于 HMPV 和 HRSV 之间的相似性,该研究旨在使用先前用于 HRSV 的温度敏感型 突变 开发减毒活 HMPV 疫苗 候选药物。

该研究涉及通过将温度敏感的 突变 引入聚合酶 (L) ORF 来生成减毒活 HMPV 疫苗 候选物。这些候选药物在仓鼠模型中进行了复制和免疫原性测试,并与野生型 HMPV 和对照进行了比较。
核心方法
研究人员将温度敏感/衰减(ts/att)突变引入HMPV L ORF中,与之前在HRSV 疫苗开发中使用的那些相对应。测试了这些 突变 在不同温度下在 Vero 细胞中的复制情况以及在仓鼠中的免疫原性和保护功效。
主要结果

解释与创新
该研究表明,将 ts/att 突变 引入 HMPV L ORF 可以产生温度敏感、减毒且遗传稳定的 疫苗 候选物。这种方法反映了成功的 HRSV 疫苗 策略,并为进一步的临床开发奠定了基础。
科研与公卫意义
这些发现为开发适合儿科使用的减毒活 HMPV 疫苗 提供了一条有希望的途径,有可能改善 监测、诊断 以及幼儿 HMPV 感染的预防策略。
局限与证据强度
该研究仅限于仓鼠模型,可能无法完全代表人类 免疫应答。然而,证据是强有力的,因为 疫苗 候选药物在温度应激下具有遗传稳定性,并且在模型中显示出免疫原性和保护功效。

Live human metapneumovirus vaccine candidates attenuated by temperature sensitivity mutations from human respiratory syncytial virus

Authors: Pramila Lamichhane, Lijuan Yang, Celia Santos, Hong-Su Park, Li Ou, Tongqing Zhou, Cyril Le Nouën, Ursula J. Buchholz
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Human metapneumovirus (HMPV, genus Metapneumovirus ) and respiratory syncytial virus (HRSV, genus Orthopneumovirus ) are pediatric respiratory pathogens of the Pneumoviridae family. We previously developed live-intranasal HRSV vaccine candidates attenuated by genetically stabilized temperature sensitivity/attenuating ( ts/att ) mutations in the HRSV polymerase (L) ORF, including sites “ 1030s ” (L codon changes S1313 (AGC-to-TCA) /Y1321K (AAA) , named after clone number 1030), and “Δ1313/I1314L,” comprising a genetically stabilized deletion of L codon 1313. In pediatric vaccine studies, HRSV candidates with “ 1030s ” or Δ1313/I1314L were attenuated, immunogenic, and genetically stable. Given the similarities between HRSV and HMPV, we introduced corresponding modifications into the HMPV L ORF, resulting in HMPV with deletion of codon 1238 (Δ1238), corresponding to HRSV Δ1313/I1314L, and HMPV with C1238S (TCA) /Y1246K (AAA) substitutions together (C1238S/Y1246K, corresponding to HRSV “ 1030s ”), or individually (C1238S, Y1246K), serving as experimental controls. All versions replicated well in Vero cells at 32°C. At 37°C, replication of Δ1238, C1238S/Y1246K, and Y1246K was delayed, and peak titers were ~80-fold, 200-fold, and 25-fold lower than wild-type HMPV. As predicted, the C1238S control was not temperature-sensitive (shutoff temperature [ T SH ] > 40°C, similar to wild-type HMPV), while Y1246K and Δ1238 ( T SH = 39°C) and C1238S/Y1246K ( T SH = 38°C) had a ts phenotype. Thus, the addition of the C1238S (TCA) substitution increased the temperature sensitivity of Y1246K. The HMPV ts/att sites were genetically stable under temperature stress. In hamsters inoculated intranasally, C1238S/Y1246K and Δ1238 were restricted in replication, yet immunogenic and protective against HMPV challenge. Thus, inclusion in the HMPV L ORF of C1238S/Y1246K and Δ1238 mutations, corresponding to HRSV ts/att sites, provided genetically stable live-attenuated HMPV, suitable for clinical development. IMPORTANCE Human metapneumovirus (HMPV) is a leading cause of lower respiratory illness in young children, and there are no licensed vaccines. Similarly to immunization strategies against the related human respiratory syncytial virus (HRSV), live-attenuated HMPV vaccines for intranasal immunization would be most suitable for young children. Live-intranasal vaccines infect and induce immunity in the presence of residual maternal antibodies without priming for enhanced respiratory disease. Genetic stability of attenuating mutations represents a challenge in vaccine design. We generated live-attenuated HMPV vaccine candidates by introducing into corresponding sites of the HMPV polymerase gene temperature-sensitivity-inducing attenuating mutations that had been originally developed for HRSV, genetically stabilized, and proven to be free of reversions in HRSV vaccine studies. The resulting HMPV vaccine candidates are temperature-sensitive, attenuated, and genetically stable under temperature stress. In the hamster model, the new HMPV candidates were immunogenic and protective. These live-attenuated HMPV candidates will be advanced to pediatric vaccine studies.

The study demonstrates that introducing ts/att mutations into the HMPV L ORF can result in temperature-sensitive, attenuated, and genetically stable vaccine candidates. This approach mirrors successful HRSV vaccine strategies and provides a foundation for further clinical development.

These findings offer a promising avenue for developing live-attenuated HMPV vaccines suitable for pediatric use, potentially improving surveillance, diagnosis, and prevention strategies for HMPV infections in young children.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited to hamster models, which may not fully represent human immune responses. However, the evidence is strong, as the vaccine candidates were genetically stable under temperature stress and showed immunogenicity and protective efficacy in the model.

Computational protein engineering of a multivalent human metapneumovirus immunogen using the self-assembling RSV M2-1 tetrameric scaffold

Authors: Mohammad Mehdi Rezaei Mirghayed, Amirhossein Amini Soror, Arezoo Rezaei, Amir Hossein Mashaikhi Sardouei, Mohammadreza Hajipour, Mohammad Reza Rahbar, Ehsan Kazemi Moghaddam
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Developing highly immunogenic subunit vaccines against human metapneumovirus (HMPV) remains a persistent challenge because isolated epitopes are inherently unstable and effective multivalent presentation strategies are lacking. In this study, we present an advanced computational protein engineering framework that uses the stable, self-assembling tetrameric architecture of the Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV) M2-1 protein as a heterologous scaffold to display structurally preserved HMPV B- and T-cell epitopes. Given the overlapping clinical presentation of RSV and HMPV, this chimeric scaffolding approach lays the groundwork for dual-targeting respiratory immunogens. By mapping the structural interfaces of the M2-1 tetramer, we rationally grafted experimentally validated HMPV fusion (F) protein epitopes into highly accessible loop regions, rigorously ensuring that the core oligomerization domain remained thermodynamically unperturbed. This structure-guided display achieved a fourfold increase in epitope-presentation density without compromising quaternary assembly. Thermodynamic stability assessments and molecular docking confirmed that the grafted epitopes retained their native conformations and achieved picomolar binding affinities (Kd = 2.3×10−12 M) with HMPV-specific neutralizing antibodies (SAN27-14). Furthermore, extensive 100 ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulations and normal mode analysis (NMA) elucidated the conformational dynamics of the multimeric complex, confirming interfacial stability and identifying specific flexible hinge regions required for optimal engagement of the pattern recognition receptor (TLR4). By shifting from sequence-based prediction to rigorous structural thermodynamics, this work provides a physically viable, highly optimized tetrameric scaffold for next-generation pneumovirus vaccine development.

Original abstract (English)

Post-Acute Sequelae of Non-COVID-19 Respiratory Viral Infections: A Systematic Review.

Authors: Esther Hui-En Chong, Ian Jun Yan Wee, Jenny Guek Hong Low, Paul Anantharajah Tambyah, Dorothy Hui Lin Ng
Original abstract (English)
Respiratory viral infections (RVIs) affect millions annually, yet long-term consequences outside COVID-19 are poorly defined. Post-acute sequelae, encompassing persistent or new physical, cognitive, and mental health impairments, are increasingly recognised as clinically important. We systematically reviewed evidence on post-acute outcomes in adults following non-COVID RVIs to characterise pathogen-specific patterns and highlight areas for future research. Major databases were searched from January 2004 to August 2025 for observational studies reporting symptoms, functional impairment, or new diagnoses ≥ 4 weeks after RVIs. Study characteristics, pathogens, follow-up duration, and organ-specific outcomes were extracted. Risk of bias was evaluated using ROBINS-I, and certainty of evidence was determined with the GRADE approach. Narrative synthesis was performed owing to heterogeneity. Thirteen studies (n = 631,863) met eligibility criteria. Influenza was the most studied pathogen and consistently associated with long-term pulmonary dysfunction, reduced diffusion capacity, dyspnoea, fatigue, and poorer quality of life. Large cohorts corroborated substantial pulmonary morbidity and less frequent cardiovascular and neuropsychiatric burdens. Two RSV cohorts (n = 1553 and n = 471) showed a predominance of cardiovascular sequelae; respiratory and neurological outcomes were less frequent. A single HMPV cohort (n = 92) reported persistent symptoms in 55% of adults at 90-270 days, most commonly dyspnoea, fatigue, and cough. Overall, the available evidence suggests that non-COVID RVIs are associated with post-acute multi-organ sequelae, particularly following severe infection. Nevertheless, evidence remains fragmented due to inconsistent study designs, limited pathogen coverage, and low certainty. Standardised, prospective, pathogen-specific studies are required to refine these preliminary signals and inform clinical follow-up and prevention strategies.

Considered the most lucrative Australian university research commercialisation agreement ever negotiated, the 2025 deal for exclusive rights to the University of Queensland’s molecular clamp technology has huge potential for vaccine development. Pharmaceutical giant Sanofi bought the university’s spin-off company Vicebio, with the rights to the clamp technology, for $US1.15 billion ($1.6 billion) up front and potential ongoing payments of $US450 million. Professor Keith Chappell, Professor Daniel Watterson and Emeritus Professor Paul Young, inventors of the molecular clamp vaccine technology, developed by Vicebio, at University of Queensland. The molecular clamp prevents the spike proteins on the surface of a virus – memorable from COVID-19 days – from shape-shifting, allowing the human body to build a strong immune response, says University of Queensland molecular virologist Professor Keith Chappell. “It’s a paperclip that keeps the vaccine in the right shape,” he says, adding his team doesn’t use the whole virus in the production of vaccines. “We just produce the small spike proteins by themselves – the small surface part, the most relevant part of the virus.” Winner of the Research commercialisation category of the Financial Review Higher Education Awards, the UQ-Sanofi molecular clamp deal began with an idea that emerged in 2011, following Chappell’s post-doc research in Madrid. Advertisement On his return to Australia, he and his University of Queensland colleagues worked on the idea and went on to win grants from the National Health and Medical Research Council and elsewhere, and finally funding from a venture capital group for further development. The virus clamp technology research was spun out into the company Vicebio, with most of the pre-clinical research based at the University of Queensland. Easier to administer The Sanofi agreement provides funding for ongoing research by Chappell’s team, to expand beyond the lead vaccine program targeting respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and human metapneumovirus (HMPV). RSV alone kills 100,000 children under five every year in developing countries, Chappell says. Although RSV vaccines already exist, the UQ researchers are working on a stable version in a liquid-filled syringe, making it easier and faster to administer. This new vaccine will target multiple respiratory diseases along with RSV, including HMPV and parainfluenza. Altogether, the range of targeted viruses puts more older adults in hospital than flu does. Chappell and his team are now working on Sanofi’s priority diseases: those with the largest potential financial returns and the biggest global impact.
