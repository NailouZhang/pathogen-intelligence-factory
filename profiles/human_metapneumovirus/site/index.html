人偏肺病毒A融合基因的分子进化揭示了谱系特异性多样化和结构保守性

Molecular Evolution of the HMPV-A Fusion Gene Reveals Lineage-Specific Diversification and Structural Conservation
作者： Kazuhiko Harada, Fuminori Mizukoshi, Tatsuya Shirai, Ryusuke Kimura, Koo Nagasawa, Kazuto Ito, Isao Kurosawa, Kazuya Shirato, Akihide Ryo, Hirokazu Kimura
摘要中文翻译人偏肺病毒A(HMPV-A)基因型包含遗传多样性谱系,但F基因的多样化是否伴随保守融合(F)蛋白的变化仍不清楚。我们利用时间尺度系统发育分析、贝叶斯天空线分析、进化速率和系统发育距离估计、基于密码子的选择分析、序列相似性分析以及结构表位建模,分析了从1982年至2025年间在25个国家收集的500个PARNAS选择的HMPV-A F基因序列。识别出三个系统发育上不同的簇。簇1在簇2和簇3的共同祖先分化于约1927年。簇3包含435个代表性序列,显示出最高的估计进化速率、最大的系统发育多样性以及最显著的近期种群动态变化。整体进化速率为9.20 × 10−4个替换/位/年。尽管在核苷酸层面存在多样化,所有簇的整体蛋白相似性均超过97%。299个密码子(约55%)始终处于负选择下,而正选择信号有限且方法特异性,其中MEME识别出密码子188和271,FUBAR识别出密码子528。主要中和抗体结合区域在各簇中基本保持保守,六个建模代表显示出相似的全球F蛋白骨架结构和预测的表位分布,尽管A61S和K143T突变发生在中和抗体结合位点,需要实验验证。这些发现表明HMPV-A F基因在强功能约束下表现出谱系特异性多样化,支持HMPV F蛋白作为广泛保守的干预靶点。

科研与公卫意义
鉴于其在各谱系中的结构保守性和功能重要性,研究结果支持将HMPV F蛋白作为干预措施(如疫苗或抗病毒药物疗法)的靶点。

本研究的局限性在于依赖序列数据而没有对所识别的表位进行实验验证,且由于缺乏直接的功能实验,证据强度中等。

Molecular Evolution of the HMPV-A Fusion Gene Reveals Lineage-Specific Diversification and Structural Conservation

Authors: Kazuhiko Harada, Fuminori Mizukoshi, Tatsuya Shirai, Ryusuke Kimura, Koo Nagasawa, Kazuto Ito, Isao Kurosawa, Kazuya Shirato, Akihide Ryo, Hirokazu Kimura
Original abstract (English)
Human metapneumovirus genotype A (HMPV-A) comprises genetically diverse lineages, but whether F-gene diversification is accompanied by changes in the conserved fusion (F) protein remains unclear. We analyzed 500 PARNAS-selected HMPV-A F gene sequences from 25 countries collected between 1982 and 2025 using time-scaled phylogenetics, Bayesian skyline analysis, evolutionary-rate and patristic-distance estimation, codon-based selection analysis, sequence-identity profiling, and structural epitope modeling. Three phylogenetically distinct clusters were identified. Cluster 1 diverged from the common ancestor of Clusters 2 and 3 around 1927. Cluster 3 comprised 435 representative sequences and showed the highest estimated evolutionary rate, greatest phylogenetic diversity, and the most pronounced recent demographic fluctuations. The overall evolutionary rate was 9.20 × 10−4 substitutions/site/year. Despite nucleotide-level diversification, whole-protein identity exceeded 97% in all clusters, 299 codons (approximately 55%) were consistently under negative selection, whereas positive-selection signals were limited and method-specific, with MEME identifying codons 188 and 271 and FUBAR identifying codon 528. Major neutralizing antibody-binding regions were largely conserved, and the six modeled representatives showed similar global F-protein backbone structures and predicted epitope-surface distributions, although A61S and K143T occurred within neutralizing antibody-binding sites and require experimental evaluation. These findings demonstrate lineage-specific F gene diversification under strong functional constraint and support the HMPV F protein as a broadly conserved intervention target.

The study analyzed 500 HMPV-A F gene sequences from 25 countries collected between 1982 and 2025, focusing on lineage-specific diversification patterns and structural conservation across clusters.
Core methods
The researchers employed time-scaled phylogenetics, Bayesian skyline analysis, evolutionary-rate and patristic-distance estimation, codon-based selection analysis, sequence-identity profiling, and structural epitope modeling to assess genetic variation and functional constraints.

Whole-protein identity exceeded 97% across all clusters, and 299 codons (approximately 55%) were under consistent negative selection.
Interpretation and novelty
The findings reveal lineage-specific diversification of the HMPV-A F gene under strong functional constraints, highlighting the conservation of the F protein structure and its potential as a broadly conserved intervention target.
Scientific and public-health significance
The results support the use of the HMPV F protein as a target for interventions such as vaccines or antiviral therapies, given its structural conservation and functional importance across lineages.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its reliance on sequence data without experimental validation of the identified epitopes, and the evidence strength is moderate due to the absence of direct functional assays.

Clinical Factors Associated with Documented Radiographic Pneumonia in Adults with Human Metapneumovirus Infection: A Hospital-Wide Cohort Study in Japan

Authors: Takeshi Saraya, Junki Tashiro, Hajime Furuno, Yoshitaka Sakamoto, Kei Nakajima, Sho Yamada, Takatora Akizawa, Narishige Ishikawa, Hiromi Ishida, Tatsuya Shirai
Original abstract (English)
Background/Objectives: Human metapneumovirus (hMPV) is increasingly recognized as an important cause of adult respiratory infection, but data on pneumonia remain limited. We evaluated clinical characteristics, temporal detection patterns, and clinical factors associated with radiographic pneumonia among adults with laboratory-confirmed hMPV infection. Methods: We conducted a single-center retrospective observational study at Kyorin University Hospital, Tokyo, Japan, from December 2020 to February 2026. Adults aged ≥18 years with hMPV detected by multiplex polymerase chain reaction were included. The primary outcome, documented radiographic pneumonia, was defined by newly identified pneumonia-compatible parenchymal abnormalities on chest radiography and/or chest computed tomography (CT). Logistic regression was used to identify clinical factors associated with this outcome. Results: Among 590 hMPV-positive patients, 192 adults were included; 121 were managed as outpatients and 71 were hospitalized. Pneumonia was identified in 68 adults, including 43 of 71 hospitalized patients. Adult hMPV detection shifted from summer predominance in 2022–2023 toward increased spring and winter detections in 2024–2025 after the onset of the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic. By 2025, pneumonia was identified in 24.0% of outpatients and 65.5% of hospitalized patients. In the primary multivariable model, higher C-reactive protein (CRP) concentrations (adjusted odds ratio per 1 mg/dL, 1.12; 95% confidence interval (CI), 1.03–1.22; p = 0.008) and abnormal auscultation (adjusted odds ratio, 3.45; 95% CI, 1.21–9.83; p = 0.021) were independently associated with pneumonia. Conclusions: Adult hMPV infection was frequently associated with radiographic pneumonia, particularly among hospitalized patients. Elevated C-reactive protein concentrations and abnormal auscultation may help identify adults with hMPV infection who are more likely to have pneumonia.

The review compares clinical outcomes of HMPV lower respiratory tract infections (LRTI) with those of influenza and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) LRTI, focusing on mortality, ICU admission, mechanical ventilation, and length of hospital stay.
Evidence base and method
A systematic review was conducted following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, with dual independent review for study selection, data extraction, and quality assessment using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale. Four databases were searched from inception to October 2025 for observational studies comparing clinical outcomes of HMPV LRTI with those of RSV and influenza pneumonia in adults undergoing chemotherapy for hematological malignancies.
Consensus and conclusions
The review found that all-cause mortality for HMPV (1.4%) was not higher than for influenza (2.2–33.3%) or RSV (0–25%). ICU admission rates were highly variable and overlapping across all viruses. A single study reported a substantially prolonged median length of hospital stay for HMPV (9 days) compared to RSV (18 days) and influenza (10–13 days).
Controversies and gaps
Data for mechanical ventilation were particularly scarce, and notably absent for HMPV and influenza. The findings suggest that HMPV infection may have a distinct clinical profile, but the evidence is limited and requires further validation. The review highlights the need for prospective studies to better understand the morbidity burden of HMPV in this population.
Research and practice implications
Prospective studies are urgently needed to guide optimized management and resource allocation for HMPV LRTI in adults with hematological malignancies. The review emphasizes the importance of further research to clarify the clinical significance of HMPV infections and to improve surveillance and diagnostic strategies.

Discussion: HMPV continues to pose a substantial public health burden due to its seasonal outbreaks, clinical similarity to other respiratory viruses, and absence of specific therapies. Although supportive care is currently the mainstay, emerging antivirals and vaccines may offer future solutions. Improved diagnostic access, public awareness, and targeted research are essential for controlling HMPV infections. Conclusion: Human Metapneumovirus continues to pose a public health challenge due to the lack of targeted treatments and vaccines. Ongoing research into antiviral agents, monoclonal antibodies, and vaccines is critical for effective disease management. Researchers at the Henan Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention and their collaborators analyzed 36,143 respiratory specimens collected from patients with influenza-like illness or severe acute respiratory infection during 2024 and 2025, screening each sample with multiplex PCR panels covering HMPV and 14 other respiratory pathogens. The results, published in BMC Infectious Diseases, offer one of the most detailed post-pandemic portraits of HMPV epidemiology to date and reveal striking patterns in age distribution, bacterial co-detection, and the temporal choreography between competing respiratory viruses. The headline finding is a sharp inter-year contrast. Overall HMPV positivity across the two-year surveillance period stood at 3.96 percent, with 1,432 of 36,143 specimens testing positive, but that average conceals two very different years. In 2024, 7.1 percent of tested patients carried the virus; in 2025 the figure fell to just 1.7 percent, a more than fourfold decline. The 2024 epidemic peaked with remarkable intensity: during week 48 of that year, 26.2 percent of all tested respiratory specimens were positive for HMPV, a level few endemic respiratory viruses reach even at the height of a seasonal wave. The authors emphasize that the marked resurgence warrants continued monitoring to determine whether such patterns represent a short-term anomaly or a sustained shift in how HMPV circulates in the region. Age played a decisive role in who was infected. Children aged 3 to 5 years recorded the highest positivity of any group, at 8.6 percent, corresponding to an enrichment ratio of 2.18 relative to the overall population under surveillance. The concentration became even more extreme during the peak transmission window: supplementary data show that during weeks 48 to 50 of 2024, HMPV positivity among children aged 3 to 5 climbed to 40.7 percent, meaning that roughly two in five young children tested during that brief period carried detectable virus. Preschool children, it appears, were the engine of the 2024 outbreak, a pattern consistent with HMPV’s biology as a virus that spreads efficiently in settings where young children mix closely and have limited accumulated immunity. Co-infections were detected in 56.2 percent of HMPV-positive cases, and the predominant partners were bacteria rather than other viruses: Streptococcus pneumoniae and Haemophilus influenzae were the most frequently co-detected organisms. Both are common colonizers of the human nasopharynx that can turn pathogenic when viral infection disrupts mucosal defenses, and their frequent co-detection with HMPV raises questions about the contribution of bacterial carriage to disease severity in pediatric respiratory illness. The study’s statistical framework went beyond simple co-detection rates, quantifying pathogen associations through odds ratios that distinguish genuine ecological interaction from chance overlap in a busy respiratory season. Those odds ratios paint a picture of competitive and facilitative relationships within the respiratory microbiome. HMPV showed a positive association with Streptococcus pneumoniae, with an odds ratio of 2.01, and a weaker positive association with Haemophilus influenzae, at 1.47. Human bocavirus, a virus whose clinical significance remains debated, also tracked positively with HMPV at an odds ratio of 1.66. In contrast, the relationships with the two major winter respiratory viruses were strongly negative: HMPV was negatively associated with influenza virus (odds ratio 0.20) and with respiratory syncytial virus (odds ratio 0.17). Such inverse associations suggest that these viruses do not simply peak independently; each appears to suppress or be suppressed by the others, whether through interference at the host level, depletion of susceptible individuals, or behavioral and environmental factors that favor one agent at a time. Using cross-correlation and regression techniques on weekly surveillance data, the researchers found that respiratory syncytial virus positivity at a one-week lag had a positive effect on subsequent HMPV positivity, with a regression coefficient of 0.15 that reached statistical significance at P equals 0.048. In practical terms, an RSV wave in a given week slightly preceded and predicted a rise in HMPV detections the following week. This lead-lag relationship is intriguing because it coexists with the overall negative association between the two viruses across the full dataset, hinting at a nuanced interaction in which one virus may briefly facilitate conditions for another before competition or immunity-driven exclusion takes over across the longer arc of the season. Methodologically, the study illustrates the value of dense, multiplexed sentinel surveillance. By testing every specimen against 15 pathogens simultaneously, the team could compute enrichment ratios identifying which age groups disproportionately contributed to transmission, pairwise odds ratios revealing pathogen associations, and time-series models capturing the ordering of epidemic waves. The distinction between influenza-like illness and severe acute respiratory infection cases in the sentinel framework also matters, because viruses and co-pathogens may distribute differently across the spectrum of severity. Findings of this kind are difficult to obtain from clinical testing alone, which is typically biased toward severely ill patients and ordered on an ad hoc basis rather than systematically. The public health implications are twofold. First, the high rate of bacterial co-detection argues for integrated surveillance that monitors respiratory bacteria alongside viruses, since pneumococcus and Haemophilus influenzae are both vaccine-preventable in their major serotypes, and their co-circulation with HMPV may shape the clinical burden of an HMPV season. Second, the temporal lead-lag relationship with RSV underscores the authors’ call for integrated pathogen surveillance: signals from one virus’s epidemic curve may serve as early indicators for another, improving the timeliness of clinical preparedness in pediatrics. The strong concentration of cases among children aged 3 to 5 further supports age-targeted prevention strategies, whether through caregiver hygiene measures, cohorting in preschool settings, or, eventually, vaccines and monoclonal approaches now in development for HMPV, which currently has no licensed specific countermeasure. Open questions remain. Henan, a province of nearly 100 million people in central China, provides a robust but geographically specific vantage point, and whether the 2024 surge reflects an immunity debt accrued during pandemic-era contact reductions, viral evolutionary change, or ordinary multi-year epidemic cycling cannot be resolved from two seasons of data. The steep drop in 2025, to 1.7 percent positivity, could indicate that the susceptible pool was largely replenished and depleted within a single intense season, resetting the epidemic clock. The authors themselves frame the 2024 resurgence as a phenomenon requiring continued monitoring, and further seasons of sentinel data will be needed to determine whether HMPV in the region settles into a predictable alternation with RSV and influenza or follows a new post-pandemic rhythm. For now, the study stands as a detailed empirical record of an HMPV outbreak at its peak, capturing a virus that can, in a matter of weeks, infect more than a quarter of young children presenting with acute respiratory illness before receding almost entirely from view. News Brief
A new seroepidemiological study conducted in Taizhou city, Zhejiang Province, China, reveals that toddlers aged 16 to 36 months show the weakest immune defenses against human metapneumovirus (hMPV). Published in BMC Infectious Diseases, the research analyzed serum samples collected between June 2 and June 30, 2024, from 1,303 participants aged 16 months to 93 years, with a focus on children under five. The findings indicate that only 67.8% of toddlers in this age group were seropositive, with a geometric mean titer of 217. This suggests that one in three toddlers in their second and third years of life may lack detectable neutralizing immunity against hMPV, which commonly causes bronchiolitis and pneumonia in early childhood. The study also notes that immunity increases rapidly with age, reaching near 100% seroprevalence by school age. The researchers emphasize that the observed correlation between hMPV and RSV immunity does not prove causation and may reflect shared exposures or immune response variability. The results highlight the need for targeted vaccine and prevention strategies for hMPV, especially in young children, as the pandemic disrupted natural exposure patterns.

The findings, published in BMC Infectious Diseases, reveal a striking vulnerability window in toddlers and suggest that population-level immunity against the virus follows a distinctive age-related trajectory with important implications for future vaccine and prevention strategies. The research team, led by Chang Liu and Qiang Wang of the Shanghai Institute of Infectious Disease and Biosecurity at Fudan University, together with David Hodgson of Charité Universitätsmedizin Berlin and collaborators at the Taizhou City Center for Disease Control and Prevention and the University of Hong Kong, analyzed serum samples collected between June 2 and June 30, 2024. The samples came from a community-based cross-sectional survey that used a multistage, age-stratified sampling design, with deliberate oversampling of children under five years of age to ensure that the youngest and most epidemiologically critical age groups were adequately represented. In total, 1,303 participants aged between 16 months and 93 years were included in the analysis. These were quantified using a fluorescence-based microneutralization assay, a laboratory technique in which serum is serially diluted and mixed with live virus before being applied to cell cultures; the presence of neutralizing activity is then detected through fluorescence-based readouts. A participant was classified as seropositive if their serum neutralized the virus at a dilution of 1:30 or greater. This approach provides a more functionally meaningful measure of protective immunity than binding-antibody tests alone, because neutralization titers correlate more closely with resistance to infection. Study Biology The picture changes rapidly with age. Among children aged three to four years, the geometric mean titer rose to 331, and by ages five to eleven it exceeded 1,000, a level that was then maintained throughout adulthood. The oldest participants showed the strongest responses of all: adults aged 65 years and older had a geometric mean titer of 1,829, with a confidence interval of 1,620 to 2,064. From the age of six onward, seroprevalence was close to or had reached 100 percent, indicating that nearly everyone in the population had accumulated neutralizing antibodies by school age, most likely through repeated natural infections during early childhood. Within the under-five group, the age gradient was particularly steep. The researchers calculated that for each additional year of age, neutralizing antibody titers increased by 74.7 percent, with a 95 percent confidence interval ranging from 14.4 percent to 167.0 percent. This rapid accumulation of immunity in the first years of life is consistent with the known epidemiology of human metapneumovirus, which circulates seasonally and infects a large proportion of children before their fifth birthday. The low titers observed in the 16-to-36-month window likely reflect the combination of waning maternal antibodies, limited cumulative exposure, and the immature antibody responses characteristic of early childhood. The timing of the study adds a layer of epidemiological significance. The COVID-19 pandemic and the non-pharmaceutical interventions that accompanied it, including masking, school closures, and physical distancing, dramatically suppressed transmission of many respiratory pathogens worldwide, and human metapneumovirus was no exception. When these measures were relaxed, several respiratory viruses returned with unusual intensity and atypical seasonality, raising concerns about whether cohorts of children who grew up during the interruption had missed their usual early exposures and were now entering a period of heightened susceptibility. The Taizhou data suggest that by mid-2024, transmission had resumed sufficiently to rebuild immunity in older children, but that toddlers remained a partially unprotected reservoir of susceptibility.
