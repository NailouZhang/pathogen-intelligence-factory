在共享病童护理环境中呼吸道合胞病毒和 人偏肺病毒 之间没有交叉感染:一项前瞻性观察研究

No Cross-Infection between Respiratory Syncytial Virus and Human Metapneumovirus in a Shared Sick-Child Care Setting: A Prospective Observational Study
作者： Eitaro Suzuki, Yasuo Suzuki, Kiyoshi Ichihara
摘要中文翻译摘要 背景 2025 年 1 月至 4 月,日本山口县宇部市同时发生了呼吸道合胞病毒 (RSV) 和 人偏肺病毒 (hMPV) 的 暴发 病毒。这两种病毒均属于肺病毒科,会引起临床上相似的儿童呼吸道疾病。此前尚未研究过 RSV 感染者和 hMPV 感染者共用一间护理室是否会导致交叉感染。方法将在铃木儿科诊所快速抗原检测检测出 RSV 或 hMPV 阳性的 11 个月至 11 岁儿童纳入该诊所的病童护理机构。RSV 阳性和 hMPV 阳性儿童在同一房间接受护理。出院后1至4个月前瞻性监测交叉感染。结果 共有 54 名儿童参与(RSV:22,hMPV:32;男性:31,女性:23;中位年龄:3 岁,范围:11 个月至 11 岁)。2025 年 1 月至 4 月期间,共进行了 23 次共享护理,每次护理 2 至 7 名儿童,房间面积为 10.4 至 22.4 平方米。随访1~3个月未发现交叉感染。一名 RSV 感染婴儿(11 个月大)随后在出院 21 天后出现 hMPV 感染;然而,考虑到这一间隔超过了已知的 hMPV 潜伏期(3-6 天),该病例被认为是社区获得性感染。结论 在 1 至 3 个月的随访期内,RSV 和 hMPV 感染儿童的共同病童护理并未导致交叉感染。病毒干扰可能是由先天 免疫应答 介导的,包括干扰素诱导,可以解释这一发现。在共流行期间,对感染 RSV 和 hMPV 的儿童进行共用房间护理可能是患病儿童护理机构的一种安全实用的选择。

问题与背景
该研究旨在调查呼吸道合胞病毒 (RSV) 和 人偏肺病毒 (hMPV) 在共享病童护理环境中是否发生交叉感染,从而解决了解这些病毒在临床环境中 传播 动态的空白。

解释与创新
作者将这些发现解释为证据,表明 RSV 和 hMPV 感染儿童共同照顾患病儿童不会导致交叉感染,这表明病毒干扰(可能通过先天的 免疫应答)可能解释了 传播 的缺乏。
科研与公卫意义
研究结果表明,在同时流行期间,RSV 和 hMPV 感染儿童的共同患病儿童护理可能是一种安全且实用的选择,可能为儿科护理机构中管理 暴发 病毒的公共卫生策略提供信息。
局限与证据强度
该研究受到其观察设计和潜在混杂因素的限制,但考虑到在随访期间对交叉感染的直接监测以及从分析中排除社区获得性病例,证据是强有力的。

No Cross-Infection between Respiratory Syncytial Virus and Human Metapneumovirus in a Shared Sick-Child Care Setting: A Prospective Observational Study

Authors: Eitaro Suzuki, Yasuo Suzuki, Kiyoshi Ichihara
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background A simultaneous outbreak of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and human metapneumovirus (hMPV) occurred in Ube, Yamaguchi Prefecture, Japan, from January to April 2025. Both viruses belong to the family Pneumoviridae and cause clinically similar respiratory illnesses in children. Whether sharing a care room between RSV-infected and hMPV-infected children leads to cross-infection has not been previously investigated. Methods Children aged 11 months to 11 years who tested positive for RSV or hMPV by rapid antigen tests at Suzuki Pediatric Clinic were enrolled in the sick-child care facility of the same clinic. RSV-positive and hMPV-positive children were cared for in the same room. Cross-infection was prospectively monitored for 1 to 4 months after discharge. Results A total of 54 children participated (RSV: 22, hMPV: 32; male: 31, female: 23; median age: 3 years, range: 11 months to 11 years). Shared care was conducted over 23 sessions from January to April 2025, with 2 to 7 children per session in rooms measuring 10.4 to 22.4 m2. No cross-infection was observed during the 1- to 3-month follow-up period. One RSV-infected infant (aged 11 months) subsequently developed hMPV infection 21 days after discharge; however, given that this interval exceeds the known incubation period of hMPV (3–6 days), this case was considered a community-acquired infection. Conclusion Shared sick-child care of RSV- and hMPV-infected children did not result in cross-infection over a follow-up period of 1 to 3 months. Viral interference, possibly mediated by the innate immune response including interferon induction, may explain this finding. Shared-room care of children with RSV and hMPV may be a safe and practical option for sick-child care facilities during co-epidemic periods.

Question and background
The study aimed to investigate whether cross-infection occurs between respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and human metapneumovirus (hMPV) in a shared sick-child care setting, addressing a gap in understanding the transmission dynamics of these viruses in clinical environments.
Design and population
A prospective observational study was conducted at Suzuki Pediatric Clinic in Ube, Yamaguchi Prefecture, Japan, from January to April 2025. Children aged 11 months to 11 years who tested positive for RSV or hMPV were enrolled in the shared sick-child care facility.
Core methods
RSV-positive and hMPV-positive children were placed in the same care room, and cross-infection was monitored prospectively for 1 to 4 months after discharge using clinical observations and follow-up testing.
Main results
A total of 54 children participated (RSV: 22, hMPV: 32). No cross-infection was observed during the 1- to 3-month follow-up period. One RSV-infected infant developed hMPV infection 21 days after discharge, but this was considered a community-acquired infection due to the interval exceeding the known incubation period of hMPV.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted the findings as evidence that shared sick-child care for RSV- and hMPV-infected children does not lead to cross-infection, suggesting that viral interference, possibly through the innate immune response, may explain the lack of transmission.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings suggest that shared sick-child care for RSV- and hMPV-infected children may be a safe and practical option during co-epidemic periods, potentially informing public health strategies for managing viral outbreaks in pediatric care settings.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study was limited by its observational design and potential for confounding factors, but the evidence is strong given the direct monitoring of cross-infection over a follow-up period and the exclusion of community-acquired cases from the analysis.

Clinical characteristics and factors associated with severe disease in hospitalized infants with human metapneumovirus pneumonia and respiratory bacterial co-detection: a single-center retrospective study

问题与背景
人偏肺病毒 (hMPV) 是婴儿 下呼吸道感染 的重要病因,但呼吸道细菌联合检测婴儿的数据有限。我们的目的是描述该人群的临床和微生物学特征,并探索与严重疾病相关的因素。
设计与对象
我们对 2024 年 10 月至 2025 年 3 月期间在河南省儿童医院住院的 12 个月及以下婴儿进行了一项单中心回顾性研究,这些婴儿患有 hMPV 肺炎并进行了临床裁定的呼吸道细菌联合检测。

从医疗记录中提取人口统计、临床、实验室、影像、治疗 和 6 个月的随访数据。组比较使用卡方或费舍尔精确检验和曼-惠特尼 U 检验。进行探索性逻辑回归;主要模型排除了嗜睡,因为它与严重程度标准重叠,次要警告信号模型包括嗜睡。
主要结果
Among 309 infants, 190 (61.5%) were male and 42 (13.6%) were classified as severe cases. In the primary exploratory model, artificial feeding (OR 2.60, 95% CI 1.27–5.34), underlying diseases (OR 2.43, 95% CI 1.14–5.21), and lower albumin levels (OR 0.85 per 1 g/L increase, 95% CI 0.78–0.94) were associated with severe disease.
解释与创新
Severe disease was classified according to the pediatric community-acquired pneumonia guideline and the discharge diagnosis. In the secondary model, lethargy was strongly associated with severe presentation (OR 5.02, 95% CI 2.38–10.62) and should be interpreted as a warning sign rather than an independent causal factor.
科研与公卫意义
基础疾病、人工喂养和较低的白蛋白水平可能有助于识别患有 hMPV 肺炎和呼吸道细菌共检测的婴儿,这些婴儿需要更密切的监测。嗜睡应被视为早期预警信号。
局限与证据强度
这些基于关联的发现需要通过标准化病原体定量和判定进行多中心前瞻性研究的确认。提供的证据没有报告具体的统计功效或细菌联合检测的确切数量。

Clinical characteristics and factors associated with severe disease in hospitalized infants with human metapneumovirus pneumonia and respiratory bacterial co-detection: a single-center retrospective study

Authors: Yalin Guo, Yuanzhe Li, Xiaomin Sun, Shaocong Zhao, Wanwan Li
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Human metapneumovirus (hMPV) is an important cause of lower respiratory tract infection in infants, but data on infants with respiratory bacterial co-detection are limited. We aimed to describe the clinical and microbiological characteristics of this population and to explore factors associated with severe disease. Methods We conducted a single-center retrospective study of infants aged 12 months or younger who were hospitalized at Henan Children's Hospital between October 2024 and March 2025 with hMPV pneumonia and clinically adjudicated respiratory bacterial co-detection. Demographic, clinical, laboratory, imaging, treatment, and 6-month follow-up data were extracted from medical records. Severe disease was classified according to the pediatric community-acquired pneumonia guideline and the discharge diagnosis. Group comparisons used chi-square or Fisher's exact tests and the Mann–Whitney U test. Exploratory logistic regression was performed; the primary model excluded lethargy because it overlapped with severity criteria, and a secondary warning-sign model included lethargy. Results Among 309 infants, 190 (61.5%) were male and 42 (13.6%) were classified as severe cases. Streptococcus pneumoniae was the most frequently co-detected bacterium. Among 143 infants who underwent chest CT, multilobar pneumonia (90.9%) and ground-glass opacity (56.6%) were the most common findings. In the primary exploratory model, artificial feeding (OR 2.60, 95% CI 1.27–5.34), underlying diseases (OR 2.43, 95% CI 1.14–5.21), and lower albumin levels (OR 0.85 per 1 g/L increase, 95% CI 0.78–0.94) were associated with severe disease. In the secondary model, lethargy was strongly associated with severe presentation (OR 5.02, 95% CI 2.38–10.62) and should be interpreted as a warning sign rather than an independent causal factor. Conclusions Underlying diseases, artificial feeding, and lower albumin levels may help identify infants with hMPV pneumonia and respiratory bacterial co-detection who require closer monitoring. Lethargy should be regarded as an early warning sign. These association-based findings require confirmation in multicenter prospective studies with standardized pathogen quantification and adjudication.

Original abstract (English)
Human metapneumovirus (hMPV) has emerged as a significant pathogen responsible for a wide spectrum of respiratory illnesses across all age groups, with the greatest burden observed in infants, older adults, and immunocompromised individuals. Since its discovery in 2001, extensive research has revealed its epidemiology, clinical manifestations, modes of transmission, and impact on public health. It is now recognized as a leading cause of both upper and lower respiratory tract infections. Despite significant advances in diagnostic technologies—particularly nucleic acid amplification methods such as RT-PCR—and a growing understanding of the virology and immune response, no licensed vaccine or specific antiviral therapy is currently available. Supportive care remains the mainstay of management. Co-infections with hMPV and other respiratory pathogens, such as RSV or SARS-CoV, present additional challenges and may complicate disease severity. Moreover, the limited understanding of hMPV immunopathogenesis, particularly in vulnerable populations, emphasizes the necessity for further research. In conclusion, hMPV represents an important target for future therapeutic and preventive interventions. With ongoing research and development, especially in vaccine design and antiviral therapy, there is hope for reducing the global disease burden of hMPV and improving outcomes for those most at risk. News Brief
A pilot study conducted in 108 Swiss households from May to September 2023 explored the feasibility of self-sampling for respiratory infections, including human metapneumovirus. Participants collected nasal swabs for testing, and online questionnaires were used to report symptoms. The study found that 63% of index case samples were positive for any of the 13 respiratory viruses tested, with human metapneumovirus being one of them. Potential within-household transmission was observed for several viruses, including human metapneumovirus. The study, published online on July 29, 2026, in the International Journal of Infectious Diseases, highlighted the viability of decentralized surveillance but noted limitations such as missing samples and the absence of bacterial pathogen testing. The findings support the use of remote sampling and telemedicine for pandemic preparedness, though further research is needed to confirm the specific role of human metapneumovirus in transmission and disease.

METHODOLOGY Researchers conducted a pilot study to evaluate whether household members could collect their own respiratory samples for testing and whether online symptom questionnaires plus telemedicine could support decentralised surveillance. They enrolled 108 households, including 161 adults, 32 children, 29 cats, and 15 dogs, in Switzerland from May to September 2023. The first household member to develop new respiratory symptoms (sore throat, runny or stuffy nose, cough, or shortness of breath) for at least 24 hours collected a nasal swab as the index case; other members collected swabs 7 days later. All samples were mailed to a central biobank. Samples were tested using a multiplex polymerase chain reaction panel for 13 common respiratory viruses, including SARS-CoV-2, influenza A and B, human rhinovirus, human respiratory syncytial virus, human metapneumovirus, and human parainfluenza virus. A short survey was conducted to assess the clarity of instructions and acceptability. The total duration of the study was 21 months. TAKEAWAY Overall, 70% (131/188) of samples from index cases, 69% (105/152) of samples from household contacts, and 51% (24/47) of samples from pets were analysed. Swabs from symptomatic participants were positive far more often than those from asymptomatic participants (60% vs 6%); potential within-household transmission was observed for rhinovirus (14 cases), SARS-CoV-2 (three cases), and parainfluenza (two cases). Among index cases, 63% of samples were positive for any of the 13 viruses, most commonly rhinovirus and SARS-CoV-2; among human household contacts, 23% were positive for any virus, with rhinovirus predominating. In the survey, 70% of respondents said the nasal swab was not unpleasant, and 52% said the nasal swab was not unpleasant for the pet; among participants from 10 households who became ill during the first month, 80% reported that telemedicine consultations were helpful. IN PRACTICE "It is feasible to establish a decentralized household-based cohort for pandemic preparedness using remote sampling and online questionnaires. Our study will inform investigations of emerging diseases," the authors wrote. SOURCE The study was led by Karin Grimm, MD, Bern University Hospital, University of Bern, Bern, Switzerland. It was published online on July 29, 2026, as a short communication in the International Journal of Infectious Diseases. LIMITATIONS About one third of the expected samples were missing, and the study did not test for bacterial pathogens such as pneumococcus and Mycoplasma pneumoniae. DISCLOSURES The study was funded by the Multidisciplinary Center for Infectious Diseases at the University of Bern. The authors reported having no competing interests. News Brief
A retrospective study conducted at two Canadian pediatric centers, The Hospital for Sick Children in Toronto and BC Children’s Hospital in Vancouver, analyzed 2,585 hospitalized children with acute respiratory tract infections (RTIs) between July 2022 and June 2023. The study found that more than one in five children developed severe disease, with those transferred from a referring hospital facing the highest risk. Chronic conditions, particularly neurologic, developmental, and genetic disorders, were strongly associated with increased severity. Viral coinfections were common but not linked to worse outcomes. Severe illness occurred in 21.3% of cases, with 6.7% of patients dying. The findings emphasize the importance of focusing on patient-specific risk factors rather than the viral pathogen alone, and highlight the need for targeted prevention and early supportive care for high-risk children. The study was published in JAMA Network Open, and its results suggest that clinicians should remain vigilant for nonrespiratory complications, such as cardiac dysfunction and septic shock, particularly in children with worsening clinical status.

Q&A: Which Hospitalized Children With RTIs Face the Highest Severe Disease Risk? By Lorraine L. Janeczko, MPH - Last Updated: August 7, 2026 Maria Deza Leon, MD More than one in five children hospitalized with acute respiratory tract infections (RTIs) develop severe disease, and children transferred from a referring hospital face the highest risk, according to a retrospective study conducted at two Canadian pediatric centers. “In this cohort study of hospitalized children with acute RTIs, many developed severe illness requiring intensive interventions. Chronic conditions were associated with increased risk, whereas viral coinfections were common but not associated with disease severity,” lead author Haifa Mtaweh, MD, PhD, of The Hospital for Sick Children in Toronto, Ontario, and her coauthors wrote in JAMA Network Open. “Despite low mortality, the complication burden remained high, underscoring the need for targeted prevention and ongoing surveillance.” Risk Factors for Severe Disease The 2,585 enrolled children (median age, 2.5 years) were hospitalized with acute RTIs between July 2022 and June 2023 at The Hospital for Sick Children and BC Children’s Hospital in Vancouver, British Columbia. Roughly half (50.6%) had at least one chronic condition, with neurologic, developmental, or genetic diagnoses most common. Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and enterovirus-rhinovirus were the predominant pathogens identified, at 30.4% and 25.6%, respectively. Severe illness, including cardiac arrest, the need for noninvasive or invasive ventilation, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO), or kidney replacement therapy, occurred among 551 children (21.3%), and of these, 37 (6.7%) died. In multivariable analysis, children transferred from a referring hospital (adjusted risk ratio [aRR], 4.73; 95% CI, 4.01-5.59), children with a single identified virus compared with none (aRR, 1.65; 95% CI, 1.30-2.08), children with two or more chronic conditions compared with none (aRR, 1.62; 95% CI, 1.36-1.93), and patients aged less than 1 year and more than 10 years were at increased risk for severe outcomes. Viral coinfections, detected among approximately 15% of patients, were not associated with severity. An Expert Shares Insights Maria Deza Leon, MD, a pediatric infectious disease physician at Children’s Mercy in Kansas City, Missouri, who was not involved in the study, spoke with Physician’s Weekly about the study and its implications for pediatric acute RTI care. Physician’s Weekly: Which complications should clinicians anticipate among children hospitalized with RTIs, and do these risks vary by viral pathogen? Dr. Deza Leon: Common severe complications included acute respiratory distress syndrome, cardiac complications, septic shock, pneumothorax, and empyema, with some children requiring ECMO or kidney replacement therapy. Although complication rates were generally similar across viruses, influenza was more often associated with ARDS and septic shock, whereas human metapneumovirus was more often associated with cardiac complications and extracorporeal support. These findings suggest that viral identification may help inform risk, but clinical management should remain focused on the patient’s overall trajectory. Which chronic conditions place patients at the highest risk for severe outcomes, and how can clinicians use this information to guide individualized care strategies? Children with neurologic, developmental, and genetic disorders carried the greatest risk for severe disease, with chronic pulmonary and cardiac conditions also associated with increased risk. Risk was highest among children with multiple chronic conditions, highlighting the impact of medical complexity on patient outcomes. Clinicians can use this information to identify high-risk patients early and guide management decisions. Children with complex medical conditions may benefit from closer monitoring, a lower threshold for escalation of care, and careful discharge planning. These findings also support prioritizing preventive measures, including routine immunizations, RSV prevention strategies when appropriate, and timely access to specialty care for medically complex children. What factors might explain why the highest risk concentrates in two groups—infants and older children/adolescents? This elevated risk among infants is not unexpected. Smaller airways, reduced physiologic reserve, and immature immune function make young children especially vulnerable to respiratory infections. The increased risk observed among older children and adolescents is particularly noteworthy. Although hospitalization for respiratory tract infections is less common in these age groups than in infancy, those requiring admission may represent a more medically complex population with underlying comorbidities or more severe illness at presentation. These findings suggest that while the overall burden of respiratory disease is highest in infancy, the potential severity of respiratory tract infections in hospitalized adolescents should not be overlooked. How does coinfection influence disease course and outcomes for these patients? One of the more reassuring findings was that viral coinfection did not appear to increase the risk of severe disease. Coinfections were relatively common, occurring in about 15% of cases, but their frequency was similar among children with severe and nonsevere illness. These findings suggest that detecting multiple respiratory viruses should not automatically be considered a predictor of worse outcomes. The study also highlights the challenges of interpreting highly sensitive molecular testing, which can detect residual viral nucleic acid that may not reflect active infection or meaningfully contribute to disease severity. What key insights does this study offer about identifying high‐risk patients, anticipating complications, and optimizing early supportive care? This study serves as an important reminder that severe respiratory infection remains a significant source of pediatric morbidity, even in the post-pandemic era. More than one in five hospitalized children met criteria for severe disease, and nearly one-quarter required pediatric ICU admission. The findings reinforce the importance of focusing on patient-specific risk factors rather than the viral diagnosis alone, with particular attention to children with medical complexity and multiple chronic conditions. Transfer from an outside hospital may also serve as a useful marker of greater illness severity. In addition, the results support continued use of preventive strategies, including vaccination and respiratory syncytial virus-targeted prevention when indicated. Clinicians should also remain alert for nonrespiratory complications, such as cardiac dysfunction and septic shock, particularly in children whose clinical status is worsening. Overall, although mortality was uncommon, the burden of severe illness, intensive care utilization, and associated complications remained substantial, underscoring the importance of early recognition, close monitoring, and aggressive supportive care.
