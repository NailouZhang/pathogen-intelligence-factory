人偏肺病毒感染住院儿童:意大利巴勒莫一家三级儿科医院四年的回顾性研究

Human metapneumovirus infection in hospitalized children: a four-year retrospective study from a tertiary pediatric hospital
作者： Chiara Albano, Valeria Garbo, Laura Venuti, Giovanni Boncori, Giulia Linares, Alessandra Cuccia, Sara Ashtari, Laura Saporito, Irene Ganci, Antonio Cascio
摘要中文翻译人偏肺病毒(hMPV)是急性呼吸道感染的重要病原体,尤其是在婴幼儿中,可能导致下呼吸道疾病住院。然而,关于住院儿科患者人偏肺病毒感染的详细临床数据仍然有限。本研究结合了意大利巴勒莫一家三级儿科医院的两个互补数据集。医院微生物学数据集包括从2021年10月至2025年5月期间通过PCR确认的所有hMPV检测记录(n=636,其中包含560例儿科病例),仅用于描述时间分布。临床数据集包括2022年1月至2025年5月期间在儿科感染性疾病病房住院的78例儿科病例,这些病例具有完整的临床记录。通过IBM SPSS Statistics收集和分析了人口学、临床、实验室、影像学和治疗数据,采用描述性统计和探索性双变量分析。连续变量以中位数和四分位数范围(IQR)进行总结,而分类变量则以绝对频数和百分比进行报告。使用Pearson卡方检验或精确检验(根据情况)比较接受抗生素治疗和未接受抗生素治疗的儿童。使用Mann–Whitney U检验探索分类预测变量与住院时间之间的关联。使用Spearman系数评估与年龄的相关性。住院儿童的中位年龄为14个月(IQR 3–30)。最常见的临床表现和症状包括咳嗽(64例;82.1%)、发热(59例;75.6%)以及呼吸窘迫迹象,包括呼吸急促(48例;61.5%)和肋间凹陷(38例;48.7%)。食欲减退(32例;41%)和流涕(29例;37.2%)也较为常见。41例(52.6%)患者进行了胸部X光检查,所有病例均显示异常结果,最常见的异常为肺实变。25.6%的患者检测到病毒合并感染,24.4%检测到细菌病原体。60.3%的病例需要氧气支持,其中5例(6.4%)需要儿科重症监护病房入院。尽管仅24.4%的患者检测到细菌病原体,但仍有70.5%的患者开具了抗生素。我们的研究结果描述了在三级儿科中心住院的人偏肺病毒感染儿童的临床特征和管理情况,突显了人偏肺病毒在我们中心住院儿童中的临床重要性。此外,本研究中观察到的高抗生素处方率突显了在区分病毒性与细菌性呼吸道感染方面的诊断挑战,并强调了在儿科呼吸道疾病中改善抗菌药物使用和病毒学监测的重要性。

Human metapneumovirus infection in hospitalized children: a four-year retrospective study from a tertiary pediatric hospital

Authors: Chiara Albano, Valeria Garbo, Laura Venuti, Giovanni Boncori, Giulia Linares, Alessandra Cuccia, Sara Ashtari, Laura Saporito, Irene Ganci, Antonio Cascio
Original abstract (English)
Human metapneumovirus (hMPV) is an important viral cause of acute respiratory infections, particularly in young children, and may lead to hospitalization for lower respiratory tract disease. However, detailed clinical data from hospitalized pediatric cohorts remain limited. This study combined two complementary datasets from a tertiary pediatric hospital in Palermo, Italy. The hospital microbiology dataset included all PCR-confirmed hMPV detections recorded from October 2021 to May 2025 ( n = 636, including 560 pediatric cases) and was used only to describe temporal distribution. The clinical cohort comprised 78 of the pediatric cases who were hospitalized in the Pediatric Infectious Diseases Unit between January 2022 and May 2025 and had complete clinical records. Demographic, clinical, laboratory, radiological, and therapeutic data were collected and analyzed through IBM SPSS Statistics using descriptive statistics and exploratory bivariate analyses. Continuous variables were summarized as median and interquartile range (IQR), while categorical variables were reported as absolute frequencies and percentages. Comparisons between children who received antibiotics and those who did not were performed using Pearson’s chi-square or exact tests, as appropriate. Associations between categorical predictors and length of hospital stay were explored using the Mann–Whitney U test. Correlation with age was assessed using Spearman’s coefficient. The median age of hospitalized children was 14 months (IQR 3–30). The most frequent signs and symptoms were cough (64;82.1%), fever (59;75.6%), and signs of respiratory distress, including tachypnea (48;61.5%) and subcostal retraction (38;48.7%). Hyporexia (32;41%) and coryza (29;37.2%) were also common. Chest X-ray was performed in 41 (52.6%) patients and showed abnormal findings in all cases, most commonly consolidation patterns. Viral coinfections were detected in 25.6% of patients, and bacterial organisms were detected in 24.4%. Oxygen support was required in 60.3% of cases, and five patients (6.4%) required pediatric intensive care admission. Antibiotics were prescribed in 70.5% of patients, although bacterial organisms were detected in only 24.4%. Our findings describe the clinical characteristics and management of hospitalized children with hMPV infection in a tertiary pediatric center, highlighting the clinical relevance of hMPV among hospitalized children in our center. Moreover, the high rate of antibiotic prescription observed in this cohort highlights the diagnostic challenges in distinguishing viral from bacterial respiratory infections and underscores the importance of improved antimicrobial stewardship and virological surveillance in pediatric respiratory disease.

人偏肺病毒感染儿童入院时严重肺炎风险分层:基于854例回顾性数据的机器学习模型开发

Risk classification of severe pneumonia at admission in children with human metapneumovirus infection: development of a machine learning model based on retrospective data from 854 cases
作者： Lijiao Yuan, Ying Zhu, Yin Li, Ruonan Li, Qingping Tang, Penghao Cui, Haifeng Jin, Yanchun Wang, Yonghan Luo
摘要中文翻译方法 本项回顾性队列研究研究连续纳入了2019年12月至2025年3月期间昆明儿童医院收治的854例与人偏肺病毒(hMPV)相关的肺炎患儿的临床数据。结果 基于LASSO回归,确定了9个核心预测因子:年龄、喘息、肺部湿啰音、胸腔积液、铁蛋白、乳酸脱氢酶、中性粒细胞百分比、中性粒细胞计数和白蛋白。结论 我们成功开发并验证了基于机器学习的模型,特别是XGBoost和逻辑回归模型,用于对hMPV感染儿童严重肺炎风险进行分类。伦理声明 伦理批准和参与同意 本研究已获得昆明医科大学儿童医院伦理审查委员会的批准。由于本研究为回顾性设计,昆明医科大学儿童医院伦理审查委员会(KMSETYY-IIT-2025-002(GC))豁免了知情同意的要求。此外,所有方法均按照相关指南和法规或赫尔辛基宣言进行。摘要 目的 开发并验证一个基于机器学习的模型,以准确分类hMPV感染儿童严重肺炎的风险。根据基于指南的标准,患者被分为严重肺炎组(n = 203)和非严重肺炎组(n = 651)。数据集以7:3的比例随机分为训练集和测试集。使用最小绝对收缩和选择算子(LASSO)回归进行特征选择。包括逻辑回归、k近邻、朴素贝叶斯、多层感知机、随机森林和极端梯度提升在内的六种机器学习模型被用于分析。
查看研究七要素
问题与背景
本研究旨在开发并验证一种机器学习模型,以准确分类人偏肺病毒(hMPV)感染儿童发生严重肺炎的风险,以应对在此背景下对更优诊断工具的需求。
设计与对象
一项回顾性队列研究研究于2019年12月至2025年3月在昆明儿童医院开展,纳入了854名因人偏肺病毒(hMPV)相关肺炎而住院的儿童,并将其分为重症肺炎组和非重症肺炎组。
核心方法
该研究采用了一种机器学习方法,包括使用LASSO回归进行特征选择,并在训练集上比较了六个模型(逻辑回归、k近邻、朴素贝叶斯、多层感知机、随机森林和XGBoost),使用AUC、准确率、灵敏度、特异性和F1分数在测试集上评估模型性能。
主要结果
XGBoost和逻辑回归模型表现出相当的性能,XGBoost在训练集和测试集上的AUC分别为0.87(95% CI:0.84–0.90)和0.78(95% CI:0.72–0.85)。SHAP分析确定喘息、年龄和肺部啰音是最具影响力的预测因子。
解释与创新
该研究成功开发并验证了用于人偏肺病毒感染儿童严重肺炎风险分类的机器学习模型,特别是XGBoost和逻辑回归模型,展示了强大的判别性能和临床应用价值。
科研与公卫意义
这些模型可能作为有价值的决策支持工具,用于高风险儿科患者人偏肺病毒感染的风险分层,有助于临床决策和资源分配。
局限与证据强度
该研究受到其回顾性设计和单一医院的限制,这可能会影响其结果的普遍适用性。样本量适中,且模型未与其他已建立的方法进行比较。

Question and background
The study aimed to develop and validate a machine learning model to accurately classify the risk of severe pneumonia in children infected with hMPV, addressing the need for better diagnostic tools in this context.
Design and population
A retrospective cohort study was conducted on 854 hospitalized children with hMPV-associated pneumonia at Kunming Children’s Hospital from December 2019 to March 2025, stratifying them into severe and non-severe pneumonia groups.
Core methods
The study utilized a machine learning approach, including feature selection with LASSO regression, and compared six models (logistic regression, k-nearest neighbors, naïve Bayes, multilayer perceptron, random forest, and XGBoost) on a training set, with model performance evaluated on a testing set using AUC, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and F1 score.
Main results
XGBoost and logistic regression models demonstrated comparable performance, with XGBoost achieving an AUC of 0.87 (95% CI: 0.84–0.90) and 0.78 (95% CI: 0.72–0.85) on the training and testing sets, respectively. SHAP analysis identified wheezing, age, and pulmonary rales as the most influential predictors.
Interpretation and novelty
The study successfully developed and validated machine learning models, particularly XGBoost and logistic regression, for risk classification of severe pneumonia in children with hMPV infection, demonstrating strong discriminative performance and clinical utility.
Scientific and public-health significance
The models may serve as a valuable decision-support tool for risk stratification of high-risk pediatric patients with hMPV infection, aiding in clinical decision-making and resource allocation.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its retrospective design and the use of a single hospital, which may affect generalizability. The sample size is moderate, and the models were not compared against other established methods.

Emerging Trends in the Pathogenesis, Diagnosis, and Management of Human Metapneumovirus-Associated Respiratory Infections

Authors: Sayad Ahad Ali, Shamim Shamim, Nasiruddin Ahmad Farooqui
Original abstract (English)
Introduction: Human Metapneumovirus is a significant respiratory pathogen causing infections across all age groups, particularly in young children, immunocompromised individuals, and the elderly. Despite its clinical relevance, there are currently no approved vaccines or specific antiviral treatments for Human Metapneumovirus, necessitating further research into therapeutic and preventive strategies. Methods: A systematic literature review was conducted using peer-reviewed journals, clinical trials, and recent studies focusing on Human Metapneumovirus infection. Data were extracted and analyzed to evaluate the effectiveness of antiviral therapies, monoclonal antibodies, immunomodulators, and vaccine candidates. Results: Studies indicate that Human Metapneumovirus primarily targets the respiratory epithelium, triggering immune responses that contribute to both viral clearance and disease pathogenesis. Investigational therapies, including fusion inhibitors and protease inhibitors, show promise but require further validation. Immunomodulatory agents and herbal remedies may offer supportive benefits, but their efficacy against Human Metapneumovirus remains unproven. Advances in vaccine development are ongoing, with several candidates in preclinical and early clinical stages. Discussion: HMPV continues to pose a substantial public health burden due to its seasonal outbreaks, clinical similarity to other respiratory viruses, and absence of specific therapies. The virus evades immune responses via multiple mechanisms, contributing to disease severity, particularly in vulnerable populations. Although supportive care is currently the mainstay, emerging antivirals and vaccines may offer future solutions. Improved diagnostic access, public awareness, and targeted research are essential for controlling HMPV infections. Conclusion: Human Metapneumovirus continues to pose a public health challenge due to the lack of targeted treatments and vaccines. Ongoing research into antiviral agents, monoclonal antibodies, and vaccines is critical for effective disease management. Further clinical trials are needed to establish safe and effective therapeutic options.
