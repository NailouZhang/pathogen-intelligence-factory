hMPV 的无声战争:病毒对 宿主 免疫的干扰

The Silent War of hMPV: Viral Interference with Host Immunity
作者： Grabiel J. García-Velázquez, Matías Moraga-Astete, Alison Sepúlveda-Pontigo, Karissa Chávez-Villacreses, Benjamín Díaz-López, Valeria Salazar-Montoya, Felipe Melo-González, Katina Schinnerling, Abel E. Vasquez, Claudio Cabello‐Verrugio
摘要中文翻译人偏肺病毒(hMPV)是一种重要的呼吸道病原体,是呼吸道感染的主要病因,尤其在易感人群中。尽管人偏肺病毒于2001年被发现,但其研究程度仍远不及其他主要呼吸道病毒(如流感病毒和呼吸道合胞病毒),这阻碍了有效预防和治疗策略的开发。本综述探讨了人偏肺病毒逃避宿主免疫应答的主要机制。该病毒干扰早期固有免疫信号通路,特别是参与诱导和信号传导的I型和III型干扰素(IFN)的通路,并调节炎症反应。在适应性免疫层面,人偏肺病毒损害T细胞活化及长效免疫记忆的形成,可能导致再次感染易感性增加。该病毒还改变多种免疫细胞和结构细胞的功能,包括巨噬细胞、树突状细胞和呼吸道上皮细胞。此外,人偏肺病毒可能调节宿主微RNA表达以促进免疫逃避并延长感染。人偏肺病毒感染的反复性凸显了进一步研究其免疫逃避机制的必要性。此类研究对开发更安全有效的抗病毒药物、免疫调节剂和免疫调节疗法至关重要。

The Silent War of hMPV: Viral Interference with Host Immunity

Authors: Grabiel J. García-Velázquez, Matías Moraga-Astete, Alison Sepúlveda-Pontigo, Karissa Chávez-Villacreses, Benjamín Díaz-López, Valeria Salazar-Montoya, Felipe Melo-González, Katina Schinnerling, Abel E. Vasquez, Claudio Cabello‐Verrugio
Original abstract (English)
Human metapneumovirus (hMPV) is an important respiratory pathogen and a major cause of respiratory tract infections, particularly among vulnerable populations. Although hMPV was identified in 2001, it remains less extensively studied than other major respiratory viruses, such as influenza virus and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), hindering the development of effective preventive and therapeutic strategies. This review examines the primary mechanisms by which hMPV evades host immune responses. The virus disrupts early innate immune signaling pathways, particularly those involved in the induction and signaling of antiviral interferons (IFNs) and modulates inflammatory responses. At the level of adaptive immunity, hMPV impairs T-cell activation and the development of long-lasting immunological memory, which may contribute to susceptibility to reinfection. The virus also alters the functions of several immune and structural cell types, including macrophages, dendritic cells, and respiratory epithelial cells. In addition, hMPV may modulate host microRNA expression to promote immune evasion and prolong infection. The recurrent nature of hMPV infections highlights the need to further investigate the immune-evasion mechanisms. Such research is essential for developing safer and more effective vaccines, antiviral agents, and immunomodulatory therapies.

Scope and question
This review focuses on human metapneumovirus (hMPV) infection and disease, specifically examining the mechanisms by which hMPV evades host immune responses. The central question is to understand how hMPV disrupts immune signaling and modulates immune cell functions to establish persistent infections.
Evidence base and method
The review is a narrative synthesis of existing literature on hMPV. It does not report specific databases, search dates, or meta-analysis methods. The evidence is derived from general observations and findings about hMPV's immune evasion strategies, with no explicit mention of study selection criteria or included-study counts.
Consensus and conclusions
The review concludes that hMPV disrupts early innate immune signaling pathways, particularly those involved in antiviral interferon (IFN) induction and signaling, and modulates inflammatory responses. It also highlights that hMPV impairs T-cell activation and the development of long-lasting immunological memory, which may contribute to susceptibility to reinfection.
Controversies and gaps
The evidence suggests that hMPV may modulate host microRNA expression to promote immune evasion and prolong infection, but this finding is not supported by specific experimental data. The review notes that the recurrent nature of hMPV infections underscores the need for further investigation into immune-evasion mechanisms.
Research and practice implications
The review emphasizes the importance of researching hMPV's immune evasion mechanisms to develop safer and more effective vaccines, antiviral agents, and immunomodulatory therapies. It highlights the need for targeted studies to address the gaps in understanding hMPV's impact on host immunity.

Moderna 在早期儿科隔离后将 RSV/hMPV 疫苗 带回诊所 - AllSci

Moderna takes RSV/hMPV vaccine back to clinic after earlier pediatric hold - AllSci
新闻简报Moderna 已在 60 岁及以上成年人中启动了 mRNA-1365(Profile 2)的 I 期研究,mRNA-1365 是一种针对呼吸道合胞病毒 (RSV) 和 人偏肺病毒 (hMPV) 的二价 mRNA 疫苗。在此之前,2024 年一项儿科 I 期研究出现了安全性挫折,其中 疫苗 接受者中严重 RSV 下呼吸道感染 的发生率高于安慰剂,促使美国 FDA 暂停该项目的临床。新试验排除了未接触过 RSV 的婴儿,重点关注先前自然接触过 RSV 的成年人。它计划在加拿大和英国招募 375 名参与者,预计将于 2027 年 7 月完成初步完成。该研究将评估 mRNA-1365 的三种剂量水平以及 Moderna 的 RSV 疫苗、mRNA-1345 的两种制剂作为活性比较剂。该试验旨在提供安全性和免疫原性数据,包括二价候选药物和仅 RSV 制剂之间的直接比较。美国 FDA 的 疫苗 和相关生物制品咨询委员会此前曾建议在未接触过 RSV 的婴儿中进一步开发 RSV 疫苗 时应谨慎行事。Moderna 在为老年人开发组合 RSV/hMPV 疫苗 方面面临竞争,Clover Biopharmaceuticals 在 II 期测试基于蛋白质的 疫苗。该试验的结果将有助于评估添加 hMPV 成分是否会影响 Moderna 已建立的 RSV 方法生成的 RSV 免疫应答。

该试验于2026年8月11日启动,预计主要完成时间为2027年7月。此前的儿科项目于2024年7月暂停,原因是接种疫苗的婴儿中出现了严重的呼吸道合胞病毒(RSV)感染。

该I期研究计划在加拿大和英国两国共招募375名参与者,目标人群为年满60岁且此前自然暴露于RSV的成人,排除已接种获批RSV疫苗(疫苗)或此前参与过RSV或人偏肺病毒(hMPV)疫苗(疫苗)研究的个体。
事件
Moderna已启动一项名为mRNA-1365(Profile 2)的I期研究,该研究采用一种针对RSV和人偏肺病毒(hMPV)的双价mRNA疫苗(疫苗),针对60岁及以上成人。此前,2024年一项儿科I期研究曾因安全性问题受挫,该研究中,接种疫苗组的严重RSV感染发生率高于安慰剂组。
规模、影响与风险
35 名 疫苗 接受者中有 5 人经历了严重或住院的 RSV 感染,而 18 名安慰剂接受者中只有 1 人经历了严重或住院的 RSV 感染。美国 FDA 的 疫苗 和相关生物制品咨询委员会建议在未接触过 RSV 的婴儿中进一步开发 RSV 疫苗 时应谨慎。该试验将提供截至 2027 年的安全性和免疫原性数据。
应对、状态与不确定性
该试验正在进行中,预计到 2027 年才能获得安全性和免疫原性数据。美国 FDA 已暂停儿科项目的临床试验,但成人项目仍在进行中。该研究使用 mRNA-1345 制剂作为活性比较物来评估 hMPV 成分对 RSV 免疫应答 的影响。

Moderna takes RSV/hMPV vaccine back to clinic after earlier pediatric hold - AllSci

News Brief
Moderna has launched a Phase I study of mRNA-1365 (Profile 2), a bivalent mRNA vaccine targeting respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and human metapneumovirus (hMPV), in adults aged 60 and older. This follows a safety setback in a pediatric Phase I study in 2024, where severe RSV lower respiratory tract infections occurred more frequently among vaccine recipients than placebo, prompting the US FDA to place the program on clinical hold. The new trial excludes RSV-naive infants and focuses on adults with prior natural exposure to RSV. It plans to enroll 375 participants across Canada and the United Kingdom, with primary completion expected in July 2027. The study will evaluate three dose levels of mRNA-1365 alongside two formulations of Moderna's RSV vaccine, mRNA-1345, as active comparators. The trial aims to provide safety and immunogenicity data, including direct comparisons between the bivalent candidate and the RSV-only formulations. The US FDA's Vaccines and Related Biological Products Advisory Committee had previously recommended caution in further development of RSV vaccines in RSV-naive infants. Moderna faces competition in developing combined RSV/hMPV vaccines for older adults, with Clover Biopharmaceuticals testing a protein-based vaccine in Phase II. The findings from this trial will help assess whether adding the hMPV component affects the RSV immune response generated by Moderna's established RSV approach.

The study brings the RSV/hMPV combination back into clinical development after an earlier pediatric program involving mRNA-1365 was placed on clinical hold following severe RSV infections in vaccinated infants. The randomized, open-label, staggered dose-escalation study (NCT07764237) plans to enroll 375 participants across Canada and the United Kingdom. Three dose levels of mRNA-1365 (Profile 2) will be evaluated alongside two formulations of mRNA-1345, Moderna's RSV vaccine, as active comparators. The trial started August 11, 2026, according to the registry, with primary completion expected in July 2027. Immunogenicity endpoints include RSV and hMPV neutralizing antibody titers and seroresponse rates at Day 29. The adult program follows a safety setback in RSV-naive infants in 2024. Moderna paused dosing in a pediatric Phase I study of mRNA-1345 and mRNA-1365 in July that year after severe RSV lower respiratory tract infections occurred more frequently among vaccine recipients than placebo, prompting US FDA clinical holds and concerns about possible vaccine-associated enhanced respiratory disease. Five of 35 vaccine recipients experienced severe or hospitalized RSV infections, compared with one of 18 placebo recipients. The US FDA's Vaccines and Related Biological Products Advisory Committee reviewed the findings in December 2024 and recommended caution in further development of RSV vaccines in RSV-naive infants. The new mRNA-1365 study instead targets adults aged at least 60, a population with previous natural exposure to RSV, and excludes participants who have received a licensed RSV vaccine or previously participated in an RSV or hMPV vaccine study. mRNA-1365 combines antigens targeting RSV and hMPV, two respiratory viruses associated with lower respiratory tract disease in older adults. The RSV component targets the prefusion F protein also used by Moderna's approved RSV vaccine mRESVIA, while the hMPV component targets the virus's F protein. No hMPV vaccine is currently approved.
