多克隆抗体反应的结构和功能表征揭示了 RSV 和 hMPV 融合蛋白共有和不同的抗原特征

Structural and functional characterization of polyclonal antibody responses reveals shared and distinct antigenic features of RSV and hMPV fusion proteins
作者： Erick Bermúdez-Méndez, Sebastian Ols, Leonardo V. Cufaro, Shuhao Xiao, Zhitong Peng, Florian Gegenfurtner, Elise R. Eray, Marcos C. Miranda, Rodrigo Arcoverde Cerveira, Mikel Segura García
摘要中文翻译呼吸道合胞病毒 (RSV) 和 人偏肺病毒 (hMPV) 属于肺病毒科成员,广泛传播,是婴儿和老年人因急性呼吸道感染住院的主要原因。融合前 (preF) 构象的病毒融合 (F) 蛋白是中和抗体的主要靶标,但自然引发的循环多克隆抗体如何分布在其抗原图谱中仍知之甚少。在这里,我们结合了血清学分析、位点特异性抗体竞争测定和基于电子显微镜的多克隆表位作图(EMPEM)来表征健康成人对 RSV 和 hMPV 的 preF 定向免疫。所有供体都表现出可检测的前F结合抗体,这些抗体与病毒中和呈正相关,并广泛识别多个抗原位点,与以表面为中心的计算模型预测的免疫优势模式一致。尽管总体相似,但 RSV 和 hMPV 之间各个抗原位点的反应分布不同。EMPEM 分析显示,两种病毒均以高度中和敏感的 preF 顶端(位点 Ø)为目标,同时针对赤道和中上表位(位点 II、III、IV 和 V)的反应较少。我们还观察到抗体结合的不稳定 preF,支持这样的模型:抗体结合促进 preF 三聚体的瞬时打开,并可能干扰膜融合所需的构象重排。总之,这些发现强调了顶端定向抗体是成人自然获得性肺病毒免疫的一个显着特征,并揭示了抗原识别的共同和独特模式,这可能为下一代 疫苗 设计和基于抗体的预防措施提供信息。

Structural and functional characterization of polyclonal antibody responses reveals shared and distinct antigenic features of RSV and hMPV fusion proteins

Authors: Erick Bermúdez-Méndez, Sebastian Ols, Leonardo V. Cufaro, Shuhao Xiao, Zhitong Peng, Florian Gegenfurtner, Elise R. Eray, Marcos C. Miranda, Rodrigo Arcoverde Cerveira, Mikel Segura García
Original abstract (English)
Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and human metapneumovirus (hMPV), members of the Pneumoviridae family, circulate widely and are leading causes of hospitalization for acute respiratory infections in infants and older adults. The viral fusion (F) protein in its prefusion (preF) conformation is the primary target of neutralizing antibodies, yet how naturally elicited circulating polyclonal antibodies are distributed across its antigenic landscape remains poorly understood. Here, we combined serological profiling, site-specific antibody competition assays and electron microscopy-based polyclonal epitope mapping (EMPEM) to characterize preF-directed immunity to RSV and hMPV in healthy adults. All donors exhibited detectable preF-binding antibodies that positively correlated with virus neutralization and broadly recognized multiple antigenic sites, consistent with immunodominance patterns predicted by a surface-centric computational model. Despite this overall similarity, the distribution of responses across individual antigenic sites differed between RSV and hMPV. EMPEM analyses revealed consistent targeting of the highly neutralization-sensitive preF apex (site Ø) in both viruses, alongside less abundant responses against equatorial and upper-middle epitopes (sites II, III, IV and V). We additionally observed antibody-bound destabilized preF, supporting a model in which antibody binding promotes transient opening of the preF trimer and may interfere with the conformational rearrangements required for membrane fusion. Together, these findings highlight apex-directed antibodies as a prominent feature of naturally acquired pneumovirus immunity in adults and reveal both shared and distinct patterns of antigenic recognition that may inform next-generation vaccine design and antibody-based prophylactics.

Stroke Triggered by Respiratory Viruses: Ecological Study of Temporal and Spatiotemporal Associations

Authors: T. Q. Nguyen, Tim D. Spelman, Lachlan L. Dalli, Muzhi Zhao, Benjamin D. Atkins, Aaron L. Weinman, Hazel J Clothier, Suzanne Mavoa, Monique F. Kilkenny, Christopher Michael Reid
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Infections have been linked as short-term triggers for stroke, but the extent of their contribution is unclear. We examined population-level temporal and spatiotemporal associations between common respiratory viruses and stroke in an ecological study in Victoria, Australia. METHODS: The statewide Victorian Admitted Episodes data set, covering all hospital admissions for stroke between June 2010 and December 2022, was analyzed against statewide representative data from SnotWatch respiratory polymerase chain reaction tests. Population size, climate, and air pollution data sets for the same period were treated as population-level confounders. Time-series analysis was used to model weekly stroke events and respiratory virus levels (% of polymerase chain reaction tests positive for each pathogen), and attributable fractions were computed using distributed lag nonlinear modeling. To examine spatiotemporal variation, the weekly association by Statistical Area Level 2 was modeled using a Bayesian Integrated Nested Laplace Approximation. RESULTS: We analyzed 153 730 stroke events (61% ischemic, 25% hemorrhagic, and 14% unspecified) and 6 180 896 respiratory specimens. In the time-series analysis, higher weekly levels of specific respiratory viruses were associated with increased rates of stroke after controlling for confounders. Our model estimated that the fractions of strokes attributed to enterovirus and human metapneumovirus 4.6% (95% CI, 2.5-6.6) and 1.3% (95% CI, 0.3-2.3), respectively, which rose during peak viral weeks. The attributable fractions differed between ischemic stroke (human metapneumovirus 3.7%) and hemorrhagic stroke (enterovirus, 5.8%; parainfluenza, 3.2%). Spatiotemporal analysis revealed greater effect sizes in areas potentially associated with agricultural/industrial activity, as well as in outer-urban areas, based on the regionality of the Statistical Area Level 2 of usual patient residence. CONCLUSIONS: At the population level, there were clear, observable time- and space-time-related associations between respiratory virus activity and stroke. People living in higher-risk areas during peak respiratory virus periods may be more susceptible to acute stroke. This warrants further investigation.

According to a retrospective cohort investigation conducted at the Children’s Hospital Affiliated to Shandong University in Jinan, China, individual viral pathogens elicit characteristic hematological responses that differentiate them from one another and from pathogen-negative controls. The research evaluated clinical data from 7,132 pediatric cases gathered between September 1, 2025, and February 28, 2026, aiming to assess whether conventional cellular parameters and composite ratios can assist in early diagnostic differentiation during seasonal outbreaks. While individual inflammatory indices demonstrated constrained standalone discriminatory accuracy across patient cohorts, multi-parameter statistical models integrating demographic characteristics with multiple complete blood count metrics achieved improved diagnostic performance, offering accessible supportive tools alongside definitive nucleic acid testing. Acute respiratory tract infections represent a leading infectious cause of mortality among children across the globe, with viral pathogens accounting for roughly 80 percent of documented clinical cases. Among children under five years of age, worldwide respiratory infection mortality was recorded at 94.8 per 100,000 person-years in 2023, creating substantial disease and economic burdens on global healthcare infrastructure. While the influenza virus frequently induces severe systemic manifestations such as high fever and myalgia in preschool and school-aged children, respiratory syncytial virus predominantly attacks infants and young toddlers, functioning as the foremost trigger of acute bronchiolitis and viral pneumonia. Concurrently, adenovirus exhibits a wide pathogenic spectrum ranging from the common cold to pneumonia, with virulent subtypes B3 and B7 associated with severe pneumonia in children. Early identification of the infection type and precise assessment of disease severity are important for guiding appropriate clinical drug utilization and enhancing the prognosis for pediatric patients. Although automated complete blood counts represent one of the most accessible and cost-effective clinical laboratory tests worldwide, the diagnostic utility of standard leukocyte parameters in separating specific viral etiologies has historically remained incompletely established. Conventional hematological parameters such as total white blood cell count, neutrophil percentage, and lymphocyte percentage have traditionally served to distinguish bacterial infections from viral illnesses. Typical bacterial processes frequently induce prominent leukocytosis accompanied by elevated neutrophil percentages, whereas viral infections typically manifest with normal or diminished total white blood cell counts alongside relative lymphocytosis. However, extensive physiological overlap among infections driven by distinct respiratory pathogens frequently impairs the diagnostic sensitivity and specificity of isolated cellular markers. To overcome these diagnostic constraints, clinicians have increasingly evaluated composite systemic inflammatory ratios, which integrate cellular counts across divergent leukocyte and thrombocyte lineages without demanding supplemental blood draws or increasing financial expenditures. The retrospective study cohort encompassed 7,132 pediatric patients presenting with acute respiratory symptoms—such as persistent cough, shortness of breath, or dyspnea, presenting with or without documented fever—who underwent concurrent nucleic acid polymerase chain reaction testing and automated hematological analysis during the identical outpatient clinical consultation. Patients were categorized into five well-defined comparative groups based upon definitive laboratory findings: an influenza A cohort of 1,169 children, an influenza B cohort of 361 children, a respiratory syncytial virus cohort of 1,672 children, an adenovirus cohort of 201 children, and a negative control cohort comprising 3,729 children who exhibited acute respiratory symptoms but tested negative across all four target pathogens. Stringent exclusion criteria eliminated individuals with repeated sampling lacking blood counts, positive results for non-target pathogens, multi-pathogen co-infections, primary or secondary immunodeficiencies, active malignancies, and severe underlying systemic diseases or organ malformations. Demographic evaluations across the five clinical cohorts revealed no statistically significant differences regarding sex distribution, with male patients constituting 58.03 percent of the negative control group, 54.49 percent of the influenza A group, 60.11 percent of the influenza B group, 57.78 percent of the respiratory syncytial virus group, and 62.69 percent of the adenovirus group. In contrast, patient age varied considerably across the diagnostic categories in alignment with established epidemiological pathogen tropisms. The respiratory syncytial virus group exhibited the lowest median age at 2.33 years, reflecting the heightened physiological vulnerability of immature infant airways and waning maternal antibody protection during the initial months of life. The negative control group demonstrated a median age of 4.58 years, while the adenovirus group presented an intermediate median age of 4.83 years. Children diagnosed with influenza A and influenza B exhibited the highest median ages, both centering precisely at 6.00 years, consistent with increased social interaction, crowded classroom exposures, and peer contact rates characteristic of school-aged children. Laboratory comparisons of standard hematological parameters revealed significant overall heterogeneity across the five study cohorts, demonstrating that each viral family induces a unique footprint within peripheral blood lineages. Total white blood cell counts were elevated in children with influenza A, influenza B, and respiratory syncytial virus relative to negative controls, whereas adenovirus infections produced total white cell counts that did not differ significantly from controls. Absolute lymphocyte counts rose markedly in influenza A cases to a median of 3.12 times 10 to the ninth cells per liter, while remaining unchanged in influenza B and dropping significantly in respiratory syncytial virus and adenovirus cases. Adenovirus produced the most pronounced lymphopenia, recording a median lymphocyte count of only 1.89 times 10 to the ninth cells per liter. Conversely, absolute neutrophil counts were significantly heightened in influenza B, respiratory syncytial virus, and adenovirus infections, whereas absolute monocyte counts were uniquely elevated in both influenza A and influenza B cohorts. Composite inflammatory indices mathematically derived from peripheral blood counts mirrored these underlying immunological alterations, displaying characteristic gradients across the distinct viral groups. In pediatric patients diagnosed with influenza A, the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, derived neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio, and systemic immune-inflammation index were significantly lower than those observed in negative controls, driven by robust relative lymphocytosis. Conversely, influenza B, respiratory syncytial virus, and adenovirus cohorts exhibited marked elevations across the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio, derived neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, systemic immune-inflammation index, systemic inflammation response index, and aggregate index of systemic inflammation. These composite elevations reached their peak values within the adenovirus cohort, which demonstrated a median systemic immune-inflammation index of 603.86 compared to 319.66 in influenza A and 375.56 in negative controls. Although systemic inflammation response index and aggregate index of systemic inflammation were elevated in influenza B, respiratory syncytial virus, and adenovirus relative to controls, they showed no significant differences among those three viral groups. These divergent cellular patterns directly reflect established biological mechanisms governing viral immunopathogenesis and host inflammatory signaling cascades. Early influenza A infection stimulates an intense type I interferon response, activating and mobilizing CD8-positive cytotoxic T lymphocytes and natural killer cells, which accounts for the prominent adaptive lymphocytosis and low neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio observed in uncomplicated pediatric cases. In respiratory syncytial virus infection, airway epithelial cells release massive quantities of potent neutrophil chemoattractants, including interleukin-8 and CXCL1, via signaling pathways that recruit vast numbers of bone marrow neutrophils to pulmonary tissues where their apoptosis is delayed. In adenovirus infection, severe lymphopenia arises because the double-stranded DNA virus deploys specialized immune evasion proteins, such as early region 1A, virus-associated RNA, and early region 3 products, which systematically inhibit host lymphocyte proliferation, suppress interferon-stimulated gene pathways, block antigen presentation through major histocompatibility complex class I molecules, and induce apoptotic destruction of circulating host immune cells. Multivariable logistic regression models adjusting for patient age and sex further clarified which hematological markers represent stable, independent indicators for each specific viral illness. For influenza A, independent risk factors included female sex, older age, and absolute monocyte count, with each unit rise in monocytes conferring an 80.1 percent increase in infection odds, while platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio and systemic inflammation response index functioned as protective factors. For influenza B, absolute monocyte count emerged as the sole stable independent risk factor, increasing infection odds by 82.7 percent per unit increase, although monocyte levels could not differentiate influenza A from influenza B. In respiratory syncytial virus cases, monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio and derived neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio operated as independent risk factors, whereas increasing age and systemic inflammation response index were independently protective. For adenovirus, multivariable regression identified absolute lymphocyte count as the strongest independent protective factor, with each unit increase reducing infection odds by 43.3 percent, demonstrating that composite ratio elevations in adenovirus are primarily driven by severe lymphocyte depletion rather than numerator shifts. Diagnostic accuracy assessments using receiver operating characteristic curve analyses revealed that isolated complete blood count indices possess limited capacity to discriminate among specific viral respiratory infections when deployed in isolation. Single-indicator area under the curve values ranged from 0.494 to 0.691 across the entire cohort. For influenza A, single-parameter values ranged from 0.494 for systemic inflammation response index to 0.583 for platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio, while influenza B single-indicator values peaked at 0.574 for absolute monocyte count. In respiratory syncytial virus, patient age achieved the highest single discriminatory value with an area under the curve of 0.691, followed by derived neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio at 0.622, while absolute lymphocyte count yielded the highest individual discriminatory value for adenovirus at 0.662. These constrained single-parameter metrics reinforce prior clinical observations that no isolated leukocyte count or inflammatory ratio possesses adequate diagnostic sensitivity or specificity to guide decisive therapeutic decisions in routine pediatric practice. To overcome the diagnostic constraints of isolated cellular measurements, the investigators constructed combined multivariable prediction models incorporating patient demographic characteristics alongside multiple complete blood count indices for each target pathogen. These composite models substantially improved diagnostic discrimination across all evaluated respiratory viruses, generating overall area under the curve values between 0.612 and 0.712. The combined model achieved an area under the curve of 0.622 for influenza A, with a sensitivity of 62.6 percent and specificity of 58.3 percent, and an area under the curve of 0.612 for influenza B, yielding a sensitivity of 80.6 percent and specificity of 37.0 percent. The highest discriminatory capabilities were achieved for respiratory syncytial virus, which reached an area under the curve of 0.712 with a sensitivity of 88.3 percent and specificity of 49.4 percent, and adenovirus, which reached an area under the curve of 0.694 with a sensitivity of 77.1 percent and specificity of 51.5 percent. Based on their empirical findings, the authors outlined preliminary clinical considerations to assist pediatricians in evaluating acute respiratory presentations prior to obtaining confirmatory molecular testing. A clinical presentation featuring fever, elevated absolute lymphocyte count exceeding 3.0 times 10 to the ninth cells per liter, and a low neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio below 1.2 should heighten clinical suspicion for influenza A, warranting rapid confirmatory testing and consideration of timely antiviral therapy. Conversely, young children under three years of age presenting with an elevated neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio exceeding 1.5 and reduced lymphocyte counts below 2.5 times 10 to the ninth cells per liter should raise suspicion for respiratory syncytial virus, prompting close clinical monitoring for signs of impending bronchiolitis, respiratory distress, and hypoxemia. In patients presenting with high fever, marked lymphopenia below 2.0 times 10 to the ninth cells per liter, elevated neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio above 1.8, and platelet counts under 280 times 10 to the ninth cells per liter, clinicians should maintain vigilance for adenovirus and assess pulmonary imaging and hepatic function, recognizing that lymphopenia correlates with severe clinical progression. The study authors highlighted several methodological limitations that necessitate careful interpretation of these hematological associations. As a single-center retrospective analysis, the investigation is inherently susceptible to selection and information biases, and the clinical records lacked standardized documentation regarding pre-admission antibiotic or antipyretic administration. Furthermore, bacterial cultures were not universally performed across the cohort, meaning the control population may have harbored unmeasured bacterial infections or alternative respiratory viruses such as parainfluenza, human metapneumovirus, or seasonal coronaviruses. The cross-sectional complete blood count models were not internally cross-validated or adjusted for traditional acute-phase reactants like C-reactive protein and procalcitonin, nor did the study track downstream clinical severity metrics such as intensive care admission, duration of hospitalization, or supplemental oxygen requirements. Consequently, the investigators emphasized that while complete blood count parameters and derived inflammatory ratios provide valuable, cost-effective adjunctive clues for risk stratification, they cannot replace definitive pathogen-specific nucleic acid polymerase chain reaction testing in clinical practice.
