Predicting Measles Vaccination Defaulting in Uganda and Kenya: Comparative Evaluation of Custom Soft Voting and TabNet Stacked Ensemble Architectures

Authors: Bartha Alexandra Nantongo, Josephine Nabukenya, Peter Nabende
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Measles is a major cause of death among infants despite free routine immunization (RI) in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Vaccination defaulting contributes to measles outbreak occurrences. Conventional immunization defaulter identification methods are expensive to implement, prone to data falsification, reactive, and overestimate uptake. Several machine learning studies leveraged the use of child and maternal characteristics as predictors of immunization defaulting, overlooking community, demographic, and socioeconomic factors. Similarly, there is limited research regarding the use of interpretable deep learning and advanced ensemble methods to improve model performance in predicting RI defaulters and establishing significant predictors at the national level in the LMIC context, like Uganda. Objective This study predicts infants at a risk of defaulting on measles vaccination in Uganda and Kenya using advanced ensemble learning architectures. Methods The study benchmarked classical and deep learning models using Demographic and Health Survey (DHS) datasets (2016-2022) with child, maternal, community, social-economic, and demographic characteristics as predictors. The robust modeling pipeline began with feature selection according to the literature and the expert domain. It was followed by vaccination eligibility, missing value handling, and feature elimination using the phi-k correlation framework. To mitigate class imbalance, synthetic minority over-sampling technique for nominal continuous features was used in a training pipeline after the RobustScaler. Nine baseline classifiers, including Tabular Network (TabNet), multilayer perceptron (MLP), random forest (RF), gradient boosting (GB), adaptive-boosting (AB), among others, underwent stratified k-fold cross-validation and hyperparameter optimization via grid search. Custom soft voting was evaluated alongside TabNet stack architecture for superior ensemble prediction. Models were evaluated for accuracy, precision, recall, and F 1 -score. Model explainability was decoded using Shapley additive explanations (SHAP), while Streamlit and SQLite formed the tool architecture. Results The proposed TabNet+GB+RF custom soft voting achieved significant, nonrandom performance over tuned base models with an F 1 -score of 0.7111, area under the curve (AUC) of 0.8847 for Kenya. Conversely, for Uganda, the TabNet hybrid ensemble (GB+RF+ AB→TabNet) was the best with AUC of 0.8862 and F 1 -score of 0.6756, which was statistically insignificant. SHAP identified child’s age and vitamin A1 as universal key predictors in both countries; others varied. In Kenya, time to water source, respondents’ education, district lived previously, and current district were the most influential global features, respectively. In Uganda, Polio inactive, Pentavalent3, current district, and respondents’ education were the most influential factors to defaulting, respectively. Conclusions The proposed advanced ensemble learning models improved the classification of measles vaccine defaulters compared to individual models. The study recommends distinct country-tailored strategies. In Kenya, outreaches are recommended to areas with low literacy, distant water sources, and mobile populations. Conversely, in Uganda, the study recommends reminders to avoid missing preceding vaccines and outreaches to high-default districts.

Rapid Differentiation of Vaccine and Wild-Type Measles Virus During an Ongoing Outbreak using a new molecular assay

Authors: Efrat Dahan Bucris, Tal Levin, Roberto Azar, Batya Mannasse, Hadar Assraf, Vardit Moshayoff, Roaa Shatrubi, Yara Kanaaneh, Victoria Indenbaum, Neta S. Zuckerman
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Differentiating wild and vaccine measles virus (MeV) strains is critical for appropriate healthcare response during outbreak vaccination campaigns. Sequencing, the current standard, is expensive, time-consuming, and limited to specialized laboratories. We developed and implemented a novel differential MeV RT-qPCR assay to address these challenges, which we describe herein. Methods We designed a differential two-step assay; the first step, termed MRRV, distinguishes between rubella, wild MeV, and vaccine MeV. The second, termed “MeV differential”, differentiates between genotypes A (vaccine), B and D. Results The assay detects 10-50 copies per reaction and accurately classifies weak clinical samples (Cq > 32), with 100% accuracy, compared with the N gene sequencing gold standard. Evaluated on over 4,000 clinical samples retrospectively and during the 2025-2026MeV outbreak in Israel, the assay revealed that ∼9% of tested originated from vaccine strain. Among tested municipalities, those running active vaccination campaigns had significantly higher vaccine strain detections (>10 cases each) than those with routine vaccination (1-2 cases). Retrospective analysis of samples from 2018-2024 identified a mix of B and D strains. The novel assay successfully identified double infection cases (exposure to a wild strain shortly before or after vaccination). Conclusion The novel assay proved useful both as a diagnostic tool that enables focused, efficient healthcare response, and as an epidemiological tool that provides valuable information in real-time. This assay is faster, cheaper and more amendable for high throughput testing, compared with the gold-standard sequencing classification.

Authors: Adam H. Boldt, Sarah J. Boldt, Megan L. Donahue
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Objective To evaluate changes in measles-containing vaccine (MCV) uptake, inclusive of MMR and MMRV, by age group and over time during the 2025 Texas measles outbreak. Methods We conducted a retrospective, single-center, observational study comparing January through August 2024 (pre-outbreak) with the same period in 2025 (outbreak) at a military medical center in San Antonio, TX. The study evaluated MCVs administered and recorded in the electronic health record (EHR) to all patients from 0-18 years enrolled during these periods. Original abstract (English)
Globally, measles is re-emerging in countries that have previously achieved elimination, including the United States. As immunization rates decline, characterizing the dynamics and evolving spatial pattern of measles re-emergence is critical to inform the public health response. We analyze annual county-level measles cases and vaccine coverage data in the United States (US) from 2011-2025 and find a shift in measles epidemiology in recent years indicative of re-emergence. Original abstract (English)
Integrated serosurveillance (ISS), using multiplex bead assays to measure antibodies to multiple pathogens, can provide insights into population immunity and pathogen exposure. We estimated seroprevalence for neglected tropical diseases (NTDs), arboviruses, vaccine-preventable diseases, and enteric protozoa in Niue through a cross-sectional survey embedded within a World Health Organization (WHO) STEPwise survey from 24 February to 4 April 2025. Dried blood spots from participants aged ≥ 3 years were tested for 26 pathogen-specific antibodies. Among 750 participants (median age 37 years; range 3–93; 53% female), NTD seroprevalence was low (0% for yaws [among 165 participants aged ≤ 14 years] to 2.7% for cysticercosis). Arbovirus seroprevalence ranged from 3.1% (Zika) to 35.1% (dengue serotype 4). Dengue seropositivity increased with age ( p < 0.05). Measles and rubella seroprevalence exceeded 98%, while diphtheria and tetanus seropositivity was lower in older age groups ( p ≤ 0.03). Cryptosporidium seroprevalence (68.7%) increased with age ( p < 0.001), while Giardia seroprevalence was low (4.1%). Findings demonstrate the utility of ISS to generate policy-relevant surveillance data, although interpretation is limited by the cross-sectional design and non-probability sampling.

Determinants of Delayed and Missed Childhood Immunization Among Mothers Attending Primary Healthcare Centers in Al-Diwaniyah City, Iraq: A Cross-Sectional Study

Authors: Adel Jabbar Hussein, Suzan Sabbar Mutlag
Original abstract (English)
Background: Childhood immunization is a cost-effective public health intervention; however, delayed and missed vaccinations remain significant challenges. This study aimed to determine the prevalence and key determinants of childhood immunization non-compliance among mothers attending primary healthcare centers (PHCs). Methods: A descriptive cross-sectional study was conducted among 300 mothers of children aged 0–24 months attending PHCs. Data were collected using structured, interviewer-administered questionnaires and verified through immunization cards. Non-compliance was defined as a delay of ≥4 weeks beyond the scheduled date or omission of a due vaccine dose. Binary logistic regression was performed using SPSS version 26 to identify independent predictors. Results: Immunization non-compliance was observed in 49.7% of children (n=149). The highest non-compliance rates were reported for IPV (35.7%), measles-rubella vaccine (34.3%), and the third dose of pentavalent vaccine (25.3%). Major barriers included child illness on vaccination day (45.0%), caregiver forgetfulness (38.9%), and fear of adverse effects (32.9%). ; ; M; Primary healthcare; Vaccine hesitancy; Immunization barriers; Cross-sectional study.

Question and background
The study aimed to assess the therapeutic potential of oncolytic viruses (OVs) in hematopoietic malignancies, addressing the limitations of current treatments like autologous stem cell transplantation and chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy. The research sought to evaluate the efficacy and safety profiles of OVs targeting hematological cancers, including lymphomas, leukemias, and multiple myeloma, through preclinical and early-phase clinical trials.

Original abstract (English)
Vaccination across the life course is a cornerstone of preventive health care for girls and women. Although WHO recommends completion of essential vaccines by adolescence, global coverage remains suboptimal. Vaccine-preventable diseases-including HPV-related cancers, vaginal, vulvar, anal, oropharyngeal, influenza, pertussis, hepatitis B, herpes zoster, and pneumococcal disease-continue to impose a disproportionate burden on girls and women, particularly in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) and migrant populations. Key life stages, such as childhood (early and middle), adolescence, preconception, pregnancy, postpartum, midlife, and beyond, offer critical opportunities for immunization. Obstetricians, gynecologists, midwives, and other women's healthcare providers are uniquely positioned to integrate vaccination into routine care in all settings and influence vaccine acceptance. Strengthening vaccine advocacy within women's health care is essential to reduce preventable morbidity and mortality and advance health equity for girls and women globally. This paper presents evidence-based recommendations for equitable vaccination across the life course of girls and women, synthesizing guidance from WHO, the Strategic Advisory Group of Experts on Immunization (SAGE), the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), and national immunization technical advisory groups. Vaccines reviewed include HPV, tetanus-diphtheria-pertussis (Tdap), influenza, hepatitis B, measles-mumps-rubella (MMR), varicella, herpes zoster, pneumococcal, Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV), and COVID-19. A life-course framework is proposed, emphasizing maternal immunization, cervical cancer prevention, and integration of regular and opportune vaccination into routine obstetric and gynecologic services. Practical implementation strategies and policy recommendations are outlined to support FIGO member societies.

Original abstract (English)
Multiple sclerosis (MS) is a complex, multifactorial neurodegenerative disease with an aetiology that is still partially elusive. Several risk factors are associated with MS development including genetic predisposition and various environmental stressors; however, recently, viral infections have gained significant attention as a crucial environmental trigger. Despite the well-established link between Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) and MS susceptibility, a single-pathogen model fails to clearly characterise the molecular cascades driving the disease, suggesting that the transition from infection to autoimmune response involves more than one viral agent. In this review we examine the speculation that the interactions among different neurotropic viruses could serve a collective causative role in MS, which is under-researched in the literature at present. Through interplay within shared molecular pathways, immune modulation, and activation of latent infections, these viruses may collectively influence the inflammatory and demyelinating processes that characterise MS. This review evaluates the etiological role of viral interplay in MS through the cumulative and interactive effects of these pathogens. The available epidemiological, immunological, and experimental data converge on the concept that viral co-exposures act synergistically to shape MS susceptibility in genetically predisposed individuals. Characterising these viral interactions presents a new research direction for the identification of novel biomarkers and the development of preventative strategies through the targeted control of viral networks.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract Oncolytic virotherapy has emerged as a promising cancer treatment that combines selective tumor destruction with potent stimulation of antitumor immunity. Unlike conventional therapies, oncolytic viruses (OVs) preferentially infect and replicate within malignant cells while sparing normal tissues, leading to direct tumor lysis and the release of tumor-associated antigens, pathogen-associated molecular patterns, and damage-associated molecular patterns that activate both innate and adaptive immune responses. Advances in molecular virology and genetic engineering have transformed naturally occurring viruses into programmable therapeutic platforms capable of delivering immunomodulatory payloads that enhance antitumor efficacy and remodel the tumor microenvironment. Consequently, oncolytic virotherapy has become an attractive component of combination strategies involving immunotherapy, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapies, and cellular therapies. Nevertheless, several challenges continue to limit its broader clinical application, including antiviral immune clearance, inefficient systemic delivery, restricted intratumoral spread, tumor heterogeneity, manufacturing complexity, and the lack of robust predictive biomarkers. It further summarizes the major oncolytic virus platforms, recent preclinical and clinical advances, emerging combination strategies, current translational challenges, and future directions expected to advance precision oncolytic virotherapy and expand its role in modern cancer treatment. Goldsmith; William Bellini, Ph.D./Centers for Disease Control and Prevention/TNS/TNS) By David Mowery | dmowery@readingeagle.com | Reading Eagle PUBLISHED: October 16, 2026 at 4:36 PM EDT Getting your Trinity Audio player ready... Berks County has added a confirmed measles case, bringing the total this year in the county to seven. The case was included in the state Department of Health’s latest update Friday that reported a total of 1,260 cases in 43 counties. The state continues to report five deaths, acknowledging that is investigating a recent death in Franklin County. The Franklin County coroner on Monday reported the measles-associated death of a 39-year-old woman on Oct. 11. State officials in their update said that to be considered measles-associated, the department’s epidemiological definition must meet the following criteria: • The death must occur within 30 days of symptom onset in a person with clinical evidence of a measles infection and a positive laboratory test. • The death must not be due to unrelated causes (e.g., motor vehicle accident). • Additional information, such as whether an individual was exposed to a confirmed measles case prior to death or supporting medical records, is also considered. Officials noted a death occurring more than 30 days after the onset of symptoms would not be counted. The department’s update noted the following: • New positive cases since Oct. 14: 38 • Total counties: 43 • Total cumulative cases: 1,260 • Total hospitalizations: 238 • Total confirmed measles-associated deaths: 5 • Measles cases among vaccinated individuals: 4 (less than 1% of cases) According to the department’s dashboard at pa.gov/measles, Lancaster County remains the leader in cases with 452. Cases in the region: Chester County, 91; Lebanon County, 21; Montgomery County, 3; Schuylkill County, 3; and Lehigh County, 2. State health center staff have administered more than 6,100 measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccinations at 210 pop-up vaccination clinics in affected communities since the outbreak began in late April, officials said. Overall, the department has given nearly 9,300 MMR vaccine doses this year. Officials said health care providers continue to see an increase in residents interested in the MMR vaccinations. Providers typically administer about 25,000 doses of the vaccine a month. They said more than 56,000 doses were given in September and that more than 23,000 MMR vaccines were given in the first two weeks of October. The department continues to advise that the best protection against measles is to get fully vaccinated. The two-dose MMR vaccine boosts a person’s immune system and provides 97% lifetime protection, they said. Because measles continues to spread, the department said it recommends an additional early zero dose for infants between the ages of 6 and 11 months who live in or travel to counties flagged on the measles dashboard for sustained community transmission. They said the early dose does not replace the two routine doses. Children 12 months and older who have not received any dose should get one now and a second dose at least 28 days later. Those who have two documented doses of MMR do not need another, the department said. Adults who have only one dose or cannot find their vaccination records should talk with a health care provider or pharmacist about getting vaccinated. Measles is spread when someone who is infected breathes, coughs or sneezes. The virus remains infectious in the air and on surfaces for up to two hours after the infected person leaves the area, the department said. Symptoms begin with fever, cough, runny nose, and red, watery eyes (conjunctivitis), followed by a rash that starts on the head and spreads downward. Without proper protection or supportive care, measles can be deadly, officials said. Symptoms can start seven to 21 days after exposure. If you believe you were exposed to measles and are experiencing symptoms, you are advised to contact your health care provider or call the health department’s hotline at 877-PA-HEALTH (877-724-3258).
