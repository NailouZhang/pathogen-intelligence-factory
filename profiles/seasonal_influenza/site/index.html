A qualitative study exploring poultry farmers’ barriers and motivations to seasonal human influenza vaccine uptake as a preventive public health measure because of avian influenza
作者： E. J. van den Berg1, E. Warmelink1, T. van Loenen2, K.V.C. Wevers-de Boer1, Berg1 EJvd, Loenen2 Tv

A qualitative study exploring poultry farmers’ barriers and motivations to seasonal human influenza vaccine uptake as a preventive public health measure because of avian influenza

Authors: E. J. van den Berg1, E. Warmelink1, T. van Loenen2, K.V.C. Wevers-de Boer1, Berg1 EJvd, Loenen2 Tv
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background: The global spread of highly pathogenic avian influenza (H5N1) has increased concerns about reassortment between avian and human influenza viruses and the potential emergence of novel pandemic influenza strains. Annual seasonal influenza vaccination can reduce this risk and is recommended by the Dutch government and the World Health Organization (WHO) for risk groups, including poultry farmers. This study explored Dutch poultry farmers’ perceptions regarding this vaccination and identified motivations and barriers influencing their willingness to be vaccinated. Methods: This qualitative study involved semistructured interviews with twelve poultry farmers in the Netherlands. Interviews were conducted online via Microsoft Teams between June and October 2024. The interview guide was based on the attitude‒social influence‒self-efficacy (ASE) model. Interviews were transcribed verbatim and analysed using thematic analysis. Results: Attitudes towards vaccination differed both generally and regarding seasonal influenza vaccination due to avian influenza risk. Approximately half considered vaccination a reasonable preventive measure. The main motivation for vaccination was preventing reassortment between avian and human influenza viruses and thereby reducing the risk of a future pandemic. Participants also viewed vaccination as a sign of societal responsibility and a way to contribute positively to the public image of the poultry sector. Important barriers included diminished trust in the government, perceived regulatory burden within the poultry sector, and perceived limited benefits of vaccination. Participants emphasized that vaccination should remain voluntary. Participants generally perceived their personal risk of avian influenza infection as low, while acknowledging potentially severe consequences for public health if a novel influenza virus were to emerge. Trusted sources of information included veterinarians, general practitioners, poultry advisers, and agricultural organizations. Accessibility was considered essential: local, flexible in timing, and free of charge. Conclusions: Acceptance of seasonal influenza vaccination among poultry farmers is influenced by a combination of societal responsibility, perceived risk, trust in the government, and practical considerations. Vaccination strategies should emphasize the societal benefits of vaccination and the potentially serious public health consequences of the emergence of a novel influenza virus. Improving vaccine uptake among poultry farmers requires engagement of trusted intermediaries and vaccination programs that are accessible and tailored to farmers’ practical needs.

InfluProto: Predicting Cross-Species Transmission of Influenza A Virus via Prototype Learning

Authors: Zongyi Geng, Lun Li, Rongye Ye, Yi Wang, Shuhui Song
Original abstract (English)
Zoonotic influenza A viruses pose constitute a significant persistent threat to global public health, yet quantitative approaches for assessing their cross-species transmission potential remain limited. Here, we present InfluProto, a deep learning model that introduces prototype learning into host prediction for influenza A virus, framing conceptualizing host adaptation as a continuous process rather than a discrete outcome. InfluProto embeds viral sequences into a continuous representation space anchored by human, swine, and avian prototypes, using cosine distances as a quantitative measure of host adaptation. InfluProto accurately discriminated among the three host classes, achieving recall and precision exceeding 0.97, and substantially outperformed conventional classification models models—particularly on challenging zoonotic strains, including the 2009 pandemic H1N1 (pdm09) lineage. Beyond host classification, the learned representations captured biologically meaningful host-adaptive signatures,; notably, with pdm09 viruses occupying occupied an intermediate position between human and swine clusters, consistent with their evolutionary origin. Analysis of simulated reassortment further revealed that the introduction of human–origin segments into swine influenza viruses reduced their distance to the human prototype, indicating that InfluProto can detect reassortment-associated shifts in host adaptation. To facilitate broad public useaccess, we have developed an interactive web service for InfluProto. Collectively, our work establishes a continuous framework for characterizing host adaptation and evaluating zoonotic risk in influenza A viruses, with potential implications for pandemic surveillance and preparedness.

学龄儿童是韩国 季节性流感 流通的主要来源,2022-2025 年

School-age Children as the Dominant Source of Inter-seasonal Influenza Circulation in the Republic of Korea, 2022–2025
作者： Young Kyu Shim, Chang Min Kang, Youn Young Choi, Young Yoo, Yoonsun Yoon, Yun Kyung Kim, Young June Choe
摘要中文翻译背景:流感在夏季跨季节期间低水平传播,可能会弥合连续的流行病。哪些年龄组对残余循环的贡献最大尚不清楚。方法:我们分析了 2022 年至 2025 年(209 周)韩国前哨 监测 系统 (K-SenS) 的每周流感检出率,按 6 个年龄组分层。季节间被定义为流行病学周 21-39。对于每个年龄组,我们计算了平均季节间检测率、剩余分数(季节间率/季节率)、优势周(检测率最高的周)、周率的 lag-1 自相关和归一化的峰值后衰减曲线。结果:季节间期间所有年龄组均检测到流感。7-12 岁儿童 (6.3%) 和 13-18 岁儿童 (5.2%) 的平均跨季节检出率最高。所有年龄段的残余分数相似(19.6%-25.6%)。然而,7-12 岁儿童在活跃的季间周中的检出率最高,占 42%,并且表现出最高的 lag-1 自相关性 (0.44),表明循环是持续的而不是零星的。在季节高峰后 15 周,7-12 岁的儿童保留了大约 55% 的峰值检出率,而 ≥50 岁的成人则低于 10%。结论:学龄儿童,特别是 7-12 岁的儿童,是韩国 季节性流感 间循环的主要来源。他们的主导地位是由高季节性负担驱动的,而不是病毒清除速度较慢。在这个年龄段保持高 疫苗接种 覆盖率可能有助于减少季节性病毒 储存宿主。

Original abstract (English)
The immunogen composition of seasonal influenza vaccines is revised biannually to match the antigenic drift of the hemagglutinin of circulating influenza virus strains. Antibodies directed against the influenza neuraminidase also correlate with protection against influenza, yet the antigenic evolution of influenza neuraminidase remains underexplored. To evaluate the antigenic diversity of N1 neuraminidase, we generated a panel of immune sera in mice directed against 17 N1 neuraminidases derived from human H1N1 strains that were isolated between 2009 and 2020 and determined the neuraminidase inhibition (NAI) titers of these sera against a panel of 14 HxN1 viruses. However, reciprocal substitutions in a recent N1 background did not restore the early antigenic phenotype, indicating a unidirectional effect. This finding suggests that anti-NA responses elicited against early pandemic H1N1 viruses remained effective against circulating N1 variants up to at least 2020.

Question and background
This study aimed to investigate the effects of influenza A virus infection on placental and fetal immune responses in nonhuman primates, addressing the gap in understanding how maternal influenza infection might impact fetal immunity without direct vertical transmission.
Design and population
The study involved pregnant pigtail macaques inoculated with IAV [A/California/07/2009 (H1N1)] and uninfected controls. A total of 11 infected and 16 uninfected pregnant animals were included, with delivery and fetal necropsy performed 5 days postinfection.
Core methods

Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Despite the availability of effective vaccines, seasonal influenza remains a substantial global public health concern. Suboptimal influenza vaccination uptake is found across many populations. Using digital platforms, individuals can self-report health information through participatory surveillance programs. Though they are mainly used for infectious disease monitoring, their potential association with preventive behaviors such as influenza vaccination uptake has not been systematically evaluated. This review is necessary and urgent given the recurrent influenza outbreaks and the severity of illness due to low vaccination rates. OBJECTIVE: This protocol details the design and methodology of a systematic review that will assess whether enrollment in participatory surveillance programs is associated with differences in seasonal influenza vaccination uptake compared with non-participation. METHODS: This systematic review protocol follows the Cochrane guidelines, is reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Protocols (PRISMA-P), and is registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251274024). Eligible studies will include quantitative comparative designs, including randomized controlled trials, as well as observational and quasi-experimental studies with appropriate comparators. The population will comprise individuals eligible for influenza vaccination in settings where participatory surveillance programs are available. The primary outcome is influenza vaccination uptake. Searches will be conducted in PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, Scopus, CABI Global Health, and CINAHL, alongside selected grey literature sources, without restrictions on language, geography, or publication date. Two reviewers will independently screen studies, extract data using Covidence, and assess risk of bias using ROB 2 and ROBINS-I. Where appropriate, data will be synthesized using meta-analysis or narrative methods. Heterogeneity will be described using I2, and certainty assessed using GRADE. CONCLUSIONS: This systematic review will provide an urgently needed summary of evidence regarding the association between participatory surveillance enrollment and influenza vaccination uptake. Findings will inform future research and the development of digital public health strategies that integrate disease surveillance and health promotion.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Estimates of the healthcare costs associated with influenza treatment are essential for cost-effectiveness analyses that inform preventative and treatment strategies such as vaccination and non-pharmaceutical interventions. Existing evidence varies across treatment settings, age groups, and countries and there is little data from low- and middle-income settings. Methods We conducted an umbrella review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses reporting direct admitted and non-admitted hospitalisation costs of seasonal influenza treatment in children, adults, and older people. We supplemented an evidence gap with a systematised review of studies reporting adult non-admitted treatment costs. Costs were extracted and adjusted to 2023 USD. We fitted a Bayesian mixed-effects model to estimate influenza treatment costs using national economic indicators, age group, and treatment setting as a priori predictors. We predicted admitted and non-admitted treatment costs for children, adults, and older people in 195 countries and territories. Results Seven reviews were included in the umbrella review, supplemented by 17 primary studies for adult non-admitted care. Extracted data points most frequently described admitted treatment costs and came from high-income countries. The best-fitting model used healthcare expenditure per capita to predict treatment costs, with country-level random effects. Estimated costs increased with healthcare expenditure per capita, were higher for admitted than non-admitted care, and were highest for adults and lowest for children. Conclusions This study provides novel estimates of influenza-associated healthcare costs across age groups and treatment settings by synthesising the available global evidence. By extrapolating to settings without data, our estimates can inform future economic evaluation studies of influenza interventions worldwide.

Authors: Katrina Dionne, Étienne Bélanger, Mehdi Benlarbi, Guillaume Beaudoin-Bussières, Claudia Santos Saenz, Shi-Lei Ding, Catherine Bourassa, Halima Medjahed, Morgan R. Jennings, Geneviève Laroche
Original abstract (English)
UNLABELLED: A rise in cases of highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) clade 2.3.4.4b in humans has been reported since 2023. Although influenza seasonal vaccination (H1N1/H3N2) was shown to be effective against cases of reinfection with endemic influenza A viruses (IAVs), its capacity to elicit cross-reactive antibodies against (H5N1) remains unclear. Here, we measured vaccine-elicited antibody responses against seasonal (H1N1/H3N2) and various avian subtypes A (H5Nx) using plasma samples from the CRCHUM Health Care Workers cohort in Montreal, Canada, during the 2024 influenza seasonal vaccination. Here, following influenza seasonal vaccination in a cohort of adults, we report highly heterogeneous serological responses, possibly due to pre-existing immunity and HA sequence similarity. Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Matrix metalloproteinase‐8 (MMP‐8) regulates inflammatory responses, but its contributions to the pathogenesis of influenza A viral (IAV) disease are not known. Plasma MMP‐8 levels were increased 30‐fold in subjects infected with H1N1 or seasonal IAV and were inversely associated with the PaO 2 /FiO 2 ratio, suggesting potential value as a candidate marker of lung injury severity that requires validation in larger cohorts. MMP‐8 levels were elevated in the lungs of H1N1‐infected wild‐type (WT) mice and localized to airway epithelial cells, macrophages, and polymorphonuclear neutrophils (PMNs). H1N1‐infected Mmp‐8 −/− mice exhibited lower mortality and reduced lung viral burdens, but developed more severe lung injury than WT mice. Gene expression profiling revealed enhanced necroptosis and an exaggerated immune response in the lungs of infected Mmp‐8 −/− mice. Infected Mmp‐8 −/− mice had increased airway epithelial necroptosis with higher pulmonary levels of Type I interferons (IFNs), and increased frequencies of Class II MHC molecule‐expressing macrophages and CD4 + and CD8 + T cells, and higher levels of some Th1 cytokines. In vitro, MMP‐8 cleaved IL‐1β and IL‐6, but not Type I IFNs. These findings suggest that Mmp‐8 deficiency is associated with restricted influenza virus spread and increased lung injury, accompanied by enhanced Type I IFN‐associated necroptotic signaling and augmented antiviral immune responses. The latter may partly involve loss of MMP‐8‐mediated processing of pro‐inflammatory mediators. MMP‐8 may represent a host‐response target in severe influenza infection, although therapeutic strategies targeting MMP‐8 will need to balance enhanced viral clearance against the risk of increased lung immunopathology.

Authors: Francesca Fortunato, Angelo Campanozzi, Angelo Acquafredda, Matteo Mariano, Valeria Delli Carri, Tiziana Rollo, Loris Micelli, Dalila Tedeschi, Rosa Prato, Domenico Martinelli
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Seasonal influenza is an important cause of hospitalization in young children, yet real-world evidence on vaccine effectiveness (VE) against severe outcomes remains limited in Italy. We estimated influenza VE against influenza-associated hospitalization among children aged 6-72 months in the district of Foggia, Southern Italy. METHODS: We conducted a test-negative case-control study among children hospitalized for acute respiratory infection and tested for influenza during the 2022-2023 to 2025-2026 influenza seasons. Cases were influenza-positive children and controls were influenza-negative children. VE was estimated as (1 - odds ratio) x 100; adjusted VE was derived from multivariable logistic regression models. RESULTS: The study included 935 children, of whom 207 were influenza-positive cases and 728 were influenza-negative controls. Adjusted VE was 62.5% (95% CI: 34.4%-78.5%) for receipt of at least one influenza vaccine dose and 61.9% (95% CI: 29.3%-79.4%) for full vaccination. By vaccine type, adjusted VE was 80.3% (95% CI: 43.8%-93.1%) for at least one dose of LAIV and 50.9% (95% CI: 6.9%-74.2%) for at least one dose of IIV. Season-specific adjusted VE was highest in 2024-2025, reaching 92.2% (95% CI: 65.6%-98.2%) for at least one dose and 90.7% (95% CI: 58.5%-97.9%) for full vaccination. CONCLUSIONS: Influenza vaccination was associated with a reduced risk of influenza-associated hospitalization among children aged 6-72 months, with the strongest protection observed during the 2024-2025 season. These findings support continued efforts to improve pediatric influenza vaccination uptake.

Economic analysis of adjuvanted trivalent influenza vaccine for older adults in the Korean National Immunization Program

Authors: Eunha Shim, Byung Wook Eun, Won Suk Choi
Original abstract (English)
Background Seasonal influenza causes substantial morbidity and mortality among older adults in South Korea despite high vaccination coverage. The MF59-adjuvanted trivalent influenza vaccine (aTIV) has shown superior immunogenicity in older populations, but its economic value within the Korean National Immunization Program (NIP) has not been evaluated using a dynamic transmission model that captures indirect protection. This study evaluated the budget impact of introducing aTIV into the Korean NIP for adults aged ≥65 years. Methods We developed an age-structured dynamic compartmental model to simulate seasonal influenza transmission in South Korea, allowing estimation of both direct and indirect protection effects. From the healthcare payer perspective and over a single influenza season, we performed a budget impact analysis complemented by a cost–benefit comparison of avoided medical costs against incremental vaccination expenditures. Two aTIV strategies were compared against standard quadrivalent influenza vaccine (QIV) within the NIP framework: Strategy I (aTIV for adults aged ≥75 years) and Strategy II (aTIV for all adults aged ≥65 years). Deterministic one-way sensitivity analyses examined relative vaccine effectiveness (rVE) of aTIV versus QIV across 5–30% and seasonal attack rate across ±20% of the 8.0% baseline, and a break-even analysis identified the rVE threshold at which incremental costs equal avoided treatment costs. A probabilistic sensitivity analysis was also conducted to quantify joint parameter uncertainty. Results At baseline (rVE 25%, attack rate 8.0%), Strategy I required $24.6 million in additional vaccine investment, with 52% offset by treatment savings, yielding a net budget impact of $11.7 million (+0.9% of total influenza-related healthcare expenditure). Strategy II required $54.4 million in additional costs with 57% offset ($31.2 million in savings), resulting in a net impact of $23.2 million (+1.8%). Strategy II prevented 535 deaths and over 15,000 hospitalizations—2.2-fold greater than Strategy I. The incremental cost of aTIV decreased with increasing transmission intensity, approaching cost neutrality during severe epidemic seasons. The break-even rVE at which aTIV becomes cost-saving was 44%, above central meta-analytic estimates (10–25%) but potentially attainable in severe seasons or specific subpopulations. Conclusion Introduction of aTIV into the Korean NIP is budget-feasible with substantial health benefits, requiring only a 0.9–1.8% increase in total influenza-related healthcare expenditure. At current rVE estimates (≤30%), aTIV does not achieve cost savings, but the additional fiscal burden remains modest. Strategy II offers superior health outcome value and is preferred when budget permits, while Strategy I provides a fiscally conservative entry point. The value proposition of aTIV is most favorable during severe influenza seasons.

Original abstract (English)
Influenza viruses continuously evolve, resulting in genetic diversity that contributes to influenza epidemics and pandemics. This high mutation rate increases the probability of immune evasion, thereby reducing vaccine effectiveness. This study aimed to investigate the genomic and molecular changes in influenza A/H1N1 viruses during 2023–2025. In this study, whole-genome sequencing (WGS) of 30 influenza A(H1N1)pdm09 strains circulating in Iran during the 2023–2024 and 2024–2025 seasons was performed using Oxford Nanopore Technology. Subsequently, genomic, molecular, and phylogenetic analyses were performed, and vaccine efficacy was estimated using the epitope method. Whole-genome sequencing of influenza A/H1N1 viruses revealed that the strains circulating during the 2024–2025 season had a slightly lower similarity to the vaccine strain (98.78%) than those circulating during the 2023–2024 season (99.13%). Epitope A was the most diverse region during both seasons, with estimated vaccine efficacy against the dominant epitope ranging from 38.1% to 50.5% during 2023–2025. Several substitutions were identified in the internal proteins, of which the virulence-associated I123V substitution in NS1 was detected in both the vaccine and analyzed strains. Moreover, the studied strains remained largely susceptible to neuraminidase inhibitors, although one strain carried the S247N substitution. Phylogenetic analysis revealed that 90% of Iranian strains clustered within the 6B.1 A.5a.2a clade, which included globally circulating viruses from the same seasons. Despite topological differences among the phylogenetic trees, no reassortment was detected. The epitope landscape of Iranian A(H1N1)pdm09 viruses circulating during the 2023–2024 and 2024–2025 influenza seasons showed that their genetic makeup underwent seasonal changes due to antigenic drift in HA and NA. Despite these changes, the circulating strains remained closely related to the corresponding vaccine strain, and the estimated vaccine efficacy against the epitopes with the highest number of substitutions was acceptable, consistent with the WHO recommendation to retain A/Wisconsin/67/2022 for the 2025–2026 seasonal vaccine. This study emphasizes the importance of national influenza surveillance within global frameworks to monitor antigenic changes, assess pandemic risks, guide vaccine strain selection, and evaluate antiviral resistance.

Influenza vaccine protection and contact patterns among outpatients with influenza-like illness during the 2024-2025 season in China: a test-negative case-control study.

Authors: Luping Chen, Jia Mi, Yingxuan Wang, Zhisheng Chen, Juping Wang, Zihao Guo, Haogao Gu, Qiaoge Chi, Lirong Cao, Ruijuan Ji
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Continuous monitoring of both influenza vaccine protection and behavioural determinants of infection is important for understanding related risks. Evidence on influenza vaccine effectiveness (VE) in China during the 2024-2025 season, however, remains limited. We aimed to assess influenza VE among healthcare-seeking outpatients with influenza-like illness (ILI) and to examine the contact patterns associated with infection risk. METHODS: We enrolled 928 outpatients with ILI from three sentinel hospitals in Ili Kazakh Autonomous Prefecture, China, between December 2024 and March 2025 in a test-negative case-control study. We confirmed influenza infection using reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction and collected vaccination history and social contact frequency and duration through questionnaires. We estimated VE using logistic regression with covariate adjustment, and otherwise analysed social contact patterns and performed exploratory sequencing of six randomly selected influenza-positive samples. RESULTS: Our full sample comprised 928 participants. We excluded 21 infants younger than 6 months from the VE analysis and included 907 participants, of whom 121 tested positive and 786 tested negative. The overall adjusted VE against medically attended influenza was 42.3% (95% credible interval = 11.1-62.5). In the analysis of social contacts for all 928 participants, test-positive children aged 0-10 years and adults aged 65 years or older reported more frequent and longer social contact than their test-negative counterparts. Exploratory sequencing of six samples suggested no major antigenic mismatch with the vaccine strains, but the very small number of sequenced samples limits the generalisability of this finding. CONCLUSIONS: Influenza vaccination provided moderate protection against medically attended influenza, while contact patterns were associated with infection risk among children and older adults. Combined vaccination and behavioural interventions warrant further evaluation, as the observational design of this study and its use of self-reporting data limit causal inference.

Authors: Vishnuga Raveendran, Claire Thorne, Charlotte Jackson, Rashmi D’Souza, Pia Hardelid, Rashmi D'Souza
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Post-viral sequelae following the COVID-19 pandemic attention to post-viral fatigue and long COVID, but the impact on children and young people's (CYP) primary care use is unknown. AIM: To examine temporal, seasonal, and sociodemographic patterns in primary care consultations for long COVID and PVFS in CYP DESIGN AND SETTINGS: We conducted a retrospective, population-based dynamic cohort study using Clinical Practice Research Datalink Aurum data for CYP aged <18 years in England, between 1st January 2006 and 31st December 2022 (from 1st March 2020 for long COVID); a period covering the influenza A(H1N1)pdm09 and COVID-19 pandemics. METHOD: Consultation rates per 100 000 child-years were calculated for long COVID and post-viral fatigue syndrome (PVFS) by calendar year, month, and sociodemographic characteristics. Trends, seasonal patterns, and differences between pandemic and non-pandemic periods (for PVFS) were described. Negative binomial regression was used to estimate associations with sociodemographic characteristics. RESULTS: The cohort included 7,442,572 CYP (3 624 692 registered in primary care during the COVID-19 pandemic). There were 6700 long COVID and 20,440 PVFS consultations. Long COVID consultation rates were highest in 2022 (140.3 per 100,00 child-years). PVFS consultation rates peaked in 2009 and 2022 (during H1N1 and COVID pandemics) with rates of 57.1 and 56.9 per 100 000 child-years, respectively. Consultation rates were highest among older CYP, girls, and those from least deprived areas. CONCLUSION: Long COVID consultations were highest in 2022, and PVFS consultation rates peaked during 2009 and 2022. Our findings that rates were highest in less deprived areas and girls need further investigation.

The study aimed to address the lack of understanding about the impact of post-viral sequelae, specifically long COVID and post-viral fatigue syndrome (PVFS), on children and young people's (CYP) primary care use.

A retrospective, population-based dynamic cohort study was conducted using Clinical Practice Research Datalink Aurum data for CYP aged <18 years in England, between 1st January 2006 and 31st December 2022, with a focus on the period from 1st March 2020 onwards for long COVID.

Editorial: Measuring vaccination perception, confidence, and uptake: advancing instrument development and validation

Authors: Divya S. Subramaniam, Aida Bianco
Original abstract (English)
Two contributions in this Research Topic directly advance the measurement science that motivated this Topic. ground the development of a new general measure of adult vaccine hesitancy for U.S. adults in the 3C framework, reporting content and face validity findings, including a content validity index derived from expert panel review, for an instrument intended to capture hesitancy across vaccine types rather than a single indication. report the development and psychometric evaluation of the Vaccine Hesitancy and Perception Scale toward COVID-19 vaccination (VHAPS-CV19), employing principal axis factoring with varimax rotation to derive a two-factor structure that distinguishes a hesitancy factor (confidence, complacency, and convenience) from a perception-and-trust factor (perceived benefit, barrier, susceptibility, severity, and trust), together with internal consistency estimates for each subscale. Together, the two studies trace the stages that valid measurement of a construct this complex demands, from item generation and content validity through factor structure and reliability testing, and they leave the field with two complementary tools, one general-purpose and one vaccine-specific, for future validation and cross-cultural adaptation work.Several contributions in this Topic examine hesitancy and uptake among healthcare workers, a population whose own vaccination behavior and patient communication directly shape public confidence. investigated uptake, willingness, and practices toward seasonal influenza vaccination among healthcare workers in China using a cross-sectional design. similarly employed a cross-sectional survey to explore attitudes toward COVID-19 vaccination among healthcare workers at a national infectious disease center in Singapore, while Aksoy et al. surveyed community pharmacists in Türkiye regarding their knowledge, attitudes, and barriers toward vaccination, extending the focus to frontline advocates outside the hospital setting. took a distinct, qualitative approach, interviewing healthcare workers at a London hospital to identify barriers and facilitators to measles vaccination uptake; this design complements the quantitative work elsewhere in the Topic, surfacing workplace, informational, and interpersonal factors that structured surveys tend to miss.A second cluster of contributions addresses hesitancy and uptake determinants in community and general-population samples, extending the scope of this Topic beyond influenza and COVID-19 to additional vaccine types and life stages. Rounding out the collection, Ali et al. provide a narrative review of enablers and barriers to influenza vaccine uptake among healthcare workers in Gulf Cooperation Council countries, synthesizing evidence from a regional context that is comparatively underrepresented in the vaccine hesitancy literature relative to North America, Europe, and East Asia. contribute a distinct but complementary perspective, presenting a multivariate analysis of risk factors for adverse events following rabies vaccination using real-world data. This work centers on vaccine safety rather than perception, but it is a reminder that confidence in vaccination rests on lived experience with tolerability as much as on attitudes and access, and that perception-focused instruments are best read alongside pharmacovigilance data.A few patterns stand out across this collection (Figure 1; Table 1). Geographically, contributions concentrate in China (six studies), with additional representation from the United States, Singapore, Türkiye, Vietnam, the United Kingdom, and the Gulf Cooperation Council region; populations in Sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and Latin America are absent, which limits how far these findings generalize and points to a direction for future Research Topics. Methodologically, most contributions rely on cross-sectional, self-report surveys. These designs are useful for generating hypotheses about correlates of hesitancy, but they cannot establish temporal precedence between attitude and behavior, and they remain vulnerable to social desirability and recall bias. Only one contribution employed qualitative interview methods, and none employed a longitudinal or experimental design; this concentration of designs mirrors the broader hesitancy literature and marks a gap that future work should close.Taken together, the contributions to this Research Topic make clear that vaccine decision-making has many layers. Hesitancy and confidence are shaped at once by individual psychology, professional role, cultural context, service accessibility, and direct experience with vaccine safety. The instrument development studies respond to a real methodological gap: the field needs measurement tools that are psychometrically sound and adaptable across populations. The determinant studies argue the opposite case for context. Findings from healthcare workers in the Gulf region, older adults in western China, and pharmacists in Türkiye are not interchangeable; each offers insight for the setting studied rather than a universal account of vaccine hesitancy.A few gaps remain for future research. Cross-cultural validation of the instruments introduced here, including measurement invariance testing and comparison against benchmarks such as the WHO SAGE Vaccine Hesitancy Scale (2), would strengthen confidence in their applicability across populations. Longitudinal designs tracking how measured hesitancy or confidence relates to actual vaccination behavior over time, rather than to intention alone, would help close the well-documented gap between attitudinal measurement and uptake outcomes. Greater use of qualitative and mixed-methods designs, alongside the cross-sectional surveys predominant here, would help clarify mechanisms that quantitative models identify but cannot fully explain. Finally, as vaccine platforms and communication environments evolve, measurement tools will need periodic re-validation to remain sensitive to emerging determinants of hesitancy.

Scope and question
The review focuses on seasonal influenza viruses, specifically human influenza A(H1N1)pdm09, A(H3N2), and B/Victoria lineage. The central question is about measuring vaccination perception, confidence, and uptake, particularly in healthcare workers and other populations, to advance instrument development and validation.
Evidence base and method
The review is based on a collection of studies, primarily cross-sectional surveys, with one qualitative study. The evidence includes studies on seasonal influenza vaccination among healthcare workers in China, attitudes toward COVID-19 vaccination in Singapore, and knowledge, attitudes, and barriers toward vaccination among community pharmacists in Türkiye. The review does not explicitly report a meta-analysis or consensus process, but it synthesizes findings from multiple studies.
Consensus and conclusions
The review highlights that vaccine hesitancy and confidence are influenced by individual psychology, professional role, cultural context, service accessibility, and direct experience with vaccine safety. It also emphasizes the need for psychometrically sound and adaptable measurement tools across populations.
Controversies and gaps
The review identifies a lack of studies in Sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and Latin America, which limits the generalizability of findings. Additionally, it notes that most studies rely on cross-sectional designs, which cannot establish temporal precedence between attitude and behavior, and are vulnerable to social desirability and recall bias. There is also a need for longitudinal designs to better understand the relationship between measured hesitancy and actual vaccination behavior.

Future research should focus on cross-cultural validation of the instruments introduced, including measurement invariance testing and comparison against benchmarks such as the WHO SAGE Vaccine Hesitancy Scale.
