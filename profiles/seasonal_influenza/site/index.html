Cross-Protection against Influenza A(H5) Clade 2.3.4.4b Viruses after A(H1N1)pdm09 Infection.

Authors: Xiang-Jie Sun, Jessica A. Belser, Zhu-Nan Li, Nicole Brock, Joanna A. Pulit-Penaloza, Troy J. Kieran, Claudia Pappas, Hui Zeng, Jessie C. Chang, Paul J. Carney
Original abstract (English)
We challenged ferrets previously infected with influenza A(H1N1)pdm09 virus with H5N1 or H5N5 clade 2.3.4.4b viruses to assess cross-protection. Results show that previous A(H1N1)pdm09 virus immunity conferred partial protection against both viruses, with slightly reduced protection against H5N5 compared with H5N1, potentially reflecting the absence of cross-reactive N5 neuraminidase antibodies.

Question and background
This study aimed to assess whether immunity from influenza A(H1N1)pdm09 infection provides cross-protection against H5N1 and H5N5 clade 2.3.4.4b viruses, addressing a gap in understanding potential immune overlap between seasonal influenza and avian influenza strains.
Design and population
The study involved challenging ferrets previously infected with influenza A(H1N1)pdm09 virus with H5N1 or H5N5 clade 2.3.4.4b viruses to evaluate cross-protection.
Core methods
Ferrets were infected with influenza A(H1N1)pdm09 virus and then challenged with H5N1 or H5N5 clade 2.3.4.4b viruses to assess immune responses and protection.
Main results
Results showed that prior A(H1N1)pdm09 virus immunity conferred partial protection against both H5N1 and H5N5 clade 2.3.4.4b viruses, with slightly reduced protection against H5N5 compared to H5N1, potentially due to the absence of cross-reactive N5 neuraminidase antibodies.
Interpretation and novelty
The findings suggest that immunity to seasonal influenza A(H1N1)pdm09 may offer some cross-protection against H5N1 and H5N5 viruses, but the level of protection varies, highlighting the importance of viral antigenic differences.
Scientific and public-health significance
These results have implications for understanding potential cross-reactive immunity in humans and could inform surveillance and vaccine development strategies for emerging influenza strains.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited to ferret models, which may not fully reflect human immune responses, and the evidence is based on a single challenge experiment, suggesting moderate confidence in the findings.

Digoxigenin is a Broad-Spectrum Antiviral Agent against Influenza A Virus via Degradation of Nucleoprotein.

Authors: Shengxiang Yin, Pengfei Zhang, Wenjun Song, Xiaoxiao Xie, Bangjin Zheng, Yichao Chen, Yongxiang Liu, Jiaqi Sun, Huisheng Liu, Shaobo Wang
Original abstract (English)
Seasonal influenza A viruses (IAVs; H1N1 and H3N2) and their zoonotic variants (e.g. H5N1 and H3N8) pose persistent threats to public health, highlighting an urgent need for innovative therapeutic strategies. Through high-throughput screening with the constructed reporter virus H3N8-GFP, we initially identified a panel of potential antiviral agents from FDA-approved compounds. Further validation revealed that digoxigenin (Dig), the aglycone of digoxin, significantly inhibited the proliferation of H3N8, H1N1, H3N2, and H5N1 in infected primary murine alveolar epithelial cells and ESC-derived human alveolar organoids. Dig treatment protected mice from lethal challenge with H1N1 and H3N8 by increasing survival rates and reducing viral loads.Mechanistically, Dig exerts its antiviral activity by directly binding to the host Na + /K + -ATPase α1 subunit (ATP1A1), which triggers the degradation of viral nucleoprotein via both the ubiquitin-proteasome and autophagy pathways, without affecting other components of the viral ribonucleoprotein (vRNP). Our study reveals the Dig-mediated antiviral mechanism that targets the host cell degradation machinery, and identifies Dig as a promising broad-spectrum antiviral agent against IAVs.

ZFYVE21 is an essential host factor for influenza A virus by regulating endosomal acidification

Authors: Ye Xu, Jing-Min Li, Wen-Hao Hang, Zi-Lun Ruan, Xin‐Mei Yang, Yu Zhang, Li-Jun Bian, Xin-Yue Zhang, Shu Li, Qing Yang
Original abstract (English)
Influenza A virus (IAV) remains a major global health threat, causing recurrent seasonal epidemics and sporadic pandemics. Despite extensive studies on IAV-host interactions, cellular regulation of the viral life cycle is not fully understood. Here, using genome-wide CRISPR/Cas9 screens, we identified zinc finger FYVE domain-containing protein 21 (ZFYVE21) as a cellular factor required for completion of the IAV life cycle. We show that ZFYVE21 acts upstream of PTEN to maintain endosomal acidification, which triggers the conformational change of viral hemagglutinin (HA) required for membrane fusion and viral uncoating. Deficiency of ZFYVE21 abrogates this acidification step, thereby blocking viral genome release into the cytosol and subsequent replication across multiple IAV strains. Importantly, ZFYVE21-deficient mice exhibit significantly lower lung viral loads, diminished histopathological damage, and markedly improved survival upon lethal IAV H3N2 challenge. Collectively, our findings uncover a critical role of ZFYVE21 in endosomal acidification-dependent IAV genome release into the cytosol and establish ZFYVE21 as a promising target for the development of broad-spectrum anti-influenza therapeutics.

Broader functional and memory antibody responses in adult females than males in response to seasonal H3N2 vaccine virus

Authors: Han-Sol Park, Anna Yin, Maclaine A. Parish, Jingping Hu, Ainslee Zou, Rishi Pandey, Katherine Z.J. Fenstermacher, Richard E. Rothman, Andrew S Pekosz, Franklin R. Toapanta
Original abstract (English)
Females of reproductive ages often mount greater antibody responses than males following influenza vaccination, but the breadth and depth of these differences, including antibody-dependent Fc receptor (FcR)-mediated functions have not been explored. Plasma samples were collected from male and female healthcare workers (range of 21-48y; mean of 31y) at 0, 28, and 180 days post-vaccination (DPV) during the 2022-2023 influenza vaccine season to assess antibody responses to A/H1N1, A/H3N2, and B/Victoria vaccine viruses. Females had greater A/H3N2, but not A/H1N1 or B/Victoria, neutralizing antibody (nAb) responses compared to males through 180 DPV, with differences being most pronounced among A/H3N2 responders ([≥]4 fold-change in nAb titers). Memory B cells isolated from female responders secreted more IgG in response to ex vivo H3 stimulation than those from male responders at 28 and 180 DPV. Before vaccination, female responders had greater anti-H3 IgG responses and Fc{gamma}RIIB (CD32b) antibody binding than males, with females maintaining greater responses through 180 DPV. Female responders had greater H3-specific antibody-dependent monocyte phagocytosis (ADMP) and natural killer cell (ADNK) secretion of IFN{gamma} and CCL4 prior to and after vaccination, with ADMP positively correlating with greater H3N2 neutralization through 180 DPV in females only. Among responders, the durability of ADMP, ADNK, and nAb in females as compared to males defined the sex differences in influenza-vaccine induced immunity. The greater Fc{gamma}R-binding antibodies prior to vaccination in females might improve the durability of both neutralizing and non-neutralizing antibody responses to diverse vaccines, including next generation influenza vaccines.

Epidemiology of influenza C in Hong Kong inferred from pediatric hospitalization data

Authors: Yanlin Cao, Mike Y.W. Kwan, Caitriona Murphy, Eunice L.Y. Chan, Joshua S C Wong, Malik Peiris, Peng Wu, Benjamin John Cowling, So-lun Lee
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Influenza C virus (ICV) circulates globally causing generally mild respiratory infections. Limited data have been reported, including epidemiologic features and its potential interactions with other influenza viruses and respiratory pathogens. METHODS: We analyzed laboratory-confirmed pediatric influenza hospitalizations in Hong Kong from October 2015 to March 2025. Respiratory specimens were tested by multiplex RT-PCR for influenza A, B, and C viruses and other respiratory pathogens. We described the seasonality, age distribution, co-detections, hospitalization burden, and explored effectiveness of seasonal influenza vaccines against ICV-associated hospital admissions in comparison with seasonal influenza A and B viruses using the test-negative analysis. RESULTS: Of a total of 39,146 admissions, 356 (0.9%) tested positive for influenza C, and 4554 (11.6%) and 1600 (4.1%) for influenza A and B, respectively. A high proportion of ICV cases (64.3%) had at least one co-detected respiratory pathogen, compared with influenza A (18.4%) and influenza B (15.3%). Seasonal influenza vaccination was not effective against ICV-associated hospitalization (vaccine effectiveness: -6.2%, 95% confidence interval: -44.6% to 22.0%) but effective for influenza A (58.2%, 54.3% to 61.8%) and influenza B (68.7%, 63.0% to 73.5%). CONCLUSIONS: Our study provided important epidemiologic information on ICV infections in pediatric patients. The estimated null protection against influenza C from seasonal influenza vaccination implied the potential immunological relationship between seasonal influenza viruses and underscored the importance of continuous surveillance on influenza viruses.

Authors: Junwen Liu, Jiaxin Huang, Lu Han, Xuegang Zhang, Caoqi Lei, Shuai Xu, Qiyun Zhu, Liu J, Huang J, Han L
Original abstract (English)
The continuous evolution of influenza A virus (IAV) necessitates the exploration of host-based antiviral strategies. Our previously published study demonstrated that the tyrosine kinase LYN (LYN proto-oncogene, Src family tyrosine kinase) is a novel anti-influenza factor through directly phosphorylating the IAV nucleoprotein (NP). However, more potential antiviral functions of LYN remained unclear. Here, we reveal a kinase activity-independent mechanism by which LYN restricted IAV replication. LYN functioned as a scaffold, recruiting the E3 ubiquitin ligase TRIM28 (tripartite motif containing 28) to catalyze K48-linked ubiquitination of IAV-NP at lysine 470. Original abstract (English)
Despite the widespread use of seasonal influenza vaccines, the high antigenic variability of influenza A viruses necessitates the development of recombinant vaccines capable of eliciting broadly cross-protective immune responses. Authors: Ahmed Abdulsattar Salman, Ali Alshalah, Sinan Ghazi Mahdi, Hussein Alwan Hasan, Ziyad Hazim Ibrahim, Iman M. Aufi
Original abstract (English)
Seasonal influenza affects individuals of all age groups and represents a significant global public health concern. In Iraq, influenza surveillance is a part of the national surveillance framework for influenza-like illness (ILI) and severe acute respiratory infection (SARI). This cross-sectional study described the epidemiological characteristics of ILI/SARI cases and circulating influenza viruses using data from six sentinel surveillance sites across Iraq between November 2021 and December 2025. Demographic and clinical data were collected and respiratory specimens were tested using RT-PCR. A total of 7,131 cases were reported, of which 3,571 (50.1%) were classified as SARI and 3,560 (49.9%) as ILI. Individuals aged 16-50 years constituted the majority (55.9%), and sex distribution was nearly equal. Logistic regression identified increasing age and the presence of diabetes mellitus, heart disease, chronic respiratory diseases, and chronic hematological diseases as independent predictors of SARI (p < 0.001). Out of 7,131 collected specimens, 7,037 (98.7%) were successfully tested. Among these, 1,552 (22.1%) tested positive. Influenza accounted for 812 cases (52.3%) and SARS-CoV-2 for 740 (47.7%). Influenza positivity was highest among individuals aged 6-15 years and among patients with comorbidities. Increasing age was independently associated with lower odds of influenza positivity, whereas diabetes mellitus, heart disease, chronic respiratory diseases, chronic hematological diseases, and chronic kidney diseases were associated with higher odds. Influenza A predominated throughout most of the study period, with subtype dominance alternating between A/H1N1pdm09 and A/H3N2. All typed Influenza B cases belonged to the Victoria lineage with no evidence of Yamagata circulation. Influenza activity demonstrated a consistent winter seasonal pattern, which emphasize the importance of implementing vaccination campaigns prior to winter before peak season. Overall, these findings highlight the importance of enhancing sentinel surveillance systems and laboratory capacity to improve detection, guide vaccination strategies, and support public health preparedness.

In Germany, they are subject to a number of surveillance systems, including both pathogen-specific and syndromic indicators. In this paper we present a collaborative multi-target and multi-model real-time forecasting system rolled out during the 2024/25 season, and discuss differences to earlier efforts carried out during the COVID-19 pandemic. CHILDREN AS KEY TARGETS IN THE PORTUGUESE SEASONAL VACCINATION STRATEGY: CAN WE SEE A DIFFERENCE ALREADY?

Authors: Sofia Ramos Lopes, Catarina Martins Raposo, Maria Ortigão Bentes, Francisca Vilas Boas, Elsa Gonçalves, Rita Belo Morais
Original abstract (English)
Background: Influenza causes significant morbidity and mortality, particularly among high-risk groups. More countries are recognizing the impact of influenza in children, as well as this age group’s important role in viral transmission. This is leading to the inclusion of this demographic in vaccination strategies. In Portugal, the 2025/26 Seasonal Influenza Vaccination Campaign expanded to include children aged 6 months to 5 years, being free for those under 2 years of age. We aimed to study the impact of this new vaccination strategy in subjects under 18 years of age observed at a secondary level hospital in Lisbon, comparing positive influenza cases between seasons before and after the change. Methods: A total of 5844 antigen and molecular respiratory pathogen panels were analysed (3062 from 2024/25 and 2782 from 2025/26), with 1507 tests positively identifying influenza A or B. Patients’ sex, age, and clinical data were collected to evaluate disease burden between both seasons. To compare differences between groups the Chi-Square Test was used, with a significance level of 0.05. Results: Of the 1507 positive test results, 59.9% (n=903) were from the 2024/25 season and 40.1% (n=604) from the 2025/26 season, corresponding to 29.5% and 21,7% of all tests done in each season, respectively. During the first season, Influenza B accounted for 517 cases (57.3%), while Influenza A made up the remaining 386 (42.7%). 2025/26 saw a drastic shift, with Influenza A accounting for 601 cases (99.5%) and Influenza B 3 cases (0.5%). Other factors such as virus characteristics, population immunity, and environment may also play a role in yearly disease activity. The 2024/25 influenza epidemic has been considered one of the most severe in recent years, so long-term surveillance is needed to establish the impact of the new vaccination strategy in Portugal.

Influenza B Virus as a Mucosal Vaccine Vector for Respiratory Pathogens: A Critical Review

Authors: Irina Isakova-Sivak, Victoria Matyushenko, Nikolay Zaramenskikh, Maria Bolgar, Pei Fong Wong, Tatiana Kotomina, Larisa Rudenko
Original abstract (English)
Respiratory pathogens need vaccine platforms that induce immunity at the portal of entry. Live attenuated influenza virus vectors are attractive because they are administered intranasally, replicate transiently in the upper respiratory tract, and express heterologous antigens in an immunostimulatory mucosal environment. Most influenza-vector research has focused on influenza A viruses (IAV), whereas influenza B virus (IBV) remains underexplored. We review evidence for IBV as a vaccine vector against respiratory pathogens. IBV has an eight-segmented negative-sense RNA genome and can be manipulated by plasmid-based reverse genetics. Its segment-specific architecture creates both constraints and opportunities for engineering. The most developed vaccine-relevant backbone is B/Yamagata/16/1988 with truncated NS1. It has supported chimeric influenza A hemagglutinin ectodomains and SARS-CoV-2 receptor-binding domain expression; the latter induced mucosal IgA, serum IgG, neutralizing antibodies, T-cell responses, and protection in mice. We discuss how NS1 truncation and cold-adapted phenotypes may alter innate sensing and vector adjuvanticity, particularly RIG-I-MAVS signaling. We also assess unresolved pre-existing IBV immunity and manufacturing constraints. Direct evidence remains sparse beyond influenza viruses and SARS-CoV-2, and no IBV vector expressing a respiratory bacterial antigen was identified. IBV is an emerging complementary mucosal vector requiring matched comparisons, rigorous insert-stability studies, and early manufacturing integration.

News Brief
The National Flu and COVID-19 Surveillance Report for 19 November 2026 (week 47) highlights that influenza activity in England increased during week 46, with emergency department attendances for influenza-like-illness (ILI) above seasonally expected levels. The report notes that influenza-confirmed acute respiratory infection (ARI) incidents rose compared to the previous week. Vaccine uptake among certain groups, such as those aged 65 years and over, patients in clinical risk groups, and those aged 2 and 3 years, was lower than the previous season. However, uptake among pregnant women was higher. The report also mentions that RSV activity increased, while COVID-19 activity remained stable. The data is based on the Moving Epidemic Method (MEM) and Mean Standard Deviation Method (MSD), which are experimental and may be revised. The report warns that testing policies and practices can affect positivity rates, and data is provisional, subject to updates. The findings are based on surveillance systems capturing test results from laboratories in England.
