Epidemiological Modeling of Seasonal Influenza Through Periodic Mean-Reverting Stochastic Processes

Epidemiological Modeling of Seasonal Influenza Through Periodic Mean-Reverting Stochastic Processes

Authors: Fahad B. Mostafa, Linda Allen
Original abstract (English)
Seasonal influenza follows annual cycles, but variation in epidemic magnitude and timing complicates preparedness. We applied periodic mean reverting stochastic differential equations (SDEs) to seven seasons of weekly Texas influenza surveillance data. Missing observations were imputed using MissForest and compared with four alternative methods. Stochastic variability was modeled using {sigma}X(t) dW(t), with p = 1/2,2/3,5/6, and 1, representing square root, intermediate sublinear, and multiplicative diffusion. Each specification was evaluated for direct influenza modeling and within a susceptible, exposed, infectious , and recovered model incorporating waning immunity, where the SDE governed transmission. Parameters were estimated using least squares and approximate maximum likelihood for direct models and sample-proportion weighted least squares for stochastic SEIR models. Model-based parametric bootstrap assessed direct-model parameter uncertainty. For direct modeling, p = 1/2 achieved the lowest RMSE (712.8) and AIC (5383.0), with similar AIC for p = 2/3 (5383.2). For stochastic SEIR modeling, p = 1 achieved the lowest RMSE (0.02926), compared with 0.03506 for p = 1/2. The deterministic SEIR model yielded R = 1.158. Transmission peaked approximately 10 weeks before infectious prevalence, while stochastic transmission generated variation in epidemic peak timing. Bootstrap analysis identified dependence between mean-reversion and diffusion parameters. Overall, the appropriate diffusion structure depended on the epidemiological quantity modeled, supporting flexible characterization of seasonal transmission variability while requiring prospective validation before operational forecasting.

Authors: Alberto Rodríguez-Castillo, Pablo Herrero Del Barrio, Felipe Pérez-García, Harold Bermúdez-Marval, Beatriz Jurado-Sánchez, Alberto Escarpa, Felipe Pérez‐García, Beatriz Jurado‐Sánchez
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Monitoring respiratory viruses is crucial for mitigating their spread and the associated healthcare costs, especially during the cold season. This study presents an on-the-fly OFF–ON fluorescence approach for the H3N2 subtype of Influenza A virus determination using magnetically propelled WS2/Ni micromotors. The micromotors are modified with a rhodamine B-labeled peptide highly specific toward the hemagglutinin A HA1 protein antigen. The improved fluid mixing imparted by the micromotor motion allows for efficient operation during an assay time of 1 min using just 1 μL of sample. The developed fluorescent assay represents a reliable and robust screening platform for influenza clinical samples, achieving exceptional selectivity and limits of detection in the ng/L range. The method’s performance was validated using nasopharyngeal specimens, demonstrating high reliability and analytical specificity for the Influenza A H3N2 subtype. Notably, the protocol provided high fluorescence signatures for H3N2-infected samples even when cycle threshold (Ct) values were comparable those of to H1N1-infected samples and negative samples. These results highlight the reliability of the proposed micromotor-based strategy for rapid viral subtyping and demonstrate its potential as a fast sample-to-result diagnostic platform requiring minimal sample volume.

新闻简报The National Flu and COVID-19 Surveillance Report for 5 November 2026 (week 45) provides an overview of influenza activity in England, based on data up to week 44 (26 October to 1 November 2026). It notes that influenza is circulating at low levels, with a slight increase in emergency department (ED) attendances for influenza-like-illness (ILI) above seasonally expected levels. Influenza-confirmed acute respiratory infection (ARI) incidents decreased compared to the previous week. Vaccine uptake data shows lower rates among older adults and those in clinical risk groups compared to the previous season, while pregnant women had higher uptake. The report also mentions that there are ongoing challenges in accurately attributing vaccinations administered in pharmacies to the correct provider setting, which may affect reported activity levels. Additionally, it highlights that COVID-19 activity decreased, with ARI incidents also declining, and RSV activity increased but remained within baseline levels. The report serves as a summary of surveillance data for multiple respiratory viruses, including influenza, RSV, and SARS-CoV-2, and is intended to inform public health responses.
