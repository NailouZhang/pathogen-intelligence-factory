Smallpox Vaccine Elicits CD8 + T Cell Immunity Persisting Over Four Decades with Cross-Recognition of Monkeypox Virus

Authors: Yujuan Du, zongmian yang, Yinying Xiao, Huajun Duan, Hong Kong, Chanchan Xiao, Jun Su
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Monkeypox, caused by MPXV, has become a global public health concern; however, the T-cell immunity against MPXV following smallpox vaccination remains unclear. In this study, we screened the immunogenicity of 21 predicted MPXV epitopes and compared immune responses between young and elderly groups. Two immunogenic CD8+ T-cell epitopes, M15 and M17, were identified, both of which effectively stimulated specific immune responses, with significantly stronger responses observed in the young group. Notably, this study provides the first evidence that smallpox vaccination induces CD8+ T-cell immunity that cross-recognizes the MPXV epitopes M15 and M17 and remains detectable for more than four decades after vaccination, as demonstrated in vaccinated individuals aged 53–75. These findings provide experimental evidence for understanding the cross-protective mechanisms of the smallpox vaccine and informing monkeypox vaccine development.

Question and background
This study aimed to investigate the T-cell immunity against MPXV following smallpox vaccination, as monkeypox has become a global public health concern and the long-term immune response to MPXV after smallpox vaccination remains unclear.
Design and population
The study screened the immunogenicity of 21 predicted MPXV epitopes and compared immune responses between young and elderly groups to assess the persistence and strength of CD8+ T-cell immunity.
Core methods
The researchers identified two immunogenic CD8+ T-cell epitopes, M15 and M17, and evaluated their ability to stimulate specific immune responses, comparing the results between young and elderly participants.
Main results
The study found that smallpox vaccination induces CD8+ T-cell immunity that cross-recognizes MPXV epitopes M15 and M17 and remains detectable for more than four decades after vaccination, as shown in individuals aged 53–75.
Interpretation and novelty
The findings offer experimental evidence for the cross-protective mechanisms of the smallpox vaccine against MPXV, highlighting its potential role in informing monkeypox vaccine development.
Scientific and public-health significance
These results contribute to understanding the long-term immune protection provided by smallpox vaccination against monkeypox, which could guide public health strategies for disease prevention and control.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study provides the first evidence of long-term CD8+ T-cell immunity against MPXV following smallpox vaccination, but it is limited to a specific age group and does not account for broader population variability.

Panton-Valentine Leucocidin-positive Staphylococcus aureus Skin Infection Mimicking Mpox in a Pediatric Returning Traveler.

Authors: Alice Hucko, Dennis Nurjadi, Sébastien Boutin, Andreas K. Lindner, Renate Krüger
Original abstract (English)
A 4-year-old boy developed a fever followed by painful, polymorphic skin lesions after travel to Cameroon, raising suspicion for mpox. Viral testing was negative; wound culture grew Panton-Valentine leucocidin-positive Staphylococcus aureus with additional, potentially synergistic virulence genes. This case demonstrates that the differential diagnosis of skin lesions following travel should include this increasingly recognized pathogen, prompting appropriate Panton-Valentine leucocidin testing.

Question and background
This study aimed to address the challenge of differentiating mpox from other infectious diseases in a pediatric traveler with skin lesions, highlighting the need for accurate diagnosis in the context of emerging pathogens.
Design and population
A single-case study was conducted on a 4-year-old boy who developed fever and polymorphic skin lesions after returning from Cameroon, with the focus on identifying the causative agent of his illness.
Core methods
The patient's clinical presentation and travel history were evaluated, and viral testing was performed on skin lesions. Wound cultures were conducted to identify bacterial pathogens, including Panton-Valentine leucocidin-positive Staphylococcus aureus.
Main results
Viral testing was negative, and wound culture confirmed the presence of Panton-Valentine leucocidin-positive Staphylococcus aureus, which carried additional virulence genes.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted this case as a reminder that skin lesions in travelers may not always be caused by mpox, and they emphasized the importance of considering other pathogens, such as Staphylococcus aureus, in the differential diagnosis.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings underscore the need for broader differential diagnosis in cases of skin lesions following travel, particularly in regions where mpox is endemic, to avoid misdiagnosis and ensure appropriate treatment.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its single-case design, which may not be representative of broader trends. However, the evidence is strong given the direct identification of the causative agent through wound culture.

Willingness to receive monkeypox vaccine and associated factors among the Ghanaian population: Findings from a nationwide survey

Authors: Edem Kojo Dzantor, Peter Mensah, Shamsu-Deen Ziblim, Stephen Owusu, Samuel Obeng, Edmore Masaka
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background The World Health Organization recommends vaccination as an important public health intervention for controlling mpox outbreaks globally. The willingness to receive mpox vaccine and associated determinants is not fully understood in Ghana. The study therefore assessed Ghanaians' willingness to receive mpox vaccine and the associated factors using the 5 C vaccine uptake model. Methods This was a nationwide cross-sectional online survey conducted between July and October 2025 in Ghana. The study was conducted and reported in line with the STROBE Checklist for cross-sectional studies. The questionnaire was adopted and modified based on the five psychological antecedents of vaccination (confidence, complacency, constraints, calculation, and collective responsibility) and the WHO frequently asked questions on mpox. Descriptive statistics, chi-square test, and multivariate analysis were performed using Stata version 17. Results The study involved 1670 respondents, with most of them in the 18–22 years age category (761, 45.61%) and predominantly females (1296, 77.60%). Most of the respondents indicated their willingness to take the vaccine (1006, 60.24%). Original abstract (English)

Original abstract (English)
Monkeypox virus (MPXV) has caused two Public Health Emergencies of International Concern since 2022, with co-circulation of distinct clades (Ia, Ib, IIa, IIb) that differ in virulence and transmission patterns. Rapid discrimination of these clades is critical for outbreak control and clinical management. Authors: Mkululi Sikosana
Original abstract (English)
Background: Health misinformation circulating through online social networks is associated with reduced adherence to public health guidance, vaccine hesitancy, and erosion of institutional trust. Many computational approaches still treat detection as text-only classification or model diffusion using network structure alone, with limited integration of psychological susceptibility and linguistic framing. Methods: This thesis develops a multi-strand framework combining machine learning, behavioural theory, network analysis, and linguistic profiling. Using Twitter datasets spanning COVID-19 and mpox, including the Monkeypox misinformation: Twitter dataset, hereafter referred to as the Monkeypox dataset, the detection strand benchmarks conventional and transformer-based baselines and then evaluates two theory-informed models: a multi-branch fusion architecture and an ELM-informed CNN–LSTM classifier. These models integrate semantic, rhetorical, stance, and TPBand ELM-informed features. The term mpox is used throughout this thesis in accordance with the World Health Organisation’s recommendation of November 2022. The former term is retained in the official title and shortened designation of the Monkeypox misinformation: Twitter dataset, as well as where it appears in original publication titles, direct quotations, file names, or variable names [314]. When engagement metadata are missing, the Cognitive Propagation Score (CPS) provides an auxiliary ranking signal for within-dataset comparison. The propagation strand develops susceptibility-aware influence measures, fits an ELM-augmented SIRMMM model with behaviour-modulated transmission, and introduces BERTopic-VP to prioritise themes using observed engagement where available and propensity-to-spread proxies otherwise. Results: Across datasets, the proposed detection models perform competitively against representative baselines and support interpretable analysis of cues associated with misinformation and elevated diffusion risk. Propagation results indicate that structural centrality does not consistently align with influence under proxy-grounded evaluation, and that behavioural covariates can shift outbreak timing and magnitude where behavioural variance is present, subject to identifiability constraints. BERTopic-VP prioritises coherent themes that are relatively rare yet highlyranked under engagement or proxy-based signals. Linguistic profiling shows that misinformation is more rhetorically marked and affectively loaded than factual content, with pandemic-specific framing differences. Conclusion: The thesis contributes a theoretically informed and empirically evaluated toolkit for health misinformation detection, characterisation, and prioritisation, supporting monitoring and risk-informed public health communication under uneven engagement availability.

Authors: Meirou Lu, Jiaxuan Li, Jia Xue, Xingsheng Yang, Changyue Xu, Qudi Qiao, Chongwen Wang, Jierong Chen, Bing Gu
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Real-time and highly sensitive monitoring of pathogens in environmental and host-derived samples is critical for controlling emerging and acute infectious diseases, yet remains technically challenging. Here, we report a multifunctional nanozyme-based immunochromatographic assay (ICA) that enables rapid, universal, and multiplex detection in different scenarios through visual readouts. We constructed a corona-structured magnetic nanozyme (CSMN) with a triple signal-amplification mechanism involving “magnetic enrichment-synergistic enhancement-multiple catalytic sites,” enabling efficient enrichment of trace pathogens in complex matrices and generation of dual visual outputs (colorimetric and catalytically enhanced signals) on ICA. The CSMN-based nanozyme ICA requires only 2 min of catalysis to achieve a ≥92.65-fold increase in sensitivity and a 100-fold expansion of the detection range. Based on this platform, dual-mode detection strategies were developed for SARS-CoV-2 and monkeypox virus. The direct mode enables rapid screening within 12 min, with detection limits as low as 19.4 and 22.5 pg/mL for the two targets, respectively. The enrichment mode enables ultrasensitive detection within 20 min, with detection ranges of 0.0012–1000 ng/mL and 0.0026–1000 ng/mL, respectively, while mitigating interference from the hook effect, making it suitable for detecting low-abundance targets in complex samples. The method demonstrated high accuracy and reliability across environmental and clinical samples, with a detection time approximately one-sixth that of qPCR and ELISA, indicating strong potential for infectious disease surveillance and outbreak control.

Professor Maluquer de Motes, Professor of Virology, University of Surrey During the study, led by Professor Maluquer de Motes, scientists analysed the Mpox strain (Clade IIB, lineage B.1) which infected over 100,000 people in 120 countries in 2022 and compared it to an ancestral form of the virus (Clade IIB lineage A.1) from which it derived. This strain was found to have a reduced ability to disseminate between cells and had the ability to evade the body's immune response. At the centre of the initiative is the International Coordinating Group on Vaccine Provision (ICG), a mechanism established in 1997 following major meningitis outbreaks in Africa. ICG was created to coordinate emergency supplies of vaccines and antibiotics for countries facing major disease outbreaks. Its role is based on the recognition that although outbreaks are difficult to predict, timely vaccination can help prevent further transmission and reduce the impact of infectious diseases. Traditionally, ICG has been responsible for ensuring equitable access to licensed vaccines against cholera, meningitis, yellow fever, and Ebola virus disease during emergencies. Its responsibilities include managing global emergency vaccine stockpiles and working with partners, donors, and vaccine manufacturers to determine the size and composition of the stocks required for effective outbreak response. The expansion of the mechanism to include mpox is expected to provide a more structured approach to the distribution of mpox vaccines, particularly during emergencies when demand can rise rapidly. The move builds on measures introduced during the global mpox response. In 2024, WHO and its partners established an Access and Allocation Mechanism (AAM) specifically for mpox vaccines, treatments and diagnostic tests. AAM was designed to support the allocation of limited medical countermeasures to countries and communities where they were most urgently required. Under the new, longer-term arrangement, ICG will continue and strengthen the work initiated through the temporary mechanism. The development also follows a decision by the Gavi Board in 2025 to formally approve the establishment and financing of a global mpox vaccine stockpile under the long-standing ICG framework. Vaccines& Immunizations The stockpile is intended to ensure that vaccine supplies can be mobilised rapidly during outbreaks instead of relying solely on vaccine procurement after an emergency has already escalated. The initiative comes amid continued concern over mpox, a viral disease that can cause fever, rash, swollen lymph nodes and painful lesions. While many people recover, severe disease and death can occur, particularly among vulnerable populations. WHO has stressed the importance of vaccination as part of a broader outbreak response that also includes surveillance, testing, case management, contact tracing, infection prevention and community engagement. The global vaccine initiative is expected to complement, rather than replace, national public health measures. Health authorities and international partners will also need to address challenges around vaccine supply, financing, delivery and equitable access to ensure that the availability of a global stockpile translates into timely vaccination on the ground. The expansion of the ICG mechanism to include mpox represents a shift from short-term emergency arrangements towards a more sustainable system for vaccine preparedness.
