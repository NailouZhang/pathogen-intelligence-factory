Mpox Vaccine Acceptance Among Health Care Workers in Central Asia: The Role of COVID-19 Experience.
作者： Aidos Bolatov
摘要中文翻译摘要 背景 COVID-19 大流行可能影响了医护人员 (HCW) 对新出现的传染病威胁的看法以及采用新 疫苗 的意愿。本研究调查了中亚医护人员中与 COVID-19 相关的经历和心理因素是否与 猴痘 疫苗 接受程度相关。方法 在这项横断面研究中,来自哈萨克斯坦、吉尔吉斯斯坦和乌兹别克斯坦的 3,054 名医护人员在 2022 年 7 月至 11 月期间完成了一项在线调查。使用单个 5 点李克特量表项目评估 猴痘 疫苗 接受度,并作为连续结果进行分析。衡量标准包括与 COVID-19 相关的经历、对 COVID-19 和 猴痘、DASS-21 的恐惧以及职业倦怠。分析包括比较测试、多变量线性回归和探索性中介模型。结果 总体而言,1,868 名医护人员 (61.2%) 表示愿意接受 猴痘 疫苗接种,而 1,186 名 (38.8%) 则表示没有意愿。在双变量分析中,不同国家/地区的接受程度有所不同,具有 COVID-19 前线经验或与高危人群生活在一起的医护人员的接受程度较高。在多变量回归中,对 猴痘 的恐惧是与接受程度最强的相关性,而倦怠则呈负相关。中介分析表明通过恐惧和痛苦相关途径存在间接关联。结论 中亚医护人员对 猴痘 疫苗 的接受度与大流行经历和对传染病风险的心理反应相关。策略应将风险沟通与减少倦怠和医护人员福祉支持结合起来。

Mpox Vaccine Acceptance Among Health Care Workers in Central Asia: The Role of COVID-19 Experience.

Authors: Aidos Bolatov
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background The COVID-19 pandemic may have shaped health care workers’ (HCWs’) perceptions of emerging infectious threats and willingness to adopt new vaccines. This study examined whether COVID-19-related experiences and psychological factors were associated with Mpox vaccine acceptance among HCWs in Central Asia. Methods In this cross-sectional study, 3,054 HCWs from Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, and Uzbekistan completed an online survey between July and November 2022. Mpox vaccine acceptance was assessed using a single 5-point Likert-scale item and analyzed as a continuous outcome. Measures included COVID-19-related experiences, fear of COVID-19 and Mpox, DASS-21, and occupational burnout. Analyses included comparative tests, multivariable linear regression, and exploratory mediation models. Results Overall, 1,868 HCWs (61.2%) reported some willingness to receive Mpox vaccination, whereas 1,186 (38.8%) reported none. In bivariate analyses, acceptance varied by country and was higher among HCWs with COVID-19 frontline experience or living with high-risk individuals. In multivariable regression, fear of Mpox was the strongest correlate of acceptance, while burnout was inversely associated. Mediation analyses indicated indirect associations through fear- and distress-related pathways. Conclusions Mpox vaccine acceptance among Central Asian HCWs was associated with pandemic experience and psychological responses to infectious disease risk. Strategies should combine risk communication with burnout reduction and HCW well-being support.

Validation of self-reported mpox vaccination in men who have sex with men.

Authors: Fatima Mozaffari, Ei T. Aung, Nyi Nyi Soe, Danushi Wijekoon, Janet M. Towns, Phyu Mon Latt, Eric P. F. Chow
Original abstract (English)
The Australian Government introduced a nationwide mpox MVA-BN vaccine to all eligible individuals at higher risk of mpox exposure, specifically gay, bisexual, and other men who have sex with men (GBMSM). Self-reported vaccination status is commonly used in surveillance and behavioural research, but its accuracy for mpox vaccination is unknown. We conducted a retrospective cross-sectional study of GBMSM aged ≥16 years who attended the Melbourne Sexual Health Centre (MSHC) between 7 December 2023 and 31 December 2024. Self-reported mpox vaccination status using computer-assisted self-interviewing (CASI) was compared to verified electronic health records. Sensitivity, specificity, and agreement (κ) were calculated alongside other measures of performance. Of 6050 eligible men, 5885 men self-reported their mpox vaccination status: 48.6% (n = 2859) vaccinated, 44.6% (n = 2623) unvaccinated, and 6.8% (n = 403) unsure. Self-reported mpox vaccination had a sensitivity of 96.9% (95% CI: 95.9% to 97.7%), a specificity of 65.4% (95% CI: 63.9% to 66.9%), a positive predictive value of 52.4% (95% CI: 50.6% to 54.3%), a negative predictive value of 98.2% (95% CI: 97.6% to 98.6%), and an accuracy of 74.3% (95% CI: 73.1% to 75.5%). Our results demonstrated that the majority of GBMSM correctly reported their vaccination status. While self-reported mpox vaccination demonstrates high sensitivity, moderate agreement indicates residual misclassification and incomplete capture of vaccinations in the electronic health record used for validation. This suggests that while self-report is reliable for confirming non-vaccination, it is less reliable for identifying vaccinated individuals, underscoring the importance of electronic health record verification for accurate vaccine surveillance.

Although MPXV’s host entry and immune evasion mechanisms have been studied, little is known about how it can exploit host reproductive tissues, proteins, and associated pathways. We investigated whether MPXV Clade II proteins exhibit putative structural resemblance to host reproductive proteins with the potential to dysregulate tissue-specific signalling pathways. Differentially expressed genes (DEG) identified in infected host datasets were intersected with reproductive tissue-enriched proteins derived from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA). Comparative analysis revealed potential targets that could be altered in reproductive tissues during MPXV infection, especially via sexual transmission. To evaluate host–pathogen interactions based on structural resemblance, the host and viral protein sequences of identified DEGs were aligned, followed by structural modelling with AlphaFold2, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics simulation. Key viral proteins- OPG037 and OPG187- showed high structural similarity to host HACE1 and TTBK2, respectively, generating the hypothesis that OPG037 may bind RAC1, a crucial GTPase controlling cellular migration. Similarly, the Kelch and BTB-BACK domains of KLHL3 showed putative structural similarity to the Kelch and BTB-BACK domains of MPXV Kelch-like protein. It raises the hypothesis that it may compete with the host Kelch domain of KLHL3 to bind WNK3 and the BTB-BACK domain of KLHL3 to bind CUL3. These findings suggest MPXV Clade II proteins exhibit putative structural resemblance with potential functional implications requiring validation. This study elucidates docking-based host–pathogen interactions to generate hypotheses on molecular mechanisms behind viral pathogenesis in sexual organs. This provides insights essential for mitigating the spread of emerging MPXV variants with enhanced potential for human-to-human sexual transmission.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract Mobile laboratories (MLs) offer a practical solution for decentralized wastewater and environmental surveillance in settings with limited laboratory infrastructure, cold-chain logistics, and trained personnel. However, field-deployable molecular workflows suitable for diverse environmental matrices have not been systematically evaluated. Here, we present an integrated ML workflow combining Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) sequencing for shotgun metagenomics, multiplex small-subunit (SSU) rRNA metabarcoding, and portable qPCR for targeted pathogen detection. Nucleic acid extraction was optimized and evaluated across wastewater and sediment matrices. Workflow performance was assessed using the ZymoBIOMICS Microbial Community Standard and wastewater spiked with inactivated mpox virus (MPXV), demonstrating comparable taxonomic recovery and consistent MPXV detection across spike concentrations. Multiple displacement amplification combined with rapid barcoding reliably reproduced mock community composition across environmental matrices. As a proof-of-concept, the workflow was applied to environmental samples from sub-Saharan Africa, detecting priority pathogens and antimicrobial resistance genes consistent with the local epidemiological context. These findings demonstrate the feasibility and field adaptability of the workflow for One Health pathogen and antimicrobial resistance surveillance in resource-limited settings.

Strengthening Border Health Security Through Digital Surveillance: Evaluation of the Satu Sehat Health Pass System in Indonesia

Authors: Asniya Asniya, Andi Zulkifli, Ridwan Amiruddin, Tubianto Anang Zulfikar, Rosa Devitha Ayu, Indra Dwinata
Original abstract (English)
Background: The increasing volume of international travel has heightened the risk of cross-border transmission of infectious diseases, necessitating effective surveillance systems at points of entry. In response to the global mpox outbreak and other emerging public health threats, Indonesia implemented the Satu Sehat Health Pass (SSHP), a digital health declaration platform for international travelers. This study evaluated the performance of the SSHP surveillance system at Sultan Hasanuddin International Airport, Makassar, Indonesia. Methods: A mixed-method surveillance system evaluation was conducted from September 2024 to April 2025 using the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) surveillance evaluation framework. Quantitative assessment examined sensitivity, positive predictive value (PPV), representativeness, data completeness, and timeliness using secondary surveillance data from 113,051 international travelers. Qualitative assessment explored simplicity, flexibility, acceptability, stability, data quality, and usefulness through in-depth interviews and focus group discussions with surveillance stakeholders. Results: During the study period, 627 (0.55%) travelers were classified as high-risk or symptomatic by SSHP. The sensitivity of self-reported SSHP screening was 63.2%, while the PPV was 4.94%. Health officer assessment demonstrated superior performance, with sensitivity of 73.0% and PPV of 90.3%. Representativeness was satisfactory, with approximately 98% completeness for demographic variables. However, 81% of records contained incomplete or invalid telephone numbers, limiting follow-up investigations. The system showed good timeliness, with notifications transmitted within 24 hours. Qualitative findings indicated that SSHP was useful, flexible, and acceptable, but challenges remained regarding data quality, interoperability, and operational stability during peak travel periods. Conclusion: SSHP contributes to the early detection and monitoring of infectious disease risks among international travelers. Nevertheless, improvements in data validation, traveler compliance, system integration, and infrastructure support are needed to optimize surveillance effectiveness and strengthen border health security in Indonesia.

A Bayesian-weighted Adaptive Ensemble for Early Multi-disease Outbreak Classification from Multi-modal Big Data Streams

Authors: Rejoice E. Ogini, F. O. Okorodudu
Original abstract (English)
Background: Across much of sub-Saharan Africa, disease-surveillance systems remain largely reactive: health authorities are often notified only after an outbreak has already been under way for days or weeks. Aim: this study develops and evaluates a Bayesian-weighted ensemble classifier that determines, from a 47-dimensional multi-modal feature vector spanning epidemiological, environmental, and digital signals, whether a weekly state–disease record represents an outbreak. Methods: four complementary learners — Random Forest, a long short-term memory (LSTM) network, XGBoost, and a hybrid combining the autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) model with a learned residual correction — are fused by a Bayesian-weighted adaptive meta-learner whose component weights are estimated by constrained cross-entropy minimisation on a validation set and whose posterior is calibrated by isotonic regression. The model was trained and evaluated on a benchmark of weekly records for five diseases endemic to Nigeria (Lassa fever, cholera, cerebrospinal meningitis, measles, and mpox) across 37 jurisdictions; the benchmark combines real NCDC and WHO AFRO surveillance statistics with a calibrated epidemiological simulation used to achieve the record volume and completeness required for controlled algorithm development. Results: On a held-out 2022–2023 test partition (N = 279,500), the ensemble attained 96.3% accuracy (95% CI: 96.2–96.4%), an F1-score of 0.939, and an AUC-ROC of 0.963, exceeding a re-implemented single-LSTM baseline on every metric (McNemar and DeLong tests, both P < .001). Probability estimates were well calibrated (Brier score 0.048), and a leave-one-learner-out ablation confirmed that every component contributed positively, with the LSTM contributing the largest marginal gain. Conclusion: disciplined model diversity combined with probability calibration yields well-behaved, decision-ready outbreak-risk scores; because the evaluation relies in part on simulated data, these figures characterise performance on a controlled benchmark rather than demonstrated real-world effectiveness, and prospective validation on live surveillance feeds is identified as the necessary next step.

Meta-analysis of Asymptomatic Monkeypox Virus Prevalence in Nonendemic Regions, 2022-2024.

Authors: Kelsey J. MacCuish, Robert Snyder, Kelly A. Johnson, Edwin Chojolan, Awa Keinde, Kathleen Jacobson, Kayla Saadeh
Original abstract (English)
In this meta-analysis, we performed a literature search for articles relating to asymptomatic monkeypox virus (MPXV) detections published worldwide during May 12, 2022-September 13, 2024. The primary outcome was to determine the pooled prevalence of asymptomatic MPXV detections. We conducted a random-effects analysis that yielded a final pooled prevalence of 1.0% (95% prediction interval 0.1%-11.2%). Results did not substantially differ by geographic region or study design. Although those findings are reassuring, we did not evaluate infectivity or transmission potential among asymptomatic persons. MPXV continues to circulate globally; the viral dynamics that enable its persistence remain poorly understood and merit further study.

Timely evidence generation in epidemic response: lessons from an attempted analytical epidemiological investigation during an mpox outbreak, Ireland, 2022.

Authors: Charlotte Salgaard Nielsen, Eve Robinson, Patricia Garvey, Kate O'Donnell, Noel McCarthy, Randal Parlour, Fiona E Lyons, Derval Igoe, Kate O’Donnell
Original abstract (English)
The emergence of mpox cases in Ireland in May 2022 triggered an urgent public health response, including an attempted analytical epidemiological investigation aimed at informing vaccination strategies and risk communication, particularly among gay, bisexual and other men who have sex with men. At the time of implementation, case numbers had declined to an extent that limited recruitment, resulting in early suspension of data collection. We present a structured reflection of the investigation, drawing on a lessons learnt review and the experiences of those involved, to offer actionable recommendations to strengthen evidence generation in future outbreak preparedness and response efforts. Key lessons include the critical importance of stakeholder engagement, clear governance and legal frameworks, and the need for enhanced procedural preparedness and pre-established surge capacity arrangements to enable rapid analytical investigations during health emergencies. These insights align closely with Ireland's National Health Protection Strategy and the extended mandate of the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control. Beyond existing international frameworks for outbreak preparedness, this reflection highlights the practical implementation barriers that must be addressed to enable timely and coordinated epidemiological investigations across Europe.

Authors: Lisumi Hewa Ranchigoda Kankanamge, Amal Remtulla, Maedeh Naghibosdat, Emma Wilson, Robert Andrew Kozak
Original abstract (English)
Monkeypox virus (MPXV) is an ongoing global public health concern. Historically associated with zoonotic infection, in 2022, a major epidemiological shift occurred when sustained transmission was identified across multiple countries that previously had not had endemic circulation of the virus. While mpox typically presents with characteristic clinical features, infection can also lead to a spectrum of less common manifestations involving multiple organ systems. The extent of viral dissemination and disease severity appear to be modulated by host factors such as immune status, vaccination history, co-infections, and potentially the viral clade. Although complications associated with mpox are infrequent, they can lead to significant morbidity and mortality. Moreover, the underlying pathogenesis and to what extent the viral clade may be playing a role remain incompletely understood. This review summarizes the less common clinical complications of mpox, explores the underlying mechanisms of viral pathogenesis, and discusses what insights can be gained from cell culture and animal models. Additionally, we highlight knowledge gaps that can guide future research priorities.

Authors: Arole Darwin Touko, Ruth Tabi Amin, Gordon Takop Nchanji
Original abstract (English)
Background: Mpox is a zoonotic viral disease caused by an Orthopoxvirus endemic to sub-Saharan Africa (SSA), where its resurgence underscores the urgent need for improved preparedness among health care workers (HCWs). Despite the potential of a One Health approach, current implementations remain fragmented. This rapid review synthesises evidence on HCWs' knowledge, attitudes, information sources, and barriers to preparedness regarding Mpox in SSA to identify training needs and policy implications. FOLIC ACID AND RELATED FOLATES AS PROPOSED INHIBITORS OF ORTHOPOXVIRUS MRNA CAP METHYLTRANSFERASES: A NARRATIVE REVIEW OF EVIDENCE AND LIMITATIONS

Authors: Ali Al‐Kaleel, Lubna Al‐Gailani, Martha Orendu Oche Attah
Original abstract (English)
Monkeypox virus (MPXV) replicates in the host cell cytoplasm and must therefore encode its own messenger RNA capping machinery, which makes the viral cap methyltransferases attractive antiviral targets. Folic acid has recently been proposed as an inhibitor of these enzymes and, on that basis, as a candidate treatment for MPXV. This narrative review examines that proposition and the level of evidence supporting it. PubMed, Scopus, and Google Scholar were searched through May 2026; no original experimental work was undertaken. The proposal rests almost entirely on a single virtual screening study in which folic acid emerged as a high-scoring ligand in molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations, supported indirectly by reports that folates and folate antagonists modulate the replication of unrelated viruses. No published study demonstrates concentration-dependent inhibition of a purified orthopoxvirus methyltransferase by folic acid, and no kinetic parameters have been determined. Folate biochemistry offers no straightforward mechanism because S-adenosylmethionine, rather than folate, is the methyl donor for these enzymes and folate contributes only indirectly by supplying that pool. Furthermore, reported antiviral activity at millimolar concentrations exceeds achievable plasma exposures by approximately three orders of magnitude. By contrast, sinefungin-derived inhibitors of the MPXV 2′-O-methyltransferase VP39 have been characterized structurally, inhibit the purified enzyme at submicromolar concentrations, and suppress viral replication in cell culture, confirming that this machinery is druggable. Folic acid is therefore best described as a computationally derived hypothesis requiring enzymatic validation, rather than an established methyltransferase inhibitor or a therapy for MPXV.

Scope and question
The review evaluates the effectiveness of active versus passive surveillance strategies under the IDSR 3rd Edition framework in Central Africa, focusing on outbreak detection, reporting completeness, and cost-effectiveness. The central question is whether active surveillance approaches, such as community-based surveillance (CBS), active case finding, event-based surveillance (EBS), and rapid response teams, are more effective than passive facility-based surveillance in detecting and responding to mpox outbreaks.
Evidence base and method
The review is a mixed methods implementation analysis that combines a narrative review of policy documents, surveillance performance data (2018-2024), peer-reviewed literature, and grey literature from Central African countries. Data were synthesized using thematic analysis and a comparative assessment guided by IDSR core functions, IHR (2005) capacities, and health system resilience dimensions (absorption, adaptation, transformation).
Consensus and conclusions
Active surveillance approaches, including CBS, active case finding, EBS, and proactive rapid response teams, substantially outperformed passive facility-based surveillance in outbreak detection, reporting completeness, and cost-effectiveness. The review highlights the importance of strengthening surveillance systems to improve early warning and response capabilities against mpox.
Controversies and gaps
The review notes that implementation of the IDSR 3rd Edition framework remains uneven across Central Africa, which may affect the consistency of surveillance outcomes. There is a lack of detailed data on the long-term sustainability and scalability of active surveillance strategies in resource-limited settings.
Research and practice implications
The review recommends prioritizing active surveillance strategies to enhance outbreak detection and response in Central Africa. It also suggests the need for further research on the cost-effectiveness and scalability of these strategies, as well as the development of targeted interventions to address the specific challenges of mpox surveillance in the region.

GeoVax Highlights Likely Sustained Clade Ib Mpox Transmission in the United States NewMediaWire · GeoVax, Inc. NewMediaWire Tue, September 29, 2026 at 8:37 AM EDT 5 min read GOVX -0.65% CDC Reports Changing U.S. Transmission Dynamics Underscore Importance of Continued Surveillance, Vaccine Preparedness and Resilient MVA Vaccine Supply ATLANTA, GA - September 29, 2026 (NEWMEDIAWIRE) - GeoVax Labs, Inc. (Nasdaq: GOVX), a clinical-stage biotechnology company developing vaccines against infectious diseases and therapies for solid tumor cancers, today highlighted an important change in the U.S. epidemiology of Clade I mpox reported by the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). In its September 25 update, CDC reported more than 60 laboratory-confirmed Clade I mpox cases across at least 20 U.S. states. Since August, an increasing proportion of U.S. Clade I cases have occurred among individuals with no recent international travel or known links to recent travelers. Cases with presumed domestic transmission have now been reported in more than 10 jurisdictions, which CDC said indicates a "likely transition to sustained Clade Ib transmission in the United States." CDC continues to assess the risk from the Clade I outbreak as low for most people in the United States, while noting that additional travel-related and domestically acquired Clade Ib cases are expected. David Dodd, Chairman and Chief Executive Officer of GeoVax, commented: "CDC's latest assessment represents an important change in the epidemiology of Clade Ib mpox in the United States. While the risk to the general population remains low, presumed domestic transmission across multiple jurisdictions reinforces the importance of continued surveillance, vaccination of appropriate at-risk populations and sustained preparedness." Dodd continued: "Preparedness should not begin when an infectious-disease threat becomes a crisis. The appropriate time to strengthen vaccine capacity, diversify sources of supply and establish scalable manufacturing infrastructure is before those capabilities are urgently needed. The changing geographic footprint and public health threat of mpox is another reminder that infectious diseases do not respect national borders." Advancing GEO-MVA as a Potential Additional MVA Vaccine Option GEO-MVA is GeoVax's Modified Vaccinia Ankara (MVA)-based vaccine being developed for protection against mpox and smallpox. Following formal guidance from the European Medicines Agency (EMA), GeoVax is advancing GEO-MVA through an expedited clinical development pathway, with traditional Phase 1 and Phase 2 clinical trials not required, allowing the program to proceed directly to a pivotal Phase 3 immunobridging study comparing immune responses generated by GEO-MVA with those generated by the licensed MVA-BN comparator. GeoVax expects to initiate the 500-participant pivotal Phase 3 immunobridging study in the fourth quarter of 2026, with results expected in mid-2027, having completed the manufacturing/fill-finish and clinical operations aspects in support of initiating and completing the pivotal study. Story Continues GeoVax is developing GEO-MVA to expand global access to MVA vaccines and scalable production capabilities, with the potential to provide governments and international public health organizations with an additional source of MVA vaccine. The Company believes GEO-MVA can become an important strategic preparedness asset by supporting diversified vaccine supply, scalable manufacturing capacity and greater supply resilience, while strengthening biosecurity, orthopoxvirus preparedness and the ability to respond to evolving global health threats. Dodd concluded: "Low risk to the general population today and the need for long-term preparedness are not contradictory. CDC's latest assessment illustrates why preparedness must anticipate changes in infectious-disease transmission rather than react to them after they occur. We believe diversified vaccine supply and scalable MVA manufacturing capacity can become increasingly important as the epidemiology of mpox continues to evolve." A Changing Mpox Landscape The development is particularly noteworthy because the 2022 global mpox outbreak, including the large U.S. outbreak, was caused by Clade IIb. Clade I mpox has historically been associated with more severe disease and higher mortality than Clade II. However, disease severity varies by viral subclade, population and access to medical care. Recent Clade Ib cases identified in high-income countries have generally involved relatively mild illness, underscoring the importance of distinguishing the changing epidemiology of the virus from the immediate risk it poses to the general population. The significance of CDC's latest assessment therefore extends beyond disease severity alone. The emergence of likely sustained Clade Ib transmission represents an important change in the geographic footprint and transmission dynamics of mpox in the United States. The new CDC assessment also represents an evolution from earlier U.S. surveillance. As recently as August, CDC reported that sustained Clade Ib transmission had not been detected in the United States during 2024–2025. The September 25 assessment now indicates a likely transition toward sustained domestic transmission. Globally, Clade I outbreaks have continued in Central and Eastern Africa since late 2023, with subsequent geographic expansion beyond the region, including transmission in parts of Europe and introductions into the United States. The evolving epidemiology follows other recent public-health preparedness actions, including updated CDC vaccination recommendations for certain U.S. travelers at increased risk and international initiatives to strengthen access to mpox vaccines and outbreak-response capabilities. This follows reports by the World Health Organization of 780 laboratory-confirmed cases of monkeypox as of 2nd June 2022. The cases have been reported to or identified by the WHO from 27 Member States across four WHO regions that are not endemic to monkeypox virus, while Monkeypox is endemic in some African countries. According to the WHO, 1,392 suspected cases of monkeypox have been reported this year until mid of May in seven African countries. Out of these, 44 cases are confirmed. This is in the 4.5 months slightly less than half the number of cases reported for the whole of last year. WHO says the sudden appearance of monkeypox in multiple countries across the world indicates the virus has been spreading undetected for some time outside the West and Central African nations where it is usually found. Christophe Bazivamo, the EAC Deputy Secretary General in charge of the Productive and Social Sectors called on the EAC to education their citizens on monkeypox to limit spread. “Because of the proximity of the EAC Partner States to some of the affected countries, it is important that we take precautionary measures to minimize its spread,” said Bazivamo. “It is important that people are given necessary information on the nature of the disease and how they can protect themselves and prevent the disease from spreading, “This will also help in avoiding unnecessary panic and stigmatization particularly now that people easily associate any disease outbreak to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic,” added Hon. Bazivamo. The DSG further emphasized on the importance of factual risk communication to communities, that provides necessary information without causing unnecessary concern, and need for stepping up surveillance. Monkeypox outbreaks are not new. The virus was first discovered in monkeys in 1958, with the first human case in the African region detected in 1970. Since then, there have been multiple outbreaks of the viral disease that can spread from animals to humans but can also spread between people. Transmission is possible through close contact with an infected person, or objects including clothes and bedsheets as well as droplets. Symptoms typically include skin rash or lesions, fever, intense headache, muscle aches, back pain, general body weakness and swollen lymph nodes and last from two to four weeks. In many patients, the symptoms are moderate and clear up on their own but severe cases and even death can occur. WHO states the case fatality ratio, or the percent of people dying compared to those diagnosed, to be around 3-6 percent. In comparison to COVID-19, which is a highly contagious disease, transmission of monkeypox is more difficult. WHO assesses the current risk posed by monkeypox to human health and for the general public as low. To reduce the risk of contracting the virus, According to WHO, people should: Avoid coming into contact with people recently diagnosed with the virus or those who may have been infected. Wear a face mask when in close contact with someone who has symptoms. Use personal protective equipment when caring for patients with confirmed or suspected monkeypox infection. Wash hands with soap and water or use alcohol-based sanitizers, especially after coming into contact with infected or infection-suspected animals or humans. Regularly wash clothing and bedsheets at high temperature Only eat meat that has been cooked thoroughly. Avoid coming into contact with sick animals that could be carrying the virus, such as rodents or non-human primates and refrain from eating or handling so-called bush meat. A person who is suspected to have contracted monkeypox should self-isolate him or herself from physical contact with others and seek medical advice immediately. The virus causing monkeypox belongs to the same group as the smallpox virus, however, it is a much milder and less deadly form of it. Vaccination against smallpox has been shown to be protective against monkeypox and a new vaccine against smallpox and monkeypox has been approved but is not yet widely available. An antiviral to treat the monkeypox virus has been recently approved in the United States of America and in the European Union. Otherwise, treatment aims to ease the symptoms and includes, for example, painkillers. ReadMed Journals The initiative builds on the work of the World Health Organisation (WHO) and its partners to strengthen international coordination of vaccine supplies and improve preparedness for mpox outbreaks, particularly in countries with limited access to vaccines. At the centre of the initiative is the International Coordinating Group on Vaccine Provision (ICG), a mechanism established in 1997 following major meningitis outbreaks in Africa. ICG was created to coordinate emergency supplies of vaccines and antibiotics for countries facing major disease outbreaks. Its role is based on the recognition that although outbreaks are difficult to predict, timely vaccination can help prevent further transmission and reduce the impact of infectious diseases. Traditionally, ICG has been responsible for ensuring equitable access to licensed vaccines against cholera, meningitis, yellow fever, and Ebola virus disease during emergencies. Its responsibilities include managing global emergency vaccine stockpiles and working with partners, donors, and vaccine manufacturers to determine the size and composition of the stocks required for effective outbreak response. The expansion of the mechanism to include mpox is expected to provide a more structured approach to the distribution of mpox vaccines, particularly during emergencies when demand can rise rapidly. The move builds on measures introduced during the global mpox response. In 2024, WHO and its partners established an Access and Allocation Mechanism (AAM) specifically for mpox vaccines, treatments and diagnostic tests. AAM was designed to support the allocation of limited medical countermeasures to countries and communities where they were most urgently required. Under the new, longer-term arrangement, ICG will continue and strengthen the work initiated through the temporary mechanism. The development also follows a decision by the Gavi Board in 2025 to formally approve the establishment and financing of a global mpox vaccine stockpile under the long-standing ICG framework. ReadMed Journals The stockpile is intended to ensure that vaccine supplies can be mobilised rapidly during outbreaks instead of relying solely on vaccine procurement after an emergency has already escalated. The initiative comes amid continued concern over mpox, a viral disease that can cause fever, rash, swollen lymph nodes and painful lesions. While many people recover, severe disease and death can occur, particularly among vulnerable populations. WHO has stressed the importance of vaccination as part of a broader outbreak response that also includes surveillance, testing, case management, contact tracing, infection prevention and community engagement. The global vaccine initiative is expected to complement, rather than replace, national public health measures. Health authorities and international partners will also need to address challenges around vaccine supply, financing, delivery and equitable access to ensure that the availability of a global stockpile translates into timely vaccination on the ground. The expansion of the ICG mechanism to include mpox represents a shift from short-term emergency arrangements towards a more sustainable system for vaccine preparedness. By maintaining emergency stocks and establishing clearer mechanisms for allocation, global health agencies aim to reduce delays in responding to future mpox outbreaks and ensure that countries most affected are not left behind in accessing lifesaving vaccines. Following a huge outbreak in 2022 that affected over 100 countries, the World Health Organization declared a public health emergency of international concern. “People might have heard it used to be called monkeypox, but monkeys don't have a lot to do with this. So, we now call it the disease Mpox.” Rees said it’s part of a family of viruses that relate to the far more severe smallpox. When Mpox affects people, it causes a range of symptoms, but commonly fever, headaches, muscle aches and feeling lousy. “But the really characteristic thing that we look for are the lesions that develop. And these lesions can be just a few and they can, in more severe cases, be quite a lot. And they start like a sort of red spot and then they grow into a pustule and then they scab over.” If you have the symptoms and the lesions, see a healthcare professional, and once diagnosed, isolate advised Rees. How do people become infected? “It's not like COVID-19, which was spread as a respiratory disease, and it spread through the air and through droplets. And it spread from person to person very easily. Mpox is much more difficult to contract because it's spread through contact, either with somebody who's got lesions.” It can also spread, although much less frequently through little particles that have come from somebody who has got Mpox. So, for example healthcare workers who are changing sheets or towels could get it, Rees said. She went into detail about how the disease is spread. “In the African region in particular, Mpox often occurs in households and is often brought in through children and then it spreads just within the household. “What we're seeing in the outbreaks that have occurred, the large outbreaks that we've seen now globally, is that we're seeing sexual transmission of Mpox. In this case, where we've got a lot of cases in the Western Cape, we're aware of the fact that this is probably within the men who have sex with men community and pretty certainly sexually transmitted through contact.” Can someone recover completely from Mpox or can the infection cause longer term health problems or complications? Rees said if you're a healthy person, you will 99.9% recover completely. “A group that we're very worried about... are people who are what we call immunocompromised. In the South African setting the commonest reason for immunocompromised when your immune system isn't robust and isn't working as well is HIV when you're not on treatment.” She added that in some cases, particularly if there are children, there can be some sequelae. “And in some cases you might see things like lesions occurring in eyes, in which case you can have sort of after effects there. But nearly everyone else will recover completely.” There are 10,000 vaccines which the Department of Health has access to. “And we're encouraging people who are either contacts, in which case go as soon as you can, and you'll get this vaccine called MVA-BN. But the other group we would encourage is people who think they're at high risk.” Are there any medicines that actually treat the virus itself or is treatment mainly about managing the symptoms while the body clears the infection? Rees said treat it just as you do if you have influenza. “Things that will bring down a fever or address muscle aches, etc. So, it really is about managing the symptoms. And obviously, if you've got a lot of lesions on your body, you need to be careful how you manage those. We don't want you to scratch them. Scratching obviously will delay healing.” She said there is one drug called Tecovirimat being used for severe cases in South Africa and globally. Abstract Pathogen genomics has become increasingly integrated into outbreak response, with recent virus epidemics better characterized by large numbers of viral genetic sequences. Effective international communication of viral diversity requires a scalable, adaptable nomenclature, and existing systems may not accommodate rapid sequence generation. The Pango lineage nomenclature system was designed to address these problems and has been applied to SARS-CoV-2 since early 2020. Here we generalize the underlying principles into a framework for virus surveillance. Lineages are defined as epidemiologically meaningful clusters on a phylogenetic tree, initiated from founding lineages and expanded across a phylogeny through a hierarchical system of alphanumeric sublineage names. Six years on from the inception of the Pango system, we discuss challenges and considerations for implementation. We present a Pango lineage framework for chikungunya virus, providing an alternative to the geography-based clade system and designating 21 lineages across 8 founding lineages. This generalization provides a foundation for future genomic surveillance of many viruses, including for emerging outbreaks and the surveillance of endemic pathogens. Systems of virus nomenclature and classification are required to organize and make sense of this genomic information. The International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses classifies viruses down to the species rank1. Description of viral diversity below the species rank has been historically undertaken by the scientific community, and virus variation has been subdivided using a wide variety of terms including type, subtype, genotype, group and clade (Extended Data Table 1)2,3,4. These naming systems typically categorize virus genetic diversity using a phylogenetic tree that is split into a set of clades (monophyletic or paraphyletic groups). The clades are often approximately equally divergent and well supported, thereby capturing persistent groups and enabling robust classification on a scale of months or years. However, heterogeneity among virus species in genetic diversity, in the evolutionary processes driving that diversity and in the availability of genomic data has led to a proliferation of classification systems across viral families. Many such systems were designed when far fewer viral genomes were available and may not provide sufficient granularity with the current pace and scale of virus genome generation. Genomic sequencing during the COVID-19 pandemic has yielded to date more than 17 million publicly shared virus genomes and supported global real-time tracking of the virus’ evolution and spread. Public health actions during the pandemic were directly informed by virus genomics, and a critical enabler of this was the use of shared systems for naming virus genetic diversity, which facilitated unambiguous communication about virus clusters and variants worldwide. In 2020, Rambaut et al. proposed a dynamic nomenclature for SARS-CoV-2 lineages, called Pango, which was designed as a real-time framework for categorizing virus genetic diversity at a high level of phylogenetic resolution5. Over the subsequent years, a voluntary community has curated thousands of Pango lineages from the >17 million SARS-CoV-2 genome sequences, with the aim of capturing and characterizing the leading edge of the pandemic and achieving a globally consistent horizon scanning framework6,7. The widespread adoption of the Pango lineage nomenclature for SARS-CoV-2, coupled with substantial increases in global sequencing capacity as a result of the pandemic, has led to the development of similar lineage systems for other viruses, including mpox virus (MPXV)8, rabies virus9, human respiratory syncytial virus10 and dengue virus11. As genomics becomes a fully integrated component of viral outbreak response and surveillance, many other viruses will require a fine-scale nomenclature capable of categorizing epidemiologically relevant diversity12. Here we outline the key principles of a unified dynamic virus lineage system that formalizes and generalizes the Pango lineage nomenclature. We highlight the applications that are most amenable to a Pango-style nomenclature framework and offer practical guidance for implementation. We present this framework with genomic epidemiology in mind, although Pango lineages could also be applied to well-sampled non-human outbreaks in the case of epizootics. Results Principles of a Pango lineage system nomenclature A Pango lineage nomenclature aims to capture the epidemiologically relevant diversity of a given pathogen on the phylogenetic scale that is most informative to address public health questions. Any such system should therefore be established with a clear public health or evolutionary objective in mind—such as informing control or eradication efforts, tracking outbreak transmission dynamics or monitoring viral evolution in response to new vaccines or therapeutics. By providing a common language for describing emerging viral clusters, a nomenclature system enables consistent communication across institutions, regions and national borders. It can serve as a practical approach to the phylogenetic placement of newly generated virus genomes when datasets are exceptionally large or when users lack the computational resources or time to reconstruct phylogenetic trees de novo, thus enabling rapid data interpretation and decision-making. A Pango lineage is a set of virus sequences that form an epidemiologically relevant cluster on a phylogenetic tree. The data in question can represent either complete genomes or partial genome sequences; however, greater granularity will be achieved if complete genomes are used13. A range of signals may justify the definition of a new lineage, depending on the epidemiology, evolution and socio-medical context of the virus in question. Possible signals include (1) introduction into a new region with onward transmission, (2) changes in phenotype, (3) a notable set (constellation) of mutations that may have phenotypic relevance, (4) recombination or reassortment or (5) evidence of rapid growth relative to other lineages (Fig. 1a). Depending on the virus and its context, only some of these, or distinct, signals may be relevant. For SARS-CoV-2, early lineage definitions were informed by geographical clustering, as movement of people was limited owing to travel restrictions14,15. As the pandemic progressed and global travel resumed, lineage criteria shifted to other epidemiologically relevant signals, including growth advantage, recombination and mutations associated with possible immune escape or vaccine evasion16,17,18,19. In the Pango lineage system, sets of sequences and a phylogenetic tree are used to define which sequences belong to which lineage (Fig. 1b). The system discriminates between two different modes of categorization: designation and assignment. In the designation process, a set of designated sequences are defined, which represent a curated subset of high-quality sequences that definitively and unambiguously belong to that lineage. Thus, the designated sequences form a canonical reference set for a lineage within a given Pango lineage system (although summary information, such as defining mutations, may also accompany this definition). For any given phylogenetic tree, the designated sequences define the scope of the phylogenetic clade of the given lineage. In some cases, the designated sequences will comprise all the genomes within a lineage; however, in many cases, the designated sequences will comprise only a fraction of the total lineage. This will depend on the rate of data generation. When data generation is slow, all high-quality sequences can be feasibly designated. However, for very large or rapidly changing datasets, only a small fraction of new genomes will be designated, with the remainder classified through assignment tools (as was the case for SARS-CoV-2). Sequences that are not designated to a lineage can still be classified through a different process called assignment. Assignment is performed by one or more of a range of methods, including traditional phylogenetics or time-saving approaches such as phylogenetic placement, assessment of defining mutations or machine learning-based classifiers7,20,21. The assignment process is an inferential procedure and therefore carries some uncertainty; it is possible (although unwelcome) for different assignment methods to give different classification results, and this uncertainty should be accounted for when interpreting the results. In addition, assignment may be performed on lower-quality or incomplete sequences, whereas only high-quality sequences are suitable for designation. Definition of a Pango lineage A lineage is defined formally as all viruses that subtend from the most recent common ancestor defined by the set of designated sequences (Fig. 1b). A new lineage must meet the following criteria (Fig. 1c): - 1. It is genetically distinct from the ancestral lineage by one or more mutations. - 2. It has epidemiological relevance, as assessed by a previously defined set of relevant signals. - 3. There is evidence of onward community transmission. - 4. There are a sufficient number of high-quality genomes to evaluate these criteria. The exact thresholds for these criteria will necessarily differ among viruses owing to differences in evolutionary rates, sequencing intensity, the length of time the virus has been circulating in humans and public health use cases. For example, the number of mutations that separate a new lineage from its ancestral diversity may be lower in slower-evolving viruses, or when fine-scale resolution is needed, and the number of genomes required to define a lineage may depend on the volume of data available for a given outbreak. Specific criteria and thresholds should be set for each virus or use case, but can be applied flexibly to allow for unforeseen circumstances, and may also change through time as the epidemiological context changes. A generalized framework for Pango lineages A Pango lineage system provides a hierarchical partition of a phylogenetic tree for a given virus, with the explicit goal of supporting genomic epidemiology for public health (Fig. 2). A lineage system is anchored on a set of founding lineages present at an initial time point of interest. For SARS-CoV-2, the earliest sequenced genomes were grouped into two founding lineages, A and B, distinguished by two mutations, which were later found to correspond to a scenario of at least two separate introductions of the virus into the human population22. For the sustained human outbreak of clade IIb MPXV, a single founding lineage, A, captured the initial diversity5,8. For any Pango lineage system, we recommend choosing an initial time point to divide existing viral diversity into one or more founding lineages, each labelled with an alphabetic code on a phylogeny (Fig. 3a,b). In the case of zoonotic spillover giving rise to a human outbreak, it would be intuitive to use the point of spillover as the initial time point. For endemic viruses with pre-existing genetic diversity, the starting point should capture currently circulating diversity while excluding extinct lineages in order to minimize complexity and reduce the burden of characterizing historic data (Fig. 3). Nomenclature systems that are less granular and use more persistent lineage labels may be better suited to describing longer-term endemic diversity. Genetic diversity that predates the chosen initial time point can be treated as ‘outside’ the nomenclature system (O). There is no requirement for Pango lineages to be similar in size, nor a need to designate all pre-existing diversity. Any undesignated diversity that later becomes relevant for public health can be added to the Pango lineage system as an additional founding lineage (Fig. 3c). Given the set of founding lineages, the designation of further descendant lineages is thereafter guided by the lineage designation rules and associated genotypic and epidemiological criteria (Fig. 3d). In situations where an established clade system already exists, the founding lineages for a Pango-style system may align directly with existing nomenclature labels, which may be particularly useful for re-emerging outbreaks of an endemic pathogen. Lineage names follow a dot-separated alphanumeric structure that encodes local phylogenetic (ancestor–descendant) relationships (Fig. 2). A new sublineage is defined by appending a dot and the next available number (for example, the 75th sublineage of BA.2 to be designated becomes BA.2.75). The names themselves should not attempt to encode any other features, such as phenotypic information or specific mutations; these features can be stored in associated metadata entries for each lineage. To maintain lineage name readability as the virus diversifies, an alias is introduced at a defined hierarchical level. SARS-CoV-2 lineages adopt an alias after three levels, whereas the MPXV system introduces an alias following two. The alias replaces the entire prefix using the next available alphabetic code (excluding X, I and O, which are reserved letters). Once all single letters have been applied, aliases will be formed using two letters; these aliases continue to be incremented alphabetically with the right-hand-most letter incremented first. For example, AA is the first alias following Z, while AB immediately follows AA, and BA immediately follows AZ. Should aliases of greater length than two letters be required, their alphabetic incrementation begins with the right-hand-most letter and proceeds with letters from right to left. When a circulating recombinant is identified, a new founding lineage is designated with the next alphabetic code prefixed with an X (Fig. 3d). For example, SARS-CoV-2 lineage XBB is a recombinant of BJ.1 (BA.2.10.1.1) and BA.2.75 (BA.2.75.3.1.1.1), and the next circulating recombinant identified and designated following XBB would be XBC, and so on. Knowing the number and exact location of precise recombination breakpoints is not required for designation, although they should be recorded where known in the associated lineage metadata entry. Note that designation can proceed even when there is uncertainty in the identification of the parental lineages of a recombinant lineage. For reassorting viruses, in which virus genome segments follow distinct evolutionary ancestries, a Pango lineage nomenclature can be anchored to a single representative segment. The choice of segment can be adapted for the intended application; for example, an appropriate choice for genomic epidemiology will be the one most relevant for transmission or antigenicity, which is often the fastest evolving and can provide the most resolution over short timescales. Reassortment of the other segments that are epidemiologically relevant could trigger a new lineage designation, an event that could be specifically encoded in the phylogeny of the representative segment for assignment purposes. Broader clade nomenclatures can track the diversity of the other segments and inform the identification of reassortants23,24. Because the goal of Pango is to capture the emerging edge of an outbreak, the phylogeny and lineage definitions must remain dynamic in response to the discovery of new information (Fig. 3c). Lineages can be withdrawn from use if, in the light of additional data or new epidemiological information, their designation criteria are no longer met (any such lineage names should be permanently deprecated and never reused). Lineages can also be split if a previously monophyletic group subsequently resolves into multiple epidemiologically relevant clusters or revised to include different sequences following new sampling. This flexibility allows early action, rather than waiting for stable clades to form through further ongoing transmission and evolution. For SARS-CoV-2 lineages, minor revisions were often needed to refine definitions as new data were shared; however, these definitions were available to the research community much more rapidly as a result of a willingness to act early and review them. Granularity of a Pango-style nomenclature No single nomenclature can serve all purposes, and a Pango lineage system is not intended to supersede existing taxonomy. Rather, it functions as a complementary framework alongside naming schemes with different granularities and timeliness. For endemic pathogen surveillance that largely samples deep, historic diversity, a fine-scale lineage nomenclature such as Pango may be of limited utility. Traditional phylogenetic analyses and clade-based nomenclatures, which incorporate minimum size and persistence criteria, are better suited to capturing long-term evolutionary patterns such as antigenic turnover8,23. In these contexts, a fine-scale system may introduce unnecessary complexity by overcategorizing the phylogenetic diversity without a clear benefit to public health. However, a Pango lineage system may be valuable for endemic pathogens in cases when new selective pressures are expected to shape short-term evolution, such as following the introduction of a novel vaccine or antiviral treatment (for example, dengue virus or respiratory syncytial virus), or when eradication programmes require close tracking of contemporary transmission (for example, measles virus or poliovirus).
