Heterogeneous co-circulation of multiple DENV-1 and DENV-2 genotypes in Vung Tau during the 2023 dengue outbreak in Vietnam

Authors: Melissa Alifa Da Costa, Hau Thi Bich Vu, Adeline Syin Lian Yeo, Jessica Manalaysay, Betül Ay, Kazumi Haga, Hina Horio, Islam Bakhrom Karimov, Thuy Thi Thu Nguyen, Hang Le Khanh Nguyen
Original abstract (English)
Dengue virus (DENV) remains a significant global health threat, particularly in hyper-endemic regions like Vietnam, where the co-circulation of multiple serotypes and genotypes complicates disease control. In 2023, 6.5 million dengue cases and 6,800 deaths were reported globally, marking the largest dengue outbreak in the world. Vietnam recorded 149,557 dengue cases and 36 deaths, with a notable dominance of the DENV-2 serotype. This study provides a comprehensive genomic characterization of the 2023 outbreak using isolates collected from hospitalized patients (n = 148; 66.89% adults). To ensure robust viral recovery, we propagated 90 isolates using a low-passage, antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE)-based system. Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) of 53 isolates yielded 47 consensus sequences, including 26 complete genomes. Phylogenetic analysis revealed the simultaneous co-circulation of diverse DENV-1 and DENV-2 genotypes. DENV-1 isolates were predominantly genotype I (86%), clustering closely with prior Vietnamese strains and suggesting sustained endemic maintenance, while genotype IV (14%) was detected sporadically. Among DENV-2 isolates, the Cosmopolitan genotype was predominant (98%), with a smaller proportion (2%) belonging to the Asian 1 genotype. Both genotypes clustered with previously reported Vietnamese and regional Southeast Asian strains, indicating ongoing local circulation and regional viral exchange. Comparative mutational analysis of DENV-2 Cosmopolitan isolates identified multiple non-synonymous amino acid substitutions across the viral genome. Fourteen substitutions were shared between Vietnamese historical (2019-2022) and 2023 isolates, while 24 substitutions were unique to the 2023 outbreak strains. The majority of amino acid changes were concentrated within the NS5, E, NS1, and NS3 proteins, suggesting continued genetic diversification within the circulating Cosmopolitan lineage. Overall, these findings demonstrate the persistence of established DENV lineages alongside ongoing viral evolution during the 2023 outbreak in Vietnam. This study highlights the value of whole-genome surveillance for monitoring dengue virus genetic diversity and provides insight that may support future epidemiological surveillance, vaccine evaluation, and outbreak preparedness.

Characterization of acute dengue immune status and serotype surveillance in three regions of Burkina Faso: a hospital-based cross-sectional study.

Authors: Louis Robert Wendyam Belem, Arsène Zongo, Raymond Karlhis Yao, Christelle Adelaide Zilsamba Lankouande, Armand Vital Wenceslas Taïta, Tarwendpanga François Xavier Ouédraogo, Benewêndê Philippe Kabore, Abdoul Bamogo, Raïssa Muriel de Souza, Yacouba Kouadima
Original abstract (English)
INTRODUCTION: In recent years, dengue has become a significant public health concern in Burkina Faso, associated with severe disease and death. Rapid characterization of acute dengue serostatus is useful for identifying patients at risk, informing transmission dynamics, and supporting public health decision-making. This study aimed to characterize dengue immune status in three regions of Burkina Faso to inform pre-vaccination screening, evaluate a commercial combined DENV NS1, IgM, and IgG rapid diagnostic test (RDT) kit, and investigate DENV serotype circulation. METHODS: A multicenter study was conducted between 2023 and 2025 in Bobo-Dioulasso, Houndé, Ouagadougou, and Gaoua. Blood samples were collected from patients with suspected dengue. Acute DENV infections and serotypes were identified using real-time RT-PCR, and immune status was characterized by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) according to WHO criteria. Serum samples were screened using the reference standard (RT-PCR combined with ELISA) to evaluate the performance of a commercial DENV NS1, IgM, and IgG RDT kit in characterizing dengue immune status. RESULTS: Primary dengue accounted for 75.9%, 80.4%, and 71.4% in 2023, 2024, and 2025, respectively. The sensitivity of the RDT for determining primary and post-primary dengue during the first 5 days of illness was 93.3% and 94.7%, respectively. DENV-1, DENV-2, and DENV-3 accounted for 47.0%, 5.6%, and 47.4% of cases, respectively. DENV-4 was not detected. The diagnostic specificity of the RDT for both primary and post-primary dengue was 100%. CONCLUSIONS: Primary dengue cases predominated, while severe dengue was associated with post-primary infection. The RDT proved to be a useful point-of-care tool for discriminating dengue immune status. DENV-3 and DENV-1 were the predominant serotypes driving recent transmission dynamics. The findings may help inform future vaccination strategies.

作者： Erika Vanessa Jimenez-Posada, Oscar Marino Mosquera-Martínez, Sara M Robledo, Diego Durango, Wiston Quiñones, Samuel Vizcaíno-Páez, Marlen Martinez-Gutierrez
摘要中文翻译蚊媒虫媒病毒(包括登革病毒(DENV)、寨卡病毒(ZIKV)和基孔肯雅病毒(CHIKV))构成了日益增长的全球健康负担,但目前针对这些感染的特异性抗病毒药物疗法尚不普及。这种未满足的医疗需求凸显了从生物相关来源(如植物来源的天然产物)中识别新型抗病毒药物化合物的重要性。本研究采用基于生物活性的筛选方法,对八种茄科植物的甲醇提取物进行分析,以鉴定具有抗病毒药物活性的化合物,针对Vero细胞中DENV-2的感染。五种提取物显著降低了病毒的感染性,其中Solanum ovalifolium和Cestrum sp.在非细胞毒性浓度下实现了完全抑制。随后的分级和纯化过程分离出四种螺甾烷型甾体皂苷,其结构通过超高效液相色谱-二极管阵列检测-质谱(UPLC-DAD-MS)和一维/二维核磁共振(1D/2D NMR)光谱进行表征。功能实验表明,这些化合物抑制DENV复制,表现为病毒RNA水平、NS1和NS3蛋白表达以及传染性病毒滴度的显著下降。阶段特异性分析显示,其抗病毒药物活性主要与病毒生命周期的细胞内阶段相关,这与抑制病毒复制和/或蛋白质加工一致。分子对接分析支持这些发现,预测这些化合物与关键病毒酶(特别是NS5 RNA依赖性RNA聚合酶和NS3解旋酶/蛋白酶)具有有利的相互作用。重要的是,分离出的化合物对ZIKV和CHIKV也表现出抗病毒药物活性,表明其具有跨虫媒病毒的疗效。综上所述,这些发现表明螺甾烷皂苷是具有前景的抗病毒药物骨架,并支持其进一步开发为广谱抗病毒药物药物。

Solanaceae-Derived Spirostane Saponins Inhibit Dengue Virus Replication and Viral Protein Expression and Exhibit Activity Against Multiple Arboviruses.

Authors: Erika Vanessa Jimenez-Posada, Oscar Marino Mosquera-Martínez, Sara M Robledo, Diego Durango, Wiston Quiñones, Samuel Vizcaíno-Páez, Marlen Martinez-Gutierrez
Original abstract (English)
Mosquito-borne arboviruses, including dengue virus (DENV), Zika virus (ZIKV), and chikungunya virus (CHIKV), represent a growing global health burden, yet no specific antiviral therapies are currently available for most of these infections. This unmet need highlights the importance of identifying novel antiviral compounds from biologically relevant sources such as plant-derived natural products. In this study, a bioactivity-guided approach was applied to methanolic extracts from eight Solanaceae species to identify compounds with antiviral activity against DENV-2 in Vero cells. Five extracts significantly reduced viral infectivity, with Solanum ovalifolium and Cestrum sp. achieving complete inhibition at non-cytotoxic concentrations. Subsequent fractionation and purification led to the isolation of four spirostane-type steroidal saponins, structurally characterized by UPLC-DAD-MS and 1D/2D NMR spectroscopy. Functional assays demonstrated that these compounds inhibit DENV replication, as evidenced by marked reductions in viral RNA levels, NS1 and NS3 protein expression, and infectious viral titers. Stage-specific analyses revealed that their antiviral activity is primarily associated with intracellular phases of the viral life cycle, consistent with inhibition of viral replication and/or protein processing. Molecular docking analyses supported these findings by predicting favorable interactions with key viral enzymes, particularly NS5 RNA-dependent RNA polymerase and NS3 helicase/protease. Importantly, the isolated compounds retained antiviral activity against ZIKV and CHIKV, indicating cross-arboviral efficacy. Together, these findings identify spirostane saponins as promising antiviral scaffolds and support their further development as broad-spectrum antiviral agents.

Authors: Dwaipayan Chaudhuri, Kalyan Giri
Original abstract (English)
The Dengue virus (DENV) capsid protein plays an important role in the viral lifecycle, including encapsidation of the genome, recruitment of lipid droplets, and assembly of particles. Capsid protein includes a hydrophobic pocket, which plays a central role in these functions and switches between closed and open conformations. This work studies the conformational change pathway of the capsid protein, specifically how the molecular mechanisms controlling the opening and closing of the hydrophobic pocket are controlled. By using a combination of different types of molecular dynamics simulations and enhanced sampling methods, we share insights on the structural rearrangements that play a crucial role in governing these transitions. Our results show that lipid binding modulates the accessibility of the hydrophobic pocket through environmental cues. In the open conformation, the pocket is involved in the interaction with lipids, stabilization of capsid dimers, and the promotion of immature virus assembly. Conversely, the closed conformation protects the hydrophobic pocket from the surrounding aqueous media as also preventing premature interactions in the solution phase that might disrupt protein stability. Such insights allow a deep understanding of capsid protein's conformational dynamics and represent a promising set of potent targets for antiviral strategies aiming to interfere with its functional transitions.

Targeting CLK1 by CRISPR tools and pharmacological inhibition modulates the innate immune response and dengue virus replication

Authors: Berta Pozzi, Agustina Magalí Lucero, Valeria Buggiano, Miguel Angel Pelaez, Cybele Garcia, Anabella Srebrow
Original abstract (English)
Alternative splicing is a key regulatory mechanism known to be altered upon viral infection. These alterations can arise both from direct viral interference with the splicing machinery and from cellular responses such as activation of innate immunity. Here, we investigated splicing changes shared between dengue virus (DENV) infection and interferon (IFN) treatment in cultured cells, the latter serving as a model for virus-independent innate immune activation. Among the common events, we identified an increased production of non-coding mRNA isoforms from the CLK1 gene, which gives rise to a kinase that phosphorylates splicing factors including SR proteins and spliceosomal components. Consistent with this finding, IFN treatment led to a reduction in CLK1 protein levels. Using stable cell lines with CRISPR/dCas9-mediated modulation of CLK1 expression, we found that silencing CLK1 enhanced the induction of immune response genes, while its over-expression attenuated it. Inhibition of CLK1 kinase activity with the pharmacological inhibitor TG003 further potentiated IFN-induced gene expression and reduced DENV replication. Altogether, these results identify CLK1 as a proviral negative regulator of IFN-stimulated gene expression and suggest that its inhibition could enhance antiviral defenses and become a target for therapeutic strategies.

A single dose of Dengvaxia is associated with transient reduction in dengue infection risk among dengue-naïve children

Authors: Maria Abad Fernandez, Jedas Veronica Daag, Lindsay D. Hein, Kristal An Agrupis, Maria Vinna Crisostomo, Michelle Ylade, Jacqueline Deen, Laura J. White, Lakshmanane Premkumar, Ana Lena Lopez
Original abstract (English)
Dengvaxia ® induces antibody responses in dengue virus (DENV)-naïve children, but the durability of these responses and their impact on subsequent wild-type (WT) DENV infection remain unclear. Although previous studies have primarily evaluated vaccine efficacy against dengue disease, the impact of Dengvaxia ® on susceptibility to WT DENV infection and subsequent immune responses remains less well defined. We evaluated vaccine-induced immunity and susceptibility to WT DENV infection in a longitudinal cohort of 186 seronegative children, including 114 who received a single dose of Dengvaxia ® and 72 who remained unvaccinated, followed for 40 months. Samples collected at baseline and two follow-up time points (20 months [T1] and 40 months [T2]) were analyzed using yellow fever virus (YFV) non-structural protein 1 (NS1) antibody assays, which is expressed by Dengvaxia ® but not by WT DENV, enabling discrimination between vaccine-associated and WT infection-induced responses. At T1, 61% of vaccinated children were responders. Additional low-level YFV NS1 antibody responses were detected in some children originally classified as vaccine non-responders, suggesting that the predefined binary classification may not capture the full range of vaccine-associated antibody responses. Low-level YFV NS1 responses remained detectable through T2. During the first follow-up period (baseline-T1), vaccination was associated with a reduced risk of WT DENV infection (vaccine effectiveness [VE]=45%). During the second period (T1-T2), no difference in infection risk was detected between groups. Following breakthrough infection during the first follow-up period, vaccinated children developed antibody responses with greater magnitude and broader serotype breadth than unvaccinated children with primary infection, consistent with vaccine-associated immune priming following WT DENV infection. In contrast, during the second period (T1-T2), differences in antibody magnitude and breadth following infection were no longer detected between groups. Together, these findings suggest that a single dose of Dengvaxia ® in DENV-seronegative children was associated with a reduced risk of WT DENV infection and with differences in subsequent antibody responses during the first follow-up period, but these associations were no longer observed during the second period.

Novel Omics Strategies To Combat Dengue Infection and Disease Progression

Authors: Aria Jazdarehee
Original abstract (English)
The dengue virus (DENV) is a continuing global health threat rapidly spreading from its origins in tropical and subtropical regions. The virus is capable of causing severe and life-threatening diseases including dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF) and dengue shock syndrome (DSS). Fortunately, mortality is drastically reduced with rapid diagnosis and treatment. Effective and efficient disease diagnosis and treatment, however, is limited by our knowledge of how the virus causes the disease; thus, no specific treatments are currently available to mitigate DENV infection. Novel state-of-the-art Omics approaches, which involve detecting and quantifying biological molecules, may be used to investigate how molecular and cellular pathways are perturbed during viral infection. These new approaches may serve as promising tools for discovering host biomarkers associated with infection and viral disease progression, and may unravel new host-directed therapeutic targets. In this article, we discuss the potential of these Omics approaches to revolutionize the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of emerging and re-emerging viral diseases. Specifically, we ask how Omics methods can be integrated in the clinic to facilitate data collection for researchers, and how these data can influence our ability to diagnose, prognose, and treat those infected with DENV. We then investigate the host pathways involved in the progression from dengue fever to severe dengue (DHF and DSS), based on data found in preliminary Omics studies. Finally, in line with our holistic approach, we ask whether infection with the Zika virus influences DENV disease progression. Omics approaches have the potential to revolutionize our understanding of viral disease pathogenesis, but careful planning and collaboration with clinicians are required before such findings can be made.

The orthoflaviviral nonstructural protein 2A (NS2A) is critical for both viral replication and virion assembly. However, due to its small size, hydrophobicity, and the absence of structural information or protein-specific antibodies, we lack a clear understanding of how NS2A participates in these disparate processes. Authors: Alberta Trilestari, Maria Widyarini, Kevin Grahadian
Original abstract (English)
The implementation of Indonesia's Case-Based Groups (INA-CBGs) payment system aims to improve efficiency in hospital financing. However, discrepancies between reimbursement and actual treatment costs remain a significant challenge for hospital financial sustainability. This study aimed to analyze the Cost Recovery Rate (CRR) by comparing hospital tariffs, INA-CBGs reimbursement, and the actual cost of treating Grade I Dengue Haemorrhagic Fever (DHF) patients while identifying factors contributing to cost discrepancies. A qualitative single embedded case study was conducted at a Type C private hospital in Palembang, Indonesia. Secondary financial and clinical data from 35 Class III inpatients with Grade I DHF were analyzed alongside semi-structured interviews with five key informants representing hospital management, clinical services, finance, and the casemix unit. Data were analyzed through document review, cost comparison, CRR calculation, and thematic analysis. The findings revealed differences between the hospital's internal tariff (Rp82,925,300), INA-CBGs reimbursement (Rp69,730,500), and the actual treatment cost (Rp72,479,198). The resulting Cost Recovery Rate (CRR) of 96.21% indicated that INA-CBGs reimbursement was insufficient to fully recover the actual cost of care. The financing gap was influenced by the interaction of several factors, including the package-based INA-CBGs payment mechanism, length of hospital stay, patients' clinical conditions, diagnosis and procedure coding accuracy, adherence to clinical pathways, laboratory and pharmaceutical utilization, and overall hospital resource efficiency. This study demonstrates that financial sustainability under the INA-CBGs system depends not only on reimbursement policies but also on integrated clinical and operational management. Strengthening clinical pathway adherence, documentation and coding accuracy, resource utilization, and cost monitoring may improve hospital financial performance while maintaining the quality and safety of patient care.

Florida Dengue Outbreak 2026: Symptoms, Testing, Treatment, and Prevention Florida’s dengue outbreak highlights the importance of recognizing symptoms, identifying warning signs, and preventing mosquito bites. Ubong Johnson, MD, explains local transmission, diagnostic testing, treatment, and practical prevention measures. Florida has recorded a recent spike in the number of dengue fever cases, with one death confirmed in September. The latest weekly update posted by the Florida Department of Health (Week 39, September 27 to October 3) confirms 296 locally acquired cases across 8 counties.1 Hillsborough County takes the biggest hit, with 259 cases and one confirmed death: an 80-year-old Tampa woman who died in September. Other affected counties are Pinellas (20), Miami-Dade (8), Orange (3), Pasco (3), Palm Beach (1), Polk (1), and Broward (1).1 The Florida dengue spike is likely due to international travel, an increase in local mosquito population, and low population immunity. These are plausible contributing factors; however, the surveillance report does not show that any one of them caused this outbreak. As an international travel hub, trade route, and center for tourism, Florida sees a continuous influx of visitors from endemic areas across Latin America and the Caribbean, some of whom are infected with the dengue virus.2 When Aedes aegypti, a mosquito species that prefers to feed in the daytime, or, less frequently, Aedes albopictus, bites a person with the dengue virus circulating in the blood, it can pick up the virus. This person may be an international visitor, a Florida resident returning from travel, or someone already infected locally, and may have no symptoms. When an infected mosquito bites someone else, it can spread the virus. There are 4 serotypes of dengue virus: DENV-1, DENV-2, DENV-3, and DENV-4. Of the local cases serotyped in the Week 39 report, 273 were DENV-2, and 2 were DENV-3. Because Aedes aegypti is a nervous mosquito that is easily disturbed while feeding, it will usually bite more than one person before completing a blood meal.3 More bites create more opportunities for transmission. As transmission bites and transmission continue, the dengue virus eventually enters the local population and starts to spread amongst locals without the need for international hosts.4 Heat contributes to the increasing rate of dengue fever transmission by increasing the local mosquito population and by shortening the extrinsic incubation period. The virus typically replicates in a mosquito’s midgut and spreads to other tissues, including the salivary gland, before transmission is possible. WHO says this takes about 8 to 12 days at 25–28°C. In hotter seasons, the virus can mature more quickly, leading to faster transmission.5 Since prior infection generally gives long-lasting protection against the same dengue virus serotype and only temporary protection against the other serotypes, people living in endemic areas usually develop a type of immunity against dengue fever called homotypic immunity.6 A second infection with a different dengue virus serotype can result in a more severe disease, as previously acquired antibodies act as “Trojan horses” that permit easier entry of the virus into the cell. Symptoms of dengue fever, if they appear, usually begin 4 to 6 days after the infecting bite. CDC’s yellow book describes a typical incubation of 5 to 7 days, within a range of about 3 to 10 days.7 Some people experience no symptoms. Where symptoms are present, dengue fever has 3 courses: a febrile phase, a critical phase, and a recovery phase.8 The febrile phase lasts 2 to 7 days and can occur in 2 phases. It begins with a high fever that reaches up to 104°F (40°C), a headache, and pain around the eyes. There may be muscle soreness, joint pain, fatigue, facial flushing, and loss of appetite.9 A rash usually shows up around 2 to 6 days after the fever starts, and there may be some bleeding trouble. In children and in patients who later develop a severe form of the disease, there may be signs of stomach trouble, such as nausea and vomiting. Most patients recover at the end of the febrile phase. The critical phase typically begins when the fever subsides and lasts 24 hours. About 1 in 20 people who become ill with dengue develop severe dengue.8 In severe dengue, fluid seeps out of blood vessels. This can cause fluid build-up around the lungs and in the abdomen, presenting as difficulty in breathing or abdominal swelling, respectively. Severe dengue is characterized by plasma leakage that causes shock or respiratory distress, severe bleeding, or severe organ involvement. Early compensated shock can occur before hypotension. A marked reduction in blood sodium can also occur, although this doesn’t define severe dengue. Some patients may experience bleeding due to a drop in platelet count, which shows up as blood in stool, blood in vomit, and excessive menstrual bleeding.10 As fluid leakage from blood vessels subsides, patients in the critical phase enter the recovery phase and begin to improve. Clinicians suspect dengue when a person has an acute fever and 2 or more classic symptoms, such as pain behind the eye, severe muscle ache, nausea/vomiting, or a maculopapular rash, especially if this person lives in or has recently traveled to an area with dengue transmission. Dengue fever is not confirmed clinically. Some lab work is needed to distinguish the disease from other conditions that resemble it, such as COVID-19, acute gastroenteritis, or leptospirosis. Testing helps confirm the diagnosis, but management of suspected dengue should begin without waiting for results. Reverse Transcription-Polymerase Chain Reaction (RT-PCR) detects dengue virus in serum and can identify the infecting serotype. Sensitivity depends on the assay, the specimen, and the timing. CDC recommends a nucleic acid test, such as RT-PCR, plus IgM or NS1 antigen plus IgM, during days 0 to 7 of illness. After day 7, IgM is the primary test. A negative RT-PCR or NS1 doesn’t rule out dengue.11 Dengue fever can be treated at home or in the hospital. At home, patients can benefit from drinking enough fluids, getting adequate rest, and taking acetaminophen for fever or pain. Aspirin and other NSAIDs, including ibuprofen, should be avoided because they can increase the risk of bleeding. Patients with warning signs, certain underlying health conditions, or an inability to keep down oral fluids may need hospital care. In the hospital, patients may be given IV fluids with careful monitoring to avoid fluid overload. Blood transfusion is considered when there is clinically significant bleeding or suspected occult bleeding. If you are currently experiencing any symptoms of dengue fever or know someone who is, seek medical care or encourage them to do so. Dengue fever is preventable.12 Prevention aims to break transmission cycles by stopping the vector from multiplying and biting people, as well as directly protecting individuals from the effects of a mosquito bite, should one get bitten. You can protect yourself from mosquito bites by using insect repellent, wearing loose-fitting long-sleeve clothes, using insecticide-treated nets, using air conditioners, and controlling mosquito populations around you. You can eliminate potential mosquito breeding grounds by emptying, covering, or cleaning containers that hold stagnant water. Public health agencies may support this by making larvicides available for use in non-potable water systems. Travel precautions can reduce the risk of the virus being introduced and spreading. CDC recommends that travelers returning from dengue-risk areas prevent mosquito bites for 3 weeks, even if they feel well, and watch out for symptoms for 2 weeks. There are currently no dengue vaccines available in the United States, under CDC guidance. Dengvaxia has been discontinued and is no longer available. And Qdenga is not available or recommended in the United States. Travelers should review destination-specific notes, receive pretravel advice, and prevent mosquito bites.
