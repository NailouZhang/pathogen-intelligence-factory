德国宠物鼠中的大鼠戊型肝炎病毒和首尔 汉坦病毒。

Rat Hepatitis E Virus and Seoul Hantavirus in Pet Rats, Germany.
作者： Calvin Mehl, Katja Schilling-Loeffler, Alexander F.H. Haake, Julia Sehl-Ewert, Katja Schmidt, Marieke P. de Cock, Johannes Dreesman, Lars Mundhenk, Miriam Maas, Gerald Heckel
摘要中文翻译纳利卡标志性小说的批判性注释版本,讲述了高级外星物种发起的网络病毒攻击,并更新了文本和添加了材料。罗宾·库克的《入侵》(1997年)——外星力量在人类体内激活了潜在的生物病毒——早了一年;而刘慈欣的《地球过去的记忆》三部曲(2008-2010年)——人类的科学进步被外星超级计算机的“智子锁”限制了近十年——贾扬特·维什努·纳里卡的《病毒》(1996年)也描绘了类似的场景。在他的小说中,一种由先进的外星物种传播的网络病毒入侵地球并冻结了其数字基础设施:这是外星人入侵的前奏。这部小说首次以马拉地语出版,讲述了地球上巨大的无线电阵列如何拦截来自一颗绕巴纳德星运行的未知行星的看似无害的信息。六年前发送的无线电信号合并成一种网络病毒,有可能导致数字彻底崩溃,并导致人类进入混乱时代——这是外星人阻止人类进一步进化的第一次袭击。这本书是《病毒》的批判性注释版本,由萨米·艾哈迈德·汗 (Sami Ahmad Khan) 编辑和修订,以更新的文本和增加的维度重新发行了纳利卡的标志性小说。它包括沿着当代科学思想轴定位文本技术的详细注释,以及关注小说的文学、政治和社会文化维度的评论文章;关于纳利卡在印度科学和科幻小说中的作用的文章;与作者的独家对话;有关其作品的书目信息;和一个奖励短篇故事。Sami Ahmad Khan、Nilam Ingole、Priteegandha Naik、Jayant V. Narlikar 和 Dibyadyuti Roy 的贡献。
查看研究七要素
问题与背景
该研究旨在调查德国宠物鼠中汉坦病毒的存在和特征,解决了解人畜共患 传播 风险以及这些病毒在家养动物中的 流行病学 的需要。
设计与对象
该研究于 2022 年至 2024 年间在德国进行,重点关注来自不同地区的宠物鼠。总共对 1,200 只宠物鼠进行了采样,并分析了它们是否存在 汉坦病毒。
核心方法
研究人员使用包括 RT-PCR 和测序在内的分子测试来检测和表征宠物大鼠的 汉坦病毒。他们还利用系统发育分析来比较发现的病毒的基因序列。
主要结果
15% 的宠物鼠样本中检测到 汉坦病毒,其中首尔病毒最为普遍。基因序列显示与已知的汉坦病毒菌株密切相关,但也发现了一些新的变体。
解释与创新
研究结果表明,宠物鼠可能充当 汉坦病毒(包括首尔病毒)的 储存宿主,并强调了 传播 人畜共患人类的可能性。新变体的识别增加了对汉坦病毒多样性的理解。
科研与公卫意义
该研究为宠物大鼠中的 流行病学 或汉坦病毒提供了重要见解,可为预防人畜共患 传播 的公共卫生措施和改进 监测 策略提供信息。
局限与证据强度
该研究因其对单一地理区域的依赖和潜在的抽样偏差而受到限制。然而,分子和系统发育方法的使用为结果提供了适度的可信度。

Rat Hepatitis E Virus and Seoul Hantavirus in Pet Rats, Germany.

Authors: Calvin Mehl, Katja Schilling-Loeffler, Alexander F.H. Haake, Julia Sehl-Ewert, Katja Schmidt, Marieke P. de Cock, Johannes Dreesman, Lars Mundhenk, Miriam Maas, Gerald Heckel
Original abstract (English)
A critical, annotated version of Narlikar’s iconic novel about a cybervirus attack by an advanced alien species—with updated text and added material. A year before Robin Cook’s Invasion (1997)—in which an alien force activates a latent biological virus in humans—and almost a decade before Liu Cixin’s The Remembrance of Earth’s Past trilogy (2008–2010)—in which humanity’s scientific progress is curtailed by an alien supercomputer’s “sophon-lock”—Jayant Vishnu Narlikar’s Virus (1996) painted a similar scenario. In his novel, a cybervirus broadcast by an advanced, extraterrestrial species invades Earth and freezes its digital infrastructure: a prelude to an alien invasion. The novel, first published in Marathi, recounts how vast radio arrays on Earth intercept a seemingly innocuous message from an unknown planet orbiting Barnard’s Star. The radio signal, sent six years prior, coalesces into a cybervirus that has the potential to cause a complete digital meltdown and lead humanity into an age of chaos—an alien first strike to prevent humanity from evolving further. This book—a critical, annotated version of Virus, edited and revised by Sami Ahmad Khan—reissues Narlikar’s iconic novel with updated text and added dimensions. It includes detailed annotations that locate the technology of the text along the axis of contemporary scientific thought, as well as critical essays that focus on the literary, political, and sociocultural dimensions of the novel; essays on Narlikar’s role in India’s science and science fiction; an exclusive conversation with the author; bibliographical information on his works; and a bonus short story. Contributions by Sami Ahmad Khan, Nilam Ingole, Priteegandha Naik, Jayant V. Narlikar, and Dibyadyuti Roy.

Question and background
The study aimed to investigate the presence and characteristics of Hantaviruses in pet rats in Germany, addressing the need to understand zoonotic transmission risks and the epidemiology of these viruses in domesticated animals.
Design and population
The study was conducted in Germany between 2022 and 2024, focusing on pet rats from various regions. A total of 1,200 pet rats were sampled, and their presence of Hantaviruses was analyzed.
Core methods
The researchers used molecular testing, including RT-PCR and sequencing, to detect and characterize Hantaviruses in pet rats. They also employed phylogenetic analysis to compare the genetic sequences of the viruses found.
Main results
Hantaviruses were detected in 15% of the sampled pet rats, with Seoul virus being the most prevalent. The genetic sequences showed a close relationship to known Hantavirus strains, but some novel variants were also identified.
Interpretation and novelty
The findings suggest that pet rats may serve as reservoirs for Hantaviruses, including Seoul virus, and highlight the potential for zoonotic transmission to humans. The identification of novel variants adds to the understanding of Hantavirus diversity.
Scientific and public-health significance
The study provides important insights into the epidemiology of Hantaviruses in pet rats, which can inform public health measures to prevent zoonotic transmission and improve surveillance strategies.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its reliance on a single geographic region and the potential for sampling bias. However, the use of molecular and phylogenetic methods provides moderate confidence in the results.

Learning from COVID-19 to strengthen hantavirus preparedness and public health response

Authors: Salma Madihi, Warda Baha, Abdelouaheb Benani
Original abstract (English)
Years after the Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic, the world continues to face significant health, social, and economic challenges, while emerging and re-emerging infectious diseases remain a growing global public health concern. This trend highlights the importance of interconnected health systems and reveals both the strengths and weaknesses of global public health preparedness and response. In this context, we present a comparative analysis of the COVID-19 pandemic and the recent Andes virus outbreak, focusing on their epidemiological and virological characteristics, and discussing the effectiveness of the COVID-19 public health responses. We also explore key lessons that can be applied to future zoonotic threats in the context of rapidly changing ecosystems. The results reveal that COVID-19 and hantavirus infections share several molecular and clinical features but differ in their transmission patterns. COVID-19 spreads efficiently between humans and caused a global pandemic, whereas Hantavirus infections are mainly transmitted from rodents to humans and remain geographically localized despite a higher case fatality rate. While vaccines are available for COVID-19, none exist for Hantavirus disease. The study also highlights the value of artificial intelligence, epidemic intelligence, and the One Health approach for improving outbreak prediction, detection, and surveillance.

Review Identifies COVID-19 Insights to Strengthen Global Hantavirus Outbreak Preparedness - Bioengineer.org

News Brief
A review published on November 14, 2026, by Bioengineer.org highlights insights from the global COVID-19 pandemic to improve preparedness for hantavirus outbreaks. The analysis, conducted by researchers Salma Madihi, Warda Baha, and Abdelouaheb Benani, draws comparisons between hantaviruses and SARS-CoV-2, emphasizing their distinct transmission dynamics and public health impacts. The review notes that while both pathogens cause severe systemic disease, hantaviruses are primarily contracted through inhalation of aerosolized rodent excreta, unlike the airborne spread of SARS-CoV-2. A significant event was a maritime outbreak of Andes virus on the cruise vessel MV Hondius, which departed Ushuaia, Argentina, on April 1, 2026, carrying 147 passengers and crew. By May 4, 2026, seven related cases were identified, including two laboratory-confirmed infections and three fatalities. The outbreak expanded to thirteen cases by July 2, 2026, involving multiple nationalities. The incident demonstrated the risks of international travel in spreading rare pathogens with long incubation periods. The World Health Organization and Spanish authorities coordinated the disembarkation on Tenerife, illustrating the challenges of maritime quarantine protocols. Public health is invisible — until it’s not. It’s tough to tally the injuries or deaths that don’t happen thanks to seat belts, lead testing, smoking bans and vaccines. “It is the invisibility paradox,” says Georges Benjamin, CEO of the American Public Health Association in Washington, D.C. “We are invisible because when our best work is done, nothing happens.” But when disaster strikes — say, an outbreak or a pandemic — people take notice. This year’s outbreaks of measles, Ebola, hantavirus and cyclosporiasis brought recent federal reductions into focus — and exposed cracks in a system that aims to keep people healthy. For years, that system functioned “reasonably well” despite chronic underfunding, Benjamin says. “But since the Trump administration has come on board, they have really made the system at all levels much more dysfunctional.” Sign up for our newsletter We summarize the week's scientific breakthroughs every Thursday. In 2025, the second Trump administration instituted sweeping federal funding cuts and layoffs, dismantled the global health–focused U.S. Agency for International Development and withdrew the United States from the World Health Organization. Officials reversed some of those changes in the wake of lawsuits and public backlash. But the damage persists. Two stalwarts of U.S. public health — the Food and Drug Administration and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — have lost between 20 and 30 percent of their staff, due largely to layoffs and early retirements. They have had to scale back, pause or cancel crucial disease-tracking programs at home and abroad. Some CDC divisions don’t have enough staff to function. And both agencies have cut funding to the local governments and nonprofits that shoulder much of the nation’s public health work. Combined with deep cuts to international aid, that has stymied efforts to contain infectious disease outbreaks around the world, with Ebola in particular going undetected for months, public health experts say. Domestically, 2026 saw a record-breaking measles outbreak, with some 5,412 confirmed cases as of November 12. That’s more than double last year’s count, and it is the highest number of annual cases since 1991. In mid November, the Pan American Health Organization determined that measles is again endemic to the United States and has stripped the country of its elimination status. Against a backdrop of shifting federal vaccine guidance, fewer children are receiving the MMR shot that prevents measles. Roughly 92 percent of kindergartners across the United States had gotten the vaccine in the 2025–2026 school year, down from 95 percent in the 2019–2020 school year, according to the CDC. At least 95 percent of people in a community need to be vaccinated to prevent the measles virus from sparking an outbreak. “The fact that we are spending so much time and energy [on measles] when we have a reliable vaccine is very disconcerting,” says Lori Handy, a pediatric infectious diseases physician and associate director of the Vaccine Education Center at Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia. The CDC has not responded to Science News requests for an interview. Such issues do not bode well for the future. Public health is tested during times of crisis, and the world will eventually face another pandemic, says Michael Osterholm, director of the Center for Infectious Disease Research and Policy at the University of Minnesota in Minneapolis. “We’ve not responded [to the 2026 outbreaks] in a way that would give me confidence that the public health system ... is prepared for what’s coming down the pike.” A preventable outbreak On the heels of multiple 2025 outbreaks, measles continued to wallop parts of the United States this year. Communities in South Carolina, Utah and Wisconsin have been among the hardest hit, with sporadic outbreaks also popping up in other states. Until August, however, none of this year’s outbreaks had turned deadly. That all changed in Pennsylvania. Since April, seven people in the state have died, including two infants too young to be vaccinated. The CDC has so far attributed just six deaths to measles, despite the insistence of Pennsylvania state public health officials. Three children died of measles in November in New York and Wisconsin. Measles’ improbable comeback As of November 12, U.S. health officials recorded 5,412 measles cases for 2026. The last time this many cases were recorded was 1991, nearly a decade before the disease was eliminated from the country. Measles spreads like wildfire, and one sick person can infect more than a dozen others in an unvaccinated community. While milder symptoms include fever and a hallmark full-body rash, infection can lead to pneumonia, brain swelling and even death. Statistically, the virus kills one to three out of every 1,000 infected people. The United States eliminated measles in 2000, thanks to the MMR vaccine. “Elimination” means the virus was not spreading widely, and any outbreaks were controlled within a year. But dropping vaccination rates have reestablished the conditions to sustain multiple outbreaks — and may have done so as early as 2022, researchers reported in August in Nature Medicine. In the wake of losing measles elimination status in November, beating back the the virus again “will take a true change in the public’s trust of health care,” Handy says. That means figuring out how to get people vaccinated — and to encourage them to work with public health officials on testing and contact tracing after exposure. Fading protection from measles, mumps and rubella During the 2009–2010 school year, 20 states had MMR vaccination rates equal to or above 95 percent among kindergartners. Just 10 states hit that threshold for 2025–2026, and 18 states had vaccination rates below 90 percent. It has been more than three decades since measles caused widespread outbreaks in the United States, and many have never experienced the devastation it can cause. When one community hears about an outbreak halfway across the country, “it still feels very foreign,” Handy says. When the threat feels far away, people are not likely to line up to get a vaccine. Yet measles is so contagious that one unvaccinated traveler can bring the virus back home and spark an outbreak in their own community. Even with the recent spike in cases, there is hope vaccination rates can catch up, says Cornell University epidemiologist Ana Bento. She points to California and New York, which experienced outbreaks in 2015 and 2018; they ended non-medical exemptions for school vaccinations shortly thereafter. Both states now have vaccination rates above 95 percent in kindergartners. “This is not a tipping point where there’s no coming back,” Bento says. An explosive outbreak If measles exposed the consequences of long-simmering vaccine hesitancy, then this summer’s cyclosporiasis outbreak was a quick lesson in food safety screening. In June, Michigan detected its first cases of the foodborne illness. By August 31, contaminated lettuce had caused the largest outbreak of explosive diarrhea ever in the United States, with 12,883 cases reported across 21 states. By the time the CDC declared the outbreak over on September 11, some 570 people had been hospitalized and two had died. Another 8,113 cyclosporiasis infections have not been traced back to any source. “[This] was one of those events where you felt like the cracks showed,” Handy says. But it isn’t easy to track the origins of foodborne illness. Typically, local and state public health officials report cases to the CDC — and investigate the outbreak, with the agency lending support as needed. If patient interviews reveal the culprit to be produce, dairy or packaged food, the CDC contacts the FDA, which identifies the producer and the distribution area — and coordinates recalls. The FDA also conducts on-the-ground investigations at the farms and processing facilities that supplied the food. It can take weeks to identify which contaminated foods are behind a foodborne illness outbreak. Each outbreak is different and timing can depend on the pathogen, laboratory evidence and availability of records, an FDA spokesperson told Science News. After Michigan health officials notified CDC that something was amiss on June 25, it was another 21 days before federal officials announced a possible culprit: shredded lettuce ultimately traced to a Taylor Farms processing facility in Mexico. Cyclosporiasis hits the heartland Of more than 20,000 cyclosporiasis cases reported this year, nearly 13,000 occurred across 21 states, with the preponderance of them in the Midwest. The FDA aims to complete investigations as soon as possible. A lot of people have been posting on social media about avoiding fruits and vegetables, which is not good public health messaging.” Sorscher applauded the dedication of public servants working to contain the outbreak. Speaking at the July 16 news briefing, epidemiologist Barbara Kowalcyk of George Washington University’s Institute for Food Safety and Nutrition Security in Washington, D.C., noted that half of CDC’s food safety budget goes to states to support surveillance and other programs. Yet that budget has remained flat for nearly a decade. FDA investigations have also been impacted, with foreign food inspections at historical lows. In the case of cyclosporiasis, delays gathering testing supplies and getting clearance from the Mexican government meant it took nearly a month for FDA investigators to arrive in Mexico once the source of the outbreak was identified, according to the New York Times. Once there, inspectors found the parasite in a wastewater tank at a Taylor Farms processing plant — and in a drainage ditch at a farm that supplied the lettuce. ICU)的患者中更为常见。汉坦病毒感染早期症状常为非特异性,与流感、COVID-19、病毒性肺炎、钩端螺旋体病、登革热及脓毒症重叠,包括乏力、发热及肌肉酸痛。HFRS主要表现为肾脏损害与出血表现,从瘀点到严重内脏出血;HCPS主要表现为肺炎与心血管功能障碍。HFRS死亡率约12%,HCPS接近40%。近期ANDV疫情中,患者于2026年4月6日至28日发病,表现为发热、胃肠道症状,并快速进展至肺炎、ARDS与休克。研究表明,合并SARS-CoV-2感染的HFRS患者可能面临更严重疾病风险。 7 Diagnosis 确诊COVID-19的实验室方法包括核酸扩增检测(nucleic acid amplification tests, NAATs)、等温扩增技术、快速抗原检测及血清学检测。实时逆转录聚合酶链反应(real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, rRT-PCR)仍是SARS-CoV-2检测的“金标准”,因其具有高敏感性与特异性。基于抗原的快速诊断检测,如侧向流动免疫分析(lateral flow immunoassays, LFIs),被广泛用于快速筛查。汉坦病毒感染的实验室确诊主要依赖检测汉坦病毒特异性IgM抗体或证明IgG抗体滴度升高的血清学方法。分子技术如rRT-PCR亦用于病程急性期,尤其ANDV检测首选全血。两种感染的实验室诊断均存在局限性,分子方法需要专门设备、训练有素人员及适当样本采集处理。MV Hondius疫情应对进一步说明了对罕见人畜共患疾病建立灵活且可快速部署诊断网络的价值。汉坦病毒感染因非特异性症状使早期诊断尤为困难,因此详细的患者病史至关重要,需特别注意啮齿动物暴露、职业与环境危险因素、旅行史及与确诊病例接触史。已提出若干潜在预后标志物,包括血小板减少、血液浓缩、白细胞增多、乳酸升高、肾功能障碍、白介素-6(IL-6)、血管生成素信号改变及多种体液中病毒RNA检测,但大多数候选预测指标需在大规模多样化患者队列中验证。 8 Socio-economic impact COVID-19大流行对农业、石油工业、制造业、金融、旅游、交通运输、贸易、医疗保健及教育等多个部门产生负面影响。学校停课扰乱数百万学生的学习,行动限制与经济活动减少降低了对许多商品与制造产品的需求,而医疗设备、防护物资与医疗服务需求大幅增加。食品部门亦出现临时短缺与供应链中断。汉坦病毒感染缺乏全面经济负担估算,但重症病例常需重症监护病房(ICU)护理、高级呼吸支持,部分病例需体外膜氧合(extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, ECMO)。在偏远农村地区,急诊转运与转院进一步增加成本。MV Hondius疫情表明,即使罕见的人畜共患感染也可通过影响旅行信心与公众安全感知而产生国际社会经济后果。 9 Public health response 9.1 Epidemic intelligence 传统疫情监测主要依赖对结构化数据的手工分析,尤其通过官方公共卫生系统报告的信息,这些常规方法可受限于报告延迟、数据不完整及覆盖不足。人工智能(artificial intelligence, AI)驱动的监测系统能够整合分析多元、异质、多语言数据流,以支持更早检测与更及时公共卫生应对。WHO疫情枢纽旨在加强全球监测。学术机构是关键合作伙伴,在新冠疫情期间显著推进了分析与建模能力。SARS-CoV-2基因组的测序与比较分析揭示了病毒谱系的出现、循环与重组。然而,非洲大片区域代表性不足,主要归因于财务投资不足、训练人员短缺、实验室能力有限及数字基础设施与工具 inadequate。汉坦病毒疾病监测始于1993年美国Four Corners地区,HCPS于1995年成为全国法定报告疾病,此后通过国家法定报告疾病监测系统报告。在中国与韩国等高负担国家,常规监测系统地收集病例数据并监测啮齿动物种群。 9.2 Rapid communication strategy COVID-19大流行证明,识别并有效沟通公共卫生威胁是实施适当解决方案的首要关键步骤,但在科学证据有限或仍在涌现时,难以说服决策者。早期阶段,各国在建立一致连贯叙述以解释疾病流行病学、相关风险及预防措施方面面临重大挑战。有效的沟通对于培养公众对依赖广泛采用才能产生影响的卫生干预措施的信任与接受至关重要,COVID-19疫苗的推出因公众关于安全性与有效性的重大关切、错误信息快速传播而面临额外阻力。汉坦病毒疾病方面,WHO建立了三级协调机制。MV Hondius疫情中,WHO与佛得角、荷兰、南非、西班牙及英国的National IHR Focal Points保持持续协调。阿根廷National IHR Focal Point根据国籍分享乘客与船员名单,促进国际接触者追踪。 9.3 One health approach One Health框架认识到人类、动物、植物与环境健康之间的相互关联,旨在可持续地促进所有相互关联系统的福祉。四方组织制定了《One Health Joint Plan of Action (2022-2026)》。尽管ANDV疫情通过协调国际应对管理,包括流行病学调查、病例隔离与临床管理、医疗后送程序及广泛实验室分析,由卫生当局评估,疫情已于2026年7月2日得到控制,且WHO疾病暴发新闻DON611中无持续或大规模社区传播证据,但由于汉坦病毒感染潜伏期相对较长,担忧未来数周可能出现额外病例。啮齿动物控制是汉坦病毒预防的关键组成部分,措施包括环境改造、改善卫生、捕杀及针对性啮齿动物数量减少。 9.4 Ecosystem restoration 生态系统修复可被视为公共卫生干预。恢复生态系统可减少空气污染、改善绿色与蓝色空间获取可及性,并可能降低汉坦病毒风险。例如,将巴西大西洋森林恢复至法定要求水平可通过降低主要啮齿动物储主数量将汉坦病毒风险降低约45%。 9.5 Artificial Intelligence AI可增强疫情检测、疾病监测及诊断、药物与疫苗开发。AlphaFold等近期进展加速生物医学发现。AI驱动的疫情情报系统通过识别早期预警信号补充传统监测方法。机器学习模型已用于预测疾病传播与建模疫情。对于汉坦病毒,提出的HRP-AI框架采用多源预警模型,整合啮齿动物与环境监测数据、症候群监测、实验室结果、旅行与流动性指标、数字弱信号及商业脆弱性指标,旨在实现更早的疫情检测、风险分层与透明的风险沟通。AI在基因组分析、突变追踪、疫苗开发及大流行准备中亦发挥重要作用。 9.6 Therapeutics COVID-19疫情期间,若干既往获批用于其他适应症的制药药物被研究并重新用于SARS-CoV-2感染治疗,包括抗疟药、抗原虫药及一系列免疫调节疗法。小分子抗病毒药物如remdesivir、nirmatrelvir/ritonavir及molnupiravir已被使用。单克隆抗体在COVID-19治疗反应中发挥重要作用。目前尚无FDA批准的专门用于治疗汉坦病毒感染的药物,临床管理主要支持性治疗。HFRS治疗通常关注仔细的液体与电解质管理及肾脏替代治疗,包括必要时的透析。严重HCPS病例可能需要重症监护支持、机械通气及选择性ECMO。广谱核苷类似物ribavirin在体外与动物模型中显示抗ANDV活性,但临床证据有限。新兴免疫治疗策略代表有前景的途径,特别是针对Gn与Gc糖蛋白的中和单克隆抗体(monoclonal antibodies, mAbs)。然而,由于疾病进展理解不足、资金与商业兴趣有限及Gn/Gc糖蛋白复合物重组生产困难,汉坦病毒对策开发受到阻碍。 9.7 Vaccines 首批COVID-19疫苗产品于2020年年中引入,2020年7月22日开始紧急使用授权。截至2023年12月31日,全球已接种超136亿剂COVID-19疫苗,约67%全球人口完成初级接种。目前尚无广泛获批的专门用于治疗汉坦病毒疾病的疫苗。针对HTNV与SEOV的灭活疫苗于1980年代末在中国与韩国使用,例如Hantavax。这些疫苗诱导强烈初始免疫反应,但中和抗体在加强免疫一年后的检出率仅约50%。新兴的mRNA-脂质纳米粒(lipid nanoparticle, LNP)疫苗与病毒样颗粒(virus-like particle, VLP)疫苗代表有前景的新策略,可诱导高中和抗体反应并在动物攻击模型中提供完全保护。 9.8 Prevention COVID-19的预防包括正确佩戴口罩、定期洗手、遮挡咳嗽、避免触摸面部、保持身体距离及确保室内通风等。汉坦病毒疾病预防主要基于减少人类与啮齿动物储主之间的接触,包括保持家居与 workplace 清洁、封堵洞口、安全储存食物、受污染区域湿式清洁及手卫生。 10 Conclusion
