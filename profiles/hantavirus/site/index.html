Our 2025 study found hantavirus exposure in people farming bats, bamboo rats, civets, and wild boars in Vietnam. The findings should serve as an early warning for other Southeast Asian countries, including Indonesia, where wildlife farming and markets are also common. Another 1.9% had active infections when we collected samples between August 2023 and March 2024, however no hantavirus outbreaks were reported during the study period. By 2021, the country had around 6,744 wildlife farms housing 1.8 million animals across 371 species. Question and background
Wildlife farming in Vietnam raises concerns about zoonotic disease transmission, particularly hantavirus, due to close human-animal contact and the risk of disease spillover. The study aimed to assess hantavirus exposure among wildlife farmers in the region.
Design and population
The study involved testing 210 wildlife farmers in Lao Cai and Dong Nai provinces in Vietnam between August 2023 and March 2024. These farmers managed various wild animals, including bats, bamboo rats, civets, and wild boars, which are associated with hantavirus.
Core methods
Blood samples were collected from the farmers and tested for anti-hantavirus immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies. Active infections were also identified through serological testing during the study period.

The results underscore the importance of monitoring hantavirus in wildlife farming communities and implementing preventive strategies to reduce the risk of zoonotic transmission to humans.
Limitations and evidence strength

Reading the Infodemic Against the Epidemic: A Real-Risk Benchmark for Inflated and Downplayed Disease Risk on Reddit Within the 2026 World Cup Window

Authors: Lulu Alarfaj, Nimisha Rawat, Melissa Zeynep Ertem
Original abstract (English)
During the deadly hantavirus outbreak of May 2026, the loudest online response called it a hoax, while measles, the one rising threat, drew little attention. That inversion is the half of the infodemic we rarely measure: not amplified fear, but the quiet downplaying of a real risk. Detecting it requires what the field lacks: an external benchmark for the true risk. Anchoring one in the epidemiological and mass-gathering literature, with the 2026 FIFA World Cup run-up as a dated window, two coders judged Reddit claims about measles (high), hantavirus (low), and Ebola (near zero) for accuracy and direction (Cohen's kappa 0.72 and 0.68). Of 66 coded claims, 41 were inaccurate, and distortion ran both ways: 24 downplayed risk and 17 inflated it (exact binomial p = 0.35), with downplaying at least as common as inflating. Attention ran opposite to danger, hantavirus drawing the most claims (44 of 66) and measles the fewest (8): the infodemic did not mirror the epidemic. The curated, single-platform sample makes these patterns exploratory, but they suggest that defenses built against panic alone miss its mirror image, the denial of a real threat that prior work links to weaker protection among the most exposed.

Question and background
The study aimed to address the discrepancy between public attention and actual disease risk during the 2026 hantavirus outbreak, highlighting the need to measure infodemic patterns that downplay real threats.
Design and population
The study analyzed Reddit claims about measles, hantavirus, and Ebola during the 2026 World Cup window, focusing on the relative attention given to each disease compared to their actual risk levels.
Core methods

Hantavirus outbreak forces cruise passengers to leave ship at St Helena - News On AIR

News Brief
A hantavirus outbreak on the cruise ship MV Hondius, which departed from Argentina a month ago, has led to the disembarkation of around 40 passengers at St Helena, a remote South Atlantic island. The Dutch Foreign Ministry confirmed the number of affected individuals, with three deaths reported since the outbreak began. Two passengers in serious condition were evacuated to the Netherlands for treatment, while a third remains aboard a delayed evacuation flight. The outbreak is linked to the Andes strain of hantavirus, which has limited human-to-human transmission capabilities. The ship, carrying 150 passengers and crew from 23 countries, is now sailing towards Spain’s Canary Islands under strict precautionary measures. Authorities in Europe, South Africa, and Argentina are conducting contact tracing, quarantine planning, and laboratory testing to contain the outbreak. The World Health Organisation noted the outbreak likely involves the Andes strain, which can transmit between close household contacts. The ship operator had not previously disclosed that dozens of passengers left the ship at St Helena, including at least 12 nationalities. The Dutch man who died on April 11 had his body taken off the ship at St Helena, and his wife disembarked there before flying to South Africa and dying. The outbreak has raised concerns about the spread of the Andes strain and the need for containment measures.
