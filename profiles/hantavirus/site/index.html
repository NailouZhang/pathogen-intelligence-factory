城市挪威鼠种群中莱普托斯珀里拉和汉坦病毒的生态驱动因素差异

Differences in Ecological Drivers for Leptospira and Hantavirus in an Urban Norway Rat Population.

Differences in Ecological Drivers for Leptospira and Hantavirus in an Urban Norway Rat Population.

Authors: Caio Graco Zeppelini, Ana Maria Borges da Paixão Barreto, Diogo C. C. Santiago, Rafaela S. Alves, Viviane F. Espírito Santo, Caroline Lacorte Rangel, Renata Carvalho de Oliveira, Daiana de Oliveira, Ianei O. Carneiro, Leonela Bazan
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Rodent control in urban areas is an important public health measure, among other reasons, for their capacity of harboring several zoonotic pathogens. Efficient, evidence-based control programs must consider both rodent and pathogen ecology, to achieve both rodent population and pathogen transmission risk reduction. The present study aims to determine the ecological drivers of Leptospira and Hantavirus positivity in an urban Norway Rat population. One hundred and twenty rats were trapped in four urban poor communities in Salvador, Brazil, tested for Leptospira sp. Generalized Linear Models for Leptospira (N=109) and Hantavirus (N=108) positivity were created with a pool of environmental and demographic variables, followed by stepwise model selection using Akaike’s Information Criterion. Age, presence of Calodium hepaticum scars, wounds, accumulated materials, open sewer canals and unpaved soil were positively associated with Leptospira positivity. Age and presence of accumulated materials were positively associated with Hantavirus positivity, while rodent control campaigns and being male were protective factors. Factors associated with positivity for Leptospira are also associated with rodent infestation. Different subsets of environmental and demographic factors influence pathogens with different epidemiological characteristics. Control measures that effectively reduce zoonotic risk must consider the ecological characteristics of the pathogens present in the system.

Question and background
The study was needed to understand the ecological drivers of Leptospira and Hantavirus in urban Norway Rat populations, as these rodents are known to harbor zoonotic pathogens. The research aimed to identify factors influencing the presence of these pathogens in the environment.
Design and population
A cross-sectional study was conducted in four urban poor communities in Salvador, Brazil, where 120 Norway Rats were trapped and tested for Leptospira and Hantavirus. The study focused on environmental and demographic variables associated with the rats.
Core methods
Generalized Linear Models were used to analyze Leptospira (N=109) and Hantavirus (N=108) positivity, incorporating a pool of environmental and demographic variables. Stepwise model selection was performed using Akaike’s Information Criterion.
Main results
Age, presence of Calodium hepaticum scars, wounds, accumulated materials, open sewer canals, and unpaved soil were positively associated with Leptospira positivity. Age and presence of accumulated materials were positively associated with Hantavirus positivity, while rodent control campaigns and being male were protective factors.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted that different subsets of environmental and demographic factors influence the epidemiological characteristics of the pathogens. This finding highlights the need for tailored control strategies based on pathogen-specific ecological drivers.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings can inform the development of targeted rodent control programs that consider the ecological characteristics of specific pathogens, thereby reducing zoonotic transmission risks in urban settings.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its cross-sectional design, which cannot establish causality. The evidence is moderate, as it is based on associations rather than experimental data, and the generalizability of findings may be constrained by the specific urban environment studied.

肾综合征出血热病例的临床严重程度和就医行为:中国江西省不同空间聚集区的比较研究

Clinical severity and health-seeking behaviors of cases with hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome: A comparative study across spatial clusters in Jiangxi Province, China.
作者： Guanghui Xia, Rui Gao, Huanhong Pan, Jun Zong, Zhili Zeng, Zifen Li, Chenwei Li, Shiyu Liao, Tong Luo, Guai Tang
摘要中文翻译背景:肾综合征出血热(肾综合征出血热,HFRS)是中国江西省的重要公共卫生问题,该地区同时流行汉坦病毒(HTNV)和首尔病毒(SEOV)。先前研究已识别出江西省的三个不同HFRS流行聚集区,这些聚集区在地理分布上相互分离,且肾综合征出血热水平存在异质性。我们假设这些聚集区的临床严重程度和就医行为可能存在差异,因此进行了一项回顾性研究以检验这一假设。方法:我们回顾性分析了江西省63个县在13年期间报告的4,614例HFRS病例。根据先前的空间研究,病例被分为四个区域,代表不同的山地和平原地区,且与主要水域和山脉的距离不同。病例数据由县级疾病预防控制中心(CDC)工作人员通过全国统一的病例调查问卷收集。比较了临床严重程度和就医行为(医院等级、发病至就诊间隔)。结果:Cluster I中的HFRS病例表现出最高的临床严重程度(22.41%严重,6.65%危重),而Cluster II中的病例则明显较轻(2.62%严重,1.31%危重)。发病至就诊间隔的中位数在Cluster II中最长(5天),而其他区域为2-4天。医院选择偏好存在差异:Cluster I主要使用二级医院(65.88%);Cluster II则二级和三级医院使用比例接近(48.44%和47.01%);Cluster III和非聚集区更倾向于使用更高层级的医院。结论:我们的研究结果证实,尽管江西省是一个混合流行区,但其在临床严重程度和就医行为方面表现出显著的空间异质性。这些发现强调了在HFRS预防和控制中制定区域性策略的必要性。

Clinical severity and health-seeking behaviors of cases with hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome: A comparative study across spatial clusters in Jiangxi Province, China.

Authors: Guanghui Xia, Rui Gao, Huanhong Pan, Jun Zong, Zhili Zeng, Zifen Li, Chenwei Li, Shiyu Liao, Tong Luo, Guai Tang
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS) is a significant public health concern in Jiangxi Province, China, where both Hantaan (HTNV) and Seoul (SEOV) co-circulate. Previous studies have identified three distinct clusters of HFRS epidemic in Jiangxi Province, characterized by geographical separation and heterogeneous incidence levels. We hypothesized that clinical severity and healthcare-seeking behaviors may vary across these clusters, and thus conducted a retrospective study to examine this hypothesis. METHODS: We retrospectively analyzed 4,614 HFRS cases (13-year period) from 63 counties. Cases were classified into four areas based on prior spatial study, representing distinct hilly and plain regions with different proximity to major water bodies and mountains. Data on HFRS cases were collected by county-level CDC staff using a nationally standardized case investigation questionnaire. Clinical severity and healthcare-seeking behaviors (hospital level, onset-to-visit interval) were compared. RESULTS: HFRS cases in Cluster I exhibited the highest clinical severity (22.41% severe, 6.65% critical), while cases in Cluster II were strikingly mild (2.62% severe, 1.31% critical). Median onset-to-visit interval was longest in Cluster II (5 days) vs others (2-4 days). Hospital preference varied: Cluster I primarily used Class II hospitals (65.88%); Cluster II showed near-equal use of Class II (48.44%) and III (47.01%); Cluster III and non-clustered areas favored higher-level hospitals. CONCLUSION: Our findings confirmed that Jiangxi Province, though a mixed epidemic focus, exhibited distinct spatial heterogeneity in clinical severity, and healthcare-seeking behaviors. These findings underscore the need for regionally tailored strategies in HFRS prevention and control.

肾综合征出血热(HFRS)急性肾损伤(AKI)患者中虫草菌丝制剂改善肾脏结局的疗效:来自中国肾综合征出血热急性肾损伤(HFRS-AKI)研究的证据。

The efficacy of Cordyceps sinensis preparations in improving kidney outcomes among patients with AKI during hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome: evidence from a Chinese cohort.
作者： Min Min, Yazhen Yu, Changying Xing, Jiong Zhang
摘要中文翻译
查看研究七要素
问题与背景
肾综合征出血热(HFRS)常并发急性肾损伤(AKI)。尽管大多数患者通过支持性治疗可从AKI中恢复,但部分患者会进展为慢性肾脏病(CKD)。肾综合征出血热急性肾损伤(HFRS-AKI)患者CKD进展的机制及决定因素仍不明确。
设计与对象
本研究回顾性分析了在金陵医院接受治疗的99例HFRS-AKI患者,以确定CKD进展率并识别相关危险因素。根据CKD进展情况,患者被分为完全恢复(CR)组和不完全恢复(ICR)组。
核心方法
收集并分析了基线临床和实验室数据。
主要结果
Overall, 27.3% of patients with HFRS-AKI progressed to CKD despite receiving supportive care. Multivariate analysis revealed that treatment with Cordyceps sinensis preparations during the AKI phase was an independent protective factor against incomplete kidney recovery (OR: 0.323, 95% CI: 0.114-0.916, p = 0.034). Inverse probability weighting analysis further demonstrated that the use of Cordyceps sinensis preparations was inversely associated with incomplete kidney recovery (β: -1.184, 95% CI: -2.224 - -0.144, p = 0.026). Survival analysis confirmed that Cordyceps sinensis preparation therapy was associated with improved kidney outcomes (log-rank test, p = 0.004).
解释与创新
本研究首次提供了临床证据,表明在HFRS-AKI期间使用虫草菌丝制剂作为辅助治疗与改善肾脏结局相关,提示虫草菌丝制剂可能是病毒性急性肾损伤的一种有前景的治疗手段。
科研与公卫意义
研究结果表明,虫草菌丝制剂可能是一种用于病毒性急性肾损伤的有前景的治疗手段,特别是在肾综合征出血热(HFRS)患者中,这些发现可能为未来研究提供关于急性肾损伤在病毒感染中的治疗策略的参考。

BACKGROUND: Sand fly fever virus (SFFV) infection is an acute self-limiting febrile illness caused by phleboviruses and transmitted by sand flies of the Phlebotomus species. Seasonal Dynamics, Host Network Structure, and Environmental Drivers of Gamasid Mite Communities on Small Mammals in a Dry–Hot Valley: A Year-Round Monitoring Study

Authors: Peng‐Wu Yin, Yan Lv, Wen‐Yu Song, Rong Fan, Cheng-Fu Zhao, Zhi-Wei Zhang, Ya-Fei Zhao, Wenge Dong, Xian-Guo Guo
Original abstract (English)
Ectoparasitic gamasid mites on small mammals play crucial roles in transmitting zoonotic pathogens like hantaviruses associated with hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS). To elucidate their community, network structure, and seasonal drivers in xeric habitats, a consecutive 12-month localized survey was conducted in Waxi Village, Binchuan County, a dry–hot valley in Yunnan Province, southwest China, from November 2020 to October 2021. From 1329 captured small mammals (18 species, 89.62% rodents), 12,075 gamasid mites belonging to 33 species were identified. The community exhibited overwhelming dominance by the family Laelapidae (Cr = 99.35%) and genus Laelaps (Cr = 97.37%). Laelaps algericus, L. guizhouensis, and L. nuttalli constituted the dominant mite species (cumulative Cr = 88.96%) and formed a tightly correlated core species cluster. Overall infestation prevalence (PM), mean intensity (MI), and mean abundance (MA) were 46.05%, 19.73 mites/host, and 9.09 mites/host, respectively. The bipartite host–gamasid network revealed a significantly nested architecture (NODFobs = 31.16, Z = 14.03, p < 0.001) with high specialization (H2′ = 0.599), anchored by dominant hosts (Rattus andamanensis, Apodemus chevrieri, and Mus caroli). α-diversity analysis showed that Margalef richness, Shannon–Wiener index, and Gini–Simpson index peaked in autumn (November), while troughs occurred in mid-spring (April) and mid-autumn (October). Non-metric multidimensional scaling (NMDS, stress = 0.0048) and PERMANOVA (R2 = 0.074, p = 0.001) confirmed significant seasonal shifts in community composition. Standard redundancy analysis (RDA) suggested temperature as the primary climatic driver (F = 7.205, p = 0.001; total model explanation = 8.2%). However, partial RDA (pRDA) conditioning on host species identity demonstrated that climatic variables directly accounted for only 0.1% of net variance (F = 0.662, p = 0.521). Our findings indicate that gamasid mite community assembly in this dry–hot valley is characterized by strong species dominance and network nestedness and that seasonal fluctuations are consistent with a host-mediated pattern rather than being directly governed by climate.

Hantavirus preparedness at human-rodent interfaces: lessons from China's HFRS control and Andes virus transmission in mobile populations.

Authors: Xinyu Wang, Wenhong Zhang
Original abstract (English)
Hantaviruses are globally distributed reservoir-associated viruses that cause haemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS), mainly in Eurasia, and hantavirus cardiopulmonary syndrome (HCPS; also termed hantavirus pulmonary syndrome, HPS), mainly in the Americas. Most human infections arise from exposure to rodent-contaminated environments, so clustered disease may reflect shared environmental exposure rather than interpersonal transmission. This Review uses China's long-term HFRS experience as a central case study for interface-centred preparedness. China demonstrates that sustained surveillance, reservoir monitoring, risk-defined vaccination, rodent control and environmental risk reduction can substantially reduce disease burden, but declining incidence does not mean disappearance of ecological risk. Its dual HTNV and SEOV ecology further shows that one clinical syndrome can arise from distinct agricultural and peri-domestic rat-associated interfaces. Andes virus (ANDV) provides a contrasting challenge: selected outbreaks have documented limited but consequential human-to-human transmission after close or prolonged contact. The 2026 MV Hondius event illustrates how such transmission can become an international contact-tracing problem when it occurs in mobile populations. Preparedness should therefore combine long-term management of rodent-derived interfaces, rapid discrimination between common-source clusters and ANDV secondary transmission, and response systems that remain effective when exposed persons, infected animals or close contacts cross geographical borders.

Authors: Praveen Kumar Uppala, Sita K Karanam, Naga Vishnu Kandra, Sandhya Edhi
Original abstract (English)
Hantaviruses are increasingly regarded as an important emerging risk to global health, with an estimated annual impact on more than 200000 people worldwide. These pathogens are single-stranded RNA viruses belonging to the family Han taviridae and are known to cause two major clinical syndromes in humans: Hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS) and hantavirus pulmonary syndrome (HPS). The so-called old world hantaviruses, which are associated with HFRS, are predominantly distributed across Asia, Europe, and parts of Africa. In contrast, new world hantaviruses, responsible for HPS, are largely confined to North and South America. Each year, approximately 150000-200000 HFRS cases require hospitalization globally, with the greatest burden occurring in developing regions. The mortality rate of HFRS ranges from below 1% to about 12%, depending on the infecting strain. Both disease forms are characterized by increased capillary permeability, leading to clinical features such as hypotension, thrombocytopenia, and leukocytosis. While HFRS has a broader geographic distribution, HPS carries a higher risk of death.. Moreover, the detection of new viral strains and the steady rise in reported cases have intensified global concern. Enhancing surveillance systems and expanding laboratory diagnostic capacity are therefore essential for effective monitoring and control of hantavirus diseases.

Immunopathogenesis of vascular leakage in hantavirus infections: a systematic review of mechanisms underlying HPS and HFRS

Authors: Zahra Mobarezi, Zahra Meshkat
Original abstract (English)
Hantavirus infections manifest as Hantavirus Pulmonary Syndrome (HPS) and Hemorrhagic Fever with Renal Syndrome (HFRS), both associated with increased vascular permeability despite the lack of direct cytopathic injury to endothelial cells. A systematic search of PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science (January 2020–May 2026) identified human studies investigating immunopathogenic mechanisms in HPS or HFRS. Due to substantial heterogeneity across study designs, findings were synthesized descriptively. Forty-one studies (39 cohorts, 2 case–control) were included, predominantly on HFRS-associated hantaviruses (HTNV, PUUV, DOBV); only one study evaluated HPS-associated ANDV. The reviewed literature revealed elevated inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-18, CXCL10, CX3CL1), depletion of Mucosal-Associated Invariant T (MAIT) cells linked to IRE1αdependent pyroptosis, disruption of the endothelial glycocalyx, and coagulation disturbances with platelet dysfunction. Biomarkers of glycocalyx injury (heparan sulfate, chondroitin sulfate, and soluble thrombomodulin) were associated with increased mortality risk (AUC 0.87–0.93). No human mechanistic studies on Sin Nombre virus (SNV) were identified. Hantavirus-induced vascular leakage is driven by cytokine-mediated endothelial dysfunction, MAIT cell pyroptosis, glycocalyx degradation, and immunothrombosis. While HFRS mechanisms are now well-characterized, the absence of human mechanistic data for HPS—particularly for SNV—remains a critical gap.

Original abstract (English)
The sustained growth of international maritime tourism and the expansion of the cruise industry have increased the importance of emerging zoonotic diseases as a significant challenge to global public health. Potential zoonotic concerns include orthohantaviruses and other zoonotic viruses, such as dengue, chikungunya, Zika, yellow fever, and avian influenza, which are associated with animal reservoirs and vectors in ports, tourist destinations, and tropical ecosystems. This review examines the ecology of animal reservoirs of orthohantaviruses and other zoonotic pathogens relevant to maritime tourism, including peri-urban rodents, bats, migratory birds, and other mammals. It discusses how climate change, coastal urbanization, and environmental change affect viral transmission dynamics. It highlights epidemiological risks linked to international cruises, particularly exposure during ecotourism excursions, pathogen introduction, and challenges for maritime surveillance. The review also addresses travel medicine, biosecurity, and infection control, emphasizing a One Health approach. Strengthening integrated surveillance and prevention systems is essential to ensure safer, more sustainable, and resilient maritime tourism in the 21st century.

News Brief
The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) highlighted the critical role of robust laboratory systems in preventing, detecting, and responding to health threats during a high-level event at the 81st United Nations General Assembly. Using hantavirus as an example, PAHO Director Dr. Jarbas Barbosa emphasized that laboratory preparedness is not just about advanced equipment but about creating systems that enable countries to detect unusual events, safely collect and refer specimens, provide timely diagnosis, and coordinate across borders. In 2025, eight countries in the Americas reported over 200 confirmed cases and 60 deaths linked to hantavirus pulmonary syndrome, with cases spreading internationally. PAHO supported these efforts by issuing an epidemiological alert, strengthening diagnostic protocols, and conducting regional training with reference laboratories. The outbreak was managed through rapid information exchange and genomic characterization, underscoring the need for sustained investment in adaptable molecular platforms, trained professionals, and trusted networks to ensure effective public health responses.

Dr. Jarbas Barbosa speaking at the event titled: “Crisis in preparedness: laboratories and frontline readiness in the time of Ebola.” Speaking at the event, “Crisis in preparedness: laboratories and frontline readiness in the time of Ebola,” Dr. Barbosa highlighted lessons from the Americas, emphasizing that laboratory preparedness is much more than sophisticated equipment or the ability of a national reference laboratory to perform a specialized test. It is a system that must enable countries to detect unusual events, safely collect and refer specimens, provide timely and reliable diagnosis, share information across institutions and borders, and rapidly translate laboratory findings into public health action. While Ebola is not currently circulating in the Americas, PAHO has been working with countries to prepare for the possibility of imported cases. Following the Ebola outbreak in Africa earlier this year, PAHO strengthened regional preparedness, supporting countries to reinforce surveillance, laboratory diagnosis, biosafety, infection prevention and control, and the safe referral of samples to specialized laboratories. Dr. Barbosa highlighted recent experience with hantavirus as an example of how laboratory, genomic and epidemiological capacities must work together. In 2025, eight countries in the Region reported more than 200 confirmed cases and 60 deaths. PAHO issued an epidemiological alert, strengthened diagnostic protocols and conducted regional training with reference laboratories and WHO Collaborating Centers in Argentina, the United States and Panama. These capacities were soon put to the test when a cluster of Andes hantavirus pulmonary syndrome associated with the MV Hondius cruise ship became an international public health event. PAHO helped establish communication between the countries involved and ANLIS-Malbrán in Argentina, bringing specialized Andes virus diagnostic expertise into the investigation. As contacts were identified in Spain, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands and eventually Canada, laboratories, epidemiologists and public health authorities were able to exchange information and coordinate rapidly. PAHO also collaborated with the genomic team characterizing the virus, connecting laboratory and genomic findings with the epidemiological investigation and risk assessment. Dr. Barbosa emphasized that this integration of diagnostics, genomic surveillance, epidemiology and public health decision-making is essential to effective preparedness. PAHO continues to strengthen regional genomic surveillance through PAHOGen, PAHO’s Genomic Surveillance Regional Networks, connecting capacities and expertise across the Americas. “The hantavirus experience illustrates an important principle: Not every country needs to maintain every highly specialized diagnostic or genomic capacity. But every country must be able to rapidly access those capacities through trusted national, regional and international networks when they are needed,” Dr. Barbosa said. He emphasized that when a public health event crosses borders, there is no time to begin identifying experts, reference laboratories, diagnostic protocols, specimen-referral pathways or channels for communicating results. These relationships and mechanisms must already be in place. Preparedness therefore requires sustained investment in adaptable molecular platforms, trained professionals, quality-management systems, biosafety and biosecurity, reliable specimen referral and transport, resilient supply chains, interoperable information systems, and strong national and regional laboratory networks. “Preparedness is built between emergencies,” Dr. Barbosa said. The surveillance systems, laboratory protocols, genomic expertise, trained professionals and trusted relationships strengthened during routine periods are the same capacities that allow countries to detect unusual events earlier, understand them faster and act before they become larger emergencies. “That is not simply an investment in laboratories. It is an investment in stronger health systems, health security, preparedness and resilience,” he added. The event brought together global and regional leaders to discuss how to strengthen laboratory systems as a cornerstone of pandemic prevention, preparedness and response, drawing on lessons from Ebola, COVID-19, H5N1 influenza and other emerging and reemerging infectious diseases. The event focused on practical solutions for strengthening laboratory systems for both routine health services and pandemic preparedness, including the need for sustained investment in clinical laboratory capacity. Other speakers included Dr. Chikwe Ihekweazu, Executive Director of the WHO Health Emergencies Programme; Dr. Robert Harrington, Stephen and Suzanne Weiss Dean at Weill Cornell Medicine; and Dr. Robert Sebbag, Vice President of the Mérieux Foundation. News Brief
On 23 September 2026 a systematic review was published in the European Journal of Medical Research examining how hantavirus infections cause vascular leakage in humans. The review screened literature from January 2020 to May 2026, ultimately including 41 studies—39 cohort and 2 case‐control—primarily on HFRS‐related viruses (HTNV, PUUV, DOBV) and a single study on the HPS‐associated Andes virus. Findings highlight that elevated inflammatory cytokines, loss of MAIT cells, glycocalyx damage, and platelet‐related coagulopathy drive endothelial dysfunction, with biomarkers such as heparan sulfate, chondroitin sulfate and soluble thrombomodulin correlating with higher mortality risk. No human mechanistic data were found for Sin Nombre virus, underscoring a key knowledge gap for HPS. The authors stress that while HFRS mechanisms are well characterised, further research is needed for HPS, especially SNV. This synthesis does not report new cases or outbreaks but consolidates existing evidence.

Study shows high need for respiratory support in patients with severe Andes hantavirus infections - CIDRAP
新闻简报A new study by Chilean researchers highlights the critical need for respiratory support in patients with severe Andes hantavirus infections, particularly hantavirus cardiopulmonary syndrome (HCPS). The study, published in Intensive Care Medicine in September 2026, analyzed data from 215 patients hospitalized in Chile between 2019 and 2024, finding that 69% required respiratory support, including extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) and mechanical ventilation. In-hospital mortality was 21%, with higher rates in patients receiving ECMO (33%) and mechanical ventilation (30%). The study also noted that mortality was nearly three times higher during the COVID-19 pandemic. An outbreak of Andes hantavirus on a cruise ship in April and May 2026 sickened 13 passengers and crew members, resulting in three deaths. The findings underscore the need for healthcare systems to be prepared for HCPS, especially in non-endemic regions. The CDC confirmed a separate hepatitis A outbreak linked to frozen clams, but this is unrelated to the hantavirus analysis. The evidence is based on a study published in Intensive Care Medicine and outbreak data.

The study, published earlier this month in Intensive Care Medicine, analyzed data on 215 patients hospitalized in Chile with hantavirus cardiopulmonary syndrome (HCPS), a severe disease caused by the Andres strain of the virus. In addition, 40% of patients received transfusions, and 16% underwent renal replacement therapy. The median hospital LOS was eight days, and in-hospital mortality was 21%, with the highest case-fatality rates observed in patients receiving ECMO (33%) and MV (30%). In-hospital mortality was nearly three times higher in HCPS patients who were hospitalized during the COVID-19 pandemic (adjusted odds ratio, 2.73). “These findings reinforce the importance for intensivists of remaining aware of HCPS, particularly in the context of the current outbreak, and of ensuring that intensive care units and healthcare systems are adequately prepared,” the authors wrote. In an interconnected world, the level of pandemic preparedness in one region or continent can influence whether a spillover event is contained or spread more widely. The response to the MV Hondius case also underscores a broader lesson: containing outbreaks with pandemic potential depends on effective global cooperation. In July 2026, Marion Koopmans of Erasmus MC and the Pandemic and Disaster Preparedness Research Center, Rotterdam, Netherlands, and colleagues examined the responses revealed in The Lancet Regional Health, Europe, highlighting the importance of international coordination. Therefore, fair and equitable collaboration is essential for protecting the interests of individual countries. This principle is reflected in the WHO Pandemic Agreement adopted in 2025. For example, Article 11 requires signatory countries to “promote, facilitate, or incentivize technology transfer,” make licenses available “for pandemic-related health technologies for which they hold rights, on a nonexclusive, transparent, and geographically broad basis, and for the benefit of developing countries,” and “encourage patent or license holders [...] to waive royalties or, alternatively, to charge reasonable royalties, particularly to manufacturers in developing countries during a pandemic emergency,” among other measures. The amendments to the IHR, adopted by the 77th World Health Assembly through Resolution WHA77.17, similarly call for “facilitating and removing barriers to timely and equitable access by participating states to relevant health products [...] during a public health emergency of international concern — including a pandemic emergency.” They also call for coordination in the exchange of information on the approval and production of materials needed to address an emergency and for the establishment of a coordination mechanism for the funding required to respond to emergencies. Despite these frameworks, international cooperation can be complicated by national priorities and geopolitical pressures. In a BMJ article on the response to the outbreak aboard the MV Hondius, Koopmans and colleagues concluded: “Efforts are further complicated by the current geopolitical climate, evolving national priorities, and pressures on multilateral systems supporting global preparedness and response.” Italy provides an example of this tension. His position was consistent with criticism of the WHO from other populist parties in Europe, including Germany’s Alternative for Germany, which called for Germany to withdraw from the WHO in November. Challenges to international cooperation extend beyond national political positions. The 2025 Pandemic Agreement over the Sharing of Data and Biological Materials remains stalled. According to a report published in the BMJ in May 2026, coalitions representing low- and middle-income countries in Africa, Latin America, and Asia were calling for binding provisions to ensure equitable access to vaccines, treatments, and technologies in exchange for the rapid sharing of data. Their position reflects the experience of COVID, during which many developing countries shared data and viral strains, but later had limited access to vaccines and treatments developed using those resources. “Other countries — largely European nations with large pharmaceutical industries — say that if the language is too strong it will reduce private companies’ incentive to create the medicines and health technologies the world needs,” the BMJ reported. Similar challenges exist in the European Union. In June 2026, the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control published an interim report detailing the preliminary findings from the first cycle of its Public Health Emergency Preparedness Assessments, covering 15 countries that did not include Italy. Although most of the countries examined had established mechanisms for international collaboration and mutual aid, some mechanisms were still lacking and often limited to neighboring countries. One Planet, One Health International cooperation is necessary not only to contain outbreaks quickly but also to help prevent them. The need for preparedness has become even more apparent as ecological changes have influenced the emergence and spread of zoonotic diseases, including Italy. Andes hantavirus is an example of a broader challenge arising from increasingly unstable interactions among climate, ecosystems, wildlife, and human societies. Wenbiao Hu, PhD, and Ting Gan, PhD, from the Queensland University of Technology, Brisbane, Australia, argued in BMC Global and Public Health that “the recent hantavirus event should not be interpreted as an isolated outbreak, but as part of a broader planetary health challenge emerging from increasingly unstable interactions among climate, ecosystems, wildlife, and human societies.” They argued that such events are often approached reactively after they occur, whereas a more proactive approach would seek to predict and anticipate the ecological conditions under which outbreaks can occur. Like other hantaviruses, the Andes virus is zoonotic and persists in rodents, particularly the small mouse Oligoryzomys longicaudatus, which lives in the Andes and Argentina. Therefore, its spread depends partly on the climatic and environmental conditions in which the reservoir species is found. Climate can play a major role in determining the likelihood of new outbreaks; however, it is not the only factor. Land use, including forest and land cover, can also influence the spread of hantavirus. Predicting when weather conditions could lead to increased transmission could help raise awareness and support early intervention. Understanding how human activities and local ecology influence viral spread could also help reduce the risk for new exposures. Investment in scientific research and surveillance in locations both remote and in Europe, where viruses with pandemic potential circulation could therefore have benefits beyond the areas being monitored. “An important future direction will be the development of integrated climate-informed early warning systems capable of linking environmental monitoring, ecological surveillance, wildlife reservoir dynamics, and human disease data in near real time. Such systems may help identify ecological conditions associated with elevated spillover risk, support spatial risk prediction, and enable earlier public health intervention before widespread transmission occurs” Hu and Gan concluded. More than eight in ten physicians say media coverage of hantavirus is overstating the actual risk to the general public, even as patient anxiety climbs in the wake of recent headlines, according to a new Sermo survey of 104 U.S. physicians across infectious disease, internal medicine, pulmonology and emergency medicine. Seventy-seven percent of respondents rated the current risk to the general public as low or non-meaningful, consistent with CDC data showing fewer than 50 hantavirus cases are typically reported in the U.S. annually. Yet 62% of physicians said they have already seen an increase in patient inquiries about the virus. Physicians described a familiar dynamic: heightened media coverage driving patient concern without a corresponding change in underlying disease risk. The most common clinical response was education on transmission, cited by 89% of respondents, followed by reassurance about low transmission risk and prevention guidance around rodent exposure. Three sentenced in Moscow-based $2B telemedicine fraud scheme targeting U.S. insurers The criminal organization ran ghost telemedicine visits, fraudulent prescriptions and remotely controlled pharmacies across more than 75 locations nationwide. Three members of a Moscow-based criminal organization have been sentenced to federal prison for their roles in a nearly $2 billion health care fraud conspiracy that used sham telemedicine visits, fraudulent prescriptions and remotely operated pharmacies to defraud private insurers, with many patients never receiving the medications billed in their names. Anthony Santamaria received 10 years; co-defendants Hershel Tsikman and Hafizullah Ebady received 120 and 97 months, respectively. Between 2017 and 2022, the organization used call centers in Utah and later Russia to contact insurance beneficiaries and generate prescriptions regardless of whether they agreed to receive medications, then submitted more than $1.97 billion in fraudulent claims through over 75 pharmacies acquired across New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Texas, Michigan and Alabama. Private insurers paid out more than $758 million before the scheme was uncovered. The defendants concealed their involvement through aliases, encrypted communications, shell companies and straw owners, laundering proceeds internationally. The organization's alleged leader, U.S. citizen Brian Sutton, is believed to be living abroad and remains at large. Three additional co-defendants are awaiting sentencing. Nearly 1 in 10 surgeons leave the profession within 8 years, study finds Mid-career surgeons are most at risk, and attrition spiked sharply in 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic. Nearly 10% of surgeons left clinical practice within an eight-year period, with attrition highest among mid-career surgeons and in subspecialties including oral and maxillofacial surgery, obstetrics and gynecology, and plastic and reconstructive surgery, according to a new study published in the Journal of the American College of Surgeons. Researchers at Ohio State University analyzed data on more than 224,000 surgeons across 19 specialties from 2013 to 2023 and found overall attrition rates held steady until 2020, when they rose sharply, likely driven by pandemic-era retirements. Five-year cumulative attrition was highest in oral and maxillofacial surgery at 25.1%, followed by obstetrics and gynecology at 23.2% and plastic and reconstructive surgery at 19.3%. The share of surgeons practicing in rural areas shrank from 10.5% to 8.5% over the decade, while the percentage of female surgeons rose from 21.2% to 28.6%. "Surgical attrition is a real problem, and we need to address it in a nuanced and tailored way," said co-author Timothy Pawlik, M.D., M.P.H., Ph.D., FACS, of Ohio State's Wexner Medical Center. proximity 各不相同。HFRS病例数据由县级疾病预防控制中心工作人员使用国家标准化病例调查问卷收集。比较了临床严重程度和就医行为(医院级别、发病至就诊间隔)。结果:聚集区I的HFRS病例临床严重程度最高(重型占22.41%,危重型占6.65%),而聚集区II的病例则明显较轻(重型占2.62%,危重型占1.31%)。发病至就诊间隔中位数在聚集区II最长(5天),而其他区域为2–4天。医院选择存在差异:聚集区I主要使用二级医院(65.88%);聚集区II在二级(48.44%)和三级(47.01%)医院的使用上几乎持平;聚集区III和非聚集区倾向于选择更高级别医院。结论:研究结果证实,江西省虽然是一个混合流行疫源地,但在临床严重程度和就医行为方面表现出显著的空间异质性。这些发现强调了在HFRS预防和控制中需要采取因地制宜的区域性策略。 syndrome,HFRS)是一种由汉坦病毒(hantavirus,HVs)感染引起的鼠传人畜共患传染病。中国是全球HFRS负担最重的国家,占全球报告病例的90%以上。2004年至2019年间,中国共报告209,209例HFRS病例,病死率(case fatality rate,CFR)为0.89%。江西省是中国HFRS高发区,具有汉滩病毒(Hantaan virus,HTNV)和首尔病毒(Seoul virus,SEOV)共同流行的特征。2005年至2024年间,江西省累计报告近万例HFRS病例,病死率为1.1%,且疫情分布于全省90%以上的县区。既往空间流行病学研究已识别出江西省存在三个特征鲜明的HFRS流行聚集区:聚集区I位于九岭山脉与鄱阳湖西侧之间的西北部区域,为历史高发区,年均发病率为12.10/10万;聚集区II和III分别位于鄱阳湖东侧和南侧及武夷山区域,发病率依次降低至4.20/10万和1.80/10万。然而,既往研究仅关注了发病率的空间分布格局,对于不同聚集区之间病例的临床严重程度是否存在差异,以及患者的就医行为是否受到区域特征的影晌,尚缺乏系统的比较研究。基于此,研究人员提出假设:高发区居民可能具有较短的发病至就诊间隔,且当地医疗机构积累了更丰富的诊疗经验;同时,受空间和环境异质性影响,不同区域的病毒型别构成和宿主携带率可能存在差异,进而导致人群临床严重程度的区域差异。为验证上述假设,研究人员开展了这项回顾性比较研究。 **二、研究设计与核心方法** 该研究依托中国法定传染病监测系统,利用国家标准化HFRS病例调查问卷,由县级疾病预防控制中心专业人员在病例报告后开展流行病学调查,收集病例的一般人口学特征、实验室诊断情况、临床严重程度及就医行为信息,必要时辅以电话访谈补充。研究期间为2011年至2023年,共报告7,172例HFRS病例。经排除缺乏标准化流行病学调查数据(2,374例,占33.10%)以及存在逻辑错误或关键信息缺失的病例后,最终纳入4,614例(占全部报告病例的64.33%)进行分析,其中聚集区I 2,320例、聚集区II 1,120例、聚集区III 542例、非聚集区632例。研究样本在性别、年龄和主要职业构成上与全部报告病例基本可比,且纳入与排除病例的年龄和性别分布相似,提示选择偏倚有限。统计分析采用Kruskal-Wallis H检验比较连续变量,采用卡方检验或Fisher精确检验比较分类变量,组间两两比较采用Bonferroni校正。为调整潜在混杂因素,分别以临床严重程度(轻/中型对比重/危重型)和医院级别(二级对比三级/三级+)为因变量构建两个多变量logistic回归模型,协变量包括聚集区、年龄、性别、职业和发病年份,报告校正优势比(adjusted odds ratio,aOR)及95%置信区间。所有统计分析使用R软件(版本4.3.0)完成,以P<0.05为差异有统计学意义。 **三、主要研究结果** **(一)病例一般特征** 2011年至2023年,江西省累计报告HFRS病例7,172例,累积发病率为1.64/10万,年报告发病率总体呈下降趋势,从2011年的1.24/10万波动下降至2023年的0.66/10万。各聚集区累积发病率差异显著,聚集区I、II、III分别为7.50/10万、3.27/10万和1.46/10万,而非聚集区仅为0.34/10万。纳入分析的4,614例病例中,男女性别比为2.09:1,四个区域间无显著差异。平均年龄为49±18岁。聚集区I中0–20岁年龄组比例显著高于其他区域(14.44%对比4.59%~6.46%),而41–60岁年龄组的比例则相反(37.20%对比46.25%~49.45%)。聚集区I学生比例显著高于其他区域,非聚集区农民比例显著低于其他区域。 **(二)临床严重程度分布** 在具有临床严重程度信息的4,403例病例中,轻型、中型、重型和危重型比例分别为49.15%、30.66%、15.17%和5.02%。聚集区II和非聚集区轻型病例比例较高,而聚集区I和III重型及危重型病例更为常见。聚集区I的危重型比例高于聚集区III。共记录79例死亡病例,总体病死率为1.79%。聚集区I(1.94%)和III(2.18%)的病死率相近,均高于聚集区II(0.47%)。值得注意的是,非聚集区的病死率最高,达3.31%。 **(三)就医行为** 在记录有就诊医院的4,533例病例中,0.55%前往一级医院就诊,51.36%前往二级医院,31.41%前往三级医院,16.68%前往三级+医院。聚集区I和II呈现从二级至三级+医院患者比例递减的趋势,而聚集区III和非聚集区则相反,其中非聚集区前往三级+医院就诊的比例最高(38.50%)。发病至就诊间隔分析在排除22例超过60天的记录错误后纳入4,592例。聚集区II的间隔中位数最长,为5天,显著长于聚集区I(3天)、聚集区III(2天)和非聚集区(4天)。这一模式在按性别、年龄和职业分层的亚组分析中保持一致。轻型病例的间隔中位数(4天)显著长于中型(3天)、重型(3天)和危重型(3天)。医院级别方面,三级医院病例的间隔最长(中位数4天),其次为二级(3天)、三级+(2天)和一级(1天)。 **(四)医院级别分布** 各聚集区的医院选择偏好差异显著。聚集区I以二级医院为主,占65.88%,仅19.17%前往三级医院。聚集区II在二级(48.44%)和三级(47.01%)医院之间呈近乎均衡的分布,且三级+医院就诊比例在所有区域中最低(3.31%)。相比之下,聚集区III和非聚集区前往三级+医院的比例大幅升高,分别为27.72%和38.50%。综合三级及三级+医院就诊比例,由高至低依次为:聚集区III(75.29%)、非聚集区(72.37%)、聚集区II(50.32%)和聚集区I(33.81%)。时间趋势分析显示,聚集区I-III的病例随时间推移越来越多地选择更高级别医院就诊。亚组分析显示,同区域内不同性别间医院级别分布相似(P=0.07)。各区域中,0–15岁年龄组前往三级/三级+医院的比例最高,且该比例随年龄增长而下降。农民前往三级/三级+医院的比例低于其他职业,而学生利用高级别医院的比例显著较高——尤其在聚集区III,92.59%的学生前往三级/三级+医院就诊。一级医院就诊仅见于轻型(0.56%)和中型(0.75%)病例,重型和危重型病例中完全没有。三级+医院就诊比例随疾病严重程度增加而上升,但轻型病例的该比例显著高于中型和重型。 **(五)实验室确认率** 各聚集区的实验室确认率差异显著。聚集区II在各严重程度类别中确认率均最低,而聚集区III在重型和危重型类别中确认率最高。不同医院级别间的确认率亦存在差异:聚集区I和III中三级+医院确认率最高,分别为79.82%和82.27%;非聚集区二级医院确认率最高(82.39%);聚集区II中二级(43.84%)和三级+(41.94%)确认率相当,均明显高于三级医院(25.30%)。 **(六)多变量回归分析** 以临床严重程度为因变量的logistic回归分析显示,与非聚集区病例相比,聚集区I和III患者发生重/危重病的风险显著增高,而聚集区II患者的风险显著降低。男性相对于女性重症风险增加22%。发病年份每增加1年,重症概率降低6%。年龄和职业亚类与严重程度无显著关联。以医院级别为因变量的回归分析显示,聚集区I和II患者在三级/三级+医院就诊的可能性较非聚集区显著降低,风险分别降低80%和58%。聚集区III呈正向趋势但未达统计学显著性。年龄增长与较低的三级/三级+入院概率相关,男性则高于女性。家务/失业及其他职业者比农民更有可能在三级/三级+医院就诊。发病年份每增加1年,三级医院入院概率增加4%。 **四、讨论与结论** 该研究首次聚焦江西省不同HFRS空间聚集区间的病例差异。聚集区I贡献了全省一半以上的报告病例,男女比例(2.09:1)低于相邻的浙江省(2.56:1)和湖北省(3:1),提示女性感染风险不容忽视。聚集区I的0–20岁年龄组比例显著高于其他区域而41–60岁年龄组偏低,研究人员推测前者与16岁以下人群未接种疫苗相关,后者可能与既往针对16–60岁人群的疫苗接种历史有关,但缺乏个体接种记录的直接支持。临床严重程度的显著区域差异可能归因于天然屏障(山脉和鄱阳湖)阻隔了宿主移动和病毒基因流动,以及海拔差异形成的生态位分化。实验室证据表明聚集区I某县仅存在HTNV而无SEOV,而聚集区II毗邻历史上HFRS流行的浙江省且两地SEOV毒株具有一定相似性。依据SEOV感染通常较HTNV感染临床表现更轻微这一既往文献证据,研究人员推测聚集区II可能以较温和的SEOV感染为主,而聚集区I则经历更高比例的HTNV相关重症,但这一基因型归属推断基于地理和文献线索而非本研究队列的直接病毒测序数据。非聚集区病死率最高可能与当地初级医疗机构对HFRS的诊疗意识有限以及转诊上级医院进行实验室确认所致的治疗延迟有关,该推测同样缺乏个体层面临床数据的直接支持。聚集区I和III严重程度分布相近可能与其地理邻近和共享主要啮齿动物宿主有关。发病至就诊间隔的区域差异与轻型病例比例、医疗可及性、交通基础设施和HFRS防治知识宣传程度等因素相关。聚集区I主要使用二级医院提示当地县级医院积累了较强的HFRS诊疗经验,而聚集区II的较低实验室确认率(34.96%对比69.79%)与较长就诊间隔相符,提示基层医院诊断能力不足可能导致转诊延迟。医院级别选择的职业分化可能反映社会经济地位差异:家务/失业者及其他职业者比农民更倾向于高等级医院,而学生与非农民无显著差异。 该研究存在以下局限:约33%的排除率可能引入选择偏倚,尽管纳入与排除病例的年龄性别分布相似,但重症患者可能因记录更完整而被优先保留,从而高估重/危重比例;各聚集区间排除率差异显著(15.79%~57.27%),排除率高的区域可能不成比例地遗漏轻型病例,干扰区域间比较;缺乏病毒基因分型数据(如HTNV对比SEOV)和个体接种记录,病毒和免疫机制的阐释属于推测性质;缺乏健康素养或医疗可及性的直接测量,以发病至就诊间隔和医院级别作为替代指标不可避免受到区域基础设施和卫生系统因素的混杂;13年回顾性数据无法确定就医行为与临床严重程度之间关联的方向性。 研究结论:该回顾性研究的发现为江西省HFRS严重程度和就医模式的区域异质性提供了探索性证据。与高发区被自然屏障隔离的聚集区II表现出较低的重症比例和较长的发病至就诊间隔,强调了实施地理差异化公共卫生策略的必要性。在重症聚集区(I和III),应优先加强预警系统和本地二级医院的重症监护能力;在轻型为主的聚集区(II),可考虑缩短发病至就诊间隔的干预措施,如社区健康教育、改善交通连接或加强基层转诊路径。在省级层面,观察到的异质性凸显了建立地理分层监测系统的重要性,以便及时发现新兴高风险区域并实现资源的适应性配置。需指出的是,这些政策建议属于假设生成性质,源于观察性关联而非已证实的因果机制。未来开展纳入病毒基因分型、健康素养评估和医疗可及性指标的前瞻性研究,对于验证这些观察结果和优化针对性干预措施十分必要。 virus,SEOV)共同流行的特征。2005年至2024年间,江西省累计报告近万例HFRS病例,病死率为1.1%,且疫情分布于全省90%以上的县区。既往空间流行病学研究已识别出江西省存在三个特征鲜明的HFRS流行聚集区:聚集区I位于九岭山脉与鄱阳湖西侧之间的西北部区域,为历史高发区,年均发病率为12.10/10万;聚集区II和III分别位于鄱阳湖东侧和南侧及武夷山区域,发病率依次降低至4.20/10万和1.80/10万。然而,既往研究仅关注了发病率的空间分布格局,对于不同聚集区之间病例的临床严重程度是否存在差异,以及患者的就医行为是否受到区域特征的影晌,尚缺乏系统的比较研究。基于此,研究人员提出假设:高发区居民可能具有较短的发病至就诊间隔,且当地医疗机构积累了更丰富的诊疗经验;同时,受空间和环境异质性影响,不同区域的病毒型别构成和宿主携带率可能存在差异,进而导致人群临床严重程度的区域差异。为验证上述假设,研究人员开展了这项回顾性比较研究。**二、研究设计与核心方法**该研究依托中国法定传染病监测系统,利用国家标准化HFRS病例调查问卷,由县级疾病预防控制中心专业人员在病例报告后开展流行病学调查,收集病例的一般人口学特征、实验室诊断情况、临床严重程度及就医行为信息,必要时辅以电话访谈补充。研究期间为2011年至2023年,共报告7,172例HFRS病例。经排除缺乏标准化流行病学调查数据(2,374例,占33.10%)以及存在逻辑错误或关键信息缺失的病例后,最终纳入4,614例(占全部报告病例的64.33%)进行分析,其中聚集区I 2,320例、聚集区II 1,120例、聚集区III 542例、非聚集区632例。研究样本在性别、年龄和主要职业构成上与全部报告病例基本可比,且纳入与排除病例的年龄和性别分布相似,提示选择偏倚有限。统计分析采用Kruskal-Wallis H检验比较连续变量,采用卡方检验或Fisher精确检验比较分类变量,组间两两比较采用Bonferroni校正。为调整潜在混杂因素,分别以临床严重程度(轻/中型对比重/危重型)和医院级别(二级对比三级/三级+)为因变量构建两个多变量logistic回归模型,协变量包括聚集区、年龄、性别、职业和发病年份,报告校正优势比(adjusted odds ratio,aOR)及95%置信区间。所有统计分析使用R软件(版本4.3.0)完成,以P<0.05为差异有统计学意义。**三、主要研究结果****(一)病例一般特征**2011年至2023年,江西省累计报告HFRS病例7,172例,累积发病率为1.64/10万,年报告发病率总体呈下降趋势,从2011年的1.24/10万波动下降至2023年的0.66/10万。各聚集区累积发病率差异显著,聚集区I、II、III分别为7.50/10万、3.27/10万和1.46/10万,而非聚集区仅为0.34/10万。纳入分析的4,614例病例中,男女性别比为2.09:1,四个区域间无显著差异。平均年龄为49±18岁。聚集区I中0–20岁年龄组比例显著高于其他区域(14.44%对比4.59%~6.46%),而41–60岁年龄组的比例则相反(37.20%对比46.25%~49.45%)。聚集区I学生比例显著高于其他区域,非聚集区农民比例显著低于其他区域。**(二)临床严重程度分布**在具有临床严重程度信息的4,403例病例中,轻型、中型、重型和危重型比例分别为49.15%、30.66%、15.17%和5.02%。聚集区II和非聚集区轻型病例比例较高,而聚集区I和III重型及危重型病例更为常见。聚集区I的危重型比例高于聚集区III。共记录79例死亡病例,总体病死率为1.79%。聚集区I(1.94%)和III(2.18%)的病死率相近,均高于聚集区II(0.47%)。值得注意的是,非聚集区的病死率最高,达3.31%。**(三)就医行为**在记录有就诊医院的4,533例病例中,0.55%前往一级医院就诊,51.36%前往二级医院,31.41%前往三级医院,16.68%前往三级+医院。聚集区I和II呈现从二级至三级+医院患者比例递减的趋势,而聚集区III和非聚集区则相反,其中非聚集区前往三级+医院就诊的比例最高(38.50%)。发病至就诊间隔分析在排除22例超过60天的记录错误后纳入4,592例。聚集区II的间隔中位数最长,为5天,显著长于聚集区I(3天)、聚集区III(2天)和非聚集区(4天)。这一模式在按性别、年龄和职业分层的亚组分析中保持一致。轻型病例的间隔中位数(4天)显著长于中型(3天)、重型(3天)和危重型(3天)。医院级别方面,三级医院病例的间隔最长(中位数4天),其次为二级(3天)、三级+(2天)和一级(1天)。**(四)医院级别分布**各聚集区的医院选择偏好差异显著。聚集区I以二级医院为主,占65.88%,仅19.17%前往三级医院。聚集区II在二级(48.44%)和三级(47.01%)医院之间呈近乎均衡的分布,且三级+医院就诊比例在所有区域中最低(3.31%)。相比之下,聚集区III和非聚集区前往三级+医院的比例大幅升高,分别为27.72%和38.50%。综合三级及三级+医院就诊比例,由高至低依次为:聚集区III(75.29%)、非聚集区(72.37%)、聚集区II(50.32%)和聚集区I(33.81%)。时间趋势分析显示,聚集区I-III的病例随时间推移越来越多地选择更高级别医院就诊。亚组分析显示,同区域内不同性别间医院级别分布相似(P=0.07)。各区域中,0–15岁年龄组前往三级/三级+医院的比例最高,且该比例随年龄增长而下降。农民前往三级/三级+医院的比例低于其他职业,而学生利用高级别医院的比例显著较高——尤其在聚集区III,92.59%的学生前往三级/三级+医院就诊。一级医院就诊仅见于轻型(0.56%)和中型(0.75%)病例,重型和危重型病例中完全没有。三级+医院就诊比例随疾病严重程度增加而上升,但轻型病例的该比例显著高于中型和重型。**(五)实验室确认率**各聚集区的实验室确认率差异显著。聚集区II在各严重程度类别中确认率均最低,而聚集区III在重型和危重型类别中确认率最高。不同医院级别间的确认率亦存在差异:聚集区I和III中三级+医院确认率最高,分别为79.82%和82.27%;非聚集区二级医院确认率最高(82.39%);聚集区II中二级(43.84%)和三级+(41.94%)确认率相当,均明显高于三级医院(25.30%)。**(六)多变量回归分析**以临床严重程度为因变量的logistic回归分析显示,与非聚集区病例相比,聚集区I和III患者发生重/危重病的风险显著增高,而聚集区II患者的风险显著降低。男性相对于女性重症风险增加22%。发病年份每增加1年,重症概率降低6%。年龄和职业亚类与严重程度无显著关联。以医院级别为因变量的回归分析显示,聚集区I和II患者在三级/三级+医院就诊的可能性较非聚集区显著降低,风险分别降低80%和58%。聚集区III呈正向趋势但未达统计学显著性。年龄增长与较低的三级/三级+入院概率相关,男性则高于女性。家务/失业及其他职业者比农民更有可能在三级/三级+医院就诊。发病年份每增加1年,三级医院入院概率增加4%。**四、讨论与结论**该研究首次聚焦江西省不同HFRS空间聚集区间的病例差异。聚集区I贡献了全省一半以上的报告病例,男女比例(2.09:1)低于相邻的浙江省(2.56:1)和湖北省(3:1),提示女性感染风险不容忽视。聚集区I的0–20岁年龄组比例显著高于其他区域而41–60岁年龄组偏低,研究人员推测前者与16岁以下人群未接种疫苗相关,后者可能与既往针对16–60岁人群的疫苗接种历史有关,但缺乏个体接种记录的直接支持。临床严重程度的显著区域差异可能归因于天然屏障(山脉和鄱阳湖)阻隔了宿主移动和病毒基因流动,以及海拔差异形成的生态位分化。实验室证据表明聚集区I某县仅存在HTNV而无SEOV,而聚集区II毗邻历史上HFRS流行的浙江省且两地SEOV毒株具有一定相似性。依据SEOV感染通常较HTNV感染临床表现更轻微这一既往文献证据,研究人员推测聚集区II可能以较温和的SEOV感染为主,而聚集区I则经历更高比例的HTNV相关重症,但这一基因型归属推断基于地理和文献线索而非本研究队列的直接病毒测序数据。非聚集区病死率最高可能与当地初级医疗机构对HFRS的诊疗意识有限以及转诊上级医院进行实验室确认所致的治疗延迟有关,该推测同样缺乏个体层面临床数据的直接支持。聚集区I和III严重程度分布相近可能与其地理邻近和共享主要啮齿动物宿主有关。发病至就诊间隔的区域差异与轻型病例比例、医疗可及性、交通基础设施和HFRS防治知识宣传程度等因素相关。聚集区I主要使用二级医院提示当地县级医院积累了较强的HFRS诊疗经验,而聚集区II的较低实验室确认率(34.96%对比69.79%)与较长就诊间隔相符,提示基层医院诊断能力不足可能导致转诊延迟。医院级别选择的职业分化可能反映社会经济地位差异:家务/失业者及其他职业者比农民更倾向于高等级医院,而学生与非农民无显著差异。该研究存在以下局限:约33%的排除率可能引入选择偏倚,尽管纳入与排除病例的年龄性别分布相似,但重症患者可能因记录更完整而被优先保留,从而高估重/危重比例;各聚集区间排除率差异显著(15.79%~57.27%),排除率高的区域可能不成比例地遗漏轻型病例,干扰区域间比较;缺乏病毒基因分型数据(如HTNV对比SEOV)和个体接种记录,病毒和免疫机制的阐释属于推测性质;缺乏健康素养或医疗可及性的直接测量,以发病至就诊间隔和医院级别作为替代指标不可避免受到区域基础设施和卫生系统因素的混杂;13年回顾性数据无法确定就医行为与临床严重程度之间关联的方向性。研究结论:该回顾性研究的发现为江西省HFRS严重程度和就医模式的区域异质性提供了探索性证据。与高发区被自然屏障隔离的聚集区II表现出较低的重症比例和较长的发病至就诊间隔,强调了实施地理差异化公共卫生策略的必要性。在重症聚集区(I和III),应优先加强预警系统和本地二级医院的重症监护能力;在轻型为主的聚集区(II),可考虑缩短发病至就诊间隔的干预措施,如社区健康教育、改善交通连接或加强基层转诊路径。在省级层面,观察到的异质性凸显了建立地理分层监测系统的重要性,以便及时发现新兴高风险区域并实现资源的适应性配置。需指出的是,这些政策建议属于假设生成性质,源于观察性关联而非已证实的因果机制。未来开展纳入病毒基因分型、健康素养评估和医疗可及性指标的前瞻性研究,对于验证这些观察结果和优化针对性干预措施十分必要。
