Original abstract (English)
In April 2026, a hantavirus pulmonary syndrome (HPS) outbreak caused by Andes virus (ANDV) occurred on a cruise ship. ANDV genome sequences were obtained for 10 of the 13 identified cases during a cross-country investigation established to share initial raw data for joint analysis with reciprocal quality control. The genomic data, symptom onsets, and plausible contact patterns strongly support a tightly linked outbreak cluster compatible with a single or very few zoonotic spillover events prior to embarkment followed by human-to-human transmission onboard. The outbreak represents the international displacement of a virus already known to be capable, under specific conditions, to transmit among humans, and does not suggest emergence of a unusual viral strain.

Immunogenicity of Hantavirus and evaluation of vaccine protective efficacy: A comparative neutralizing antibody study of vaccine strains and representative epidemic strains from selected provinces in China.

Authors: Xiaoxia Huang, Zhangqi Zheng, Shanshan Du, Aqian Li, Fangzhen Tian, Tianmeng Gu, Lanling Zheng, Li Chuan, Qin Wang, Li Yuan
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS) caused by Hantavirus (HV) remains a significant public health burden in the world. HFRS bivalent inactivated vaccine (HFRSbiv) with Hantaan virus (HTNV) and Seoul virus (SEOV) strains isolated 20 years ago have been used in key HFRS endemic provinces in China for HFRS prevention. After decades of effective implementation of comprehensive prevention and control measures, HFRS has entered a stage of epidemic fluctuation at low-level in China. However, the continuous transmission of the virus among rodent host animals may lead to dynamic changes in genetic diversity and immunogenicity of the virus, which may affect the pathogenic mechanism of the virus and the protective effect of vaccines. It is necessary to carry out timely monitoring and evaluation of the immunogenicity of HV. METHODOLOGY/PRINCIPAL FINDINGS: A panel of sera was established by screening serum samples from HFRS patients at convalescent phase and individuals who had received standardized immunization with HFRSbiv. Neutralizing antibody titers against HTNV vaccine strain 84FLi, SEOV vaccine strain L99, and three representative epidemic strains from different genotypes (HTNV: SX26, SEOV: LN03 and FJ-17) were measured using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay-based microneutralization test (MN-ELISA). HV-neutralizing antibodies were detected from all the selected serum samples. Among HFRS patients, 47.92% (23/48) were identified as HTNV infection, 27.08% (13/48) were with SEOV infection, and 25% (12/48) were untypable. There is a significant difference in the titers of neutralizing antibodies against vaccine strains and epidemic strains detected in the serum samples. For HTNV, both patient and vaccinee sera exhibited significantly higher geometric mean titer (GMT) against vaccine strain 84FLi than against epidemic strain SX26 (1:386.08 vs. 1:223.04, P < 0.001; 1:137.94 vs. 1:110.37, P < 0.05, respectively). For SEOV, patient sera showed significantly lower GMT against vaccine strain L99 than against epidemic strain LN03 (1:177.02 vs. 1:297.71, P < 0.01), and vaccinee sera showed significantly lower GMT against L99 than against epidemic strain FJ-17 (1:97.52 vs. 1:243.71, P < 0.001). Significant immunogenicity heterogeneity was observed between SEOV epidemic strains LN03 and FJ-17. CONCLUSIONS/SIGNIFICANCE: The genetic diversity and immunogenicity of HV, which causes HFRS, have certain geographical regional characteristics. The levels of cross neutralizing antibodies induced by infection of HV from different genotypes may vary significantly, but still provide sufficient cross protection. The HFRSbiv currently used in China could induce broad neutralizing antibody, the cross-protective efficacy might vary against different circulating strains and genotypes. It is of great significance to strengthen the monitoring of genetic characteristics of regional specific circulating viral strains, regularly evaluate the matching degree between vaccine protection effectiveness and epidemic strains, predict and evaluate the transmission and pathogenic risks of HV, and timely optimize HFRS prevention and control strategies and measures.

Molecular evolution of Andes virus lineages leading to recent human outbreaks

Authors: Charu Sharma, Oversti, S., Robert Stass, Alexander G. Lucaci, Jonathan Audet, David Safronetz, Lele Zhao, Katrina Lythgoe, Aris Katzourakis, Thomas A. Bowden
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Andes virus (ANDV) is exceptional among hantaviruses in its capacity for person-to-person transmission, but whether outbreaks reflect recent viral adaptation or emergence of pre-existing reservoir lineages remains unclear. We generated paired blood- and nasal-derived genomes from the MV Hondius outbreak and integrated them with a segment-linked collection of human- and rodent-derived ANDV sequences, including incomplete genomes. We developed a linkage-informed missing-segment framework that preserves isolate identity, sampling time, and cross-segment placement without imputing nucleotide states, enabling sensitivity analyses of patchy segment coverage. Across five human outbreaks, dated phylogenies placed outbreak lineages independently within geographically structured reservoir diversity, with closest sampled ancestry predating detection. Time-dependent-rate analyses indicated a deeper reservoir-associated history, while mutation reconstruction, selection tests, branch-rate comparisons, and structural mapping found no recurrent adaptive transition preceding human transmission. MV Hondius samples showed minimal consensus-level diversification. These results shift attention from repeated recent adaptation toward deeper lineage properties and ecological opportunity.

Original abstract (English)
Background: Reticulated platelets, reported by modern hematology analyzers as the immature platelet fraction (IPF%) or the absolute immature platelet count (IPC), index thrombopoiesis and have been proposed as biomarkers in the thrombocytopenia of viral infection. Methods: This systematic review, conducted and reported following PRISMA 2020 and registered in PROSPERO (CRD420261501412), examined how these parameters have been used across viral diseases. PubMed, Scopus, and the Cochrane Library were searched from January 2000 to May 2025 for observational studies of adults with viral infection reporting IPF, IPC, or reticulated platelets, and methodological robustness was appraised with the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale. Results: Twenty-two studies were included and synthesized narratively. Applications differed by pathogen: in dengue, serial IPF increases consistently preceded platelet recovery, although proposed thresholds varied; in COVID-19, higher immature platelet parameters were associated with severity and mortality but were inconsistent after adjustment. Evidence in chronic hepatitis, HIV, hantavirus, and Crimean–Congo hemorrhagic fever was mainly pathophysiological, and data in critically ill patients were almost confined to COVID-19. Conclusions: Clinical value appears disease- and outcome-specific: serial IPF best predicts platelet recovery in dengue, whereas prognostic use in COVID-19 remains insufficiently validated. Analytical heterogeneity and the absence of transferable thresholds currently limit clinical implementation.

The investigation assessed whether viral divergence across rodent reservoirs influences the humoral immunity elicited by long-standing immunization programs. Hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS), caused by Hantavirus, continues to present a significant public health burden globally. In key endemic provinces throughout China, prevention efforts have relied on a bivalent inactivated vaccine formulated with Hantaan virus (HTNV) and Seoul virus (SEOV) strains originally isolated two decades ago. Following decades of comprehensive control measures, HFRS incidence in China has transitioned into a phase of low-level epidemic fluctuation. Nevertheless, ongoing viral transmission within wild rodent host populations can spur dynamic shifts in genetic diversity and immunogenicity, creating uncertainty regarding ongoing vaccine-induced protection and underlying pathogenic mechanisms. Using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay-based microneutralization test (MN-ELISA), neutralizing antibody titers were measured against the HTNV vaccine strain 84FLi, the SEOV vaccine strain L99, and three representative epidemic strains representing distinct genotypes: HTNV strain SX26 and SEOV strains LN03 and FJ-17. Hantavirus-neutralizing antibodies were successfully detected across all tested serum samples. Among the 48 evaluated HFRS patients, laboratory identification categorized 47.92 percent (23 of 48) as HTNV infections, 27.08 percent (13 of 48) as SEOV infections, and 25 percent (12 of 48) as untypable cases. Titers against vaccine strains and circulating epidemic strains exhibited pronounced divergence. For HTNV, both convalescent patient sera and vaccinee sera demonstrated significantly higher geometric mean titers against the vaccine strain 84FLi compared to the epidemic strain SX26, registering 1:386.08 versus 1:223.04 (P < 0.001) in patients and 1:137.94 versus 1:110.37 (P < 0.05) in vaccinees. In contrast, the pattern reversed for SEOV strains. Patient sera displayed significantly lower geometric mean titers against the L99 vaccine strain than against the epidemic strain LN03, at 1:177.02 versus 1:297.71 (P < 0.01). Similarly, vaccinee sera showed significantly lower titers against L99 compared to the epidemic strain FJ-17, measuring 1:97.52 versus 1:243.71 (P < 0.001), while significant immunogenicity heterogeneity was also observed between the LN03 and FJ-17 epidemic strains. The study provides critical nuance regarding how cross-neutralization functions across divergent viral genotypes. While the bivalent inactivated vaccine used in China induces broad neutralizing antibodies, its cross-protective efficacy can vary when challenged by different circulating strains and genotypes. Natural infection with Hantavirus similarly generates cross-neutralizing antibodies whose titers differ significantly depending on the genotype involved, yet still succeed in conferring sufficient cross-protection. This sustained animal-reservoir circulation can drive dynamic changes in the genetic diversity and immunogenicity of Hantavirus. In turn, such shifts have the potential to influence viral pathogenic mechanisms and diminish the protective effectiveness of established vaccines, making timely monitoring and evaluation of viral immunogenicity an indispensable priority. To sustain epidemic control, the study underscores several critical surveillance and public health priorities. The authors highlight the great significance of strengthening surveillance of the genetic characteristics of regional-specific circulating viral strains. In parallel, public health programs must regularly evaluate the matching degree between vaccine protective effectiveness and currently circulating epidemic strains. Closeness raises risk. In a study of households in Chile , 17.6 percent of patients' sex partners developed the disease, compared with 1.2 percent of other household contacts. A risk of 2 percent or less is small and may not be zero, which fits what both the U.S. CDC and the World Health Organization have said, although they interpreted it differently. For WHO the follow-up "demonstrates effective interruption of transmission." In contrast, the CDC cautioned that the absence of infections among U.S. contacts "should not be interpreted as evidence that no transmission risk existed." Neither is wrong. I calculated those upper bounds with a statistical shortcut called the rule of three . It is an interesting (and useful!) mathematical fact. When an event has not occurred in a group, we can be about 95 percent confident that its true rate is below three divided by the group's size. (The calculation assumes that each person's outcome is independent of everyone else's and that everyone has the same risk.) WHO listed 317 high-risk and 336 low-risk contacts, but those figures include people from the ship, and anyone aboard found to be infected was counted as a case. Removing from the high-risk list the 169 people who sailed and were not counted as cases leaves about 150 who never sailed, assuming all 169 were on the list.
