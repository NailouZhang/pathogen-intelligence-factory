A Virucidal And Endosomal Trapping Peptide Against Human Adenoviruses In Respiratory Organoids And Hamsters.

Authors: Xiankun Wang, Zhifeng Guan, Zixian Song, Stephanie Joy-Ann Lam, Pok Man Lai, Kong-Hung Sze, Kwok-Yung Yuen, Kelvin Kai-Wang To, Hanjun Zhao
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Human adenoviruses (HAdVs) are common respiratory pathogens that cause severe, life-threatening infections in immunocompromised individuals and children. Despite their medical importance, no clinically approved antiviral drugs exist. Here, we showed that 4H30, a 4-branched human defensin peptide, exhibited broad-spectrum antiviral activity against multiple HAdV subtypes in A549 and Calu-3 cells with negligible cytotoxicity and a high barrier to viral resistance in A549 cells. Mechanistic studies demonstrated that 4H30 exhibited a unique dual mechanism of action: direct virucidal activity through capsid disruption and endosomal trapping by impairing protein VI-mediated membrane penetration. Notably, 4H30 remained effective against HAdV-3 in human respiratory organoids and against HAdV-2 and HAdV-5 in an immunosuppressed hamster model, significantly reducing viral replication and disease-associated pathology. These findings demonstrate that 4H30 can inhibit multiple HAdVs through a novel mechanism, offering a new therapeutic avenue against HAdV infections.

Efficacy of alcohol-based hand rubs against human adenovirus on contaminated hands.

问题与背景
病毒主要通过直接或间接的手接触,对医疗机构中的 暴发 产生显着影响。因此,使用卫生洗手液进行手部消毒是预防医疗环境中院内病毒感染的关键措施。
设计与对象
该研究使用基于 EN 17430 的交叉研究设计,评估了各种乙醇制剂和两种市售酒精混合物针对 人腺病毒 5 型 (AdV-5) 的体内功效。
核心方法
该研究采用基于 EN 17430 的交叉设计,评估浓度为 60% 至 97% 的纯乙醇制剂和两种酒精混合物(M1:45% 乙醇和 18% 1-丙醇;M2:45% 2-丙醇和 30% 1-丙醇)在 5 名或 20 名志愿者手上的体内功效。

解释与创新
AdV-5 悬浮液测试的结果不能直接转移到根据 EN 17430 在实际条件下测试的醇配方获得的结果。
科研与公卫意义
研究结果警告不要将悬浮试验的结果直接转移到实际条件中,强调需要对洗手液进行体内评估以预防 AdV-5 感染。
局限与证据强度
研究设计的局限性包括样本量小和交叉设计的使用,这可能会引入偏差。由于这些限制,证据强度中等。

Efficacy of alcohol-based hand rubs against human adenovirus on contaminated hands.

Authors: Britta Becker, Dajana Paulmann, Toni L Meister, Jörg Steinmann, Andreas Arndt, Heiko Henze, Eike Steinmann, Florian H H Brill
Original abstract (English)
Viruses significantly contribute to outbreaks in healthcare facilities, primarily through direct or indirect hand contact. Consequently, hand disinfection using hygienic hand rubs is a pivotal measure for preventing nosocomial virus infections in medical environments. In 2024, the EN 17430 standard was introduced as the first method to evaluate the virucidal efficacy of hand disinfectants under practical conditions. The new method offers an advantage over suspension tests (e.g., EN 14476) by enabling the assessment of hand rubs using human volunteers. This study aims to compare the results of these two methods by investigating the in vivo efficacy of various ethanol formulations and two commercially available alcohol mixtures against human adenovirus type 5 (AdV-5). The in vivo efficacy of pure Ethanol formulations in concentrations from 60% to 97% and two alcohol mixtures (M1: 45 % ethanol and 18 % 1-propanol; M2: 45% 2-propanol and 30% 1-propanol) were evaluated on the hands of 5 or 20 volunteers, utilizing a cross-over study design based on EN 17430. With the different ethanol formulations increasing reduction factors with higher alcohol concentrations were detected. Of the two alcohol-containing mixtures, only M1 demonstrated efficacy based on the requirements of EN 17430, the second formulation M2, however, narrowly failed effectiveness. In conclusion, results from suspension tests with AdV-5 were not directly transferable to those obtained with the alcohol formulations tested under practical conditions as per EN 17430.

A Confirmatory, Dual Center Nonhuman Primate Study on the Efficacy of Novel Oropharyngeal Spray Immunization With an Adenoviral Vector Vaccine Against RSV-Important Lessons Learned.

Authors: Tenbusch M, Koopman G, Mooij P, Roshani B, Irrgang P, Lapuente D, Kondova I, Bogers WM, Remarque EJ, Vestweber R
Original abstract (English)
Background In a randomized, blinded, dual center study across two European primate research facilities, we evaluated the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of a DNA prime-oropharyngeal adenoviral boost vaccination strategy against respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). Methods Rhesus macaques received an RSV fusion protein (F) DNA vaccine via electroporation, followed by two mucosal administrations of a recombinant adenovirus vector encoding the same antigen. Mock-vaccinated animals and a comparator group mimicking natural immunity induced by primary RSV infection were included as controls. Systemic and mucosal immune responses were monitored longitudinally. Results We detected robust cellular and humoral immune responses, but with some variability between the two centers. Nevertheless, RSV-vaccinated animals demonstrated markedly reduced viral replication in the upper and lower respiratory tract after RSV challenge infection. Unexpected RSV-specific immunity in the control group at one single study site prevented confirmation of the predefined primary endpoint. Conclusion Our results support the potential of mucosal adenoviral boosting following DNA priming to induce protective immunity against RSV, while highlighting challenges associated with multi-center preclinical vaccine studies.

Authors: Joshua Wiggins, Adthakorn Madapong, Eric A. Weaver
Original abstract (English)
Background: Emerging and re-emerging infectious diseases pose a growing global threat, underscoring the need for vaccine platforms enabling rapid deployment and durable, broad protection. Although mRNA and viral vector vaccines transformed pandemic responses, limitations include waning immunity, restricted antigenic breadth, pre-existing vector immunity, and rare vector-associated adverse events. Original abstract (English)
The unfolded protein response (UPR) controls cellular homeostasis and viral infections. It is canonically initiated by dissociation of ER chaperone BiP/GRP78 from lumenal domains of transmembrane sensor proteins, including the central UPR sensor inositol-requiring enzyme 1 alpha (IRE1a). This results in IRE1a activation and splicing of X-box binding protein 1 (XBP1) mRNA, leading to transcriptional activation of XBP1 for cellular adaptation and sustained infection. Human adenovirus (AdV) preferentially activates the IRE1a-XBP1 pathway through the viral membrane protein E3-19K, yet the mechanisms underlying this selective activation remain unclear. Here, we show that AdV uses two distinct early proteins to dissociate BiP from IRE1a and activate IRE1a-mediated XBP1 splicing. The immediate early protein E1A forms a complex with the cytoplasmic domain of IRE1a, sufficient to dissociate BiP from IRE1a without inducing XBP1 splicing or UPR activation. In contrast, E3-19K is expressed downstream of E1A, associates with the lumenal domain of IRE1a, and induces XBP1 splicing without promoting BiP dissociation from IRE1a. Both proteins were detected in IRE1a-containing complexes, consistent with stepwise viral regulation. Together, our findings show that canonical IRE1a activation hallmarks can be mechanistically separated during viral infection, revealing distinct AdV-regulated IRE1a activation states with discrete upstream and downstream signaling outputs.

Original abstract (English)

To find out, the team constructed two vaccines encoding the identical stabilized prefusion F protein, based on the DS-Cav1 design that carries four stabilizing amino acid substitutions. Female BALB/c mice received either 2 micrograms of LNP-RSV-preF saRNA or 2.5 × 109 viral particles of Ad5-RSV-preF intramuscularly on days 0 and 21, followed by immune profiling at multiple time points extending to 120 days after immunization. Quality control confirmed that both platforms were functional. The self-amplifying RNA was verified as intact by gel electrophoresis, and the lipid nanoparticles, built from SM-102, DOPE, cholesterol and a PEG-lipid, showed particle sizes around 150 nanometers with an encapsulation efficiency of approximately 90 percent. The adenoviral particles measured roughly 123 nanometers. The authors interpret this as the adenoviral platform favoring a classical, lymph node-centered priming pattern, with the RNA platform distributing B-cell activation differently across systemic and pulmonary compartments. T-cell responses showed a parallel split. In IFN-γ ELISpot assays of splenocytes, the adenoviral vaccine generated nearly twice as many IFN-γ-secreting cells as the RNA vaccine, and intracellular cytokine staining showed that Ad5-RSV-preF drove greater increases in TNF-α-producing CD4 and CD8 T cells in the spleen and greater increases in IFN-γ- and TNF-α-producing T cells in the lungs, along with stronger IL-2-associated recall potential after antigen restimulation. In the lung, however, the RNA vaccine produced more pronounced increases in CD69-expressing CD4 and CD8 T cells and higher proportions of both CD4 and CD8 tissue-resident memory T cells. In the authors’ framing, the adenoviral vector builds a readily expandable systemic effector reservoir, while the self-amplifying RNA establishes an activated, tissue-resident defensive population at the pulmonary site where RSV first encounters the immune system. Single-cell RNA sequencing of lung cells collected three days after the booster added a transcriptional dimension to this divide. The RNA vaccine reshaped the lung immune landscape toward a T cell-dominant state marked by proliferating, cycling T cells, and induced 1265 upregulated genes in pulmonary T cells compared with the unimmunized group, versus 486 for the adenoviral vaccine. Direct comparison showed the RNA platform associated with higher expression of genes linked to T cell differentiation and memory formation, including Tcf7 and Ccr7, while the adenoviral platform was associated with increased expression of cytotoxic and inflammatory genes including Gzmb, Gzma and Ifng, along with chemokine-related genes, and a more heterogeneous composition with increased NK cell representation. Broader flow cytometric profiling three days after the boost showed that both vaccines increased T cell proportions in spleen and lung, but with distinct biases: the adenoviral vaccine more prominently increased CD8 T cells in the spleen, while the RNA vaccine’s changes were more consistent with pulmonary activation and memory-associated redistribution. Long-term follow-up at 120 days preserved this compartmentalized pattern. Both vaccines maintained substantial serum IgG, with the adenoviral vaccine sustaining higher levels, and serum IgA did not differ significantly between groups. Ad5-RSV-preF elicited higher frequencies of long-lived IgG-positive antibody-secreting cells in the spleen and also in the lung, and roughly twice as many IFN-γ-secreting splenocytes upon antigen restimulation, indicating stronger durable systemic T cell memory. The RNA vaccine, by contrast, induced a greater frequency of long-lived IgA-positive antibody-secreting cells in the spleen — although the two platforms generated comparable numbers of long-lived IgA-positive cells in the lungs, a result the authors highlight as showing that the RNA platform did not provide a measurable long-term pulmonary IgA memory advantage in this model. The authors are explicit about the limits of these findings. No viral challenge experiments were performed, so the protective efficacy of either vaccine in vivo remains untested; the study used only female BALB/c mice and only the intramuscular route, leaving intranasal and other delivery routes unexplored, and the authors suggest that combined saRNA-adenoviral regimens merit future evaluation. What the study does establish, within its preclinical scope, is a framework: with antigen and route held constant, the choice between an adenoviral vector and a lipid nanoparticle-delivered self-amplifying RNA biases immunity toward systemic durability on one side or pulmonary-localized immune readiness on the other, a distinction the authors argue should inform how next-generation RSV vaccines are compared and selected.
