巴西中西部农村社区水源地 人腺病毒 检测

Human adenovirus detection in rural community water sources in central-west Brazil
作者： Graziela Picciola Bordoni, Lucas Candido Gonçalves Barbosa, Fernando Santos Lima, Thais Reis Oliveira, José Daniel Gonçalves Vieira, Paulo Sergio Scalize, Lí­lian Carla Carneiro
摘要中文翻译简介:在发展中国家的农村社区,获得改善的水、环境卫生和个人卫生 (WASH) 服务的机会很差且有限,这可能会导致肠道感染和粪便生物传播的沉重负担。在缺乏公共供水网络的情况下,农村地区从地表水和/或地下水源获取供水,而这些水源的微生物质量往往未知。由于腺病毒在水中具有高度持久性,并且在环境中广泛分布,因此它们已成为肠道病毒的重要类群,并可能潜在地参与水传播的暴发。材料和方法:通过病毒浓缩、核酸提取和分子检测,对从巴西戈亚斯州农村和传统社区的地表水和地下水源获取的总共 160 个水样进行了 人腺病毒 C 的存在分析。结果:在 9.4% (15/160) 的样品中检测到 人腺病毒 C。地下水源的阳性样本频率为 9.8% (12/122)。雨水蓄水池(15.8%)、深管井(11.1%)、浅管井(12.9%)、浅挖井(6.4%)和泉水(11.8%)的样品中也检测到了人腺病毒样品。使用地表水和洒水车样本中采用的技术没有发现病毒。结论:本研究的结果强调,除了支持决策者确定可能受到污染的人口使用的水源外,还需要对农村地区的基本卫生服务进行更多投资。这些病原体的识别和表征对于减少与水传播病原体相关的公共卫生风险非常重要,特别是在戈亚斯州。

问题与背景
发展中国家农村地区获得水、环境卫生和个人卫生服务的机会不佳可能会增加肠道感染负担和粪便病原体传播。本研究旨在检测巴西中西部农村社区使用的水源中的 人腺病毒 C,以评估潜在的水传播风险。
设计与对象
从巴西戈亚斯州农村和传统社区的地表水和地下水源采集了总共 160 个水样,并进行了 人腺病毒 C 分析。样品取自雨水蓄水池、深管井、浅管井、浅挖井、泉水、地表水和运水车。
核心方法
按照标准实验室方案对所有水样进行病毒浓缩、核酸提取和分子检测方法,以鉴定 人腺病毒 C.
主要结果
所有样品中 9.4% (15/160) 检测到 人腺病毒 C。地下水源检测频率为9.8%(12/122),其中浅管井(12.9%)、泉水(11.8%)、深管井(11.1%)、雨水蓄水池(15.8%)和浅挖井(6.4%)呈阳性。地表水或运水车样本中未检测到病毒。
解释与创新
在多种地下水源类型中检测到 人腺病毒 C 表明这些农村社区的饮用水供应存在广泛的低水平污染。本研究提供了缺乏公共配水网络的戈亚斯州私人水源腺病毒污染的基线 监测 数据。
科研与公卫意义
这些发现强调了农村水源中对病原体 监测 的需求,并为服务欠缺社区的水安全干预措施提供了信息。对地下水源进行有针对性的监测可能有助于降低该地区与水传播腺病毒暴露相关的公共卫生风险。
局限与证据强度
这项横断面调查提供了 患病率 数据,但无法确定污染的时间模式或来源归因。该研究仅关注 人腺病毒 C,与细胞培养分离相比,分子检测方法的灵敏度可能有限。鉴于其描述性且缺乏与临床病例的流行病学联系,证据强度中等。

Human adenovirus detection in rural community water sources in central-west Brazil

Authors: Graziela Picciola Bordoni, Lucas Candido Gonçalves Barbosa, Fernando Santos Lima, Thais Reis Oliveira, José Daniel Gonçalves Vieira, Paulo Sergio Scalize, Lí­lian Carla Carneiro
Original abstract (English)
Introduction: Poor and limited access to improved water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, common in rural communities in developing countries, may lead to a high burden of enteric infections and the spread of fecal organisms. In the absence of a public water distribution network, rural areas obtain their water supply from surface and/or groundwater sources, where microbial quality is often unknown. Because adenoviruses are highly persistent in water and widely distributed in the environment, they have become an important group of enteric viruses and may potentially be involved in waterborne outbreaks. Materials and methods: A total of 160 water samples obtained from surface and groundwater sources in rural and traditional communities across Goiás, Brazil, were analyzed for the presence of Human Adenovirus C following viral concentration, nucleic acid extraction, and molecular detection. Results: Human Adenovirus C was detected in 9.4% (15/160) of the samples. Positive samples from groundwater sources had a frequency of 9.8% (12/122). Human adenovirus samples were also detected in samples from cisterns with rainwater (15.8%), deep tubular wells (11.1%), shallow tubular wells (12.9%), shallow dug wells (6.4%) and spring water (11.8%). No virus was found using the techniques adopted in the surface water and water truck samples. Conclusions: The results of this study highlight the need for greater investments in basic sanitation services in rural areas, in addition to supporting decision-makers in identifying water sources used by the population that may be contaminated. The identification and characterization of these pathogens are important for reducing public health risks associated with waterborne pathogens, especially in the State of Goiás.

Question and background
Poor access to water, sanitation and hygiene services in rural areas of developing countries may increase enteric infection burden and fecal pathogen spread. This study aimed to detect Human Adenovirus C in water sources used by rural communities in central-west Brazil to assess potential waterborne exposure risks.
Design and population
A total of 160 water samples were collected from surface and groundwater sources across rural and traditional communities in Goiás, Brazil, and analyzed for Human Adenovirus C. Samples were obtained from cisterns with rainwater, deep tubular wells, shallow tubular wells, shallow dug wells, spring water, surface water and water trucks.
Core methods
Viral concentration, nucleic acid extraction and molecular detection methods were applied to all water samples following standard laboratory protocols for the identification of Human Adenovirus C.
Main results
Human Adenovirus C was detected in 9.4% (15/160) of all samples. Groundwater sources showed a detection frequency of 9.8% (12/122), with positive results in shallow tubular wells (12.9%), spring water (11.8%), deep tubular wells (11.1%), cisterns with rainwater (15.8%) and shallow dug wells (6.4%). No virus was detected in surface water or water truck samples.
Interpretation and novelty
The detection of Human Adenovirus C across multiple groundwater source types indicates widespread low-level contamination of drinking water supplies in these rural communities. This study provides baseline surveillance data on adenoviral contamination of private water sources in Goiás where public water distribution networks are absent.
Scientific and public-health significance
These findings underscore the need for pathogen surveillance in rural water sources and inform water safety interventions in underserved communities. Targeted monitoring of groundwater sources may help reduce public health risks associated with waterborne adenoviral exposure in the region.
Limitations and evidence strength
This cross-sectional survey provides prevalence data but cannot establish temporal patterns of contamination or source attribution. The study focused solely on Human Adenovirus C and molecular detection methods may have limited sensitivity compared to cell culture isolation. Evidence strength is moderate given the descriptive nature and absence of epidemiological linkage to clinical cases.

Sources and dynamics of fecal pollution and antibiotic resistance across a Mediterranean land-sea continuum.

Authors: Angela Freddi, Marco Basili, Antonella Penna, Silvia Casabianca, Benjamin J. Tscharke, Metasebia Gebrewold, Stuart L. Simpson, Elena Manini, Warish Ahmed
Original abstract (English)
A comprehensive understanding of fecal pollution dynamics and associated antibiotic resistance is critical for effective water quality management in coastal watersheds exposed to strong hydrological variability. This study aimed to identify the relative contribution of human and animal fecal sources, assess the spatial and temporal distribution of contamination, and evaluate the occurrence of selected antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs) across a Mediterranean land-sea continuum (central Adriatic Sea, Italy) under contrasting rainfall conditions. Surface water and sediment samples were collected during the bathing season and analyzed using host-associated microbial source tracking (MST) markers and selected ARGs. Human fecal contamination was the dominant source, as indicated by the consistent detection of HF183, which significantly increased during moderate and extreme rainfall events, indicating inputs from runoff and sewer overflows. The presence of human adenovirus (HAdV) further supported sewage-derived contamination, whereas animal-associated markers showed lower detection frequencies and more heterogeneous spatial patterns. ARGs, including tetracycline resistance genes tetM, were widely detected, with generally higher concentrations at freshwater sites. While ARG distribution was influenced by hydrological variability, tetM showed lower temporal variability and remained relatively abundant under drought conditions, suggesting longer-term environmental persistence. Rainfall emerged as a major driver shaping marker distribution, with water showing high temporal variability and sediments acting as secondary reservoirs enriched during heavy rainfall events. These findings provide relevant insights for improving water quality strategies and support targeted management actions to mitigate fecal pollution in Mediterranean coastal systems.

使用嵌套重叠 PCR 和 MinION 对直接从废水中提取的腺病毒 41 进行全 基因组 测序

Whole-genome sequencing of adenovirus 41 directly from wastewater using nested overlapping PCR and MinION
作者： Naziba Nusrat, John S. Meschke, Rachel Swanstrom, Roberto A. Rodríguez
摘要中文翻译摘要 人腺病毒 F41 (HAdV-F41) 是儿童急性胃肠炎的主要原因之一,最近与 2021 年至 2022 年儿童中不明原因的严重急性肝炎 暴发 有关。虽然大多数证据基于临床数据,但基于废水的 流行病学 提供了一种社区级方法来监测循环菌株并增强 暴发准备。在本研究中,我们开发了一种基于重叠扩增子的全 基因组 测序方法,使用具有 13 个引物组的巢式 PCR 直接检测存档废水样品中的 HAdV-F4​​1。存档的废水样本是在 2021 年至 2022 年期间从美国西雅图的三个 治疗 工厂收集的。病毒载量范围为每升 1.2 × 10 3 至 8.4 × 10 3 基因组 拷贝。Oxford Nanopore 平台用于全 基因组 测序。从废水样品中回收了完整或部分 (>84%) HAdV-F4​​1 基因组,平均覆盖深度范围为 103 至 105。共有序列与 NCBI 数据库中的参考 基因组 相似性超过 99%。系统发育分析显示,2a 谱系中有 2 个序列,2b 谱系中有 11 个序列,反映出当时至少有两个亚谱系在群落中流传。我们的结果表明,使用 Oxford Nanopore 平台的基于重叠扩增子的全 基因组 测序方法可以可靠地从废水中回收 HAdV-F4​​1 基因组。该方法提供了循环的、临床相关的 HAdV-F4​​1 的高分辨率 基因组监测,支持基于废水的 流行病学 作为检测新出现变异和加强未来疾病 暴发 早期预警系统的有价值的工具。重要性 人腺病毒 F41 是儿童胃肠炎的主要原因,并与最近发生的儿童重症急性肝炎 暴发 有关,但该病毒的社区水平 暴发 仍然有限。这项研究表明,通过牛津纳米孔平台上的靶向重叠扩增子测序策略,废水可用于回收几乎完整的 HAdV-F4​​1 基因组。通过将该方法应用于存档的废水样本,我们检测到了大城市中多种病毒谱系的同时循环。这些发现将基于废水的 流行病学 扩展到 SARS-CoV-2 之外,并强调了其对于监测具有临床意义的肠道病毒的重要性。这里描述的方法提供了一种可扩展的工具,用于跟踪社区中的病毒 进化 并增强未来 暴发 的预警系统。

The retrospective study analyzed clinical data from 42,044 children admitted to Hebei Children’s Hospital between 2016 and 2020. Sputum samples collected at admission were tested for viral nucleic acids, and positive samples underwent genotyping to determine which adenovirus types were circulating. The researchers combined this molecular surveillance with demographic records, clinical symptoms, and a battery of laboratory indicators, including inflammatory markers, immune cell indices, and measures of cardiac and liver injury. Statistical comparisons relied on chi-squared tests for categorical differences and non-parametric tests, including the Mann–Whitney U and Kruskal–Wallis tests, for continuous variables, while phylogenetic relationships among viral sequences were reconstructed using the neighbor-joining method. Human rhinovirus was the most frequent companion, followed by respiratory syncytial virus and human parainfluenza virus. This high rate of co-infection complicates the traditional picture of adenovirus as a standalone agent of pediatric pneumonia and suggests that in many hospitalized children, adenovirus is part of a polymicrobial assault on the respiratory tract rather than a solitary invader. Human adenovirus is a double-stranded DNA virus with more than 100 recognized genotypes grouped into seven species, but only a handful routinely cause severe lower respiratory disease. Species B viruses, particularly types 3, 7, 14, and 55, have historically been linked to outbreaks of serious pneumonia, especially in East Asia. The Hebei data confirm that types 3 and 7 are the engines of severe pediatric disease in this region of China, consistent with surveillance reports from neighboring provinces and countries. The most clinically consequential finding concerns HAdV-7. Levels of procalcitonin, a marker often associated with bacterial infection but elevated in severe viral inflammation, were higher in HAdV-7 cases. D-dimer, a fibrin degradation product that signals activation of coagulation, was also elevated, pointing toward the hyperinflammatory and prothrombotic state that has been described in severe adenovirus pneumonia. At the same time, the children showed depressed immune indices, suggesting that HAdV-7 may partially blunt the host response it provokes. Markers of cardiac injury and liver injury were also raised, indicating that the virus’s damage can extend well beyond the lungs. This constellation of findings has practical implications. Because adenovirus genotyping is not universally available in clinical laboratories, these surrogate laboratory signals could help clinicians escalate care, initiate antiviral therapy such as cidofovir in severe cases, or prioritize enrollment in trials of emerging adenovirus-specific treatments.
