TLR3–TICAM1–p38 signalling partially mediates IL-6 induction during human adenovirus type 26 infection in epithelial cells with inhibited clathrin-mediated endocytosis
作者： Isabela Drašković, Lea Vrbančić, Davor Nestić, Ksenija Božinović, Gabriela N. Condezo, Carmen San Martín, Jerome H. H. V. Custers, Dragomira Majhen
摘要中文翻译摘要 人腺病毒型26(HAdV-D26)是一种临床重要的媒介,但其进入途径、细胞内运输与先天免疫感知之间的联系尚不清楚。由于先天免疫传感器广泛分布于细胞中,病毒进入途径和亚细胞定位可能影响免疫激活。本研究探讨了HAdV-D26进入途径改变和内体滞留如何影响上皮细胞中Toll样受体(TLR)介导的信号传导。使用抑制网格蛋白介导的内吞作用(CME)或新表达αvβ3整合素的A549细胞,我们分析了HAdV-D26感染后IL-6和IL-8的表达,并研究了其潜在的信号传导机制。在抑制CME的A549细胞中,HAdV-D26感染通过TLR3–TICAM1–p38依赖机制部分诱导IL-6表达。抑制TLR3或p38,以及敲低TICAM1,均降低了在CME抑制条件下观察到的IL-6升高反应。相反,抑制TLR4或敲低MyD88则增加了HAdV-D26诱导的IL-6和IL-8表达,表明TLR4–MyD88信号传导具有负调控作用。此外,在新表达αvβ3整合素的A549细胞中,敲低MyD88降低了IL-8的表达,将整合素介导的进入与MyD88依赖的信号传导联系起来。总之,这些发现强调了HAdV-D26进入途径和细胞内定位如何影响其先天免疫感知。

TLR3–TICAM1–p38 signalling partially mediates IL-6 induction during human adenovirus type 26 infection in epithelial cells with inhibited clathrin-mediated endocytosis

Authors: Isabela Drašković, Lea Vrbančić, Davor Nestić, Ksenija Božinović, Gabriela N. Condezo, Carmen San Martín, Jerome H. H. V. Custers, Dragomira Majhen
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Human adenovirus type 26 (HAdV-D26) is a clinically important vector, but the connection between its entry pathway, intracellular trafficking, and innate immune sensing remains unclear. As innate immune sensors are distributed throughout the cell, the virus entry route and subcellular localization likely shape immune activation. Here, we investigated how altered entry routes and endosomal retention of HAdV-D26 influence Toll-like receptor (TLR)-mediated signalling in epithelial cells. Using A549 cells with inhibited clathrin-mediated endocytosis (CME) or de novo αvβ3 integrin expression, we analysed IL-6 and IL-8 expression following HAdV-D26 infection and examined the underlying signalling mechanisms. In CME-inhibited A549 cells, where HAdV-D26 persisted longer in early endosomes, HAdV-D26 infection induced IL-6 expression partially through a TLR3–TICAM1–p38-dependent mechanism. Inhibition of TLR3 or p38, as well as knockdown of TICAM1, reduced the elevated IL-6 response observed under CME inhibition. In contrast, inhibition of TLR4 or knockdown of MyD88 increased HAdV-D26-induced IL-6 and IL-8 expression in A549 cells, indicating a negative regulatory role of TLR4–MyD88 signalling. Furthermore, MyD88 knockdown reduced IL-8 expression in A549 cells with de novo αvβ3 integrin expression, linking integrin-mediated entry to MyD88-dependent signalling. In conclusion, these findings highlight how entry pathway and intracellular localization shape innate immune sensing of adenoviral vectors.

A Confirmatory, Dual Center Nonhuman Primate Study on the Efficacy of Novel Oropharyngeal Spray Immunization With an Adenoviral Vector Vaccine Against RSV-Important Lessons Learned.

Authors: Tenbusch M, Koopman G, Mooij P, Roshani B, Irrgang P, Lapuente D, Kondova I, Bogers WM, Remarque EJ, Vestweber R
Original abstract (English)
Background In a randomized, blinded, dual center study across two European primate research facilities, we evaluated the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of a DNA prime-oropharyngeal adenoviral boost vaccination strategy against respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). Methods Rhesus macaques received an RSV fusion protein (F) DNA vaccine via electroporation, followed by two mucosal administrations of a recombinant adenovirus vector encoding the same antigen. Mock-vaccinated animals and a comparator group mimicking natural immunity induced by primary RSV infection were included as controls. Systemic and mucosal immune responses were monitored longitudinally. Results We detected robust cellular and humoral immune responses, but with some variability between the two centers. Nevertheless, RSV-vaccinated animals demonstrated markedly reduced viral replication in the upper and lower respiratory tract after RSV challenge infection. Unexpected RSV-specific immunity in the control group at one single study site prevented confirmation of the predefined primary endpoint. Conclusion Our results support the potential of mucosal adenoviral boosting following DNA priming to induce protective immunity against RSV, while highlighting challenges associated with multi-center preclinical vaccine studies.
