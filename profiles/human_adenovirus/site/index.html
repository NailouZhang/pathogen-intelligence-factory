一项前瞻性随机研究:比较急性腺病毒性结膜炎不同治疗方案的疗效

A prospective, randomized study comparing different therapeutic modalities for acute adenoviral conjunctivitis
作者： Anchal Thakur, Surya Prakash Sharma, Poonam Chauhan, Kapil Goyal, Mini P. Singh, Nirbhai Singh, R.K Ratho, Chintan Malhotra, Amit Gupta
摘要中文翻译目的:比较三种不同治疗方案在急性腺病毒性结膜炎中的疗效。研究设计:前瞻性随机研究。方法:共纳入65例患者(65只眼),所有患者经巢式聚合酶链反应(PCR)证实为腺病毒性结膜炎。对照组(20只眼)接受卡波姆凝胶治疗,而治疗组(各15只眼)分为:1组:更昔洛韦(0.15%)凝胶每日5次联合地塞米松0.1%每日4次;2组:聚维酮碘(PVP-I)1%每日4次联合地塞米松0.1%每日4次;3组:在标准护理基础上加用PVP-I 1%每日4次、更昔洛韦0.15%每日5次及地塞米松0.1%每日4次。所有患者在症状出现后96小时内入组,各组均接受为期7天的抗病毒药物 治疗治疗。记录入组时、第7天及第14天的临床体征与症状及病毒载量。所有症状与体征均进行分级评估。结果:第7天时,与所有其他组相比,3组在异物感(P=0.013)、疼痛(P=0.019)及结膜充血(P=0.00)方面表现显著更优。对照组在第7天时上皮下浸润(SEIs)数量(P=0.0001)及假膜形成(P=0.035)显著高于其他各组。所有患者在各组中的病毒载量下降情况存在差异,因此无法建立相关性。结论:聚维酮碘(PVP-I)1%联合更昔洛韦0.15%及地塞米松0.1%的组合可显著降低腺病毒性结膜炎的体征与症状严重程度。地塞米松的加用在减少SEIs及假膜形成方面安全且有效。

本研究旨在比较急性腺病毒结膜炎的三种不同治疗方式的结果,解决治疗这种疾病的有效 治疗 策略的需求。

一项前瞻性随机研究,纳入65例经巢式PCR确诊的腺病毒性结膜炎患者(65只眼)。患者在症状出现后96小时内入组。
核心方法
在入组时、第 7 天和第 14 天记录临床体征和症状以及病毒载量。所有症状和体征均进行系统分级。
主要结果
第 7 天时,与其他组相比,第 3 组在异物感 (P = 0.013)、疼痛 (P = 0.019) 和结膜充血 (P = 0.00) 方面表现出明显更好的结果。第 7 天时,对照组的上皮下浸润 (SEI) (P = 0.0001) 和假膜 (P = 0.035) 数量显着高于其他组。所有组的病毒载量减少情况各不相同,并且无法建立相关性。
解释与创新
作者得出结论,PVP-I 1% 和更昔洛韦 0.15% 与地塞米松 0.1% 的组合可显着减轻腺病毒性结膜炎的体征和症状的严重程度。他们还指出,添加地塞米松对于降低 SEI 和假膜的 发病率 是安全有效的。
科研与公卫意义
这些发现可能为开发更有效的腺病毒结膜炎 治疗 方案提供信息,特别是在管理症状和预防 SEI 和假膜等并发症方面。
局限与证据强度
该研究的样本量较小,病毒载量的降低情况各不相同,因此很难建立明确的相关性。由于这些限制,证据的强度中等。

A prospective, randomized study comparing different therapeutic modalities for acute adenoviral conjunctivitis

Authors: Anchal Thakur, Surya Prakash Sharma, Poonam Chauhan, Kapil Goyal, Mini P. Singh, Nirbhai Singh, R.K Ratho, Chintan Malhotra, Amit Gupta
Original abstract (English)
Purpose: To compare the outcomes of three different therapeutic modalities in acute adenoviral conjunctivitis. Design: Prospective randomized study. Methods: A total of 65 eyes of 65 patients with nested polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-proven adenoviral conjunctivitis were enrolled. The control group (20 eyes) received carbomer gel, whereas the treatment group (15 eyes each) received group 1: Ganciclovir (0.15%) gel five times/day and dexamethasone 0.1% four times/day; group 2: Povidone–iodine (PVP-I) 1% four times/day, and dexamethasone 0.1% four times/day; group 3: PVP-I 1% four times/day, ganciclovir 0.15% five times/day, and dexamethasone 0.1% four times/day in addition to the standard of care. Patients presenting within the first 96 hours were recruited, and antiviral treatment was given for seven days in all groups. The clinical signs and symptoms, as well as the viral load, were documented at enrollment, day 7, and day 14. All the symptoms and signs were graded. Results: Group 3 performed significantly better in terms of foreign body sensation ( P = 0.013), pain ( P = 0.019), and conjunctival congestion ( P = 0.00) compared with all the groups at day 7. The control group had a significantly higher number of subepithelial infiltrates (SEIs) ( P = 0.0001) and pseudomembranes ( P = 0.035) at day 7 than all the other groups. The viral load reduction was variable in all patients in all the groups; thus, a correlation could not be achieved. Conclusion: The combination of PVP-I 1% and ganciclovir 0.15% with dexamethasone 0.1% significantly reduced the severity of signs and symptoms in adenoviral conjunctivitis. The addition of dexamethasone was safe and effective in decreasing the incidence of SEI’s and pseudomembranes.

Question and background
This study aimed to compare the outcomes of three different therapeutic modalities in acute adenoviral conjunctivitis, addressing the need for effective treatment strategies in managing this condition.
Design and population
A prospective randomized study was conducted involving 65 eyes of 65 patients with adenoviral conjunctivitis confirmed by nested PCR. Patients were recruited within the first 96 hours of symptom onset.
Core methods
Clinical signs and symptoms, as well as viral load, were documented at enrollment, day 7, and day 14. All symptoms and signs were graded systematically.
Main results
Group 3 showed significantly better outcomes in terms of foreign body sensation (P = 0.013), pain (P = 0.019), and conjunctival congestion (P = 0.00) compared to other groups at day 7. The control group had a significantly higher number of subepithelial infiltrates (SEIs) (P = 0.0001) and pseudomembranes (P = 0.035) at day 7 than the other groups. Viral load reduction was variable across all groups, and no correlation could be established.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors concluded that the combination of PVP-I 1% and ganciclovir 0.15% with dexamethasone 0.1% significantly reduced the severity of signs and symptoms in adenoviral conjunctivitis. They also noted that the addition of dexamethasone was safe and effective in decreasing the incidence of SEIs and pseudomembranes.
Scientific and public-health significance
These findings may inform the development of more effective treatment protocols for adenoviral conjunctivitis, particularly in managing symptoms and preventing complications such as SEIs and pseudomembranes.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study had a small sample size, and the viral load reduction was variable, making it difficult to establish a clear correlation. The evidence is moderate in strength due to these limitations.

Impact of Human Adenovirus Co-detection on Disease Severity in Children with Medically Attended Acute Respiratory Infections

本研究旨在描述与HAdV共同检测的其他呼吸道病毒的谱系和频率,并定义联合检测对HAdV疾病严重程度的影响。人腺病毒(HAdV)是儿童急性呼吸道感染(ARIs)的常见病因,并且常与其他常见呼吸道病毒共同检测,但联合检测对疾病严重程度的影响尚不明确。

Impact of Human Adenovirus Co-detection on Disease Severity in Children with Medically Attended Acute Respiratory Infections

Authors: Adam Gailani, Tess Stopczynski, Olla Hamdan, Haya Hayek, Laura S Stewart, Marian G Michaels, John V Williams, Jennifer E Schuster, Rangaraj Selvarangan, Leila C Sahni
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Human Adenovirus (HAdV) is frequently identified as a cause of acute respiratory infections (ARIs) in children and often co-detected with other common respiratory viruses. However, the effect of co-detection on disease severity is not well understood. This study aimed to describe the spectrum and frequency of other respiratory viruses co-detected with HAdV and define the impact of co-detection on HAdV disease severity. Methods Children admitted to the hospital or seen in the emergency department with fever and/or respiratory symptoms <14 days duration were enrolled at 7 sites across the US, from 12/01/2016 through 08/31/2023, as part of the CDC-sponsored New Vaccine Surveillance Network (NVSN). Respiratory specimens were systematically obtained and tested for HAdV and other common viral etiologies of ARI. Demographics, clinical characteristics, and disease severity (hospitalization and in-hospital oxygen use) were compared among children with HAdV single detection, HAdV co-detection with other respiratory viral pathogens, and single detection of a non-HAdV virus. Results Of 68,004 children enrolled with a respiratory specimen tested, 4,292 (6.3%) were positive for HAdV; 1,779 (41.4%) of these were HAdV single detections. Human rhinovirus/enterovirus (HRV/EV) (43.1%), respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) (14.7%), and parainfluenza virus (PIV) (7.6%) were the most common viruses co-detected with HAdV and selected for further analysis. HAdV co-detection with these 3 viruses was associated with increased odds of hospitalization and in-hospital oxygen use when compared to HAdV single detection. However, the odds of hospitalization and in-hospital oxygen use with HRV/EV, RSV, and PIV single detection were also increased compared to HAdV single detection. Conclusion HAdV co-detection with HRV/EV, RSV, or PIV was associated with increased disease severity when compared to HAdV single detection. However, HRV/EV, RSV, and PIV single detections were also associated with increased severity in comparison to HAdV single detection with these pathogens. Additionally, the increased odds of severe disease seen in HRV/EV, RSV, and PIV single detections was often greater than the increase seen with HAdV co-detection with HRV/EV, RSV, or PIV. This could suggest that the overall severity of these ARIs is being driven primarily by the co-detected viruses, HRV/EV, RSV, and PIV. These results could help guide interpretation of viral testing by clinicians, when multiple viruses are detected. Further research involving asymptomatic and mild HAdV ARI cases, as well as longitudinal studies to determine timing of co-infections, are needed to fully understand the role of HAdV in pediatric ARI of mixed viral etiology.

墨西哥城城市啮齿动物种群中腺病毒36的存在

Presence of human adenovirus 36 in an urban rodent population

摘要中文翻译摘要 腺病毒36(AdV36)是一种与人类呼吸道和胃肠道感染相关的病毒;有趣的是,其在实验性啮齿动物感染和人类脂肪组织中也表现出致脂性潜能。为检测墨西哥城内杂居性啮齿动物粪便样本中的AdV36,研究者在墨西哥城东南部公园‘Canal Nacional’捕获了55只野生啮齿动物(15只小家鼠和40只褐家鼠)。动物被安乐死后,通过尸检收集粪便样本。同时记录流行病学数据。研究通过聚合酶链反应(PCR)测序、系统发育、单倍型网络及E1a和E3 AdV36基因遗传变异性分析,检测粪便样本中AdV36的存在。在M. musculus中,E1a基因阳性粪便样本的比例为6.7%,而在R. norvegicus中为35%。按体重分组比较显示,R. norvegicus阳性大鼠间存在显著差异。系统发育和单倍型分析将这些序列归入AdV36簇,并显示与人类AdV36序列共享相同的病毒单倍型。研究结果表明,AdV36可在杂居性啮齿动物粪便中检出,并与人类AdV36序列共享单倍型,提示其具有人兽共患病潜能。

问题与背景
本研究旨在调查墨西哥城城市啮齿动物种群中腺病毒36(AdV36)的存在,因其与人类呼吸道和胃肠道感染及致脂性潜能相关。

研究通过在墨西哥城公园‘Canal Nacional’捕获55只野生啮齿动物(15只小家鼠和40只褐家鼠)并收集其粪便样本,以检测AdV36。

本研究强调需在城市啮齿动物种群中开展AdV36监测,以更好地理解其在人类疾病中的潜在作用,并为公共卫生策略提供依据。

Presence of human adenovirus 36 in an urban rodent population

Authors: Claudia I. Muñoz-Garcia, Martha L. Tamayo-Aguilar, Emilio Rendon-Franco, José A. Ocampo Cervantes, Juan P. Angulo-Lira, Fabiola Sanchez-Aguillon, Paola Zarate-Segura, Mirza Romero-Valdovinos, Pablo Maravilla, Fernando Martinez-Hernandez

Respiratory viruses induce ferroptosis-like features in lung epithelial cells

Authors: Ana L. Manzano-Covarrubias, Alessandra Tosato, Christina HT J. Mol-van der Veen, Philip Ahmadyar, Jai Walia, Manon E. Woest, Jacqueline de Vries‐Idema, Anke Huckriede, Amalia M. Dolga, Martina Schmidt
Original abstract (English)
UNLABELLED: During infection, viruses can modulate various regulated cell death (RCD) mechanisms to evade host immunity and enhance replication. However, less is known about how viruses alter the recently discovered RCD ferroptosis, which is characterized by an iron-dependent accumulation of lipid peroxidation and mitochondrial fragmentation. Influenza A virus (IAV) H1N1 and human adenovirus type C5 (HAdV-C5) are two common causes of respiratory infections of the upper and lower respiratory tracts and can lead to severe illness. While IAV has been shown to induce ferroptosis to support its replication, less is known about whether this is also true for HAdV-C5. Here, we perform a comparative study investigating ferroptosis features during IAV H1N1 and HAdV-C5 infections using alveolar (A549) and bronchial (BEAS-2B) epithelial cells. Our data reveal that HAdV-C5, similar to H1N1, induces lipid peroxidation in a time-dependent manner, which is partially suppressed in the presence of the ferroptosis inhibitor ferrostatin-1. Strikingly, HAdV-C5 infections only lead to changes in ferritin protein levels, which is responsible for iron storage, during late stages of infection. Furthermore, both H1N1 and HAdV-C5 infections trigger a profound mitochondrial network remodeling in A549, BEAS-2B, and primary human epithelial cells, comparable to that induced by the ferroptosis inducer RSL3, with effects varying by cell type and infection stage. These findings suggest that HAdV-C5, just like H1N1, can activate ferroptosis-like processes, highlighting a potential role for lipid peroxidation and mitochondrial alterations in adenoviral infection. IMPORTANCE: The findings presented here contribute to a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms involved in viral infections of the lung epithelium, particularly in the context of virus-induced lung epithelial cell death, a topic of significant relevance to the area of early viral response mechanisms. By elucidating the role of ferroptosis-like hallmarks encompassing lipid peroxidation and mitochondrial dysfunction, our work may inform the development of new therapeutic strategies aimed at enhancing lung epithelial integrity and barrier function, representing the first layer of defense against human pathogens. The topic is of particular importance given the increasing prevalence of antimicrobial resistance. We believe that our research offers significant insights into the field of viral infections and lung disease and would be of great interest to readers in the field of viral biology and respiratory infections.

A field-deployable immunofluorescence chip for adenovirus monitoring in groundwater systems

Authors: Mobarok Hossain, Nele Hastreiter, Thomas Vienken, Mark Wegmann, Dirk Roggenbuck, Irina Engelhardt
Original abstract (English)
Rapid field detection of human adenoviruses in groundwater remains a critical technological gap, particularly for riverbank filtration systems, exposed to extreme weather and contamination events. Here, we present the VIRUMEX chip, a field-deployable platform that captures and detects adenovirus proteins through popular immunofluorescence mechanism using antibody-functionalized 3D Sepharose beads integrated into a disposable flow-through microfluidic cartridge. Powered by a compact peristaltic pump, the system autonomously processes groundwater and yields fluorescence-based readouts in light intensity unit (LIU) within 4-8 h, enabling near-real time semi-quantitative pathogen surveillance. Laboratory tests produced a clear detectable signal at 0.129 ng/mL (1000 LIU) for adenovirus hexon protein, and field deployment in the Erpe River catchment produced above-background fluorescence (800 LIU) in river water, providing proof-of-concept evidence with positive signal under real riverbank filtration conditions, while groundwater samples remained negative under the tested conditions. By providing an on-site, rapid, and operationally simple detection method, the VIRUMEX chip provides an additional on-site virus screening prototype alongside qPCR and isothermal approaches and offers a promising prototype for integrating virus monitoring into groundwater safety frameworks which needs some further optimizations before it can be regarded as a fully validated detection tool.

Original abstract (English)

Although current research and commercial applications have focused on the most abundant saponins in Quillaja extracts (QS-7, QS-17, QS-18 and QS-21), the vast chemical variety in Quillaja saponins remains largely unexplored. Finally, Saponin FR showed remarkable antiproliferative activity against gastric (AGS 1739) and cervix (HeLa CRM-CCL-2) adenocarcinoma cell lines with half maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC 50 ) of 2.9 and 2.6 μM, respectively.
