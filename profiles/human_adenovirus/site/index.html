Changes in the chromatin accessibility landscape during human adenovirus infection.
作者： Katelyn M. MacNeil, Katharina E. Hayer, Tanner M. Tessier, Amir Hossein Karimi, Anthony Charles Nichols, Matthew D. Weitzman, Joe S. Mymryk
摘要中文翻译未标记:病毒感染会重新连接细胞环境,这通常是通过染色质重塑来实现的。人腺病毒(HAdV)可以直接修饰特定的细胞染色质特征;然而,我们仍然缺乏对感染过程中引起的细胞染色质景观变化的全面了解。为了解决 宿主 染色质可及性在 HAdV-C5 感染期间如何变化,我们在感染后 48 和 72 小时,使用测序 (ATAC-seq) 在生长停滞的二倍体人肺成纤维细胞 (IMR-90) 细胞中对转座酶可及染色质进行了测定。我们检测到染色质可及性发生广泛变化,感染后 48 小时和 72 小时,宿主 染色质上分别有 7,055 个和 10,736 个差异可及区域 (DAR)。在这两个时间点,这些变化主要发生在增强子/基因间区域和内含子区域。此外,蛋白质编码基因和非编码基因的可及性变化模式不同。ATAC-seq 数据与 RNA-seq 数据的整合表明,染色质可及性的显着变化与感染期间基因表达的显着变化相一致。此外,我们发现增加的 DAR 富集叉头和 TEA 结构域转录因子家族基序,而减少的 DAR 富集锌指和碱性亮氨酸拉链结构域家族的基序。通路富集分析显示,Wnt 信号通路基因在感染过程中表现出染色质可及性增加。抑制 Wnt 信号通路可减少病毒基因表达和感染性病毒后代的产生,表明该通路对于生产性感染很重要。总之,这些数据表明细胞染色质在 HAdV 感染期间被广泛重塑,并扩展了我们对 宿主 细胞转录调控的理解。重要性:人腺病毒-C5 (HAdV-C5) 感染会广泛修改细胞转录组和蛋白质组。然而,细胞环境的其他重要方面,例如染色质可及性的整体变化,在感染过程中尚未得到充分表征。在这里,我们在感染后 48 小时和 72 小时使用测序 (ATAC-seq) 对受感染的生长停滞的二倍体人肺成纤维细胞 (IMR-90) 细胞进行转座酶可及染色质分析。我们观察到 基因组 细胞染色质可及性的广泛变化,并确定了感染期间位于不同可及区域的特定转录因子基序。这项工作为我们理解 人腺病毒 感染如何重新编程 宿主 细胞环境提供了另一个关键部分,并将为未来针对 HAdV 操纵的影响 宿主 细胞染色质可及性的特定染色质修饰的工作提供信息。

Changes in the chromatin accessibility landscape during human adenovirus infection.

Authors: Katelyn M. MacNeil, Katharina E. Hayer, Tanner M. Tessier, Amir Hossein Karimi, Anthony Charles Nichols, Matthew D. Weitzman, Joe S. Mymryk
Original abstract (English)
UNLABELLED: Viral infections rewire the cellular environment, and this is often achieved through chromatin remodeling. Human adenovirus (HAdV) can directly modify specific cellular chromatin features; however, we still lack a global view of the changes in the cellular chromatin landscape induced during infection. To address how host chromatin accessibility changes during HAdV-C5 infection, we performed assay for transposase-accessible chromatin using sequencing (ATAC-seq) in growth-arrested diploid human lung fibroblast (IMR-90) cells at 48 and 72 h post-infection. We detected widespread changes in chromatin accessibility, with 7,055 and 10,736 differentially accessible regions (DARs) on host chromatin at 48 and 72 h post-infection, respectively. At both time points, these changes mostly occurred in enhancer/intergenic regions and intronic regions. Furthermore, the pattern of changes in accessibility differed between protein-coding and non-coding genes. Integration of the ATAC-seq data with RNA-seq data demonstrated that significant changes in chromatin accessibility coincided with significant changes in gene expression during infection. Additionally, we found that increased DARs were enriched for forkhead and TEA domain transcription factor family motifs, while decreased DARs were enriched for motifs from zinc finger and basic leucine zipper domain families. Pathway enrichment analysis revealed that Wnt signaling pathway genes displayed increased chromatin accessibility during infection. Inhibition of the Wnt signaling pathway reduced viral gene expression and infectious viral progeny production, suggesting that this pathway is important for productive infection. Together, these data demonstrate that cellular chromatin is extensively remodeled during HAdV infection and expand our understanding of host-cell transcriptional regulation by human adenovirus. IMPORTANCE: Human adenovirus-C5 (HAdV-C5) infection extensively modifies the cellular transcriptome and proteome. However, other important aspects of the cellular environment, such as global changes in chromatin accessibility, have yet to be fully characterized during infection. Here, we performed assay for transposase-accessible chromatin using sequencing (ATAC-seq) in infected growth-arrested diploid human lung fibroblast (IMR-90) cells at 48 and 72 h post-infection. We observed genome-wide changes in cellular chromatin accessibility and identified specific transcription factor motifs located in differentially accessible regions during infection. This work contributes another key piece to our understanding of how human adenovirus infection reprograms the host-cell environment and will inform future work directed toward specific chromatin modifications manipulated by HAdV that impact host-cell chromatin accessibility.

Inhibitors of monocarboxylate transporters as host-directed antivirals against human adenoviruses.

Authors: Sara Spjut, Mårten Strand, Martina Fitzek, Preeti Iyer, Marian Preston, David M. Smith, Andrew M. Davis, Amanda Freitas-Huhtamäki, Liane Viru, Magnus Evander
Original abstract (English)
Human adenoviruses (HAdVs) are non-enveloped double-stranded DNA viruses that cause a wide range of typically mild, self-limiting infections in humans, but are associated with high morbidity and mortality in immunocompromised individuals. There are currently no approved antivirals specifically targeting HAdV infection. To identify novel anti-adenoviral compounds and host factors critical for infection, we screened a target-annotated library of 13,000 compounds, covering 1,763 known human targets, against HAdV type 11 in K562 cells. This screen yielded numerous anti-adenoviral compounds, many of which were kinase inhibitors, well known to also show antiviral activity. We instead focused on potent and selective inhibitors of monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1) for further investigation. For 25 MCT1 inhibitors, antiviral activity correlated clearly with MCT1 inhibitory potency, indicating MCT1 inhibition as the likely mechanism. MCT1 exports lactate and is frequently overexpressed in cancers that rely on aerobic glycolysis; many viruses similarly reprogram host metabolism toward glycolysis, increasing intracellular lactate that must be effluxed. Unexpectedly, MCT1 inhibitors were ineffective in A549 cells due to compensatory activity of MCT4, as A549 cells express both MCT1 and MCT4, whereas K562 cells express only MCT1. A selective MCT4 inhibitor was inactive in both cell lines, suggesting that simultaneous inhibition of both transporters is required. Accordingly, combined MCT1 and MCT4 inhibition synergistically blocked HAdV infection in both transformed cell types and primary normal human bronchial epithelial cells. These findings underscore the importance of host cell metabolism in HAdV infection and identify MCTs as promising antiviral targets.

Original abstract (English)
Background In a randomized, blinded, dual center study across two European primate research facilities, we evaluated the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of a DNA prime-oropharyngeal adenoviral boost vaccination strategy against respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). Methods Rhesus macaques received an RSV fusion protein (F) DNA vaccine via electroporation, followed by two mucosal administrations of a recombinant adenovirus vector encoding the same antigen. Mock-vaccinated animals and a comparator group mimicking natural immunity induced by primary RSV infection were included as controls. Conclusion Our results support the potential of mucosal adenoviral boosting following DNA priming to induce protective immunity against RSV, while highlighting challenges associated with multi-center preclinical vaccine studies.

Authors: Ying Lu, Zhongying Yang, Lifen Rao, Hui Qu, Yushun Wan, Na Zang
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Human adenovirus (HAdV) can cause severe pediatric pneumonia, but peripheral blood does not directly represent the immune response in the lung. We examined independent blood and lung datasets to define shared directions and local cellular patterns. Methods Two public blood transcriptome datasets were analyzed alongside RNA-seq data from 13 children with HAdV infection and 10 non-infectious controls. Mouse lung scRNA-seq compared control, HAdV-3, and HAdV-7 groups, with three mice per group. Cell proportions and module scores used mice as biological replicates. A broadly applicable strategy for adenovirus-mediated genetic engineering of CAR-negative mesenchymal stromal cells

Authors: Ayuka Hamada, Naoto Obayashi, Yui Fukatsu, Yuriko Sakata, Hitomi Takada, Akira Kurisaki
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Mesenchymal stromal/stem cells (MSCs) are promising cellular vehicles for regenerative medicine and cancer gene therapy because of their immunomodulatory properties, tumor tropism, and favorable safety profile. Adenoviral vectors are attractive for genetic engineering of MSCs because they enable high transgene expression without genomic integration. However, their application to MSCs is limited by poor transduction efficiency due to low or absent expression of the coxsackievirus and adenovirus receptor (CAR) in these cells. Although cationic reagents, including polybrene, have been reported to improve adenoviral gene transfer in some cell types, efficient transduction of CAR-negative mesenchymal cells remains challenging. Results We developed a polybrene-assisted spin infection protocol in which the combination of polybrene and centrifugation markedly enhanced adenoviral transduction across multiple CAR-negative mesenchymal cell types without increasing the viral dose. To determine whether improved transduction enhances therapeutic efficacy, mouse adipose tissue-derived stromal cells were transduced with an insulin-expressing adenoviral vector and transplanted into streptozotocin-induced diabetic mice. Cells transduced with polybrene-assisted spin infection significantly reduced blood glucose levels, whereas cells transduced without spin infection showed limited therapeutic efficacy. Conclusions Polybrene-assisted spin infection provides a simple and broadly applicable platform for adenovirus-mediated genetic engineering of MSCs and has the potential to enhance the therapeutic efficacy of engineered MSCs.
