Shotgun metagenomic characterization of human adenovirus F40 and a non-cpe Clostridium perfringens strain in pediatric prolonged diarrhea: a genome-resolved case study.

Authors: Samar M. El-Shahidy, Amira M. Zakaria, Mohanad A. Ibrahim, Atef M. Diab, Samar M. El Shahidy
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Viral and bacterial pathogens may be concurrently detected in pediatric gastroenteritis, yet such findings are often missed by routine diagnostic methods. Shotgun metagenomic sequencing enables comprehensive detection and genomic characterization of microorganisms present in clinical specimens, including pathogens that may not be recovered by conventional testing. METHODS: Shotgun metagenomic sequencing was performed on a single pediatric diarrheic stool sample, followed by quality filtering, de novo assembly, taxonomic classification, phylogenetic reconstruction, and integrated virulome-resistome profiling, including plasmid-associated resistance elements. RESULTS: Routine diagnostics identified adenovirus as the sole pathogen. Metagenomic analysis confirmed human adenovirus F40 (HAdV-F40), showing close phylogenetic relatedness to globally distributed HAdV-F40 reference strains and a clear distinction from HAdV-F41. In addition, metagenomics uniquely detected a Clostridium perfringens strain (SAM_A8, ST-5) that was not recovered by culture. The strain lacked the classical enterotoxin gene (cpe) but harbored a conserved histotoxic virulence profile, including cpa, pfoA, colA, hyaluronidases, and sialidases. Plasmid-associated tetracycline resistance genes (tetA(P) and tetB(P)) were also identified. CONCLUSION: This case study demonstrates the feasibility of shotgun metagenomic sequencing for comprehensive detection and genomic characterization of microorganisms present in pediatric diarrheal stool specimens, including organisms not identified by routine diagnostics. The findings are descriptive and highlight the value of metagenomics as a complementary diagnostic approach. However, no conclusions regarding pathogenic synergy, causality, or the clinical contribution of the co-detected organisms can be drawn from this single case.

A Confirmatory, Dual Center Nonhuman Primate Study on the Efficacy of Novel Oropharyngeal Spray Immunization With an Adenoviral Vector Vaccine Against RSV-Important Lessons Learned.

Authors: Tenbusch M, Koopman G, Mooij P, Roshani B, Irrgang P, Lapuente D, Kondova I, Bogers WM, Remarque EJ, Vestweber R
Original abstract (English)
Background In a randomized, blinded, dual center study across two European primate research facilities, we evaluated the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of a DNA prime-oropharyngeal adenoviral boost vaccination strategy against respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). Methods Rhesus macaques received an RSV fusion protein (F) DNA vaccine via electroporation, followed by two mucosal administrations of a recombinant adenovirus vector encoding the same antigen. Mock-vaccinated animals and a comparator group mimicking natural immunity induced by primary RSV infection were included as controls. Systemic and mucosal immune responses were monitored longitudinally. Results We detected robust cellular and humoral immune responses, but with some variability between the two centers. Nevertheless, RSV-vaccinated animals demonstrated markedly reduced viral replication in the upper and lower respiratory tract after RSV challenge infection. Unexpected RSV-specific immunity in the control group at one single study site prevented confirmation of the predefined primary endpoint. Conclusion Our results support the potential of mucosal adenoviral boosting following DNA priming to induce protective immunity against RSV, while highlighting challenges associated with multi-center preclinical vaccine studies.

重组腺病毒血清型 4 在小鼠模型中的转基因表达动力学和复制潜力及其作为单纯疱疹病毒 疫苗 的用途。

Transgene expression kinetics and replication potential of recombinant adenovirus serotype 4 in a mouse model and its use as a herpes simplex virus vaccine.
作者： Alexander C Vostal, Dawid Maciorowski, James M. Readler, Isabella S. Pytel, Andy A. Patamawenu, Ceanna Cooney, Peyton M. Roeder, Rachel Roenicke, Freya Van't Veer, Tae Kim

Authors: Jiabao Han, Elena A. Kashentseva, Igor P. Dmitriev, Zhi Hong Lu, S. Peter Goedegebuure, Lijin Li, William E. Gillanders, David T. Curiel
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Adenoviral vectors have been widely validated as effective vaccine platforms for both infectious diseases and cancer due to their strong immunostimulatory properties. However, their application to personalized cancer vaccines and other time-sensitive antigens is limited by the prolonged production time required for conventional vector generation. Here, we developed an adenovirus-derived virus-like particle (Ad5VLP) platform that enables modular incorporation of antigen cargo without reconstruction of a complete antigen-encoding adenoviral genome. Ad5VLPs successfully packaged and delivered foreign mRNA, although mRNA-derived antigen expression remained insufficient to induce robust antigen-specific immunity in vivo. In contrast, direct encapsulation of protein or peptide antigens substantially improved intracellular antigen delivery and major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class I antigen presentation. Following subcutaneous prime-boost immunization in mice, antigen-loaded Ad5VLPs induced robust antigen-specific interferon-γ (IFN-γ) responses, with functional activity preferentially enriched within CD44hi CD8+ T cells and accompanied by effector-associated CD8+ T-cell differentiation. Importantly, robust cellular immune responses were also observed using an E0771 tumor neoantigen, demonstrating that the platform extends beyond the model ovalbumin (OVA) antigen. Protein-loaded Ad5VLPs also elicited antigen-specific and antivector humoral responses. Vaccination was well tolerated over the 14 day study period, with no substantial changes in body weight, clinical observations, serum markers of hepatic or renal injury and systemic inflammation, or broad splenic inflammatory transcriptional signatures. Together, these findings demonstrate that Ad5VLPs enable modular delivery of protein and peptide antigens and induce robust cellular immune responses, supporting further investigation for personalized cancer vaccination and other time-sensitive vaccine applications.
