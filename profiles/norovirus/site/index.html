Impact of dose on norovirus antibody kinetics following infection or vaccination.

Authors: Murphy John, Yang Ge, W. Zane Billings, Savannah L. Miller, Cora Hirst, Rustom Antia, Katia Koelle, Ye Shen, Benjamin A. Lopman, Robert L. Atmar
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Noroviruses are a leading cause of acute gastroenteritis worldwide, yet no licensed vaccines currently exist. Antibody-mediated immunity provides protection, but the relationship between norovirus exposure dose and antibody kinetics remains unclear. Methods We analyzed longitudinal data on histo-blood group antigen (HBGA)-blocking, IgA, and IgG antibodies from a human norovirus infection trial and a candidate vaccine trial. We developed hierarchical nonlinear Bayesian models to characterize antibody kinetics following different amounts of virus or vaccine antigen exposure within infection and vaccination settings. We fit models with exponential and power-law decay terms and compared their performance. Results Across studies and antibody types, the amount of antigen individuals were exposed to influenced early antibody kinetics, including growth, peak, and decay, but effects differed between infection and vaccination. Doses that produced larger initial antibody boosts also decayed faster, resulting in little association with one-year antibody persistence. Conclusions Our findings suggest that amount and type of antigen exposure shapes early antibody kinetics but not lasting durability. This reflects a trade-off between dose-induced initial immune boost and subsequent waning.

Immunogenicity of norovirus VP1-expressing silkworm (Bombyx mori) pupae as an edible vaccine candidate.

Authors: Tomoki Sasaki, Akitsu Masuda, Jae Man Lee, Shigeyuki Tamiya, Yusaku Mikaki, Kiyotada Naitou, Masahiro Taniguchi, Ako Kimura, Yuuka Chieda, Hiroshi Ushijima
Original abstract (English)
Norovirus (NV) gastroenteritis remains a global health challenge, particularly affecting infants and the elderly. Despite its significant clinical impact, no licensed vaccine is currently available. This study explores a novel approach to NV vaccine development using the silkworm-baculovirus expression vector system (silkworm-BEVS), focusing on non-purified VP1-expressing silkworm pupae as a potential edible vaccine candidate. We assessed the immunogenicity of orally administered VP1-expressing silkworm pupae in mice using two delivery formats: crude pupal suspensions administered by oral gavage and freeze-dried whole pupae provided by free-feeding. Oral gavage induced dose- and time-dependent antigen-specific IgG in serum and IgA in intestinal lavage fluid (ILF). The serum antibodies also showed HBGA-blocking activity. Importantly, free-feeding also elicited robust antigen-specific IgG and IgA responses. Pooled sera and ILF from free-fed mice showed neutralizing activity against NV in a human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived intestinal epithelial cell-based assay. Our findings demonstrate that oral administration of VP1-expressing silkworm pupal preparations induces both systemic and mucosal immune responses, which may be relevant to protective immunity against NV. Collectively, these results support the potential of non-purified VP1-expressing silkworm pupae as an edible vaccine candidate and provide a foundation for further development of silkworm-BEVS-based edible vaccines.

Symmetry-breaking antibody binding reveals extensive capsid malleability in pandemic human norovirus

Authors: B. V. Venkataram Prasad, Carmen Apostol, Ramakrishnan Anish, Son Pham, Khalil Ettayebi, Janam J. Dave, Frederick Neill, Marina Bok, Banumathi Sankaran, Sue E. S. Crawford
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Virus capsids are metastable assemblies with an exquisite balance between stability and flexibility, obligatory for entry, infectivity, and immune escape. Yet, the extent to which human viruses exploit capsid plasticity and how that consequently shapes antigenic landscapes remains poorly understood. Human noroviruses (HuNoVs) are the leading cause of acute viral gastroenteritis worldwide1. GII.4 HuNoVs have been evolving epochally, driving most global pandemics, and since 2012, GII.4 Sydney has been the dominant variant. The major capsid protein VP1 is central to infectivity and immune recognition, and VP1-based virus-like particles (VLPs) are considered promising vaccine candidates. Yet, how the icosahedral HuNoV capsid responds to antibody pressure at the atomic level is still unclear. Here, we present the first atomic structure of GII.4 Sydney capsid determined by cryo-EM, revealing the structural features that likely contribute to its sustained dominance. Using focused refinement, we uncovered the remarkable capsid plasticity in response to symmetry-breaking binding of neutralizing and non-neutralizing antibodies. VP1 adopts 11 distinct conformational modalities to accommodate asymmetric antibody binding, and antibody-disrupted VLPs reassemble into smaller particles with five additional VP1 states. This previously unrecognized extent of conformational adaptability provides key insights for designing next-generation VLP- or mRNA-based vaccines targeting HuNoV infections.

Original abstract (English)
Human norovirus (HuNoV), the leading cause of foodborne illness in the United States and acute gastroenteritis globally, replicates in the zebrafish embryo and larvae model. However, due to challenges in obtaining well-characterized HuNoV positive stool specimens for research, demonstrating replication of Tulane virus (TuV), a surrogate of HuNoV, in the zebrafish embryo and larvae model is valuable to study HuNoV infection mechanisms and subsequent prevention and control strategies. 摘要中文翻译双壳类软体动物被认为是肠道病毒的生物富集者,因此是微生物污染的重要环境指示生物。这些生物的污染主要与沿海水域暴露于人类和农业废水有关,这些废水将病原体引入水生环境。由于它们的滤食性活动,双壳类动物可以富集这些病原体,而由于它们通常生食或半熟食用,受污染的双壳类动物可能对公共卫生构成风险。欧洲食品安全局和美国食品药品监督管理局将甲型肝炎病毒(HAV)、乙型肝炎病毒(HEV)以及诺如病毒基因组I和II(NoV GI和NoV GII)认定为全球范围内导致多种食源性疾病的主要病原体。由于食品中病毒污染尚未建立监管限值,因此推动了分子方法的发展,以产生科学证据并支持风险评估和控制策略。在此背景下,本研究旨在调查市场上可获得的牡蛎(Mytilus galloprovincialis)中HAV、HEV、NoV GI和NoV GII的出现情况。总共收集了78个混合样本,每个样本包含4至5只牡蛎的消化组织,于2025年2月至12月以及2026年1月至2月期间从零售市场采集,并通过逆转录定量聚合酶链反应(RT-qPCR)进行分析。在5个混合样本中检测到NoV GII RNA,对应总体混合样本阳性率为6.4%(5/78;精确95%置信区间[CI]:2.1–14.3%),而HAV、HEV和NoV GI未被检测到(0/78;精确95% CI:0.0–4.6%)。阳性混合样本中的RNA浓度较低,平均为143.3 ± 132.4 copies/g,中位数为120.0 copies/g,循环阈值(Ct)值与低水平RNA检测一致。总体而言,研究结果表明在市场上可获得的脱水牡蛎中存在低但可检测的NoV GII混合样本阳性率。在零售牡蛎中检测到病毒RNA表明其曾暴露于病毒污染,并支持对市场上可获得的双壳类动物进行持续的病毒特异性监测的重要性。

Occurrence of Human Enteric Viruses in Retail Mussels Marketed in Burgos, Spain

Authors: Mariana Alves Elois, Nadine Yéramian, Daniel Pérez-Alonso, Rafael Dorighello Cadamuro, Antonio Valero, Álvaro Cañete-Reyes, Henrique Borges da Silva Grisard, Gislaine Fongaro, David Rodrı́guez-Làzaro
Original abstract (English)
Bivalve mollusks are recognized as bioaccumulators of enteric viruses and, therefore, represent important environmental sentinels for microbiological contamination. The contamination of these organisms is primarily associated with the exposure of coastal waters to human and agricultural effluents, which introduce pathogens into the aquatic environment. Due to their filter-feeding activity, bivalves can bioaccumulate these pathogens, and because they are often consumed raw or undercooked, contaminated bivalves may pose a public health risk. Hepatitis A virus (HAV), hepatitis E virus (HEV), and norovirus genogroups I and II (NoV GI and NoV GII) are recognized by the European Food Safety Authority and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration as major etiological agents responsible for numerous foodborne outbreaks worldwide. The absence of established regulatory limits for viral contamination in food has driven the development of molecular approaches to generate scientific evidence and support risk assessment and control strategies. In this context, the present study aimed to investigate the occurrence of HAV, HEV, NoV GI, and NoV GII in commercially available mussels (Mytilus galloprovincialis). A total of 78 pooled samples, each comprising digestive tissues from four to five mussels, were collected from retail markets between February and December 2025 and during January and February 2026 and were analyzed by reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR). NoV GII RNA was detected in 5 pooled samples, corresponding to an overall pool-level positivity of 6.4% (5/78; exact 95% confidence interval [CI]: 2.1–14.3%), while HAV, HEV, and NoV GI were not detected (0/78; exact 95% CI: 0.0–4.6%). RNA concentrations in positive pooled samples were low, with a mean of 143.3 ± 132.4 copies/g, a median of 120.0 copies/g, and cycle threshold (Ct) values consistent with low-level RNA detection. Overall, the findings indicate a low but detectable pool-level positivity of NoV GII in commercially available depurated mussels. The detection of viral RNA in retail mussels indicates previous exposure to viral contamination and supports the importance of continued virus-specific surveillance of commercially available bivalves.

Conditions National Norovirus Levels Sit Low but Florida Is Entering the Months When Stomach Bug Outbreaks Usually Climb Published Sep 21, 2026 9:30 PM EDT By Joseph James Editorial Illustration via AI Share on Twitter Share on Facebook Share on Pocket Norovirus, the leading cause of vomiting and diarrhea in the United States, is circulating at low levels nationally as fall begins, according to the most recent federal and academic surveillance data. For Florida, which has ranked among the states with the most confirmed outbreaks, that quiet stretch is unlikely to last through the coming months. The CDC's NoroSTAT outbreak tracker, updated Sept. 17, counted 27 outbreaks from Aug. 1 through Sept. 4 in its participating states, compared with 29 during the same period last year. That is within the normal range for this point in the season. Florida is not one of the states that report to NoroSTAT, so the tracker does not reflect conditions there. The reason to pay attention now is timing. Norovirus activity typically rises in the fall and peaks in winter. With schools in session, long-term care facilities heading into respiratory virus season, and Florida's winter cruise season approaching, the next few weeks are a practical window for families to prepare. A Quiet Start in the Latest Surveillance Numbers Wastewater testing offers the broadest early signal. In its latest nationwide wastewater monitoring update, published in early September, the WastewaterSCAN program, run by Stanford University and Emory University, reported that norovirus was detected in every sample it analyzed in August. Even so, median concentrations fell about 12% from July, and the program placed norovirus in its low category nationally with no clear trend over the prior three weeks. Its Southern region includes Florida, though the update did not break out Florida sites. That is likely to change. In the 2024 to 2025 season, WastewaterSCAN saw norovirus levels start rising in early October and peak in early February. How high each winter peak climbs can vary from year to year. These numbers have limits. Norovirus is not individually reportable to the CDC, most clinics do not test for it, and outbreak data arrive weeks after illnesses occur. The figures describe direction, not how many people in any Florida county are sick right now. Florida's Track Record and Crowded Settings Florida has ranked near the top for confirmed outbreaks. Between Sept. 1, 2023, and Oct. 31, 2024, the state reported 24 confirmed norovirus outbreaks, second only to California, according to CDC data reported by CBS News Miami. Cruise ships were hit hard that year, with 16 outbreaks reported in 2024, five of them in December. Local signals can also differ from state trends. Last January, WUSF reported that Florida's wastewater levels were mostly steady statewide while Orange County saw a noticeable rise, a reminder that Orlando, Miami, Tampa and Jacksonville can move on different timelines. Nationally, the CDC estimates norovirus causes 19 million to 21 million illnesses each year, according to its norovirus facts and statistics. The agency's outbreak overview says about 2,500 outbreaks are reported annually, and roughly half of all foodborne disease outbreaks are caused by norovirus, often when an infected food worker handles ready-to-eat food. The virus spreads through tiny particles of vomit or stool, and people can shed it for two weeks or more after they feel better. Nursing homes, schools, child care centers, and cruise ships are among the places it spreads fastest. Dehydration Signs, Home Care and When to Get Help Symptoms usually start 12 to 48 hours after exposure and include sudden vomiting, diarrhea, nausea, and stomach cramps, sometimes with fever, headache, or body aches. Most healthy people recover in one to three days. The main danger is dehydration. Young children account for many emergency visits, while most of the roughly 900 norovirus deaths each year in the United States occur in adults 65 and older, according to the CDC.
