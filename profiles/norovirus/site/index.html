Comparative Clinical and Molecular Epidemiology of Norovirus in Hospitalized Gastroenteritis Cases, Western India (2013-2023).

Authors: Madhuri S Joshi, Varanasi Gopalkrishna, Nutan A Chavan, Manohar S Shinde, Varsha B Dhembre, Triveni V Sodnawar, Santoshkumar M Jadhav, Vijay R Kalrao, Ram K Dhongade, Rajesh K Kulkarni
Original abstract (English)
Following widespread vaccination against Rotavirus A (RVA), Norovirus (NoV) has emerged as a leading cause of acute gastroenteritis (AGE). However, data on the clinical characteristics and molecular epidemiology of NoV in India remain limited. A total of 1637 fecal samples from hospitalized sporadic AGE cases collected between April 2013 and May 2023 from two cities each in Maharashtra and Gujarat, India, were screened for NoV. Positive samples were characterized by molecular genotyping. NoV was detected in 149 (9.1%) cases, with 123 (82.5%) infections occurring in children under 2 years of age. Most patients 103 (69.1%) had severe or very severe illness, and 22.8% had mixed viral infections, predominantly with RVA. Genotyping identified 11 VP1 genotypes, 9 RdRp P-types, and approximately 30 genotype combinations, demonstrating substantial genetic diversity. GII.4 (n = 66) and GII.P16 (n = 67) were the predominant variants and were associated with more severe disease than less common genotypes. The validated qRT-PCR assays demonstrated > 97% sensitivity and specificity for regional strains, enabling rapid and accurate diagnosis. The extensive genetic diversity of NoV circulating in Indian children highlights the need for enhanced molecular surveillance, improved diagnostics, and consideration of locally prevalent genotypes in future vaccine development.

离体模型中的 诺如病毒 种间 传播 取决于病毒内化能力。

Norovirus inter-species transmission in an ex vivo model is dependent on virus internalization capacity.
作者： Nele Villabruna, Myra Hosmillo, Ian Goodfellow, Niklas Arnberg, Juan Bárcena, Silke Rautenschlein, Benjamin Heidrich, Andreas Beineke, Gisa Gerold
摘要中文翻译人类 诺如病毒 (HuNoV) 是全球病毒性胃肠炎最常见的原因,导致散发病例和 暴发。感染野生和家养动物(包括猪和狗)的 诺如病毒 与人类 诺如病毒 有遗传相关性,增加了跨物种 传播 的可能性。为了研究 诺如病毒 跨越物种障碍的潜力,我们使用了猪、犬和禽类精密切割的肠道切片。我们发现,人 诺如病毒 病毒样颗粒 (VLP) 与猪和狗的肠道组织结合,并分别以与猪和犬 诺如病毒 相似的效率被吸收。相反,在鸡组织中没有检测到人、猪或犬诺如病毒的结合或内化。我们进一步表明,人 诺如病毒 在猪肠道组织中复制。相比之下,虽然动物 诺如病毒 附着在人类肠道细胞上,但细胞内的摄取受到限制。这表明人与动物之间的 传播 比动物与人之间的 传播 更有可能,并且病毒摄取可能存在物种障碍。

Norovirus inter-species transmission in an ex vivo model is dependent on virus internalization capacity.

Authors: Nele Villabruna, Myra Hosmillo, Ian Goodfellow, Niklas Arnberg, Juan Bárcena, Silke Rautenschlein, Benjamin Heidrich, Andreas Beineke, Gisa Gerold
Original abstract (English)
Human noroviruses (HuNoV) are the most common cause of viral gastroenteritis worldwide, causing sporadic cases and outbreaks. Noroviruses that infect wild and domestic animals, including pigs and dogs, are genetically related to human noroviruses, increasing the potential for cross-species transmission. To investigate the potential of noroviruses to cross the species barrier, we used porcine, canine, and avian precision-cut intestinal slices. We show that human norovirus virus-like particles (VLPs) bind to pig and dog intestinal tissue and are taken up with a similar efficiency as the porcine and canine noroviruses, respectively. In contrast, no binding or internalization of human, porcine, or canine noroviruses was detected in chicken tissue. We further showed that human norovirus replicates in pig intestinal tissue. In contrast, while animal noroviruses attached to human intestinal cells, intracellular uptake was limited. This suggests that human-to-animal transmission is more likely than animal-to-human transmission and that viral uptake likely presents a species barrier.

Treatment With Ruxolitinib and TAK-779 Enhances GII.17 Human Norovirus Replication and Enables Serial Passaging in Human Intestinal Enteroids.

Authors: Eri Hiraishi, Tsuyoshi Hayashi, Yoshiki Fujii, Hirokazu Kimura, Kosuke Murakami
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Human norovirus (HuNoV) is a major cause of acute viral gastroenteritis. Although it can be replicated in vitro using human intestinal enteroids (HIEs), generating high‐titer viral laboratory stocks remains challenging due to low infection and passage efficiencies. Interferon response and chemokine signaling may be key host pathways that restrict efficient HuNoV replication. Here, we evaluated the effect of their inhibitors, ruxolitinib—a Janus kinase (JAK) 1/JAK2 inhibitor—and TAK‐779—a C‐X‐C motif chemokine receptor 3 (CXCR3)/C‐C motif chemokine receptor (CCR) 5/CCR2 antagonist—on HuNoV replication in wild‐type jejunal HIEs. TAK‐779 increased GII.17 HuNoV replication by 2.3‐ and 6.0‐fold at 48 and 96 h postinfection (hpi), respectively, compared with the DMSO‐treated control. In contrast, ruxolitinib alone did not affect viral replication. Notably, simultaneous treatment with these compounds further enhanced GII.17 replication by 6.0‐ and 10.7‐fold at 48 and 96 hpi, respectively, which enabled serial passaging. These findings imply that the simultaneous suppression of these pathways potentially benefits GII.17 HuNoV replication.

On the Numerical Solution of the Multiscale Models Characterising Noroviral Infectious Disease Systems Using the Multistage Spectral the Relaxation Method and the Nonstandard Finite Difference Method

Authors: Kizito Muzhinji
Original abstract (English)
Multiscale models for infectious disease systems are highly nonlinear. This presents substantial difficulties for both analysis and computation. Consequently, there is a continuous demand for the development of efficient numerical methods that provide reliable solutions. Over time, various numerical techniques have been developed for single-scale models. However, multiscale models have increasingly relied on built-in solvers and, more recently, the nonstandard finite difference method. The primary aim of this study is to offer an in-depth examination of the multistage spectral relaxation method (MSRM) applied to the multiscale model characterising norovirus infection. This study also provides a comprehensive comparison with the nonstandard finite difference method (NSFDM) in terms of convergence and accuracy, as well as their handling of highly nonlinear systems of ordinary differential equations. Both schemes were validated against an adaptive ODE45 scheme in MATLAB2025a, which served as the reference solver. The numerical outcomes indicate that the MSRM achieves a better accuracy level, with relative L2 errors ranging from 10−9 to 10−7 for Δτ≤0.10 and Nch=8. In contrast, the NSFDM provides solid first-order accuracy, yielding L2 errors around 10−4, while ensuring strict unconditional positivity and stability across all tested step sizes from h=0.01 to 2.0. This study features a parameter sensitivity analysis that varies βH,δH,αh,μV,μH, as well as semi-log error graphs, convergence rate visuals, and CPU performance benchmarks. The MSRM costs about 24 times more per run, but it offers better accuracy for between-host variables. This makes it the preferred method for high-fidelity applications. In contrast, the NSFDM is the best option for quick parameter sweeps and long simulations that require guaranteed positivity.

Original source text (English)
Moderna's most-delayed vaccine program hit another wall on Thursday. The company disclosed in its Q2 2026 earnings release that mRNA-1403 — its experimental mRNA-based norovirus vaccine — did not meet the pre-specified statistical threshold for early success at a Phase 3 interim analysis, extending a trial that has now stretched across three enrollment seasons, one FDA clinical hold, and a case-accrual collapse on two continents. No approved norovirus vaccine exists anywhere in the world, and Thursday's interim miss does not mean one is coming soon. Shares of Moderna (NASDAQ: MRNA) fell roughly 7% in morning trading on the news, even as the company reported a Q2 net loss of $782 million that was modestly better than the year-ago figure and beat Wall Street's consensus estimate. The stock reaction reflected where investor attention landed: not on the financials, but on the pipeline. What "Missing the Interim Bar" Actually Means — and What It Does Not Before treating Thursday's disclosure as a verdict on mRNA-1403, it is worth being precise about what an interim analysis miss does and does not mean — because the two are often conflated and the distinction matters for the 21 million Americans who contract norovirus every year. A Phase 3 trial with a planned interim analysis is designed to allow early stopping if the evidence of efficacy is so strong that continuing the study would be unnecessary. The statistical threshold for that "early success" determination is intentionally set higher than the threshold for the trial's final readout — because looking at accumulating data multiple times inflates the risk of a false positive, and trial designers account for this by requiring stronger evidence at each interim look. Missing the early-success threshold means the data, at this point in the trial, do not clear that higher bar. It does not mean the data show the vaccine does not work. The trial remains ongoing, blinded, and scientifically sound. Moderna is now adding an additional cohort to generate more cases, and a definitive efficacy readout — held to a lower, more standard statistical bar — will come when sufficient cases have accrued. The company did not disclose the observed efficacy point estimate from the interim analysis, consistent with the blinded study design. Three Seasons, One Hold, and Why Norovirus Makes This So Hard The chronology of the Nova 301 trial illustrates a challenge that predates Moderna and will outlast it: norovirus is one of the most difficult pathogens in the world to run a vaccine efficacy trial against, as documented in epidemiological reviews of the development challenges. Moderna dosed the first participant in September 2024, timing the launch to coincide with the Northern Hemisphere norovirus season — the disease peaks between October and March in the US, Europe, Japan, and Canada. After completing Northern Hemisphere enrollment, the trial relocated to the Southern Hemisphere to track that region's winter season. It ran into a wall. In its Q3 2025 earnings release, Moderna disclosed it was unable to accrue sufficient cases in the south and would need to return to the Northern Hemisphere for a second season, 2025–2026. The trial's trouble was compounded in February 2025, when the FDA placed Nova 301 on clinical hold following a single adverse event report of Guillain-Barré syndrome — a rare neurological condition where the immune system attacks the peripheral nervous system. Moderna said at the time that it did not expect the hold to affect the efficacy readout timeline, since Northern Hemisphere enrollment had already been completed. The FDA lifted the hold approximately in May 2025. The GBS case remains under investigation; a single case in a trial of approximately 25,000 participants does not establish a causal link to the vaccine. What Thursday's interim miss adds is a third failure of the case-accrual process — this time at the statistical threshold level rather than the geographic level. Moderna is now preparing to enroll yet another cohort. The Technical Reason mRNA Cannot Fix This Problem The norovirus trial's struggles highlight a distinction that is easy to miss: the mRNA platform's celebrated speed advantage addresses a manufacturing timeline problem, not a disease-surveillance problem. When TechTimes covered Moderna's mFLUSIVA flu vaccine in June 2026, a central argument for the platform was that mRNA can compress strain selection to final vaccine from six months to two to three months, allowing better matching to late-breaking flu variants. That advantage is real for flu, where the pathogen circulates reliably every winter and the bottleneck is manufacturing speed. Norovirus is different. The pathogen circulates in seasonal waves, but those waves are geographically uneven, year-to-year variable, and driven by outbreak clusters in healthcare settings, schools, military bases, and cruise ships rather than by broad community transmission, as epidemiological research has documented. Designing an mRNA sequence for a norovirus target takes days. Waiting for enough norovirus cases to appear in the vaccinated population — so that researchers can measure whether vaccinated people got sick less often than unvaccinated people — can take years. The mRNA platform does not accelerate that wait. mRNA-1403 is a trivalent vaccine, encoding the major capsid protein (VP1) of three norovirus genotypes — GII.4, GI.3, and GII.3 — encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles, the same delivery system as Moderna's flu and COVID vaccines. Those three genotypes together represent the dominant strains causing norovirus-related acute gastroenteritis globally, as described in Phase 1/2 immunogenicity data published in Open Forum Infectious Diseases. Phase 1/2 data confirmed robust HBGA-blocking antibody responses and Th1-predominant CD4+ T cell responses against all three targets — the immunological profile needed for protection, as detailed in cellular and humoral immunogenicity analyses from the Phase 1/2 trial. The science appears to be working. The epidemiology is the constraint. The cash position at June 30 stood at $6.9 billion in cash, equivalents, and investments. That figure does not reflect a $950 million litigation settlement payment Moderna made in July 2026, announced during Q1 2026. The company now expects year-end 2026 cash and investments of $4.7 to $5.2 billion — an improvement of about $200 million from its prior forecast, reflecting tighter cost management. If the FDA approves mFLUSIVA by that date, it would become Moderna's fifth commercial product and its first vaccine against a routine, annually recurring disease — a commercial milestone that analysts have estimated could contribute $1 billion to the company's coffers by 2028. That bet may be correct. Norovirus is responsible for approximately 21 million cases of vomiting and diarrhea annually in the United States, 109,000 hospitalizations, and 900 deaths — the majority among adults 65 and older, the same population at greatest risk of severe outcomes from flu. Globally, the disease causes an estimated 200,000 deaths per year, with the highest burden among children in low-income countries. A Phase 3 trial with a pre-specified interim analysis is designed to allow early stopping if the evidence is overwhelmingly positive. Missing the early-success threshold means the current case count does not provide that overwhelming evidence. The trial remains blinded and ongoing. Moderna is enrolling additional participants to generate more cases, after which a standard final analysis will determine whether mRNA-1403 is protective. Original source text (English)
Human norovirus is the leading cause of acute gastroenteritis worldwide and is estimated to infect 600–700 million people each year. Despite its enormous public health impact, no effective antiviral drugs are currently available. A detailed understanding of the three-dimensional structure of the virus is essential for elucidating its infection mechanism and developing effective therapeutics. However, research on human norovirus has been hampered by the inability to efficiently propagate the virus in cultured cells, making it difficult to obtain sufficient quantities of infectious particles for structural studies. In contrast, studies of murine norovirus, which can readily be propagated in cell culture, have shown that its capsid undergoes conformational changes in response to environmental conditions. Whether human norovirus exhibits similar structural dynamics has remained unknown. To address this question, the research team successfully produced virus-like particles (VLPs) of the human norovirus GII.3 strain, which has been responsible for repeated regional outbreaks, and determined their structures using state-of-the-art cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM). Structural analysis revealed that a single VLP preparation contained two distinct conformational states, referred to as the resting and rising states, which differ in the location of the protruding (P) domain. Further analysis showed that (1) in the resting state, the P domain is positioned close to the underlying shell (S) domain and forms an extensive interaction network mediated primarily by the distal P2 subdomain. (2) In the rising state, the P domain is elevated by approximately 1 nm and rotated by about 55°, accompanied by a reorganization of the interaction network to the P1 subdomain. In addition, the P domain in the rising state exhibits substantially greater structural flexibility than in the resting state. These findings provide the first evidence that the human norovirus capsid, like that of murine norovirus, is not a rigid shell but a dynamic molecular assembly capable of adopting multiple conformations in response to its environment. Because the P domain contains both the host receptor-binding sites and the major antibody epitopes, these conformational changes may play important roles in regulating viral infectivity, receptor recognition, and immune evasion. Future studies will investigate the environmental factors-such as pH, metal ions, and physiological acids-that trigger these structural transitions, determine whether the same conformational changes as shown in murine norovirus occur in infectious virions, and explore the development of antibodies or small-molecule compounds capable of stabilizing or inhibiting these transitions.
