Simultaneous visual detection of norovirus, rotavirus, and adenovirus using multiplex reverse transcription recombinase polymerase amplification combined with lateral flow strip.

Authors: Lu-Fei Yuan, Le-Le Song, Ya-Ke Zhang, Zhi-Peng Fang, Zhou-Jie Ma, Rong Jiang, Ji-Lu Shen, Shen Jilu
Original abstract (English)
Viral gastroenteritis (VGE) poses a major threat to children's health globally, with human norovirus (NoV), rotavirus (RV), and adenovirus (AdV) as the most common causative agents. In this study, a multiplex reverse transcription recombinase polymerase amplification (mRT-RPA) detection platform combined with a lateral flow strip (LFS) enables the simultaneous detection of the RNA viruses NoV and RV and the DNA virus AdV within a single-tube reaction. The platform employs a dual-labeled primer design: forward primers are labeled with fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC), while reverse primers are conjugated with specific nucleic acid tags (NATs), enabling signal transduction and multiplex recognition via a "bridging" mechanism. The platform provides sample-to-result detection within 40 min without the need for complex instruments, making it well-suited for point-of-care testing (POCT). Using serially diluted plasmid standards, the limits of detection were determined to be 10 1 copies/μL for NoV and RV, and 10 2 copies/μL for AdV. Specificity testing demonstrated no cross-reactivity with 11 other common pathogens. In clinical validation using 64 stool samples, the platform showed complete agreement with quantitative PCR (qPCR) for all target pathogens. In conclusion, this platform provides a reliable technical tool for early diagnosis and outbreak control of VGE.

ABSTRACT Noroviruses are a leading cause of acute gastroenteritis and impose a substantial global disease burden, particularly in children younger than 5 years and in older adults. Although multiple vaccine candidates are in preclinical and clinical development, no targeted intervention has been approved. We previously reported that a pentavalent mRNA vaccine candidate encoding VP1 proteins from genotypes GI.1, GII.2, GII.3, GII.4, and GII.6 elicited robust humoral and cellular immune responses in mice and nonhuman primates. Because recent surveillance data indicate increasing prevalence of GI.3 and GII.17, we designed mRNAs targeting these genotypes and evaluated an expanded seven-valent formulation in preclinical studies. We first compared candidate GI.3 and GII.17 strains in mice and selected vaccine components based on immunogenicity and cross-reactivity. The resulting seven-valent mRNA vaccine elicited strong humoral responses in mice and African green monkeys, including serum IgG binding to norovirus virus-like particles (VLPs) and functional antibodies that blocked VLP binding to histo-blood group antigens (HBGAs). Importantly, incorporation of GI.3 and GII.17 broadened functional coverage, whereas multivalent formulation introduced only limited evidence of immune interference. These findings support the feasibility of surveillance-guided updating of multivalent norovirus mRNA vaccines and highlight the adaptability of the mRNA platform for addressing emerging variants. IMPORTANCE Norovirus is a major cause of vomiting and diarrhea worldwide, but developing a broadly protective vaccine is difficult because many genetically distinct strains circulate, and their prevalence changes over time. This study demonstrates a flexible vaccine strategy in which mRNA components can be selected and added based on disease surveillance and experimental evidence. We expanded a five-component norovirus vaccine to include GI.3 and GII.17, two increasingly important genotypes. The resulting seven-component vaccine generated strong antibody responses in mice and African green monkeys and broadened the range of virus types targeted by functional antibodies. Although additional studies are needed to determine whether these responses protect humans, the findings show how the adaptability of mRNA technology could support periodic updates of multivalent norovirus vaccines as circulating strains change.

Authors: Daniela Vargas, Marcos Godoy, Aldo A. Gaggero, Molly Kibenge, Frederick Kibenge
Original abstract (English)
Background: and Objectives: In the present work, we assessed genotype annotations, genome completeness, and geographic representation of norovirus in the National Center for Biotechnology Information Virus database. Methods: We analyzed metadata for 70,632 complete and partial norovirus sequence records retrieved on 17 August 2026. Major capsid protein (VP1) genotypes and RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp) types were extracted from deposited annotations. Conclusion: This repository snapshot identified limited complete-genome representation, incomplete typing annotations, and uneven geographic representation. These findings describe deposited records rather than population prevalence. Improved genome coverage, annotation consistency, and metadata availability could support more informative comparative analyses.

Original abstract (English)
Diarrhea is a leading cause of pediatric morbidity and mortality worldwide, frequently caused by various bacterial, viral, and protozoan pathogens. Identification of these pathogens is important in clinical settings for proper management of the infection. Conventional stool culture technique is time-consuming for the detection of diarrheagenic pathogens. In addition, these traditional procedures lack sensitivity, require a specific laboratory structure, and well-trained staff. Conversely, multiplex molecular detection methods offer rapid results with less hands-on time and more sensitivity. This study compared the diagnostic performance of conventional microbiological methods and GastroFinder 2SMART multiplexed real-time PCR (real-time PCR) assay for detecting enteric pathogens in pediatric stool samples. Overall pathogen detection was significantly higher using this real-time PCR (81%) compared to the conventional assays (43.1%), reflecting differences in both sensitivity and pathogen panel breadth. Rotavirus, adenovirus, Shigella , and Giardia lamblia were frequently identified by conventional methods, whereas real-time PCR commonly detected adenovirus, Campylobacter, EPEC, ETEC, and norovirus. Certain pathogens such as Yersinia enterocolitica , Clostridioides difficile , STEC, and Dientamoeba fragilis were not tested in the conventional panel and therefore excluded for comparison. Sensitivity estimates varied, with lower sensitivity observed for EPEC, Cryptosporidium, G. lamblia , and rotavirus, partly due to the small sample numbers and wide confidence intervals. Specificity exceeded 90% for most pathogens, and diagnostic accuracy was > 90% for Shigella /EIEC, Salmonella, Cryptosporidium , Giardia lamblia , and astrovirus. Bayesian latent class analysis demonstrated the highest sensitivity for Shigella /EIEC (93.2%), followed by norovirus and adenovirus, while overall test performance, measured by the Youden Index, was greatest for Shigella /EIEC. These findings highlight the diagnostic use of real-time PCR and support its integration into routine diarrheal diagnostics to improve pathogen detection and clinical management.

Original abstract (English)

Among the various viruses that sicken your gut, some at least leave behind a tiny path of cellular destruction as they replicate, implicating the guilty pathogen. Both rotavirus and norovirus invade the small intestines, multiply there, and can break through the organ’s outer lining as one way of proliferating in the body.
