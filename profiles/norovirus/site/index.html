一项诊断管理倡议以改善癌症患者中诺如病毒感染和硝唑尼特的使用

A diagnostic stewardship initiative to improve diagnosis of norovirus and nitazoxanide utilization in patients with cancer.
作者： Guy H. Handley, Chin-Ting Wu, Jovan Borjan, Alyssa Fluker, Amin Majid Alousi, Patricia Munzy, Ameera Munzy, Sherry S. Cantu, Nancy N Vuong, Javier A. Adachi
摘要中文翻译摘要 患者和医疗工作者面临诺如病毒感染的风险。硝唑尼特常被使用,但缺乏相关数据。多重聚合酶链反应平台可能导致假阳性结果。一项质量改进倡议导致硝唑尼特使用量减少了84.4%,并实现了显著的成本节约,而不会影响患者护理或医院内感染率。

在医院环境中开展了一项质量改进倡议,以评估诊断管理对疑似患有诺如病毒感染的癌症患者中硝唑尼特使用的影响。

A diagnostic stewardship initiative to improve diagnosis of norovirus and nitazoxanide utilization in patients with cancer.

Authors: Guy H. Handley, Chin-Ting Wu, Jovan Borjan, Alyssa Fluker, Amin Majid Alousi, Patricia Munzy, Ameera Munzy, Sherry S. Cantu, Nancy N Vuong, Javier A. Adachi
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Patients and healthcare workers are at risk of norovirus infection. Nitazoxanide is often given, though data is lacking. Multiplex polymerase chain reaction platforms may yield false-positive results. A quality improvement initiative led to an 84.4% reduction in nitazoxanide utilization and substantial cost savings without compromising patient care or nosocomial infection.

Question and background
The study aimed to address the lack of data on nitazoxanide utilization in patients with cancer and the risk of norovirus infection for both patients and healthcare workers.
Design and population
A quality improvement initiative was conducted in a hospital setting to evaluate the impact of diagnostic stewardship on nitazoxanide utilization in patients with cancer who were suspected of having norovirus infection.
Core methods
The study utilized a multiplex polymerase chain reaction platform for norovirus detection, which may yield false-positive results, and implemented a diagnostic stewardship program to monitor and adjust nitazoxanide use.
Main results
The initiative resulted in an 84.4% reduction in nitazoxanide utilization and substantial cost savings without compromising patient care or increasing nosocomial infection rates.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted the findings as evidence that diagnostic stewardship can effectively reduce unnecessary nitazoxanide use in cancer patients while maintaining clinical outcomes and preventing infections.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings suggest that targeted diagnostic strategies can improve resource allocation and reduce costs in the management of norovirus infections in immunocompromised patients.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its focus on a single institution and the potential for false-positive results with the multiplex polymerase chain reaction platform, but the evidence is strong due to the clear reduction in nitazoxanide use and cost savings.

问题与背景
本研究旨在探讨2011年至2025年中国昆明市其他传染病性腹泻(OID)的长期流行病学特征及病原谱变化,因为OID是中国最常见的法定传染病之一。

本研究在中国昆明市进行,时间跨度为2011年至2025年,重点关注所有年龄段的OID病例,特别是0-4岁儿童。研究包括了研究期间报告的152,090例OID病例。

解释与创新
研究结果表明,昆明市OID的流行趋势已从传统的疫情波动转向更广泛的结构性变化,表现为受影响人群的扩大、病原体构成的演变以及空间疫情模式的变化。
科研与公卫意义
研究结果突显了OID流行病学复杂性的增加,并强调需要制定综合考虑人口多样性、空间差异和病原体构成的预防策略,以应对新兴的防控挑战。
局限与证据强度
观察到的病原体构成变化可能受到检测实践的影响,这限制了研究结果的可信度。然而,本研究提供了OID趋势和模式的全面分析,支持中等程度的可信度评估。

Spatiotemporal dynamics and pathogen spectrum of other infectious diarrhea in Kunming, China from 2011 to 2025

Authors: Xiao-Qiao Zhang, Yong-Ming Zhou, Xiang-Nan Jing, Zhi-Peng Mao, Li-Jie Zhang, Tie-Jun Shui, Xiang-Yu Yan
Original abstract (English)
Other infectious diarrhea (OID) is one of the most frequently reported notifiable infectious diseases in China. As the capital of Yunnan Province, Kunming serves as an important regional hub in Southwest China and a gateway to the Greater Mekong Subregion. Its patterns of regional population mobility and local disease transmission are complex and region-specific, making Kunming an informative setting for sentinel surveillance and epidemiological investigations. This study investigated the long-term epidemiological characteristics and pathogen spectrum changes of OID in Kunming from 2011 to 2025. Temporal, spatial, demographic and pathogen-related patterns were assessed using descriptive epidemiological analyses, time-series and trend analyses. Seasonal-trend decomposition using Loess (STL) was utilized to characterize long-term temporal trends and seasonal patterns of OID. Continuous wavelet transform was applied to identify periodic fluctuations and temporal changes in epidemic patterns. The Shannon-Wiener diversity index was used to evaluate changes in pathogen diversity. Joinpoint regression was performed to assess long-term incidence trends across different population groups. From 2011 to 2025, Kunming reported 152,090 OID cases, with a mean annual incidence of 134.3 per 100,000 population (range: 62.7-273.2 per 100,000). Incidence increased significantly over the surveillance period (AAPC = 11.2%, 95% CI 3.5 to 19.3), showing a marked acceleration after 2023. OID cases occurred across all age groups, with those aged 0-4 years accounting for the largest proportion (67.0%, 101,899/152,090), while incidence increased across all age groups (all P < 0.001). Among 38,902 laboratory-confirmed cases, viral infections predominated (99.2%). Rotavirus remained the most prevalent pathogen (69.7%), followed by norovirus (26.7%). Both were mainly concentrated among children aged 0-4 years (89.1% and 72.0%, respectively). Spatially, OID incidence varied across counties and districts, with increasingly synchronized regional increases observed in recent years, particularly after 2024. Our findings from routinely collected surveillance data indicate that OID epidemiology in Kunming has shifted from traditional epidemic fluctuations toward broader structural changes, characterized by expanding affected populations, evolving pathogen composition, and changing spatial epidemic patterns. Although the observed changes in pathogen composition may have been influenced by testing practices, the findings highlight increasing epidemiological complexity and emerging control challenges. Accordingly, prevention strategies should integrate population diversity, spatial differences, and pathogen composition.

Visual Detection of Foodborne Viruses in Leafy Greens Using an AsCas12f-Split-G-Quadruplex Sensor.

Authors: Tom Kasputis, Mathew Landivar, Lijuan Yuan, R Clay Wright, Chenming Zhang, Juhong Chen
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Foodborne viruses are responsible for a substantial burden of disease worldwide, with fresh produce serving as the most abundant vehicle for transmission. However, rapid detection in solid produce, such as leafy greens, remains underdeveloped, as conventional methods often require labor-intensive sample preparation and specialized instrumentation. Herein, we present a colorimetric CRISPR-split-G4 biosensor for rapid viral surveillance in leafy greens that combines reverse transcription recombinase polymerase amplification (RT-RPA), AsCas12f-mediated trans-cleavage, and a modular split-G-quadruplex (split-G4) reporter system. In this system, target recognition activates the trans-cleavage activity of AsCas12f, resulting in degradation of a blocker strand and subsequent assembly of a catalytically active G4-hemin DNAzyme that generates a visible signal easily detectable by the naked eye. Using norovirus as a model pathogen, the platform exhibited high analytical sensitivity and specificity, enabling reliable detection of viral particles with minimal instrumentation. To evaluate practical applicability, the assay was coupled with a simplified leaf-disk extraction workflow and successfully applied to contaminated romaine lettuce and baby spinach samples without requiring viral nucleic acid purification. The platform achieved detection limits of 1000 and 5000 viral copies per 100 mg of romaine lettuce and spinach tissue, respectively. From sample processing to visual readout, the entire assay was completed in approximately 2 h. By integrating isothermal amplification, CRISPR-based target recognition, and instrument-free signal generation into a streamlined workflow, this strategy provides a promising platform for decentralized food safety monitoring and establishes a flexible framework for the on-site detection of viral pathogens in complex food matrices.

Norovirus is a leading cause of acute gastrointestinal infection in winter and circulates from November through the following spring. It spreads particularly quickly among young children in group settings such as daycare centers and kindergartens. Unlike adults, infants and young children are prone to developing sudden vomiting as an initial symptom and face a greater risk of dehydration, making prompt early care and good hygiene essential. According to past surveillance data from the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency, sentinel surveillance at 210 hospital-level medical institutions nationwide found that norovirus infections began to increase in the first week of November 2025, and the weekly number of cases reached 617 in the third week of January 2026. Notably, infants and young children aged 0 to 6 accounted for 51.1% of all cases at the time, underscoring the importance of preventing infections and maintaining good hygiene among young children in winter. Norovirus causes symptoms such as vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain after an incubation period of 12 to 48 hours. Diarrhea is often more prominent in adults, while sudden, repeated vomiting can be a main symptom in children. Therefore, even if there is no diarrhea, norovirus gastroenteritis should be considered if a child suddenly vomits several times. It is not advisable to automatically withhold food from a child who is vomiting. If the child is breastfed, continue breastfeeding; if the vomiting is severe, offering small amounts of oral rehydration solution frequently can help. Once the vomiting subsides and the child is able to eat, it is best to offer small portions of the foods they usually eat. The main concern with norovirus gastroenteritis is dehydration. Infants and young children can lose body fluids rapidly through repeated vomiting and diarrhea, and may become dehydrated more easily than adults. If a child keeps vomiting even when given small amounts of water or rehydration solution, or has noticeably less urine and seems less energetic than usual, dehydration may be developing. In such cases, seek medical care promptly. Norovirus is highly contagious and can cause infection from even a small amount of the virus. It spreads not only through contaminated food or water, but also through an infected person’s vomit or stool and through hands or toys contaminated with the virus. In particular, if a child’s surroundings become contaminated when they vomit, the virus can remain on surfaces for a long time and spread to family members or other children at daycare. To prevent infection, it is important to wash hands thoroughly with soap and running water for at least 30 seconds rather than relying solely on alcohol-based hand sanitizer. Food should be cooked thoroughly, and areas where a child has vomited or had diarrhea should be cleaned and disinfected with a product effective against norovirus. Because the virus may continue to be shed for a period after symptoms improve, children should stay home from daycare for at least 48 hours after vomiting and diarrhea have completely stopped. Baek Jeong-hyeon, a pediatrician at Woori Children’s Hospital, said, “It is important to know how many times a child has vomited, but it is even more important to check whether the child can take in and keep down even small amounts of fluid, whether their urine output has decreased, and whether they seem less energetic than usual.” The specialist urged, “Dehydration can progress quickly, especially in very young children. The GI1.1 Panel also has IVDR CE marking for European commercialization; the Panel Mid will initially be available in the United States. bioMérieux, a world leader in the field of in vitro diagnostics, today announces that the BIOFIRE® FILMARRAY® Gastrointestinal 1.1 (GI1.1) Panel has obtained U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) 510(k) clearance and IVDR1 CE marking, and the BIOFIRE® FILMARRAY® Gastrointestinal 1.1 (GI1.1) Panel Mid has received U.S. FDA 510(k) clearance. These PCR* molecular panels test 22 and 11, respectively, of the most common bacteria, viruses, and parasites associated with gastroenteritis, all from one sample, with results available in about one hour. Acute infectious gastroenteritis remains a significant global health concern, driven by a diverse and continually evolving range of viral, bacterial, and parasitic pathogens that inflame the gastrointestinal tract. Clinical manifestations such as diarrhea, vomiting, fever, and abdominal pain are often nonspecific, making diagnostic testing even more critical. The highest disease burden affects young children, older adults, and immunocompromised individuals. Diarrheal diseases, which cause an estimated 1.7 billion cases worldwide each year,2 continue to rank among the leading causes of mortality in children under 60 months of age. As pathogen populations shift over time through genetic variation, emergence of new strains, and changes in epidemiological patterns, diagnostic technologies must evolve to ensure accurate detection, timely clinical decision-making, and effective public health responses. Redesigned norovirus assay and improved Campylobacter detection In line with its commitment to continuous improvements and to delivering high-quality diagnostic solutions, bioMérieux has developed the BIOFIRE® FILMARRAY® GI1.1 Panel and BIOFIRE® FILMARRAY® GI1.1 Panel Mid. These new versions include a redesigned norovirus GI/GII assay and a software-based improvement for Campylobacter detection, further strengthening the panels’ specificity and providing greater confidence in every result. These molecular syndromic testing solutions are capable of simultaneously detecting 22 and 11 pathogens*, respectively, directly from stool samples of individuals presenting with signs and/or symptoms of gastrointestinal infection. Designed for use on bioMérieux’s BIOFIRE® FILMARRAY® 2.0 and TORCH systems, they require about two minutes of hands-on time for setup, with an approximate run time of about one hour. By providing rapid, actionable results, we support better clinical decision-making for improved patient management and deliver greater value to laboratories and clinicians when it matters most,” commented Jennifer Zinn, Executive Vice President, Clinical Operations, bioMérieux.
