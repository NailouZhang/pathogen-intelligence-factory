对 2024 年澳大利亚首都特区一次餐饮社交活动中诺如病毒爆发的调查。

Investigation of a norovirus outbreak at a catered social event, Australian Capital Territory, 2024.
作者： Clare Kinnear, Timothy Sloan-Gardner, Natasha Waters, Victoria Wansink, Lyndell Hudson, Jenny Post
摘要中文翻译背景:据悉爆发了胃肠道疾病,影响了 2024 年澳大利亚首都领地 (ACT) 私人活动的参加者。进行了调查以确定传染源、疾病来源和传播方式,并确定预防进一步疾病所需的任何公共卫生行动。方法:使用修改后的标准胃肠炎调查问卷对活动参加者进行调查。确诊的疫情病例被定义为参加活动后身体不适且临床样本呈诺如病毒阳性的个人;可能的暴发病例被定义为参加活动后出现不适并出现临床症状的个人。进行了一项病例对照研究,以确定与疾病相关的食品。进行了食品安全检查,并在首都领地政府分析实验室对食品样本进行了测试。临床样本是从身体不适的参加者和无症状的食品处理人员那里收集的。结果:大约有 250-500 名与会者参加了此次活动;224 名与会者填写了调查问卷,共有 65 例病例(5 例确诊病例,60 例疑似病例)。一名之前身体不适的食品处理人员在症状消失超过 96 小时后准备了水果和肉类拼盘,随后诺如病毒检测呈阳性。环境健康调查显示,该场所有良好的饮食习惯和洗手习惯;调查发现公众没有持续面临的风险。食品样本检测未发现任何细菌性食源性病原体。流行病学调查发现,食用新鲜水果(比值比 [OR]:5.3;95% 置信区间 [95% CI]:2.4-12.6;p < 0.001)和腌肉(OR:2.7;95% CI:1.2-6.2;p = 0.02)与疾病显着相关。结论:流行病学和临床证据表明诺如病毒是通过受污染的新鲜水果和腌肉拼盘传播的。尽管该场地有良好的食品处理和洗手习惯,但这很可能是由于一名食品处理人员在活动前一周身体不适,并在症状消失超过 96 小时后才准备了相关食品。这次疫情调查表明,安全食品实践和排除期可以减少但不能完全消除风险。我们的研究结果强调了在食品准备环境中继续进行有效手部卫生教育的必要性,特别是在胃肠道疾病之后。

Investigation of a norovirus outbreak at a catered social event, Australian Capital Territory, 2024.

Authors: Clare Kinnear, Timothy Sloan-Gardner, Natasha Waters, Victoria Wansink, Lyndell Hudson, Jenny Post
Original Abstract
BACKGROUND: An outbreak of gastrointestinal illness was notified, affecting attendees at a private function in the Australian Capital Territory (ACT) in 2024. Investigations were conducted to determine the infectious agent, source of illness and mode of transmission, as well as to determine any public health actions required to prevent further illness. METHODS: Function attendees were surveyed using a modified standard gastroenteritis questionnaire. A confirmed outbreak case was defined as an individual who was unwell after attending the event and had a norovirus-positive clinical sample; a probable outbreak case was defined as an individual who was unwell with clinically compatible symptoms after attending the event. A case-control study was conducted to identify food items associated with illness. Food safety inspections were performed, and food samples were tested at the ACT Government Analytical Laboratory. Clinical samples were collected from unwell attendees and from an asymptomatic food handler. RESULTS: There were approximately 250-500 attendees at the function; 224 attendees completed the questionnaire and there were 65 cases (5 confirmed and 60 probable). A food handler who had previously been unwell, and who prepared fruit and meat platters for the function more than 96 hours after their symptoms had resolved, subsequently tested positive for norovirus. Environmental Health investigations showed the venue had good food practices and handwashing; the investigation identified no ongoing risks to the public. Testing of food samples did not detect any bacterial foodborne pathogens. Epidemiological investigations identified consumption of fresh fruit (odds ratio [OR]: 5.3; 95% confidence interval [95% CI]: 2.4-12.6; p < 0.001) and cured meat (OR: 2.7; 95% CI: 1.2-6.2; p = 0.02) as significantly associated with disease. CONCLUSION: Epidemiological and clinical evidence indicates that norovirus was transmitted via contaminated fresh fruit and cured meat platters. Despite the venue having good food handling and handwashing practices, it was likely due to a food handler who had been unwell in the week prior to the function and prepared the implicated foods more than 96 hours after their symptoms resolved. This outbreak investigation shows that safe food practices and exclusion periods reduce but do not eliminate risk completely. Our findings highlight the need for continued education on effective hand hygiene in food preparation settings, especially following gastrointestinal illness.

Original Abstract
Abstract Wastewater metagenomic sequencing (WW-MGS) enables simultaneous detection of hundreds of pathogens, but its use for quantitative pathogen tracking has not been robustly validated. Like wastewater PCR (WW-PCR), WW-MGS is affected by biases from variable fecal dilution and sample processing, but must additionally contend with the compositional structure of sequencing data, where a taxon’s apparent abundance depends on the abundance of every other taxon in the sample. Simple summaries such as a pathogen’s fraction of total reads may therefore be poorly suited to quantitative use. Gains were largest for year-round-circulating SARS-CoV-2 and norovirus and smaller for sharply seasonal pathogens such as influenza and respiratory syncytial virus, where baseline concordance was already high. Tobamovirus normalization rarely degraded concordance, with median gains roughly five times larger than median losses. Tobamovirus-normalized WW-MGS reached clinical concordance comparable to targeted WW-PCR, supporting its use as a quantitative trend-monitoring tool alongside pathogen-agnostic detection.

The four contributions gathered in this Research Topic, Advances in Optical Methods for Biological Sample Characterization, sample that breadth directly: a mechanistic study of second harmonic generation in the cornea, a critical review of the trade-offs built into fluorescent labelling, a forward-looking perspective on the field as a whole, and a plasmonic biosensing platform built to tell two closely related viruses apart. Read together, they show how mature certain optical techniques have become, and how much interpretive and instrumental work still remains.We opened this Research Topic with a deliberately wide scope, inviting work on bioimaging, biosensors, optical tweezers, particle tracking, and AI-assisted data analysis. In our own Perspective (Avila et al.), we used these five domains as a scaffold for a broader argument: that the next substantial gains in optical biological characterization are likely to come less from any single modality reaching a new performance ceiling, and more from combining modalities, from tighter integration with computation, and from a sharper distinction between what an optical signal shows and what it is generally assumed to mean. The three remaining contributions to this Research Topic each speak to that argument from a different angle.Boutkhil et al. take on a problem that has limited the clinical translation of second harmonic generation (SHG) microscopy for some time: backward-detected SHG signal, the only geometry realistically available for in vivo imaging, has historically been harder to interpret than the cleaner forward signal obtained from thin, ex vivo sections. Imaging intact porcine corneas in a custom pressure-controlled chamber, the authors show that the periodic high-and low-intensity bands long read in backward images as discrete "collagen bundles" are in fact an artifact of collagen crimp, the natural waviness of corneal lamellae. Raise the intraocular pressure and the fibres straighten; the bands fade in step. The authors are candid that a complete quantitative model linking the forward-to-backward signal ratio to tissue structure remains out of reach, but the structural mechanism they identify should change how the next generation of corneal SHG images, and the diagnostic claims built on them, are read.A second axis of the same general problem, quite literally whether to label a sample at all, runs through the review by Cuevas-Galindo and Jiménez-Sánchez. Small-molecule fluorescent probes are indispensable tools, but the authors build a sustained case that the same properties that make a probe bright and selective, high-affinity binding, lipophilic partitioning, excitedstate reactivity, also make it an active participant in the biology it is meant to report on. Their discussion of calcium indicators, whose buffering capacity measurably reshapes the signalling kinetics they are used to track, gives a concrete anchor to an argument that can otherwise stay abstract. The review does not call for abandoning labels; it positions label-free approaches, autofluorescence imaging of NAD(P)H and flavins, fluorescence lifetime imaging, as a benchmark against which any probe's effect on the underlying physiology should be checked, and it surveys middle-ground strategies, including HaloTag and SNAP-tag constructs, bioorthogonal fluorogenic chemistry, and ratiometric sensing, that try to narrow that gap.The fourth contribution moves from imaging to sensing. describe a plasmonic metasurface, patterned with Y-shaped nanocavity arrays and tuned to 785 nm excitation, that supports surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (SERS) sensitive enough to discriminate human from murine norovirus particles by their vibrational signature alone. Owing to the structural similarities between the two viruses, this result has implications beyond a simple proof of concept: principal component analysis of the SERS spectra recovers two components accounting for over 98% of the total variance, enough to separate the species reliably with no antibodies, no PCR, and no labelling step of any kind. Given how disruptive norovirus outbreaks remain for food safety and clinical care, a rapid, label-free screening route of this kind is an appealing complement to existing diagnostics, and the underlying nanofabrication approach should generalize to other closely related pathogens.Taken together, these four contributions converge on a theme that recurs across biophotonics more broadly: an optical signal is rarely as simple to interpret as it first appears, whether because the geometry of signal collection changes what a "bundle" or a "band" actually represents, because the probe used to generate contrast measurably perturbs the system it is meant to probe, or because telling two similar biological targets apart requires statistical tools as much as photons. None of these four pieces resolves those tensions outright, but each narrows one of them, by correcting a long-standing source of misreading, by naming a bias that is easy to overlook, by showing that closely related biological samples can be distinguished without labels, and by setting out a framework against which the field's broader progress can be measured. With this Research Topic now closed, we thank the authors and reviewers who contributed to it, and we hope it serves as a useful reference point for further work at the interface of optics and biology.
