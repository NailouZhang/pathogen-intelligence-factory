Systematic functional, genomic, and safety evaluation of food-derived anti-human norovirus candidate Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus YQ001

Authors: Xiang-Jun Zhan, Yuxuan Hao, Wan-Ying Zeng, Meng-Ge Sun, Ya-Qi Yang, Chenang Lyu, Ran An, Da-Peng Wang, Xiangjun Zhan, Wanying Zeng
Original abstract (English)
Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus YQ001 is a yogurt-derived strain previously reported to bind GII.4 human norovirus (HuNoV) and inhibit viral replication in zebrafish larvae. Here, its in vitro probiotic properties and preliminary safety were evaluated together with whole-genome analysis. The 2.87-Mb genome (46.67% GC; 2757 coding sequences) shared 97.66% average nucleotide identity with L. rhamnosus GG ATCC 53103. L. rhamnosus YQ001 showed acid and bile tolerance, retaining approximately 30% survival after 3 h at pH 2.0 and over 40% after 4 h in 0.2% bile salts. During sequential simulated gastrointestinal digestion, L. rhamnosus YQ001 retained 35.98% survival after the gastric phase and 45.06% after the intestinal phase. L. rhamnosus YQ001 showed over 90% auto-aggregation at 24 h, while its cell-free supernatant exhibited DPPH and ABTS radical-scavenging activities of 85.17% and 76.35%, respectively. Antibacterial activity detected by agar diffusion was abolished after pH neutralization, indicating that the observed inhibition was largely pH-dependent under the tested conditions. L. rhamnosus YQ001 was non-hemolytic and negative for gelatinase and nitroreductase activities. Overall, the phenotypic and genomic findings support the probiotic potential of L. rhamnosus YQ001, while further strain-specific functional and safety evaluation is warranted.

Molecular Prevalence and Determinants of Adenovirus and Norovirus Among Diarrhoeic Children in Ilorin Metropolis, Kwara State, Nigeria

Authors: Mutiat Busayo Odebisi-Omokanye, Suleiman Muhammed Mustapha, Mariam Kehinde Sulaiman, Augustine Okechukwu Udeze, AbdulBasit Bolaji Ajibade
Original abstract (English)
Viral gastroenteritis remains a major public health concern and a leading cause of morbidity among children under five worldwide. Adenovirus (Adv) and Norovirus (Nv) are important etiological agents, yet data on their prevalence and determinants in North-central Nigeria are limited. A cross-sectional study involving 100 children under 5 years with acute diarrhoea was conducted at the Children’s Specialist Hospital, Ilorin, Kwara State between February to December, 2024. Virus detection was performed using qPCR, while a semi-structured questionnaire collected sociodemographic and environmental data. Associations were analyzed using chi-square tests and multivariable logistic regression. Adenovirus prevalence was high (71%), while Norovirus GI and GII were detected in 4% and 12% of cases, respectively. Coinfection of Adv and Nv occurred in 13% of participants. Adenovirus infection was significantly associated with sociodemographic and sanitation factors whereas no significant predictors were identified for Norovirus. Multivariable analysis showed sanitation-related factors as the strongest predictors. Toilets located near water sources increased the risk of infection, whereas open defecation had lower odds than water closet use. Both infections were more prevalent during the wet season. These findings highlight key determinants of Adv and Nv infections in children under five, underscoring the need for targeted public health interventions among vulnerable populations.

Human Norovirus GII is Widespread in the Coastal Marine Waters of the San Diego–Tijuana (US–Mexico) Border Region
作者： Agarwal V, Marhoefer-Jess S, Inlow K, Barton A, Giddings S, Feddersen F, Gopalakrishnan G, Spydell M, Bowman J
摘要中文翻译
查看研究七要素
问题与背景
本研究旨在调查人类 诺如病毒 GII 在圣地亚哥-蒂华纳边境地区的沿海海域中的存在和分布,以解决对该区域病原体环境持久性和潜在传播的理解需求。
设计与对象
我们在蒂华纳河河口及相邻的沿海海洋区域开展了一项为期六个月的基于数字微滴PCR(ddPCR)的监测活动,重点关注2024年6月19日至12月30日期间在美国一侧沿海海洋边境地区的约12公里范围内的采样。蒂华纳的污水由南湾国际污水处理 治疗 植物(SBIWTP)收集,该植物由国际边界水委员会(IBWC)运营,并将污水输送到蒂华纳河口,最终流入沿海海洋。
核心方法
使用数字微滴PCR(ddPCR)对HuNoV GII的浓度进行量化,并通过分析每个样本的空白限值(LoB)来统计分析空间趋势。本研究还评估了样本回收率,通过添加MuNoV作为标准品,并将基于GMM的方法计数与Bio-Rad QuantaSoft软件的计数进行比较。
主要结果
从 2024 年 6 月到 12 月,采样地点之间的 HuNoV 平均浓度存在几个数量级的差异。在最靠近河口的海岸线位置(PP-010),最大平均浓度超过 105 拷贝 L-1,而其他海岸线位置的平均值约为 104 拷贝 L-1。近海地点的平均值为 102-103 份 L-1。MuNoV 峰值的平均样本回收率为 23.8 ± 5.3%,在河口和银线州立公园露营地收集的样本之间没有显着差异。RT-ddPCR 的抑制通常较低,只有海滩样品的洗脱液部分与无核酸酶水对照存在显着差异。
解释与创新
研究发现,HuNoV GII的浓度在河源和岸边最高,随着离岸距离和沿岸距离的增加而降低。这些空间梯度符合预期,并与之前预测该地区污水排放模式的物理模型一致。
科研与公卫意义

Authors: Cristian Jacob, Massimiliano Marvasi, Shirley Ann Micallef
Original abstract (English)
Promoting increased consumption of fresh fruits and vegetables remains a cornerstone of global public health strategies aimed at preventing noncommunicable diseases and addressing micronutrient deficiencies. However, the safety of fresh produce continues to be challenged by contamination with human enteric pathogens. The persistence and dissemination of human pathogens within agricultural ecosystems represent a complex interplay between microbial physiology, plant-associated niches, environmental stressors, and crop management practices. Following the insights gathered in Volume I of this Research Topic, Volume II brings together four contributions that advance our understanding of these dynamics. Together, these studies reveal how biological adaptation, produce surface characteristics, and environmental reservoirs influence the persistence, transmission, and control of human pathogens and antimicrobial resistance within agricultural systems.A foundational element of pathogen persistence is the ability of human enteric bacteria to adapt to non-host, plant-associated environments. In their comprehensive review, Han et al. (2025) synthesize the metabolic adaptations utilized by Salmonella enterica to survive and colonize plant tissues and agricultural substrates. The authors delineate how S. enterica dynamically reprograms its central carbon and nitrogen metabolism in response to nutrients available in root exudates, leaf apoplastic fluids, and plant tissues. enterica prioritizes glucose metabolism through glycolysis, shifts to gluconeogenesis under sugar-limiting conditions, and exploits alternative carbon sources such as glycerol, fatty acids, and organic acids. Furthermore, de novo amino acid biosynthesis, particularly cysteine via sulfate assimilation, emerges as an indispensable metabolic hub required for bacterial fitness across diverse plant hosts and stress conditions. By highlighting this metabolic plasticity, Han et al. (2025) provide insights into how human pathogens exploit plant metabolic niches, identifying physiological vulnerabilities that could be targeted to prevent preharvest colonization.Interactions at the plant surface are relevant for pathogen attachment, retention, and susceptibility to post-harvest sanitation. Addressing this physical interface, Lightbown and DiCaprio (2025) investigated the impact of leafy green microscale surface topography on the recovery and chemical sanitizer inactivation of Tulane virus (TV), a surrogate for human norovirus. Comparing fresh romaine lettuce and spinach leaves across growth stages and leaf axes, the authors demonstrated that spinach leaf maturity significantly influences chemical inactivation: younger leaves allowed higher viral inactivation by sodium hypochlorite than older leaves. Crucially, the authors engineered polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) topomimetic artificial leaf surfaces ("replicasts") to decouple physical structural attributes from biological variability. By modulating replicast hydrophobicity with lowconcentration surfactants, the artificial surfaces faithfully reproduced viral recovery trends observed on fresh plant tissue, validating topomimetic replicasts as standardized physical tools for produce safety research.Expanding this physical engineering framework to soft fruits, Lightbown and DiCaprio (2026) evaluated PDMS topomimetic artificial surfaces modeled after blueberries, strawberries, and raspberries. Soft berries present intricate surface features, ranging from smooth epicuticular wax on blueberries to achene pits on strawberries and dense trichomes on raspberry drupelets, that create micro-refuges protecting viruses from post-harvest wash treatments. Their findings demonstrated that berry type markedly dictates viral retention, with raspberries retaining higher viral titers than blueberries or strawberries. Surface replicasts of all three berry types closely mirrored the recovery levels obtained from fresh fruits, confirming that physical micro-architecture is a primary driver of viral adhesion and protection against sanitizer wash solutions. These data indicate that while berry replicasts may have utility in modeling surface adhesion of viruses to berries and in viral recovery studies, they are not suitable for studies of viral inactivation by chemical sanitizers.Beyond pathogen persistence and sanitizer efficacy, agricultural environments are increasingly recognized as reservoirs of antimicrobial resistance (AMR), introducing an additional dimension to produce safety risk assessment. (2025) conducted whole-genome sequencing on 87 multidrug-resistant (MDR) bacterial strains isolated from leafy greens, root zone soil, and bulk soil within urban agriculture systems. Representing 14 genera dominated by Pseudomonas, Serratia, Providencia, and Bacillus, these environmental isolates exhibited extensive resistomes primarily encoding multidrug efflux systems, β-lactamases, and tetracycline resistance determinants. All MDR isolates harbored mobile genetic elements (MGEs), with multiple antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs) located in close physical proximity (<5 kb) to transposons, plasmids, or prophages. Norovirus cases typically start to rise around this time of year and continue to climb until early February. Norovirus is the leading cause of vomiting and diarrhea in the United States. Although it’s always around, it thrives when people huddle indoors, which is how it got the nickname “Winter Vomiting Disease.” “We typically will start seeing a trickle of norovirus in October,” said Dr. Claudia Hoyen, director of infectious disease for University Hospitals Rainbow Babies and Children’s Hospital. “But I can tell you, we usually see a big batch of it after Thanksgiving and then at different times during December, and then by that time the horse is out of the barn.” Unlike most gastrointestinal bugs, norovirus can be aerosolized, which means people can breathe it in. Hoyen recommends wearing a mask and washing hands thoroughly when caring for someone with norovirus. If you’re wondering if what you’re dealing with is norovirus, cyclosporiasis or one of the other food-borne illnesses making rounds nationwide, Hoyen said norovirus typically doesn’t last long. “It comes on quickly and usually will go quickly,” she said. The incubation period is about 12-48 hours, though symptoms can start as soon as 10 hours after exposure. People tend to start feeling better within one to three days, though they can continue to spread the disease after they’ve recovered. An infected individual can shed billions of norovirus particles, but it only takes a few particles for someone else to get sick, according to the CDC. Alcohol-based sanitizers, which includes most hand sanitizers, are generally not effective at killing norovirus, so Hoyen recommends washing your hands thoroughly if you think you may have been exposed, and before carving into any shared meals like a Thanksgiving turkey. Frequent vomiting and diarrhea as a result of a norovirus infection can lead to dehydration. Experts say children, older adults and those with other illnesses are at increased risk. Symptoms of dehydration include decreased urination, dry mouth and throat, and dizziness. In children, crying with few or no tears and unusual sleepiness or fussiness can also be signs of dehydration. Drinking plenty of fluids, including sports drinks without caffeine or alcohol, can help stave off dehydration, but experts recommend calling a doctor in cases of suspected severe dehydration. There are about 2,500 norovirus outbreaks nationwide according to the CDC, accounting for 19-21 million illnesses annually. Possible norovirus in raspberry pastries sold at nine Montreal-area bakeries - CTV News

News Brief
Quebec’s agriculture ministry, in collaboration with the Montreal-area bakery chain Boulangerie Ange, has issued a warning about possible norovirus contamination in raspberry pastries sold at nine locations across the region. The warning stems from a recall of Alasko-brand frozen raspberries, which were distributed to several Canadian establishments and may have been used in the bakery’s raspberry tart, tartlet, and Danish products. These pastries were sold between September 8 and 11, 2026, and some may have been sold online or distributed by community organizations. The ministry reported a few cases of illness linked to the recalled raspberries and advised consumers not to eat the products, even if they show no signs of spoilage. Symptoms of norovirus include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal cramps, typically appearing within 24 to 48 hours. The ministry is investigating other Quebec retailers that may have used the recalled raspberries and is considering further recalls based on findings. Consumers are encouraged to contact the ministry via email if they have questions or concerns.
