Construction of a machine learning model for dual-region typing of norovirus RdRp and VP1 based on genomic k-mer features

Authors: Yong-Qing Liu, Jun-Shan Gao, Wen-Ling Peng, Yang Jiao, Liang Xue
Original abstract (English)
Norovirus (NoV), a major global acute gastroenteritis pathogen, has high genetic diversity. Developing precise, rapid typing methods is crucial for epidemic control and vaccine development. Thus, this study develops a machine learning tool based on Norovirus genomic k-mer (length-k nucleotide fragments) features. It enables rapid, accurate dual-region typing of RdRp (RNA-dependent RNA polymerase) and VP1 (major capsid protein) without alignment or segmentation. Using k-mer frequency features with k = 11, the MurmurHash3 algorithm maps the features to a 220-dimensional space and constructs a hash feature table. A random forest model comprising 500 decision trees is then used. Each tree randomly selects 20 features during node splitting. The model is trained and evaluated using a bootstrap sampling strategy (80% training and 20% out-of-bag estimation), and prediction confidence intervals are outputted. The typing accuracy rates for the RdRp region and VP1 region reached 97.6% and 99.3%, respectively. Analysis of precision, recall, and F1-score for each genotype in the test set showed that all metrics for the 40 RdRp genotypes and 34 VP1 genotypes were ≥ 0.9. Typing accuracy across all length intervals was ≥ 0.96. This study integrates machine learning and bioinformatics to develop a novel norovirus genome analysis tool. It enables precise genotyping despite multi-region interference and reveals hidden genomic information, supporting viral classification and tracing. The feature hashing and ensemble learning strategies overcome traditional dependencies on alignment and annotation, offering a universal, efficient, scalable framework for viral genome identification.

mRNA encoding norovirus VP1 produces antigenically functional virus-like particles in vitro

Authors: Judith Mary Reyes Ballista, Courtney David, Amanda Pirrone, Estibaliz Gonzalez-Fernandez, Jiajie Wei, Zhiyun Wen
Original abstract (English)
Human norovirus is a leading cause of acute gastroenteritis worldwide across all age groups, yet no licensed vaccine is available. The major capsid protein, VP1, self-assembles into highly immunogenic virus-like particles (VLPs) that have shown promise in clinical studies. Recently, mRNA has emerged as a compelling platform for norovirus vaccination. However, whether VP1-encoding mRNA can drive authentic VLP formation in human cells relevant to the vaccination environment has remained unknown. In this study, we used multiple human cell lines, including THP-1-derived macrophage-like cells, to model the post-vaccination environment. Transfection with norovirus GII.4 VP1 mRNA or lipid nanoparticle-formulated VP1 mRNA induced both intracellular and extracellular VP1 expression. Sucrose gradient purification and electron microscopy confirmed VLP assembly. These particles retained key antigenic and functional properties, including recognition by norovirus-specific antibodies in human serum and binding to histo-blood group antigens. Collectively, our findings provide direct evidence that VP1-encoding mRNA can generate structurally and functionally relevant norovirus VLPs in physiologically relevant human cell systems. These results strengthen the mechanistic rationale for mRNA-based norovirus vaccines and support their continued development.

According to the CDC, it spreads through direct contact with sick people, contaminated food or water and contaminated surfaces. Dr. Dana Hawkinson, medical director of infection prevention and control at the University of Kansas Health System, said the virus easily contaminates shared spaces, and its tiny particles make it hard to know when you have touched them. The analysis included 23,527 patients and combined hospital data with national population census and epidemiological survey information for the burden estimate. The findings show substantial variation in positivity, complications and medical spending by age and care setting, but the hospital-tested sample does not represent every acute gastroenteritis episode in China. The overall norovirus positivity rate was 18.80%, with a 95% confidence interval of 18.30% to 19.30%. Positivity was higher among inpatients, at 26.15% (95% CI 25.28% to 27.04%), than among outpatients, at 13.78% (95% CI 13.21% to 14.36%). Children younger than five years had the highest age-specific positivity rate, reaching 30.49% (95% CI 29.56% to 31.43%). Logistic regression models were used to analyze factors associated with norovirus infection, hospitalization risk, complications and direct medical costs, but the supplied results do not identify the individual predictors or report which ones were statistically significant. Among norovirus-positive patients, 18.97% (95% CI 17.84% to 20.16%) had at least one potential complication occurring concurrently with their acute gastroenteritis. Dehydration was the most prevalent, recorded in 11.28% of positive patients (95% CI 10.39% to 12.25%), followed by convulsions in 5.52% (95% CI 4.88% to 6.23%). The investigators described the clinical pattern as age-dependent: children younger than five tended to experience more severe manifestations, while patients aged 65 or older appeared to bear higher direct medical expenditure in the hospital-based sample. These were observational findings and do not establish that age itself caused differences in infection, severity or spending. Median direct medical costs differed sharply between inpatient and outpatient care. The median cost for inpatients was CNY 4,580 per case, with an interquartile range of CNY 3,129 to CNY 6,402, compared with CNY 230 for outpatients, with an interquartile range of CNY 128 to CNY 354. Adults aged 65 or older had the highest per-capita direct medical cost: a median of CNY 5,322, with an interquartile range of CNY 2,411 to CNY 10,798. At the national level, the model estimated that children younger than five bore an aggregate medical burden 11.2 times that of people aged 60 or older. The national annual direct medical cost of norovirus infection was estimated at CNY 20.23 billion by combining the multicenter study with national population census and epidemiological survey data. The estimate is subject to the representativeness of institutions in four provinces, the selection of patients who received norovirus testing and the assumptions used to extend hospital-based observations nationwide. The study also concerned acute gastroenteritis patients seeking medical consultation, and its age, regional and seasonal comparisons should not be interpreted as population-wide trends over time. Within these limits, the results identify a heterogeneous burden: high positivity and more severe clinical manifestations among young children, higher per-patient expenditure among older adults and a substantial cost associated with hospital care. The authors concluded that age-targeted public health strategies and regionally tailored allocation of healthcare resources are needed to address these differences.
