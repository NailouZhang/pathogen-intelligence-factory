Genotype-resolved wastewater surveillance for monitoring human norovirus GII circulation

Authors: Zichao Wang, Mengdi Tan, Meng Qin, Nan Yang, Weihong Li, Lingyu Shen, Zhaomin Feng, Daitao Zhang, Peng Yang, Quanyi Wang
Original abstract (English)
Norovirus GII causes a substantial global burden of acute gastroenteritis, and genotype-resolved wastewater surveillance offers a complementary approach for monitoring community circulation beyond cases captured by routine clinical surveillance. We monitored four municipal wastewater treatment plants in Beijing, China, from October 2023 to September 2024 and compared weekly GII genotype profiles with contemporaneous sporadic-case and outbreak surveillance. Strict-weekly genotype comparisons were the primary inference and used moving-block bootstrap confidence intervals, block-sign tests, and false-discovery-rate correction; centred 3-week profiles were used for description and sensitivity analysis. Bray-Curtis dissimilarity quantified compositional concordance, and same-week and lagged Spearman correlations compared wastewater GII concentration with clinical surveillance activity. In the centred 3-week wastewater series, GII.4 had the highest mean read-based relative abundance (31.1%), followed by GII.17 (17.5%) and GII.7 (15.2%). In strict-weekly comparisons, wastewater had lower GII.17 representation than sporadic cases (-32.6 percentage points) and lower GII.7 representation than outbreaks (-14.0 percentage points). Wastewater had higher GII.3, GII.4, GII.6, and GII.9 representation than sporadic cases and higher GII.9 and GII.13 representation than outbreaks; the GII.4 and GII.7 differences were sensitive to analytical specification, and the GII.13 effect was small. Mean strict-weekly Bray-Curtis dissimilarities were 0.590-0.679 in 32 common weeks. Same-week and lagged wastewater concentration correlations with sporadic-case positivity and outbreak case counts were not significant after temporal-dependence and multiplicity correction. Genotype-resolved wastewater surveillance documented co-circulating GII genotypes and recurrent source-specific differences, while read-based relative abundance remained dependent on catchment coverage and genotype-specific analytical performance.

Systematic functional, genomic, and safety evaluation of food-derived anti-human norovirus candidate Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus YQ001

Authors: Xiang-Jun Zhan, Yuxuan Hao, Wan-Ying Zeng, Meng-Ge Sun, Ya-Qi Yang, Chenang Lyu, Ran An, Da-Peng Wang, Xiangjun Zhan, Wanying Zeng
Original abstract (English)
Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus YQ001 is a yogurt-derived strain previously reported to bind GII.4 human norovirus (HuNoV) and inhibit viral replication in zebrafish larvae. Here, its in vitro probiotic properties and preliminary safety were evaluated together with whole-genome analysis. The 2.87-Mb genome (46.67% GC; 2757 coding sequences) shared 97.66% average nucleotide identity with L. rhamnosus GG ATCC 53103. L. rhamnosus YQ001 showed acid and bile tolerance, retaining approximately 30% survival after 3 h at pH 2.0 and over 40% after 4 h in 0.2% bile salts. During sequential simulated gastrointestinal digestion, L. rhamnosus YQ001 retained 35.98% survival after the gastric phase and 45.06% after the intestinal phase. L. rhamnosus YQ001 showed over 90% auto-aggregation at 24 h, while its cell-free supernatant exhibited DPPH and ABTS radical-scavenging activities of 85.17% and 76.35%, respectively. Antibacterial activity detected by agar diffusion was abolished after pH neutralization, indicating that the observed inhibition was largely pH-dependent under the tested conditions. L. rhamnosus YQ001 was non-hemolytic and negative for gelatinase and nitroreductase activities. Overall, the phenotypic and genomic findings support the probiotic potential of L. rhamnosus YQ001, while further strain-specific functional and safety evaluation is warranted.

Original abstract (English)
Diarrhea is a major public health problem globally and remains an important cause of death and malnutrition in children under five years of age. In Indonesia, the burden of pediatric diarrhea remains substantial, yet most previous etiologic studies have focused on single pathogens, limiting understanding of the broader spectrum of enteric coinfections. A field investigation linked a norovirus GII.17 outbreak at a university in Jinhua, China, to dining at the canteen, with 215 cases reported from December 14 to 24, 2024. The attack rate was 0.48%, with the first-floor canteen in Zone A showing the highest rate at 1.14%. Most affected people experienced diarrhea and vomiting. The study authors reported that the epidemic curve was consistent with continuous exposure to a common source followed by secondary transmission, while laboratory testing found norovirus GII in 16 of 66 clinical, environmental and food-handler samples. Four positive samples were sequenced and identified as GII.17; genotypes for the other positive samples, including the sample described as the index case, were not available. The investigation combined a descriptive description of cases by time, symptoms and location with case–control and retrospective cohort studies examining dining exposures, hygiene behaviors and illness risk. The first-floor canteen in Zone A had the highest reported attack rate, at 1.14%, compared with 0.56% in Zone B and 0.42% in Zone C. The relative risk for Zone A versus Zone C was 2.71 (95% confidence interval, 1.89–3.89). Dining at the Zone A first-floor canteen was the primary food-related risk factor identified in the analysis, with an odds ratio of 18.85 (95% CI, 2.44–145.39). The reported association increased with the number of meals consumed: relative risks were 1.82 for two or three meals and 3.45 for more than three meals. Poor hand hygiene was also associated with illness, with an odds ratio of 14.60. Of the 66 tested samples, six positive samples came from canteen workers, including two from people without symptoms. Six infected workers continued working through December 20, and four were cashiers who handled takeaway packaging. Investigators also observed improper glove use and inadequate tableware disinfection in the canteen environment. The authors stated that the epidemiological pattern, together with the laboratory and environmental findings, strongly implicated foodborne transmission involving infected canteen staff, followed by person-to-person spread. The investigation did not establish a complete transmission chain, and the positive samples that were not sequenced could not be assigned a genotype.
