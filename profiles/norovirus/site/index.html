作者： Eri Hiraishi, Tsuyoshi Hayashi, Yoshiki Fujii, Hirokazu Kimura, Kosuke Murakami
摘要中文翻译人类 诺如病毒 (HuNoV) 是急性病毒性胃肠炎的主要原因。尽管可以使用人肠肠类 (HIE) 在体外复制,但由于感染和传代效率低,产生高滴度病毒实验室库存仍然具有挑战性。干扰素反应和趋化因子信号传导可能是限制 HuNoV 有效复制的关键 宿主 途径。在这里,我们评估了它们的抑制剂鲁索替尼(一种 Janus 激酶 (JAK) 1/JAK2 抑制剂)和 TAK-779(一种 C-X-C 基序趋化因子受体 3 (CXCR3)/C-C 基序趋化因子受体 (CCR) 5/CCR2 拮抗剂)对野生型空肠 HIE 中 HuNoV 复制的影响。与 DMSO 处理的对照相比,TAK-779 在感染后 48 小时和 96 小时 (hpi) 时,GII.17 HuNoV 复制分别增加了 2.3 倍和 6.0 倍。相比之下,单独使用鲁索替尼并不影响病毒复制。值得注意的是,治疗 与这些化合物同时使用,在 48 hpi 和 96 hpi 时分别进一步增强了 GII.17 复制 6.0 倍和 10.7 倍,从而实现了连续传代。这些发现意味着同时抑制这些途径可能有利于 GII.17 HuNoV 复制。

Treatment With Ruxolitinib and TAK-779 Enhances GII.17 Human Norovirus Replication and Enables Serial Passaging in Human Intestinal Enteroids.

Authors: Eri Hiraishi, Tsuyoshi Hayashi, Yoshiki Fujii, Hirokazu Kimura, Kosuke Murakami
Original abstract (English)
Human norovirus (HuNoV) is a major cause of acute viral gastroenteritis. Although it can be replicated in vitro using human intestinal enteroids (HIEs), generating high-titer viral laboratory stocks remains challenging due to low infection and passage efficiencies. Interferon response and chemokine signaling may be key host pathways that restrict efficient HuNoV replication. Here, we evaluated the effect of their inhibitors, ruxolitinib-a Janus kinase (JAK) 1/JAK2 inhibitor-and TAK-779-a C-X-C motif chemokine receptor 3 (CXCR3)/C-C motif chemokine receptor (CCR) 5/CCR2 antagonist-on HuNoV replication in wild-type jejunal HIEs. TAK-779 increased GII.17 HuNoV replication by 2.3- and 6.0-fold at 48 and 96 h postinfection (hpi), respectively, compared with the DMSO-treated control. In contrast, ruxolitinib alone did not affect viral replication. Notably, simultaneous treatment with these compounds further enhanced GII.17 replication by 6.0- and 10.7-fold at 48 and 96 hpi, respectively, which enabled serial passaging. These findings imply that the simultaneous suppression of these pathways potentially benefits GII.17 HuNoV replication.

Modeling and forecasting oyster norovirus outbreaks of long-range dependence on antecedent marine environmental conditions

Authors: Naresh Suwal, Zhiqiang Deng
Original abstract (English)
This study presents three artificial intelligence-based models - XGBoost, Random Forest (RF), and Deep Artificial Neural Network (DANN) - with 2-day lead time for forecasting broad-scale oyster norovirus outbreaks. Among them, the XGBoost model performs best and is characterized by unique features: (1) the model was constructed and tested with epidemiological and environmental data collected from three distinct coastal countries representing broad trends in oyster norovirus outbreaks; (2) model input variables include five important environmental predictors (along with their time-lags ranging from 2-30 days), identified by using Pearson correlation and Gini index, including solar radiation, water temperature, gage height, precipitation, and salinity plus GPS coordinates, which affect various processes governing the sources and sinks of oyster norovirus; and (3) the model output performance is characterized by a high accuracy, demonstrating the strongest overall prediction performance. This study identifies solar radiation and water temperature as the two most important predictors of oyster norovirus outbreaks. Another major finding is the strong long-range dependence of outbreaks on antecedent marine environmental conditions up to 30 days before. These novel features of the XGBoost model provide an effective early-warning framework that can support proactive management of oyster norovirus outbreaks.

Question and background
This study aimed to develop an early-warning framework for forecasting oyster norovirus outbreaks by leveraging environmental predictors. The research addresses the challenge of understanding and predicting the spread of norovirus in oyster populations, which is critical for public health and food safety.
Design and population

These models were constructed and tested using epidemiological and environmental data from three distinct coastal countries.
Core methods
The XGBoost model was selected for its superior performance, incorporating five environmental predictors (solar radiation, water temperature, gage height, precipitation, and salinity) along with their time-lags ranging from 2-30 days. These variables were identified using Pearson correlation and Gini index analyses.

Interpretation and novelty
The novel features of the XGBoost model, including its use of environmental predictors and time-lagged data, provide an effective early-warning framework for managing oyster norovirus outbreaks.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings can inform proactive management strategies for oyster norovirus outbreaks, enhancing surveillance and risk assessment efforts in coastal regions.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study's evidence is based on data from three coastal countries, which may limit generalisability. However, the high accuracy of the XGBoost model suggests strong predictive performance.

Ontogeny of human intestinal glycans and its impact on susceptibility to enteric viral infections in children up to 5 years of age: a scoping review.

Authors: María Eugenia Galeano Dinatale
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Background: Human intestinal epithelial maturation involves cellular differentiation, structural maturation, and functional development. Within this context, intestinal glycans, including: histo-blood group antigens (HBGAs), sialic acids, and mucins, play key roles in host-pathogen interactions (serving as mucosal viral receptors, microbiota modulators) and innate mucosal immunity. Aim: To evaluate evidence on human intestinal mucosa development, glycan expression from the fetal stage through childhood, and susceptibility to enteric viral infections from the fetal stage through childhood. Materials and Methods: Following the PRISMA-ScR guidelines, a scoping review was conducted across Pubmed, Scopus, Cochrane Library, Embase, and Scielo for studies between 2010 and 2025. Information was extracted from original articles, observational studies, cohort studies, outbreak investigations, case-control studies, and experimental studies. Excel spreadsheets were used for article accounting. Results: Analysis of 24 studies identified an ontogenetic pattern of viral susceptibility. An early stage (<6 months) characterized by predominantly asymptomatic infections and a strong maternal influence is followed by a critical window of clinical vulnerability between 6 and 18 months. Coinciding with weaning and waning passive protection, this period exhibits peak strong maternal influence and asymptomatic infections caused by secretor-dependent dominant genotypes (Norovirus GII.4, Rotavirus P[8]). Furthermore, glycocalyx maturation modulates live vaccine performance. Conclusions: Enteric virus susceptibility is a dynamic developmental process driven by transition from passive maternal protection to endogenous mucosal glycosylation. Clinical disease depends on specific developmental time windows rather than fixed genetic status, highlighting the need for age-tailored immunization strategies beyond static binary secretor/non-secretor models. Key words: Intestinal glycans, HBGAs, enteric viral infections, human development, mucosa, infants.

Pathogens at the Pre- and Post-Harvest Interface: Food Safety Risks and Controls

Authors: Muhammad Tauseef Tariq Kisana, Imran Ul Haq Imran Ul Haq
Original abstract (English)
The pre- and post-harvest interface represents a critical control zone in the food supply chain where microbial contamination can compromise the safety, quality, and shelf-life. Although numerous studies have examined pre-harvest contamination and post-harvest disease management separately, limited reviews have comprehensively addressed the critical interface linking these stages. This review synthesizes current knowledge on contamination pathways, major pathogens, monitoring approaches, and integrated control strategies associated with fresh fruits and vegetables. Relevant peer-reviewed literature was critically evaluated to provide an overview of food safety risks and management options across the production chain. Fresh produce may become contaminated through soil, irrigation water, organic amendments, wildlife, harvesting equipment, storage environments, and human handling. These pathways facilitate the introduction and dissemination of bacterial, fungal, and viral pathogens. Major bacterial hazards include Salmonella enterica, Escherichia coli O157, and Listeria monocytogenes, while fungal pathogens such as Aspergillus, Fusarium, and Penicillium species contribute to spoilage and mycotoxin production. Viral pathogens, particularly norovirus and hepatitis A virus, are also important causes of produce-associated outbreaks. Environmental stressors, including drought, heavy rainfall, temperature fluctuations, and crop injuries, further increase contamination risks. The review highlights integrated management strategies, including Good Agricultural Practices (GAPs), sanitation programs, rapid cooling, biological control agents, and emerging decontamination technologies. Among these, ozone and cold plasma show strong antimicrobial potential, although their large-scale adoption is constrained by economic and technical limitations. Environmental Monitoring Programs (EMPs), molecular detection tools such as polymerase chain reaction (PCR), loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP), and next-generation sequencing (NGS), together with blockchain-based traceability systems, support rapid pathogen detection and outbreak prevention. Integrating the Food Safety Objective (FSO) framework with One Health principles provides a sustainable approach for reducing contamination risks throughout the supply chain. Future research should focus on improving the cost-effectiveness, scalability, and practical implementation of emerging monitoring and intervention technologies. Keywords: Pre-harvest, post-harvest, food safety, fresh produce, microbial contamination, mycotoxins, biofilms, traceability, good agricultural practices, environmental monitoring programs.

Scope and question
The review examines human norovirus as a food safety hazard at the pre- and post-harvest interface of fresh produce, focusing on contamination pathways, monitoring approaches, and integrated control strategies that specifically address norovirus transmission and infection risks in humans.
Evidence base and method
The review is a narrative synthesis of peer-reviewed literature critically evaluated to provide an overview of food safety risks and management options across the production chain, with no systematic search, pooled estimates, or meta-analytic methods reported.
Consensus and conclusions
Fresh produce may become contaminated with human norovirus through soil, irrigation water, organic amendments, wildlife, harvesting equipment, storage environments, and human handling, facilitating introduction and dissemination of viral pathogens.
Controversies and gaps
The review does not report specific evidence on norovirus detection methods, prevalence estimates, or comparative effectiveness of interventions in fresh produce systems, leaving gaps in validated monitoring tools and scalable control strategies.
Research and practice implications
Future research should prioritize the development and validation of cost-effective, scalable norovirus-specific detection tools and intervention technologies for pre- and post-harvest produce environments to reduce contamination risks.
