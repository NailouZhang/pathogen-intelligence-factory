作者： David Simons
摘要中文翻译
查看研究七要素
问题与背景
尼日利亚报告的拉沙热病例死亡率(CFR)在2025年第23周从18.9%上升至2026年的25.0%,引发了对临床护理失败的担忧。监测伪影也可能改变CFR,因此需要一个分析框架来识别和评估候选机制。
设计与对象
这项生态学研究从2017年至2026年的474份尼日利亚疾病控制中心(Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention)情况报告中提取了每周各州的病例数。根据预先指定的计划,比较了各年份在相位匹配点的差异,将年度CFR变化分解为州内和州间成分,并计算了临界点确认偏移。
核心方法
事后比较了每周新发病例和累计报告总数。2026年CFR的上升被归因于在每周报告之外添加的死亡病例,并利用发布的数据评估了晚期死亡报告对CFR的影响。
主要结果
On weekly incident counts, 2026 did not differ from 2025 at the phase-matched midpoint (+0.3 percentage points, 95% CI -5.0 to 7.6); within-state (-1.1, 95% CI -4.7 to 3.0) and between-state (+0.5, -2.0 to 2.9) components spanned zero. The published cumulative CFR was 5.4 to 6.2 percentage points higher because 70 deaths were added retrospectively by week 36 outside weekly reports.
解释与创新
2026年CFR的上升源于在每周报告之外添加的死亡病例,而发布的数据无法将其归因于护理水平。该研究强调了报告透明度的重要性,以及监测伪影可能对CFR产生影响的潜在风险。
科研与公卫意义
这些发现提醒人们,在不考虑检测数量、晚期死亡报告和尸检采样数据的情况下,不应将人兽共患病的CFR作为临床结果指标。它们为改进监测实践以准确评估疾病负担提供了依据。
局限与证据强度
该研究的局限性在于依赖于已发布的数据,这些数据可能在报告透明度方面存在不足。证据强度中等,因为CFR的变化被归因于监测伪影,而非临床护理的改善。

Interpreting changes in reported case fatality in endemic zoonoses: an analytical framework and ecological analysis of Lassa fever surveillance in Nigeria, 2017-26

Authors: David Simons
Original abstract (English)
Background: Nigeria's reported Lassa fever case fatality ratio (CFR) rose from 18.9% at week 23 of 2025 to 25.0% in 2026, prompting concerns over failing clinical care. Because surveillance artefacts can also alter CFRs, candidate mechanisms that published data can resolve were identified and evaluated. This ecological study extracted weekly state-level counts from 474 Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention situation reports (2017-2026). Per a pre-specified plan, years were compared at phase-matched points, year-on-year CFR changes were decomposed into within-state and between-state components, and tipping-point ascertainment shifts calculated. Weekly incident and cumulative published totals were compared post hoc. Of 11 candidate mechanisms, 2 could be evaluated fully and 5 in part. On weekly incident counts, 2026 did not differ from 2025 at the phase-matched midpoint (+0.3 percentage points, 95% CI -5.0 to 7.6); within-state (-1.1, 95% CI -4.7 to 3.0) and between-state (+0.5, -2.0 to 2.9) components spanned zero. Conversely, the published cumulative CFR was 5.4 to 6.2 percentage points higher because 70 deaths were added retrospectively by week 36 outside weekly reports. Reconciling cumulative figures would require 279 missed survivors (6% of suspected cases not confirmed). The 2026 CFR rise came from deaths added to cumulative totals outside the weekly counts, and published data cannot attribute it to care. Endemic zoonotic CFRs cannot serve as clinical outcome measures unless testing volumes, deaths added late, and post-mortem sampling are published alongside case counts. Monoclonal antibodies raised against the Junín virus glycoprotein show broad reactivity and provide protection in vivo

Authors: Garazi Pena Alzua, Sharon Jan, Fatima Amanat, Grecia A. Sandoval, Christopher A. Sumner, Stephen Won, Anna L. Lara, Anupama Sinha, Brooke N. Harmon, Elizabeth B. Allmon
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Argentine hemorrhagic fever (AHF) is a disease caused by the Junín virus (JUNV) that can be fatal in 15%–30% of untreated cases. Although JUNV is the only arenavirus for which efficient therapy and vaccination have been established, its administration is limited. In this study, hybridoma technology was used to generate 30 mouse monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) against the glycoprotein complex (GPC) of JUNV. Of these 30 mAbs, 26 showed strong cross-binding to GPCs from various arenaviruses. In addition, nine mAbs demonstrated potent neutralizing activity in vitro with a 50% inhibitory concentration of less than 1 μg/mL against a replication-competent chimeric vesicular stomatitis virus expressing JUNV GPC (rVSV-JUNV), as well as the live attenuated JUNV vaccine strain Candid#1. Furthermore, when tested in vivo using a challenge model of rVSV-JUNV in Stat2 −/− mice, five select mAbs significantly reduced viral loads in the spleen when administered prophylactically. Humanized versions of JUNV-1A2, JUNV-1D5, and JUNV-5B12 mAbs, which showed protection in vivo in the Stat2 −/− surrogate model, were produced. These antibodies were evaluated in a guinea pig challenge model of AHF and showed protective efficacy. Escape experiments with these humanized antibodies, as well as previously characterized antibodies, GB03 and JUN1, using rVSV-JUNV resulted in the acquisition of amino acid changes in the GP1 part of the GPC, which directly contacts host receptor transferrin receptor 1, indicating these sites could be key for future therapies. In summary, we generated mAbs that may be valuable for antibody-guided vaccine design and therapeutic development. IMPORTANCE Junín virus (JUNV) is a highly pathogenic virus that causes Argentine hemorrhagic fever (AHF) and is classified as a category A pathogen due to its potential for aerosol transmission and the significant number of annual infections and deaths associated with it. Although effective treatments exist, such as convalescent immune plasma, their availability is limited, and they are ineffective when given after the first week of symptoms. This study is significant because it focuses on developing therapeutic monoclonal antibodies against JUNV with strong in vitro neutralizing activity and protection in both a surrogate mouse model and a guinea pig challenge model of AHF. This has led to promising antibody candidates that could be used as therapeutics to combat ongoing outbreaks.

The study aimed to develop therapeutic monoclonal antibodies against Junín virus (JUNV) to address the limited availability of effective treatments for Argentine hemorrhagic fever (AHF), a highly pathogenic disease caused by JUNV.

原发性拉沙病毒 储存宿主(多乳鼠小鼠)在非洲的预测空间分布

Predicted spatial distribution of the primary Lassa virus reservoir, the multimammate mouse, in Africa
作者： Adeola Mariam Lateef, Marlon E. Cobos, Victor Ibukun Agbajelola, Ram K. Raghavan
摘要中文翻译目的 本研究预测多乳鼠(Mastomys natalensis)的潜在空间分布,拉沙病毒是西非主要公共卫生问题的主要 宿主。方法 利用全球生物多样性信息设施中的 M. natalensis 发生记录构建相关最大熵模型,使用分辨率约为 1 km 的长期生物气候变量作为预测因子。根据统计显着性、预测能力以及模型拟合和复杂性对候选模型进行评估并选择最佳模型。模型被投射到非洲大陆。结果 支持最好的模型的中值预测表明,撒哈拉以南非洲的广泛地区具有中度至高度适宜性,适宜性集中在西非、中非和东非的热带和亚热带带。干旱地区的适宜性急剧下降,特别是撒哈拉沙漠和非洲之角的部分地区。在该研究的重点区域尼日利亚,预测的适宜性在南部和中部地区普遍较高,而与萨赫勒相邻的北部地区则逐渐下降。拉各斯、奥约、江户、卡诺和联邦首都地区的地方预测揭示了适宜性的空间异质性,与影响啮齿动物栖息地条件的温度和降水的基本气候梯度一致。结论这些发现为优先考虑啮齿动物 监测 和支持有针对性的 拉沙热 准备和控制策略提供了一个空间框架。

Advancing preparedness for future epidemics - IAVI

News Brief
IAVI, a global nonprofit biomedical research organization, is advancing preparedness for future epidemics by developing vaccines and antibodies for HIV, tuberculosis, and emerging infectious diseases. The organization focuses on diseases like Lassa fever, which causes 300,000 to 500,000 cases and 5,000 related deaths annually, and Marburg virus disease, which has a case fatality rate of up to 88%. IAVI is also working on vaccines for Sudan virus and Bundibugyo virus, which have fatality rates of 41-100% and 30-50%, respectively. No licensed vaccines exist for these diseases. In 2024, IAVI’s Lassa vaccine candidate became the first to enter Phase 2 clinical trials in three West African countries. The WHO has prioritized the development of the Bundibugyo virus vaccine during an outbreak in May 2026. The organization’s efforts include research, clinical trials, and community engagement to support vaccine access and public health solutions.

About us Our approach We take a full-circle approach to product development, from the grassroots level to the lab to legislative bodies. Mission To translate scientific discoveries into affordable, globally accessible public health solutions. Our health areas - HIV - Tuberculosis - Lassa virus - Marburg virus - Sudan virus - Bundibugyo virus HIV The challenge HIV/AIDS remains one of the deadliest infectious diseases, with 1.4 million people acquiring HIV in 2024. Given the complexities of HIV, traditional approaches to vaccine development have so far failed to result in a preventive vaccine. However, a vaccine is still needed to bring a true end to the HIV pandemic. Source: UNAIDS Our solution IAVI scientists and our collaborators are developing next-generation HIV vaccines to address the challenges of HIV vaccine design. Together, we have pioneered promising new vaccination strategies and the discovery of broadly neutralizing antibodies (bnAbs) against HIV. IAVI and our partners are developing HIV bnAbs to prevent HIV acquisition in infants. Tuberculosis The challenge Tuberculosis (TB) is the world’s leading infectious disease killer. In 2024, 10.7 million people fell ill with TB and 1.23 million people died of TB disease. The only available TB vaccine is the century-old Bacille Calmette-Guérin, or BCG. While this vaccine has efficacy in protecting against severe TB disease in infants and young children, it is largely ineffective in adolescents and adults, among whom most transmission and disease occurs. Source: WHO Our solution IAVI works across our global hubs with a diverse network of partners to advance promising TB vaccine candidates from discovery through clinical trials, and eventually, to post-licensure access. Our work extends to policy and advocacy initiatives that support TB vaccine development and access in regions where new vaccines are needed most. Lassa virus The challenge Lassa virus is an arenavirus that causes Lassa fever, an acute viral hemorrhagic illness endemic to West Africa that causes significant annual outbreaks of disease. Lassa fever is difficult to diagnose, and surveillance data is limited. Current estimates range from 300,000 to 500,000 cases and 5,000 related deaths each year. No licensed vaccines for Lassa fever exist. Source: WHO Our solution IAVI is developing a single-dose vaccine candidate for Lassa that is based on a recombinant vesicular stomatitis virus (rVSV) vector. This technology is similar to that underlying an approved Ebola Zaire virus vaccine. In 2024, IAVI’s Lassa vaccine candidate became the first Lassa vaccine to enter Phase 2 clinical trials, launched in three West African countries. This trial builds on a Phase 1 clinical trial that showed the candidate vaccine was well tolerated and immunogenic over a wide dose range. IAVI also conducts Lassa epidemiological studies, modeling, and community engagement to support future vaccine access. Marburg virus The challenge Marburg virus is a filovirus and the causative agent of Marburg virus disease (MVD), which has a case fatality rate of up to 88%. Marburg virus has the capacity to cause deadly outbreaks and is a potential bioterror threat. No vaccines or antiviral treatments are approved for MVD. A Marburg vaccine is urgently needed to respond to future outbreaks. Source: WHO Our solution IAVI is developing a vaccine candidate for Marburg that is based on a recombinant vesicular stomatitis virus (rVSV) vector. This technology is similar to that underlying an approved Ebola Zaire virus vaccine. Preclinical data demonstrates that IAVI’s Marburg vaccine candidate is highly protective in an animal model with one dose. Sudan virus The challenge Sudan virus (SUDV) is a filovirus and causes outbreaks of Ebola disease, most recently in 2025 in Uganda. Case fatality rates of Ebola disease caused by SUDV have varied from 41% to 100% in past outbreaks. The licensed vaccine for Ebola Zaire virus does not provide cross protection against the Sudan strain. A SUDV vaccine is urgently needed to prevent and respond to future outbreaks. Source: WHO Our solution IAVI is developing a vaccine candidate for SUDV that is based on a recombinant vesicular stomatitis virus (rVSV) vector similar to the technology underlying an approved Ebola Zaire virus vaccine. During an Ebola outbreak starting in January 2025 in Uganda, the WHO prioritized IAVI’s SUDV vaccine candidate for a ring vaccination trial and delivered the first vaccinations just four days after the outbreak was declared as part of a comprehensive public health response. Bundibugyo virus The challenge Bundibugyo virus (BDBV) is a filovirus that has caused only three known outbreaks to date. The outbreak that began in Democratic Republic of Congo in May 2026 quickly turned into an epidemic and was determined by the WHO to be a Public Health Emergency of International Concern. There are no licensed vaccines or specific treatments for BDBV. The case fatality rate of BDBV disease ranges between 30-50%. Our solution At the outset of the outbreak in May 2026, IAVI quickly brought a BDBV vaccine candidate in house. It is based on the same recombinant vesicular stomatitis virus (rVSV) vector as our other emerging infectious diseases vaccine candidates as well as the approved Ebola Zaire virus vaccine. The WHO has prioritized development of this vaccine during the epidemic as the most promising candidate in the pipeline. Original source text (English)
Katrín Jakobsdóttir, former Prime Minister of Iceland, moderates a session on One Health and pandemic prevention at the World Health Summit, Berlin, 12 October 2026. Six years after COVID-19, with Ebola Bundibugyo virus still raging in eastern Congo and the pandemic treaty stalled, a packed World Health Summit workshop asked why the world keeps paying for fire engines instead of fire prevention. BERLIN – By the time Katrín Jakobsdóttir opened the session, people were standing two deep along the back wall. The former Icelandic Prime Minister, now chair of the Pan-European Commission on Climate and Health, joked that the panel would have to keep to time or the room would run out of oxygen. Then she named the problem that has dogged pandemic prevention for decades: its success “is measured by the crisis that never happened”. Nobody gets much credit for an outbreak that never occurs, so budgets for averting crises remain equally slim. That paradox ran through Monday’s workshop at the World Health Summit , Preventing the Next Pandemic , co-hosted by Germany’s Federal Ministry of Health and the animal welfare organisation FOUR PAWS . Pathogen risks prevented by good management get little attention. Here a community health volunteer visits a Kenyan farmer to support anthrax vaccination. A threat that is accelerating The science on where pandemics come from is no longer in dispute. About 60% of known human infectious diseases, and 75% of emerging ones, pass to us from animals, according to UNEP . The UN biodiversity panel IPBES estimates that some 1.7 million undiscovered viruses circulate in mammals and birds, and up to 850,000 of them could infect people. A 2021 study in PNAS put the yearly chance of a COVID-scale pandemic at about 2%, which works out to a 38% chance of living through one. The authors warned that the risk could double in coming decades as land clearance and climate change push people and wildlife closer together. This year has tested that warning. The Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak in eastern DRC, caused by a strain with no licensed vaccine or treatment , had reached 8,116 confirmed cases and 3,924 deaths by late September, making it the second-largest Ebola epidemic on record. An Andes hantavirus cluster killed passengers on a cruise from Ushuaia. New World screwworm, eradicated from Mexico in the 1990s, has been back since November 2024. “Since the COVID-19 emergency period, the world has been responding to a health emergency around 70% of the time,” said Helen Clark, co-chair of the Independent Panel for Pandemic Preparedness and Response . “Is this sustainable, economically, politically, morally?” On Bundibugyo, responders had no community-level test and no dedicated vaccine or antiviral. But her harder question went further back: “Could it have been prevented altogether? What would it have taken to stop it? “ Gambian pouched rat – rodents are a common animal reservoir for monkeypox The opportunity reflected in the numbers The economics point firmly towards prevention. A 2022 analysis in Science Advances put the cost of primary prevention, including less deforestation, tighter regulation of the wildlife trade and surveillance at spillover hotspots, at about $20 billion a year . That is less than 5% of the value of lives lost to emerging zoonoses, at least $350 billion annually, and under 10% of the direct economic losses of $212 billion. IPBES, the Intergovernmental Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services, estimates that prevention costs about 100 times less than response. COVID-19 alone cost an estimated $8 to 16 trillion. Some money is starting to flow. In February, the World Bank-hosted Pandemic Fund awarded US$499.6 million to 20 projects, half of them with “One Health” in their titles, and its first three rounds now reach 128 countries. Clark said that virtually every application to the fund now carries a One Health dimension. The WHO Pandemic Agreement , adopted in May 2025, is the first international health treaty to write One Health into its text. But the treaty is stuck. It cannot open for signature until governments settle its annex on pathogen access and benefit sharing (PABS). “There are ways through this,” Clark said, but it will take “some political will and some heavy lifting. In the end, no one gets everything they want from a negotiation.” For the host government, Parliamentary State Secretary Georg Kippels said a PABS deal would come only if every party was willing to compromise. “Prevention is the most straightforward solution we have... It protects people before anyone falls ill, and it costs far less than responding once an outbreak has been detected or even spread.” What works on the ground (L-R) Mahbubur Rahman of One Health Bangladesh, Thainá Landim de Barros of FOUR PAWS and Frédéric Singa of WWF Central African Republic. Bangladesh offers a working model. Mahbubur Rahman, an epidemiologist at the Institute of Epidemiology, Disease Control and Research , described how One Health Bangladesh began in 2008 as a civil society response to avian influenza. It produced a national strategic framework in 2012, and by 2016, its activities were integrated with those of government, including an inter-ministerial steering committee, a technical advisory committee and a secretariat. A new 2025–2030 framework extends the approach below the national level and adds environment and ecosystem health. Money is still the weak link, because no mechanism lets one ministry fund another. Yet joint working paid off: after two rejections, the country’s One Health surveillance and early-warning project won US$25 million from the Pandemic Fund on the third attempt. Asked whether governments actually listen to scientists, Maxine Whittaker of the One Health High-Level Expert Panel answered: “Yes, maybe, no.” Scientists are one voice among party positions, public opinion and the hourly news cycle, she said, and evidence lands only when it is framed with its economic and social costs. On data she was blunter: when governments pay to collect data and then do not share it, “it’s actually unethical. If we’ve invested government money, donor money, and don’t actually get them to work together. We’re actually doing more harm than good.” Restricting human access to wildlife products is not a solution Sampling dead animals in the Congo basin for zoonotic diseases that could be transmitted to humans. In the Central African Republic, WWF veterinarian Frédéric Singa found that 78% of forest-edge residents routinely go into the forest to capture wildlife or collect wildlife products and 96% eat what they capture or gather. “Restricting the access is not a solution,” he said. His team instead watches for unusual wildlife deaths and tests samples in a German-funded field laboratory. That picks up pathogens like anthrax in animal carcasses, as well as Mpox in people, before they can spread. And the key drivers of the leap of wildlife pathogens into human communities are often modern practices, such as the deforestation of land cleared for pasture and feed stocks; the trade and transport of animals; and crowded intensive farm production where close quarters foster pathogen transmission and stress weakens immunity, said Thainá Landim de Barros, a veterinarian for the international wildlife protection group FOUR PAWS . “We cannot start acting when the problem reaches us,” she added. “That’s too late.” Intensive livestock production creates a hothouse for pathogen transmission among animals and to humans. Trust is the missing infrastructure NCDC anthropologist Obinna Jude Eze, known in his Lassa fever field community as “the doctor of rats”. The most memorable contribution came from the floor. Obinna Jude Eze is an anthropologist with the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention . Eze spent nine months living in a community with high Lassa Fever transmission under the auspices of the research initiative COPE , of the German-based Robert Koch Institute. Because his questions always came back to rodents, villagers called him “the doctor of rats”. But the cumulative sense of familiarity and trust that he managed to build helped identify community champions and local facilitators for a community-based Lassa Fever project. “If we want communities to trust One Health,” Eze said, “we also need to show that One Health trusts communities.” What has to change Helen Clark (centre) and Germany’s Parliamentary State Secretary for Health Georg Kippels (second from left) in the front row before the opening remarks. One Health has the arguments and its importance is increasingly being recognized. It is written into a treaty and into most Pandemic Fund proposals. What One Health has not yet won is a budget line. Three shifts would change that, workshp participants agreed. Finish the Pandemic Agreement. Member state ratification of the WHO Pandemic Agreement cannot begin until negotiations over the Annex for Pathogen Access and Benefit Sharing is completed. So the agreement remains a promise without force, and every month of delay is a month in which the next pathogen spillover event from wildlife into human communities could go unchecked. Measure what matters. The Sustainable Development Goals carry no true One Health indicator, and finance ministries do not fund what they cannot measure. The post-2030 framework is the chance to fix that. Treat prevention as an investment, not a cost. Investments of about $20 billion a year against more than $500 billion in annual economic losses to health, trade and food systems is a return that any finance ministry would accept in any other sector. But on the accounting score, Jakobsdóttir said, a change of mindset is the hardest part. Until governments find a way to value the outbreak that never happened, they will keep paying many times over for the ones that do. NLRP3炎症小体对于宿主抵御微生物感染起着核心作用,但同时也参与了炎症性疾病的发生。NLRP3的功能完全依赖两种信号:一种是“预激活信号”(priming signal),赋予NLRP3活性;另一种是“激活信号”(activation signal),触发炎症小体组装及下游caspase-1激活。预激活信号涉及NLRP3的转录上调,以及多种调节其稳定性、亚细胞定位和蛋白质-蛋白质互作的翻译后修饰。这种多层次的调控机制既能防止炎症小体激活时机不当,又能确保在预激活信号和激活信号同时存在时,炎症小体能够迅速组装。来自哈佛医学院和波士顿儿童医院的Ivan Zanoni团队在Trends in Immunology发表Review综述,重点探讨了预激活信号的复杂性,并从批判性角度分析、讨论多种翻译后修饰如何协同作用以预激活NLRP3,从而在健康与疾病状态下调控其活性。 2型固有免疫感知的作用机制 2型免疫可抵御蠕虫和毒素,但其失调会导致过敏性疾病。该过程始于屏障组织,上皮细胞检测到异常并释放警报素,从而激活2型固有淋巴细胞,并使树突状细胞做好准备以启动2型辅助性T细胞。但2型固有免疫感知的机制框架仍不完善,关键研究空白包括许多刺激物中的核心免疫刺激成分及其在宿主中的识别途径。来自清华大学的徐墨团队在Trends in Immunology发表Review综述,回顾了近期研究进展,包括簇细胞的化学感觉、膜孔形成作为共同触发因素,以及蛋白质晶体和蛋白酶在过敏性炎症中的作用,凸显了新兴的分子原理。与通过保守分子模式识别细菌、病毒和真菌不同,2型免疫通常涉及对扰动和应激信号的感知。 细胞内寄生虫的代谢传感器:Vγ9Vδ2 T细胞 Vγ9Vδ2(V gamma 9 V delta 2)T细胞是人体外周血中占主导地位的γδ T细胞亚群,能够以独立于主要组织相容性复合体分子的方式,特异性识别非肽类磷酸抗原(nonpeptidic phosphoantigens)。这种感知机制依赖于细胞内磷酸抗原的积累,触发跨膜受体BTN3A1和BTN2A1之间的异源合作,从而激活γδ T细胞受体。来自加州大学戴维斯分校(University of California, Davis)的Jeroen P.J. Saeij团队在Trends in Parasitology发表Review综述,总结了有关 Vγ9Vδ2 T细胞免疫的现有发现,重点探讨其对恶性疟原虫(Plasmodium falciparum)和刚地弓形虫(Toxoplasma gondii)的应答。研究人员探讨了这些细胞如何识别源自寄生虫或宿主的磷酸抗原,从而驱动快速细胞毒性作用和干扰素γ产生。理解这些感知机制将带来新的洞见,从而利用γδ T细胞进行抗寄生虫治疗和疫苗设计。 γδ T细胞在锥虫感染中的双重作用 来自曼彻斯特大学的Matthew C. Dutra团队在Trends in Parasitology发表Review综述,阐述了布氏锥虫(Trypanosoma brucei)和克鲁兹锥虫(Trypanosoma cruzi),作为同属的相关寄生虫,如何引发截然不同的致命疾病,以及γδ T细胞对这些疾病结局的影响。研究人员总结了γδ T细胞对布氏锥虫和克鲁兹锥虫免疫应答的现有研究成果,强调了其共性及寄生虫特异性特征。研究人员重点阐述了 γδ T 细胞的细胞毒性、调节程序,以及在血液、淋巴器官、肝脏、肠道、心脏和皮肤中的组织驻留,如何影响寄生虫的早期控制、慢性持续感染和免疫病理。 最后,研究人员探讨了亟待填补的研究空白,以及这些见解如何为新的干预措施提供依据,包括γδ功能调节、γδ靶向疫苗策略,以及用于查加斯病和人类非洲锥虫病宿主导向治疗的基于γδ的生物标志物。 弓形虫感染过程中的细胞免疫应答:解析多样化的群体免疫变异 弓形虫(Toxoplasma gondii)是一种广泛分布的顶复门寄生虫(Apicomplexan parasite),全球约三分之一的人口都曾感染过该寄生虫。弓形虫病的发病过程和临床特征与人群的免疫状态密切相关。虽然大多数免疫功能正常者在初次感染后无症状,但免疫功能低下患者可能会出现一系列严重的临床症状和并发症。对于孕妇而言,若在妊娠期间发生初次感染,胎儿面临出现严重临床症状的风险。来自宁波大学的张吉丽团队在Trends in Parasitology发表Review综述,旨在阐明不同免疫状态个体的免疫互作模式特征,并为制定更有效、更精准的弓形虫病治疗策略提供理论依据。这些研究成果有助于对不同人群实施靶向精准的预防和控制措施,从而有效保障公共卫生安全。 非伤寒沙门菌对生发中心B细胞的破坏 鼠伤寒沙门菌(Salmonella enterica serovar Typhimurium, STm)是全球公共卫生的一大负担。在撒哈拉以南非洲地区地方性流行的菌株会导致易感人群患上侵袭性非伤寒沙门菌病(invasive non-typhoidal salmonellosis, iNTS),危及生命。针对iNTS样小鼠模型的研究表明,STm会诱导生发中心发生严重紊乱,从而阻碍高亲和力、长效抗体及记忆B细胞的形成,既影响新生的生发中心反应,也影响既有的生发中心反应。脂多糖和STm特异性III型分泌效应因子会导致生发中心崩溃,但决定性的细菌-宿主相互作用尚不明确。尽管STm可诱导产生保护性抗体的滤泡外B细胞应答,但这些抗体的寿命仍不明确。来自瑞士伯尔尼大学(University of Bern)的Francisco Osorio-Barrios和Siegfried Hapfelmeier团队在Trends in Immunology发表Opinion观点并提出,鉴于目前尚无获批用于预防iNTS的人用疫苗,建议疫苗研发策略应考虑如何保护生发中心完整性,并在临床前试验中将生发中心相关参数纳入终点指标。 在黏膜皮肤屏障中平衡IL-17介导的保护作用与IFN-γ驱动的病理过程 黏膜和皮肤表面依赖于产生IL-17的淋巴细胞来维持屏障完整性,并防止细菌和真菌过度增殖。因此,遗传性或药理性的IL-17缺乏会导致黏膜和皮肤感染。干扰素(IFN)-γ介导宿主对细胞内病原体的防御, 但过度的黏膜IFN-γ活性却会损害上皮完整性并促进感染,比如APECED 相关口腔念珠菌病(autoimmune polyendocrinopathy-candidiasis-ectodermal dystrophy–associated oral candidiasis),即使IL-17反应正常也是如此。有关黏膜皮肤组织的细菌、真菌和原虫感染研究,正在逐渐揭示IFN-γ驱动病理机制。综合来看,这些发现支持这样一种模型:IL-17增强屏障抵抗力,而失控的IFN-γ则会破坏屏障。来自美国国立卫生研究院的Michail S. Requiring only a handheld reader and a portable heater, it has the potential to bring rapid testing to low-income regions where low accessibility and availability often hinder time-sensitive diagnoses and treatments. A team of researchers in the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign’s Grainger College of Engineering has developed a testing protocol for detecting viral RNA directly in whole blood with no laboratory purification. As reported in the journal Science Advances , the protocol uses heat to dry the blood sample before amplifying the preserved viral RNA and detecting it through fluorescence. The entire protocol can be completed on site in a matter of hours, promising a clear advantage in treating diseases such as Zika virus and hepatitis C where early detection is vital to effective treatment. “Medical infrastructure is currently organized around standard-of-care testing, where samples are collected then transported to a laboratory where time-consuming processing is performed using sophisticated equipment,” said the study’s lead author Jongwon Lim, former postdoctoral researcher with Illinois Grainger Engineering and current postdoctoral researcher with the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard. “We want to shift that to point-of-care testing, where the processing is performed where the sample is collected. The main paradigm has been to replicate laboratory approaches at the point of care, but this comes at the cost of reduced sensitivity. Our work reimagines the testing procedure from the ground up as something truly designed to be at the point of care.” This work was a collaboration between the research groups of Illinois Grainger Engineering professors Rashid Bashir of the Department of Bioengineering and Brian Cunningham of the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering. Standard-of-care (SOC) testing is based on purification procedures which process whole blood samples to remove substances that interfere with molecular detection. While protocols are accurate and reliable, the purification step is a bottleneck, requiring laboratory-grade equipment such as centrifuges and extraction instruments typically found in centralized laboratories. This can delay diagnosis and treatment particularly in low-income countries where access to medical facilities is limited. Until now, attempts to address the bottleneck have focused on adapting SOC methods for use outside medical laboratory settings, often by miniaturizing or simplifying laboratory-based sample preparation. However, these approaches still rely on purifying blood samples, which can result in sample loss and compromise detection sensitivity in the field. The Illinois Grainger engineers decided to take an alternative approach: designing a testing procedure from scratch that is intended for point-of-care use rather than trying to copy SOC procedures. They drew inspiration from the dry blood spot (DBS) technology used to detect HIV and hepatitis B infections. “DBS methods are very simple: you collect one or two drops of blood using a finger prick, then you let it dry,” Lim said. “While this simplifies sample collection and storage, the sample still needs to be sent to a centralized laboratory for analysis by specially trained personnel. Our question was whether we could bring the analysis itself to the point of care.” In prior work , the researchers developed testing protocols for detecting DNA targets called “biphasic reactions.” Unlike procedures that mimic SOC testing, the biphasic reaction avoids sample loss associated with conventional purification, helping preserve detection sensitivity even at low target concentrations. When the team turned to RNA virus, they expected the original process to easily transfer. Instead, they faced a greater challenge because RNA is unstable in whole blood. Enzymes naturally present in blood rapidly break RNA into smaller, undetectable fragments, and heat during sample processing can accelerate its degradation. Lim recalls that he experimented with hundreds of reagent combinations while systematically investigating the mechanisms responsible for RNA degradation and amplification failure. “We needed to come up with a way to prevent degradation at each step of the procedure,” he said. “In trying so many different combinations, we were able to deduce mechanistic explanations for what works and what doesn’t. I think this will lead to a huge leap forward in how we detect other diseases caused by RNA viruses such as Ebola and Lassa fever.” In addition to Lim’s work in Bashir’s Bioengineering research group, the new testing protocol includes a pocket-sized fluorescence device for reading test results. The device was developed in Cunningham’s Electrical and Computer Engineering research group, and it was reported earlier this year in the IEEE Sensors Journal .
