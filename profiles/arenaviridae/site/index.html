出血热病毒的诊断:以 拉沙热 为例。

Diagnostics for Hemorrhagic Fever Viruses: Lassa Fever as an Example.
作者： Bidhan Dhar, Nallely Espinoza, Touraj Farzani, Ian Baudi, Dolo Nosamiefan, Coby Y Garcia, Lao-Tzu Allan-Blitz, Pedram Samani, Christian Happi, Pardis Sabeti
摘要中文翻译拉沙病毒 (LASV) 在整个西非引起严重的出血热,而快速、准确的诊断方法的发展仍然受到广泛的谱系水平遗传多样性和高级遏制实验室的有限可用性的阻碍。我们使用基于 CRISPR 的简化感染突出显示以导航流行病 (SHINE) 平台开发了 LASV 检测,以实现安全、可现场部署的检测。为了在不处理活病毒的情况下进行检测开发,我们建立了一个基于质粒的系统,可以生成跨越主要病毒谱系的 LASV RNA 对照。使用这些替代质粒,我们优化了 32 种遗传上不同的 LASV 分离株的检测方法,证明了跨主要谱系细分的检测具有谱系依赖性灵敏度。该测定保持了血清和全血的灵敏度,并使用尼日利亚的临床样本进行了进一步评估。为了减少条带解释中的主观性,我们开发了监督机器学习模型,用于侧流结果的自动分类。总之,这个集成的诊断框架支持遗传多样性 LASV 的现场定向检测,并为高后果 RNA 病毒的诊断开发提供了更广泛的方法。

Diagnostics for Hemorrhagic Fever Viruses: Lassa Fever as an Example.

Authors: Bidhan Dhar, Nallely Espinoza, Touraj Farzani, Ian Baudi, Dolo Nosamiefan, Coby Y Garcia, Lao-Tzu Allan-Blitz, Pedram Samani, Christian Happi, Pardis Sabeti
Original abstract (English)
Lassa virus (LASV) causes severe hemorrhagic fever across West Africa where the development of rapid, accurate diagnostics remains hindered by the extensive lineage-level genetic diversity and by the limited availability of high-level containment laboratories. We developed a LASV assay using the CRISPR-based Streamlined Highlighting of Infections to Navigate Epidemics (SHINE) platform to enable safe, field-deployable detection. To enable assay development without handling live virus, we established a plasmid-based system that generates LASV RNA controls spanning major viral lineages. Using these surrogate plasmids, we optimized the assay for 32 genetically distinct LASV isolates, demonstrating detection across major lineage subdivisions with lineage-dependent sensitivity. The assay maintained sensitivity in serum and whole blood, and was further evaluated using clinical samples in Nigeria. To reduce subjectivity in strip interpretation, we developed supervised machine-learning models for automated classification of lateral flow results. Together, this integrated diagnostic framework supports field-oriented detection of genetically diverse LASV and provides a broader approach for diagnostic development for high-consequence RNA viruses.

Progressive SLAMF7 downregulation is associated with terminal CD8 T-cell exhaustion and aggressive disease in renal cell carcinoma

Authors: 이경민, Hyeonjin Bae, 신재엽, 이은진, Junho Noh, Jaehyun Lee, Kyungmin Lee, Seonhwi Park, Solhwi Lee, Yujung Jang
Original abstract (English)
T-cell exhaustion is a dysfunctional state under persistent antigenic stimulation in chronic viral infections and tumors. Although immune checkpoint blockade can reinvigorate exhausted T cells, responses remain limited, emphasizing the need to better understand exhaustion. SLAMF7 is an immune-regulatory receptor, but its regulatory and functional role during CD8 + T-cell exhaustion remains unclear. Here, we reported that SLAMF7 was absent in naïve mouse and human CD8 T cells but was induced upon in vitro activation. Following acute lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV) infection, all effector and memory subsets expressed SLAMF7, with higher levels in more differentiated cytolytic populations. Under persistent antigenic stimulation, including chronic LCMV infection, murine tumor models, human renal cell carcinoma (RCC), and non-small cell lung cancer, SLAMF7 was highly expressed in progenitor and transitory exhausted subsets but downregulated in PD-1 hi terminally exhausted cells. This downregulation coincided with increased inhibitory receptor and TOX expression, attenuated cytotoxic and polyfunctional responses, and progressive epigenetic remodeling toward terminal exhaustion-associated chromatin programs. Furthermore, SLAMF7 deficiency impaired CD8 + T-cell expansion and antitumor activity. Across models, SLAMF7 downregulation was more closely associated with prolonged antigen exposure than with antigen burden. In RCC patients, SLAMF7-based stratification revealed distinct immunological states: SLAMF7-HI tumors retained progenitor-like CD8 T cells with preserved effector function, whereas SLAMF7-DN tumors were enriched for terminally exhausted cells and associated with high nuclear grade. Collectively, these findings identify SLAMF7 downregulation as a marker of terminal CD8 T-cell exhaustion and suggest its potential as a biomarker of immune competence and disease severity.

Original abstract (English)
Casitas B-cell lymphoma-b protein (Cbl-b) is an E3 ubiquitin ligase that negatively regulates signaling pathways. Although it is involved in the maintenance of immune homeostasis, Cbl-b may also be exploited by tumors or pathogens, leading to immune evasion and persistent infection. In recent years, the role of Cbl-b in tumor immunotherapy has been progressively elucidated, with related Cbl-b inhibitors (such as NX-1607) advancing into clinical trials. It has also been found that Cbl-b inhibition or knockout enhances host immune responses in infection models, such as lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV) and Candida albicans infection models. Consequently, Cbl-b-targeting strategies hold promise as potent adjuncts to infectious disease treatments, particularly when combined with therapeutic vaccines or other immune-enhancing therapies. Given the immunological parallels between infection and tumorigenesis, this review examines the immunoregulatory role of Cbl-b in both contexts and compares their similarities and differences. Additionally, this review summarizes currently available mainstream Cbl-b-targeting strategies to provide new insights for the application of Cbl-b in immunotherapy for infectious diseases.

The initiative, known as Lagos State Bioshield, is designed to strengthen disease surveillance, identify potential infections early and prevent outbreaks from disrupting livelihoods in Nigeria’s commercial hub. Commissioner for Health, Prof. Akin Abayomi, unveiled the framework at the 11th African Conference on One Health and Biosecurity, organised by the state government in partnership with the Global Emerging Pathogens Treatment Consortium (GET Consortium) at the Civic Centre, Victoria Island, Lagos. Speaking at the conference themed, “Bio-Shielding Africa’s Megacities Against Emerging Threats: The Lagos State Story,” Abayomi said the initiative was developed from lessons learnt during the Ebola outbreak and COVID-19 pandemic, particularly the need to protect public health without shutting down economic activities. He said closing Lagos during an epidemic could have devastating consequences, considering that about 70 per cent of residents depend on daily earnings for survival. According to him, the Bioshield would enable businesses, tourism, social activities and other economic engagements to continue while the government monitors and responds to biological threats. He identified Lassa fever, dengue, yellow fever, Marburg virus disease and Ebola, as well as emerging and unknown pathogens, as threats requiring stronger preparedness. Abayomi said Lagos’ estimated 30.1 million population, high population density, rapid urbanisation, migration and international travel exposed the state to heightened outbreak risks. He disclosed that Lagos receives about 70 per cent of Nigeria’s international air traffic and approximately 1.8 million cattle annually, stressing the need for an integrated approach covering human, animal and environmental health. Explaining how the system works, the commissioner said its two-layer structure monitors potential biological threats at entry points and tracks individuals who develop symptoms after arriving in the state. Its Health Radar platform uses WhatsApp, SMS and email to register and monitor travellers and participants at major events, conducting daily health checks for up to 21 days. Individuals reporting symptoms are connected to telemedicine services for initial assessment, with laboratory investigations, referrals and other interventions activated where necessary. Abayomi disclosed that the technology was successfully deployed during the recently concluded CANEX WKND, monitoring about 19,000 registered delegates, including 2,298 international visitors, amid concerns over Ebola outbreaks in Central Africa. The exercise recorded 2,454 telemedicine consultations, 241 clinical referrals and 219 individuals flagged for further attention. He said all identified cases were followed up and closed out, with no severe outcome linked to the suspected high-consequence infections reported. He said the state was developing Bioshield 3.0 to improve artificial intelligence and telemedicine capabilities, strengthen integration with airports and event centres and establish sustainable financing and legal frameworks. Special Adviser to the Governor on Health, Dr. Kemi Ogunyemi, described Lagos as a critical gateway for biological threats entering Africa, given its population, seaports, international airport and commercial activities. She called for an Africa-wide BioShield Alliance involving megacities such as Lagos, Cairo, Kinshasa, Johannesburg and Nairobi to promote data sharing, joint training and common preparedness standards. Earlier, Permanent Secretary, Ministry of Health, Dr. Dayo Lajide, said lessons from the 2014 Ebola outbreak and COVID-19 had strengthened the state’s commitment to preparedness. She cited investments in a Biosafety Level 3 laboratory and biobank at the Infectious Disease Hospital, Yaba, alongside intensified surveillance and healthcare workers’ training. In his keynote address, Deputy Vice-Chancellor (Academic), Lagos State University of Science and Technology, Prof. Abiodun Denloye, identified poor sanitation, unsafe water, inadequate housing and environmental degradation as factors worsening disease vulnerability in African cities. He advocated stronger environmental protection, disease surveillance, resilient urban planning, community protection and One Health governance. Also, GET Consortium’s Chief Operating Officer, Dr. Ayodotun Bobadoye, called for sustained investment in laboratory infrastructure, border monitoring, workforce development and community engagement. Director General, Nigerian Institute of Medical Research (NIMR), Prof. John Obafunwa, urged increased local investment in medical research, genomic sequencing, vaccine development and domestic production of essential medical supplies to reduce Africa’s dependence on imports. The conference brought together policymakers, researchers and health professionals to strengthen Africa’s preparedness against emerging biological threats. The initiative was unveiled by the Commissioner for Health, Prof. Akin Abayomi, at the 11th African Conference on One Health and Biosecurity, organised by the Lagos State Ministry of Health in partnership with the Global Emerging Pathogens Treatment Consortium (GET Consortium), on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, at the Civic Centre, Victoria Island, Lagos. The conference was themed, “Bio-Shielding Africa’s Megacities Against Emerging Threats: The Lagos State Story.” Speaking on the theme, Prof. Abayomi explained that the Bioshield framework was developed from lessons learnt during the Ebola outbreak and COVID-19 pandemic, particularly the need to balance public health protection with economic sustainability. He noted that shutting down Lagos during a public health emergency could have devastating consequences, considering that approximately 70 per cent of residents depend on daily earnings for their livelihoods. According to him, the Bioshield is designed to ensure that businesses, tourism, social activities and everyday livelihoods continue even during public health emergencies. He identified Lassa fever, dengue, yellow fever, Marburg virus disease and Ebola, alongside genetically modified and emerging unknown pathogens, as biological threats requiring specialised preparedness and response capabilities. The Commissioner identified Lagos’ high population density, rapid urbanisation, extensive domestic and international travel, migration, sanitation challenges and large informal settlements as factors that increase its vulnerability to infectious disease outbreaks. He disclosed that Lagos has an estimated population of about 30.1 million, with an average population density of approximately 6,800 persons per square kilometre, while some local government areas exceed 20,000 persons per square kilometre. He further highlighted the risks associated with the movement of people, livestock and agricultural products into the state, noting that Lagos receives approximately 70 per cent of Nigeria’s international air traffic. He disclosed that the state receives about 1.8 million cattle annually, underscoring the need to extend biosecurity measures beyond human health to include animal and environmental health under the One Health approach. Explaining the operational framework, Prof. Abayomi said the Lagos State Bioshield has two major components: an outer layer that identifies potential biological threats associated with people entering the state and an inner layer that monitors individuals who may develop symptoms after arrival. Its Health Radar platform enables the registration and monitoring of travellers and participants at major events, using WhatsApp, SMS and email to conduct daily health checks for up to 21 days. He added that individuals reporting symptoms are connected to telemedicine services for initial medical assessment, with referrals, laboratory investigations and specialised interventions activated where necessary. The system also uses artificial intelligence to support risk classification and escalation of suspected high-risk cases to the Ministry of Health and relevant public health authorities, with the objective of identifying concerning cases within 24 hours of symptom reporting, where possible. Highlighting the successful pilot deployment during the recently concluded CANEX WKND, the Commissioner disclosed that the Bioshield system monitored approximately 19,000 registered delegates, including 2,298 international visitors, amid concerns about the Ebola outbreak in Central Africa and the possibility of imported infections. The exercise recorded 2,454 telemedicine consultations, 241 clinical referrals and 219 individuals flagged for further attention, while 22.6 per cent of respondents reported symptoms requiring assessment. All identified cases were followed up and closed out, with no severe outcome associated with the suspected high-consequence infections reported. Dr. Ogunyemi disclosed that Lagos had strengthened its disease surveillance systems, Emergency Operations Centre and collaboration among human health practitioners, veterinary doctors and environmental officers. She also highlighted the Lagos One Health Strategy, approved by Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu, which provides a coordinated platform for the health, agriculture and environment sectors. He advocated a five-pillar urban bio-shielding framework encompassing environmental protection, disease intelligence and surveillance, resilient urban design, community protection and One Health governance. He also called for neighbourhood vulnerability mapping, improved primary healthcare, stronger laboratory capacity, vaccination, safe food and water systems, and training of health journalists to promote accurate public health reporting and counter misinformation. Also speaking, the Chief Operating Officer of GET Consortium, Dr. Ayodotun Bobadoye, said the conference was convened to assess Lagos State’s biosecurity capacity, identify existing gaps and develop a strategic framework for strengthening preparedness against emerging biological threats. He identified inadequate research funding, weak institutional collaboration, shortages of skilled professionals and dependence on imported vaccines and diagnostics as major challenges to Africa’s preparedness. He urged stronger partnerships among government agencies, universities, research institutions and the private sector to translate scientific discoveries into practical healthcare solutions. The conference brought together policymakers, researchers, healthcare professionals, development partners, security agencies and other stakeholders to deliberate on strengthening Africa’s capacity to anticipate, prevent and respond to emerging biological threats. Speakers emphasised that effective biosecurity requires sustained government commitment, scientific innovation, community participation, inter-agency collaboration and regional cooperation to build resilient cities and safeguard public health.
