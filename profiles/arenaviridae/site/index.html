Immunogenicity of recombinant vesicular stomatitis virus-vectored vaccines expressing the Machupo virus glycoprotein in different genome positions

Authors: Rachel Erickson, Paul Bates
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Recombinant vesicular stomatitis virus (rVSV) is a powerful vaccine platform that elicits durable cellular and humoral immune responses. rVSV vaccines have successfully been used for various hemorrhagic fever-causing viruses. Here, we create and evaluate rVSV vaccines for Machupo virus (MACV), a highly pathogenic New World (NW) mammarenavirus that causes Bolivian hemorrhagic fever. VSV genome rearrangements impact attenuation of the viral vector and the immune response to the vaccine. Therefore, in this study, we directly compare the effect of altering the MACV glycoprotein complex (GPC) position in the VSV genome on humoral and cellular immune responses to vaccination by evaluating replication, antigen expression, and immunogenicity of rVSVs expressing MACV GPC in the first position of the genome (rVSV-MACV1) and in the traditional glycoprotein gene position (rVSV-MACV5). rVSV-MACV1 expresses higher levels of MACV GPC in infected cells and in virions, but the increased antigen does not translate to a more robust immune response. rVSV-MACV5 elicits higher levels of total IgG and neutralizing antibodies than rVSV-MACV1, but similar levels of MACV-specific T cells. To further investigate vaccine strategies for MACV and other NW arenaviruses such as Junín virus (JUNV), we evaluated the humoral immune response to sequential immunization with rVSVs expressing MACV and JUNV GPCs. The highest levels of MACV and JUNV reactive antibodies were achieved using an rVSV-MACV5 prime followed by rVSV-JUNV boost. The data from our study have implications for rVSV vaccine design and provide a foundation for the development of rVSV-vectored vaccines for MACV and other NW arenaviruses. IMPORTANCE Machupo virus (MACV) is a highly pathogenic New World (NW) mammarenavirus that causes Bolivian hemorrhagic fever (BHF). Because of their high mortality rates, MACV and other NW arenaviruses represent a significant public health threat. However, no approved vaccines for MACV exist. Here, we describe recombinant vesicular stomatitis virus (rVSV) vaccines that express the MACV glycoprotein complex (GPC) in different positions of the VSV genome and characterize the humoral and cellular immune response to each. We also investigate sequential immunization with rVSVs expressing MACV GPC and a related NW arenaviral antigen, Junín virus GPC, in an effort to elicit broadly cross-reactive antibody responses. The conclusions from our study are twofold: data presented here provide further understanding of rVSV vaccine vectors and how genome rearrangements can be leveraged for vaccine development, and the results from our study may inform future vaccine development for MACV and other NW arenaviruses.

Question and background
This study was needed because no approved vaccines exist for Machupo virus (MACV), a highly pathogenic New World mammarenavirus that causes Bolivian hemorrhagic fever with high mortality rates. The study aimed to evaluate recombinant vesicular stomatitis virus (rVSV) vaccines expressing MACV glycoprotein complex (GPC) in different genome positions and investigate sequential immunization with MACV and Junín virus GPCs to elicit cross-reactive antibody responses.
Design and population
The study involved creating and evaluating rVSV vaccines for MACV by comparing two constructs: rVSV-MACV1 with MACV GPC in the first genome position and rVSV-MACV5 with MACV GPC in the traditional glycoprotein position. Additionally, sequential immunization with rVSVs expressing MACV and Junín virus GPCs was evaluated to assess humoral immune responses.
Core methods
The study evaluated replication, antigen expression, and immunogenicity of the rVSV constructs by measuring MACV GPC levels in infected cells and virions. Humoral immune responses were assessed through total IgG, neutralizing antibodies, and MACV-specific T cells, with sequential immunization strategies tested using rVSV-MACV5 prime followed by rVSV-JUNV boost.
Main results
rVSV-MACV1 expressed higher levels of MACV GPC in infected cells and virions, but this did not translate to a more robust immune response. rVSV-MACV5 elicited higher levels of total IgG and neutralizing antibodies than rVSV-MACV1, while both constructs induced similar levels of MACV-specific T cells. The highest levels of MACV and Junín virus reactive antibodies were achieved with an rVSV-MACV5 prime followed by rVSV-JUNV boost.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted that genome rearrangements in rVSV vectors impact attenuation and immune responses, but increased antigen expression does not necessarily enhance immunogenicity. The novelty lies in demonstrating that the traditional glycoprotein gene position (rVSV-MACV5) is optimal for eliciting humoral immunity and that sequential immunization with related New World arenaviruses can enhance cross-reactive antibody responses.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings provide a foundation for rVSV-vectored vaccine development for MACV and other New World arenaviruses, informing strategies to improve immunogenicity through genome positioning and sequential immunization. This could enhance surveillance, prevention, and control of Bolivian hemorrhagic fever and related hemorrhagic fevers.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study's limitations include the focus on in vitro and immunogenicity endpoints without challenge data or clinical evaluation. The evidence strength is moderate, as the study provides robust comparative data on immune responses but lacks real-world efficacy validation.

Bacterial infection in Ebola virus disease, Marburg virus disease, Crimean–Congo hemorrhagic fever, and Lassa fever

Authors: Brady Page, Elizabeth Moreton, David A Wohl, William A Fischer
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Concern for bacterial coinfection in patients with high consequence viral infections (HCVIs) has made the empiric antibiotic administration the standard of care. However, the incidence, microbiology, and clinical significance of bacterial coinfection in HCVIs remain unclear. Methods We conducted a systematic review of case reports, cohort studies, and cross-sectional analyses describing bacterial coinfection in Ebola virus disease (EVD), Marburg virus disease (MVD), Crimean–Congo hemorrhagic fever (CCHF), and Lassa fever (LF). Data were extracted on study design, setting, diagnostic methods, pathogens, antibiotic use, and outcomes. Results Thirty publications met inclusion criteria. Bacteremia was most commonly reported among patients with CCHF (n = 46), followed by EVD (n = 16), MVD (n = 16), and LF (n = 2). Of 27 EVD patients treated outside Africa, seven (26%) had bacterial coinfection. Across HCVIs, bacteremia was detected a median of 11 days after symptom onset (range 0–23) and was associated with features of sepsis. Pathogens included enteric flora, healthcare-associated organisms, opportunistic pathogens, and zoonotic bacteria—most notably Brucella spp. Ceftriaxone was the most common empiric antibiotic but is predicted to have activity against a minority of bloodstream isolates. Mortality among coinfected patients with EVD or MVD was similar to those without bacterial coinfection, whereas coinfected CCHF patients had higher mortality than those without coinfection (15.5%; 95% CI 8.0–25.9% vs 5.7%; 95% CI 5.1–6.4%). Conclusions Despite being a major concern driving empiric antibiotic use, bacterial coinfection in HCVIs remains poorly described, precluding determination of its incidence or clinical significance. Prospective studies with standardized protocols for bacterial diagnosis and characterization of antimicrobial resistance are needed to guide antimicrobial strategies.

The Legacy of the 1,000-Death Milestone The psychological threshold of 1,000 fatalities holds deep significance in global epidemiology. During the 2018–2020 Kivu outbreak in the DRC, surpassing this number in May 2019 marked a turning point that forced the World Health Organization (WHO) to escalate its intervention strategies amidst active conflict zones. The earlier 2014–2016 West African epidemic, which claimed over 11,300 lives primarily in Guinea, Liberia, and Sierra Leone, demonstrated how rapidly localized infections can spiral into transnational security threats. Today, infectious disease experts warn that while large-scale fatalities have been mitigated by new therapeutics, the baseline risk has not diminished. Deforestation, rapid urbanization, and increased human-wildlife contact ensure that spillover events involving Ebola, Marburg, and Lassa fever will occur with greater frequency. The challenge is ensuring that initial clusters are contained before they reach the triple-digit fatality marks that periodically dominate global headlines. Vaccine Deployment and the Global Response The most significant advancement since the historic outbreaks is the approval and deployment of the Ervebo vaccine (rVSV-ZEBOV), which has fundamentally altered outbreak response protocols. Utilizing a ring vaccination strategy, health workers prioritize immunizing primary and secondary contacts of confirmed patients, effectively creating a biological firewall around the virus. Despite this scientific triumph, logistical hurdles persist. The vaccine requires ultra-cold chain storage, maintaining temperatures of minus 80 degrees Celsius (minus 112 degrees Fahrenheit). In rural African regions characterized by unreliable electricity grids and underdeveloped road networks, maintaining this cold chain is a monumental operational challenge that frequently delays rapid deployment to ground zero. Ervebo Efficacy: Clinical trials demonstrated nearly 100 percent efficacy when administered within 10 days of exposure. Stockpile Targets: The International Coordinating Group (ICG) on Vaccine Provision aims to maintain a global emergency stockpile of 500,000 doses. Cold Chain Costs: Establishing temporary ultra-cold infrastructure in remote outbreak zones can exceed USD 2 million (KES 260 million) per deployment. Regional Preparedness and the Kenyan Bridge East Africa has become a critical proving ground for hemorrhagic fever preparedness. In Kenya, the Kenya Medical Research Institute (KEMRI) maintains rigorous surveillance protocols at key transit hubs, notably Jomo Kenyatta International Airport in Nairobi, which serves as a primary aviation gateway connecting Central and West Africa to the globe. Neighboring Uganda offers a recent template for effective containment. During its 2022 outbreak of the Sudan ebolavirus strain—for which the Ervebo vaccine is ineffective—the Ugandan Ministry of Health successfully contained the spread within months through aggressive contact tracing, community engagement, and rapid isolation protocols. Ugandan officials demonstrated that even without targeted vaccines, robust public health infrastructure can prevent localized clusters from reaching catastrophic historical fatality figures. International Funding and Sovereign Capability The reliance on international funding mechanisms, such as the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and European Union grants, remains a point of vulnerability. African health ministers are increasingly advocating for sovereign bio-manufacturing capabilities to reduce dependency on Western stockpiles. Initiatives like the African Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) are pushing for the localized production of diagnostics and therapeutics. For the African diaspora in the United States and the United Kingdom, outbreak reports frequently trigger xenophobic travel restrictions and severe economic disruptions. Ensuring that African nations can autonomously identify, sequence, and neutralize outbreaks is not just a health imperative; it is an economic defense strategy. As historical death tolls continue to serve as grim reminders of the pathogen's potential, the true metric of Africa's progress will be the outbreaks that are quietly extinguished before they ever make the headlines. News Brief
A comprehensive review in Animals journal highlights the expanding host range of mammarenaviruses beyond traditional rodent reservoirs to include shrews (Eulipotyphla) and pikas (Lagomorpha). Key findings include WENV in Asian house shrews and rodents, PPV in plateau pikas, and geographically clustered hedgehog-associated viruses in Europe. Human seropositivity studies suggest exposure risks, though definitive zoonotic transmission requires further validation. The review underscores the need for multi-host surveillance to assess pandemic potential and refine One Health risk frameworks.

nm的棒状糖蛋白(GP)突起,每个突起由异二聚体三聚体构成,包含远端亚基GP1、跨膜亚基GP2和稳定信号肽(SSP)。其中GP1负责结合宿主细胞受体,GP2介导低pH环境下病毒包膜与内体膜的融合,SSP则是糖蛋白成熟的关键组分。 病毒基因组为二分节结构,包含大(L)节段(约7.2 kb)和小(S)节段(约3.5 kb)。L节段编码锌结合基质蛋白(Z)和病毒RNA依赖的RNA聚合酶(L),S节段编码包膜GP前体。两个节段的5'和3'端均含有19–30 nt的保守反向互补非翻译区(UTR),可形成非共价闭合的“柄环”结构,作为复制启动子。病毒采用双义编码策略,每个节段沿相反方向编码两个非重叠基因,中间由高度结构化的基因间区(IGR)分隔,后者折叠成稳定的发夹环结构以终止转录。 4. 流行病学与宿主储存库 根据地理分布、抗原性及系统发育关系,哺乳动物沙粒病毒分为旧世界(OW)和新世界(NW)沙粒病毒,分别对应LASV-LCMV和TCRV血清复合群。两类群均为多系起源,既包含人致病株也包含非致病株,目前已确定50余个病毒种,另有近年来在蝙蝠、刺猬和鼠兔中发现的多株未分类哺乳动物沙粒病毒。旧世界群主要流行于东半球,包含原型毒株LASV和LCMV,以及新发LUJV。LASV引发的拉沙热(LF)在西非(尼日利亚、利比里亚、几内亚、塞拉利昂)流行度高、死亡率显著,估算每年感染人数超90万,被世界卫生组织列为因流行潜力和缺乏有效防控措施而构成最高公共卫生风险的疾病之一。2008年在南非识别的LUJV与输入性人类出血热暴发相关,虽人类病死率高,但其具体宿主范围和地理分布仍不明确。LCMV则呈全球分布,除南极洲外在各大陆均有流行,人类感染临床表现从轻度自限性发热到严重脑膜炎、脑炎不等,孕期感染还可导致先天性脑积水、脉络膜视网膜炎等严重致畸结局。普通人群血清学调查显示LCMV抗体阳性率可达15%,但因临床表现非特异性、临床医生认知不足、诊断工具有限及报告不规范,其公共卫生威胁长期处于低估状态。 新世界群局限于美洲,包含多种引发严重出血热的致病因子:JUNV导致阿根廷出血热;MACV和CHAPV分别导致玻利维亚出血热和查帕雷出血热;GTOV引发委内瑞拉出血热;SABV导致巴西出血热。除阿根廷出血热流行病学数据较完备外,其他南美相关疾病的流行率仍不明确。怀特沃特阿罗约病毒作为北美哺乳动物沙粒病毒,也被认为具有人兽共患病风险,与美国境内致死性出血热病例相关。已知哺乳动物沙粒病毒的主要储存宿主为鼠总科(Muroidea)啮齿动物:旧世界病毒主要关联鼠科(Muridae)鼠亚科(Murinae)成员,西非LASV的流行由 Natal多乳鼠(Mastomys natalensis)维持,该物种为撒哈拉以南非洲优势啮齿类,常栖息于农村人居周边。LASV的流行病学呈现显著季节性节律,旱季农业烧荒导致野生食物资源枯竭,迫使M. natalensis大规模迁入人居区域,显著提升家庭储粮与水源的病毒污染风险。与热带局部流行的LASV不同,LCMV的全球分布与其主要宿主小家鼠的广泛分布一致。 新世界病毒主要关联仓鼠科(Cricetidae)啮齿动物,其中南美洲以棉鼠亚科(Sigmodontinae)为核心储存宿主,北美洲以林鼠亚科(Neotominae)为主。JUNV主要由干地暮鼠(Calomys musculinus)和小暮鼠(Calomys laucha)维持;短尾蔗鼠(Zygodontomys brevicauda)是GTOV的主要储存宿主,偶见于阿尔斯通棉鼠(Sigmodon alstoni);大暮鼠(Calomys callosus)携带MACV,实验感染后可慢性携带JUNV。怀特沃特阿罗约病毒分离自白喉林鼠(Neotoma albigula)、墨西哥林鼠(Neotoma mexicana)、小足林鼠(Neotoma micropus)和岩林鼠(Neotoma cinerea)。CHAPV和SABV的自然宿主尚未明确,推测仍为啮齿动物。TCRV是另一例外,最初从特立尼达和多巴哥的叶鼻蝠及蚊类中分离,后在佛罗里达州孤星蜱(Amblyomma americanum)中检出,提示其可能在热带美洲蝙蝠种群中循环,但具体储存宿主和地理范围仍有争议。 哺乳动物沙粒病毒在自然储存宿主体内通常引发无症状感染,表现为慢性病毒血症及通过分泌物、排泄物长期排出高滴度病毒。水平感染的成年啮齿动物多可清除病毒并获得终身免疫,而发育早期发生的垂直传播常导致终身持续性感染,这是病毒在自然界维持的关键机制。人类主要通过吸入被啮齿动物尿液或粪便污染的雾化液滴或粉尘、摄入污染的食物水源、污染物接触破损皮肤等途径感染,暴露风险随人类活动与储存宿主栖息地的重叠而增加。此外,处理和食用啮齿动物肉类也是重要传播途径,尤其在依赖丛林肉的低资源社区。啮齿动物咬伤传播相对少见。人际水平传播罕见,但已有器官移植后传播及医疗机构感染防控不足导致的传播报道。LCMV可通过母婴垂直传播,性传播则极为罕见。系统发育分析显示西非LASV的人类毒株早于啮齿动物毒株,提示环境污染物暴露可能导致人类向啮齿动物的“回溢”传播。 5. 新发哺乳动物沙粒病毒与新型宿主 近年宏基因组与野生动物监测研究识别出多种新型哺乳动物沙粒病毒,大幅扩展了该属已知的宿主范围。2013年在中国温州地区首次发现的温州病毒(WENV,种名Mammarenavirus wenzhouense)是首个跨目传播的哺乳动物沙粒病毒证据。对351份啮齿动物样本的筛查显示,病毒RNA在Rattus属和Niviventer属啮齿动物中的检出率为12.0%,在真盲缺目亚洲家鼩鼱(Suncus murinus)中的检出率为4.4%。全基因组测序显示该病毒聚类于已知旧世界哺乳动物沙粒病毒群。实验感染证实WENV具有强系统性组织嗜性,可在啮齿类和鼩鼱的粪便及肝、肺、心、肾、脾中检出病毒RNA。后续研究显示WENV及其变异株在亚洲广泛分布,宿主范围覆盖多种Rattus属物种、小家鼠和黑线姬鼠(Apodemus agrarius),但由于S. murinus中病毒流行率较低且缺乏纵向排毒数据,鼩鼱在WENV流行病学中的生态角色尚未明确。柬埔寨的研究在柬埔塞褐家鼠和太平洋鼠中检测到WENV变异株Cardamones,对人类临床样本的检测显示,17.4%的流感样症状且登革热、流感检测阴性患者,以及13.2%的健康人群存在WENV IgG血清阳性反应,0.6%的有呼吸道症状患者的呼吸道标本中检出病毒RNA,但因症状轻微非特异性、半数阳性者合并其他呼吸道病原体感染及样本量有限,未能确立明确的因果关联。中国两项省份的健康人群血清学调查显示儿童WENV血清阳性率为1.5%,成人为4.6%,低于东南亚报道数据,可能与检测方法特异性更高及地理、时间差异有关,这些发现提示需加强对该新发病毒的关注与研究。 跳鼠沙粒病毒(JEAreV,后续归类为Mammarenavirus alashanense,阿拉善病毒)的发现首次证实哺乳动物沙粒病毒的宿主范围可从传统鼠科扩展至非鼠科啮齿类(跳鼠科Dipodidae),提示病毒分化可发生在孤立的生态位中。2014年从中国内蒙古的三趾跳鼠(Dipus sagitta)中识别的该病毒总体流行率为8.0%,且具有严格的宿主特异性,在同域分布的其他跳鼠物种(如长耳跳鼠Euchoreutes naso、西伯利亚五趾跳鼠Allactaga sibirica)中未检出。基于L和S节段的系统发育分析将JEAreV置于旧世界群内,但处于高度独立的分支,结合环境中无其他替代啮齿类宿主的证据,提示JEAreV是在沙漠啮齿类生态位中独立进化形成的稳定种特异性定居者,而非来自同域鼠科啮齿动物的偶然溢出。 除鼩鼱外,真盲缺目其他类群的发现进一步挑战了哺乳动物沙粒病毒严格依赖啮齿类宿主的范式。对猬科(Erinaceidae)刺猬的研究提供了该类群中病毒循环的确凿证据。匈牙利北白胸刺猬(Erinaceus roumanicus)中检测到的高度分化梅切克山病毒(MEMV)是首个证实沙粒病毒与猬科动物关联的报道。尽管该病毒与阿拉善病毒遗传亲缘性较高,但成对序列一致性低于国际病毒分类委员会(ICTV)的种划分阈值,因此被归类为旧世界群新种Mammarenavirus mecsekense。2025年匈牙利对更多刺猬粪便样本的筛查又鉴定出5个新型哺乳动物沙粒病毒毒株,虽与已知MEMV亲缘最近,但序列和系统发育分析显示其具有独特的基因组基序,可能代表第二个新种,暂定名为潘诺尼亚哺乳动物沙粒病毒(PANV),提示匈牙利的Erinaceus roumanicus至少携带两种不同的哺乳动物沙粒病毒。平行研究显示刺猬与哺乳动物沙粒病毒的关联并非局限于单一国家,而是呈现欧洲范围内的复杂共循环格局:俄罗斯欧洲部分的Erinaceus属物种口腔和肛门拭子中鉴定出的高度变异毒株被统称为刺猬沙粒病毒(EriAreV),虽归入侵蝟沙粒病毒分支,但高遗传多样性支持其作为独立新种;2025年意大利欧洲刺猬(Erinaceus europaeus)的肝、十二指肠样本中检出4个欧洲刺猬沙粒病毒(EEAV)毒株,与白胸刺猬MEMV亲缘最近,但根据种划分标准应视为独立新种。对匈牙利、俄罗斯、意大利及数据库中中国刺猬(Erinaceus amurensis)序列的系统发育分析显示,所有刺猬来源的哺乳动物沙粒病毒在旧世界群内形成一个独立分支,与MEMV和阿拉善病毒聚类,且毒株按地理起源聚集,至少代表5个潜在的刺猬相关哺乳动物沙粒病毒新种。这些发现证实猬科动物并非偶然溢出宿主,而是多个独立哺乳动物沙粒病毒种的特异性进化储存库。意大利阳性欧洲刺猬的脑、脾、肾、肺等多脏器中检出病毒RNA,且无明显组织病理学损伤;俄罗斯阳性刺猬在严格隔离的冬眠个体中仍可检出病毒,均支持病毒在刺猬体内长期持续存在,与啮齿类储存宿主中典型的慢性无症状排毒动力学高度一致。 青藏高原高原鼠兔(Ochotona curzoniae)中发现的Plateau Pika virus(PPV,种名Mammarenavirus batangense)是首个在兔形目中识别的哺乳动物沙粒病毒。6.3%的肠道样本中检出PPV RNA,且肺、肾、心、肝、脾等内脏器官中也存在高病毒载量,证实鼠兔为PPV的自然宿主。系统发育分析显示PPV与传统旧世界、新世界哺乳动物沙粒病毒分化程度极高,可能代表一个古老谱系,约在7700–8800万年前与传统哺乳动物沙粒病毒发生分歧,远早于经典旧世界与新世界群的估计分歧时间。同一研究将野生动物监测与人类临床风险直接关联,青藏高原高海拔地区的人类回顾性血清学调查显示PPV血清阳性率达2.4%,结合体外实验证实PPV可高效感染并在人类及非人灵长类细胞系中复制的证据,初步提示了该病毒跨物种传播给人类的潜力。 6. 讨论 新型沙粒病毒与新宿主的发现表明,哺乳动物沙粒病毒属的真实多样性仍远未被认知,可能还存在于大量未被研究的哺乳动物类群中。在匈牙利MEMV发现之前,学界普遍认为欧洲的哺乳动物沙粒病毒流行病学仅涉及单一流行种——全球分布的LCMV。随后在欧洲大陆猬科动物中识别出的复杂、呈地理聚集分布的独立病毒种网络(匈牙利的PANV、俄罗斯的EriAreV、意大利的EEAV),重塑了对哺乳动物沙粒病毒与宿主协同进化及其在欧洲流行病学特征的理解。重要的是,解剖和冬眠刺猬中观察到的系统性组织嗜性和长期病毒持续存在,强烈支持这些食虫动物是稳定的种特异性进化储存库,而非来自同域啮齿动物的偶然一过性溢出。 宿主范围向鼩鼱等真盲缺目动物及鼠兔等兔形目动物扩展具有重要的公共卫生意义。东南亚地区已发现WENV暴露与人类显著的血清阳性率相关,且在人类呼吸道标本中检出病毒RNA;青藏高原的回顾性血清学调查也证实了人类对PPV的暴露。虽然高原鼠兔的系统性复制支持其作为PPV储存宿主的地位,但人畜共患溢出的绝对风险仍需进一步研究验证。 除新病毒发现外,近期研究还显示已知病毒种在意外宿主中的检出率上升。典型案例如LCMV,历史上几乎仅与小家鼠关联,但目前已在wood小鼠(Apodemus sylvaticus)、松鼠(Sciurus vulgaris)、田鼠(Microtus agrestis)、巢鼠(Micromys minutus)、圈养黑尾草原犬鼠(Cynomys ludovicianus)和褐家鼠(Rattus The Biology of a Zoonotic Threat Hantaviruses are a family of viruses spread primarily by rodents. Unlike respiratory viruses such as influenza or SARS-CoV-2, which are easily transmitted human-to-human, hantavirus is typically contracted by humans through the inhalation of aerosolized virus shed in rodent urine, feces, and saliva.
