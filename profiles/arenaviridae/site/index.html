Pathogenesis and natural history of Machupo virus infection in nonhuman primates.

Authors: Declan D. Pigeaud, Jacquelyn Turcinovic, Karla A. Fenton, Abhishek N. Prasad, Krystle N. Agans, Viktoriya Borisevich, Daniel J. Deer, Rachel O'Toole, Antoinette Lona, Natalie S. Dobias
Original abstract (English)
UNLABELLED: The mammarenavirus Machupo virus (MACV) is the causative agent of Bolivian hemorrhagic fever (BHF) and is associated with high mortality rates in humans and nonhuman primates (NHPs). There are currently no approved vaccines or treatments to combat BHF. As there are eight genetically distinct lineages of MACV, medical countermeasures are needed to protect against all strains within these lineages. While rodent models have been developed for a few strains of MACV, NHPs most faithfully reproduce human disease. However, NHP models have only been described for two strains of MACV. Here, we assessed the pathogenic potential of two genetically distinct strains of MACV representing two different lineages in cynomolgus and/or African green monkeys (AGM). Both MACV strains caused severe multisystemic disease consistent with BHF, often resulting in fatality with the lineage III strain resulting in uniform lethality in both NHP species and the lineage VIII strain resulting in uniform lethality in AGM. Pathology was consistent among the MACV strains and NHP species with primary involvement of the gastrointestinal and lymphoid systems with the central nervous system showing variable but widespread dissemination of MACV antigen. Spatial proteomics showed that MACV antigen was most prevalent in neurons (MAP2+) and Purkinje cells (MAP2+/Calbindin+). Multi-omics assessment of circulating mRNA and proteins showed the overall phenotype of BHF was similar between species; however, the magnitude of the inflammatory response diverged at late timepoints. Overall, MACV infection of NHP recapitulated BHF in humans driven by a dysregulated innate response resulting in vascular inflammation and hemodynamic instability. IMPORTANCE: Machupo virus (MACV) is a deadly and understudied mammarenavirus that causes severe disease known as Bolivian hemorrhagic fever (BHF) with high mortality rates in symptomatic humans. MACV is transmitted to humans by contact with the vesper mouse and has significant outbreak potential. No licensed vaccine or antivirals against MACV exist. Well-characterized animal models are critically needed to test the efficacy of promising candidate medical countermeasures against MACV and to understand how MACV causes disease. Here, we developed several new nonhuman primate models that accurately reproduced human BHF. We utilized spatial proteomics to reveal specific cell types involved in MACV infection, including those within the central nervous system, and employed a multi-omics approach to reveal circulating mRNA and protein biomarkers driving fatal disease outcomes. Our comprehensive investigation of the response to MACV infection in a highly translational species can be exploited to develop new countermeasures to combat BHF.

An ESCRT-engineered mRNA-VLP vaccine elicits humoral and cellular immunity against Lassa virus.

Authors: Chen Li, Xinyu Liu, Panpan Lu, Xun Wang, Jing Liu, Xiaorong Chen, Yanjia Chen, Jiayan Li, Xuxu Xu, Yuchen Shao
Original abstract (English)
Lassa virus (LASV) causes severe hemorrhagic fever across West Africa, yet no licensed vaccine is available. A major barrier to LASV antigen design is that its sole neutralization target, the glycoprotein complex (GPC), lacks intrinsic late-domain motifs required to engage the host ESCRT machinery for particle release. Here, we report a rational design strategy that confers autonomous budding capacity to LASV GPC by appending a synthetic ESCRT-recruitment cassette to its cytoplasmic domain. Systematic screening of a sixteen-construct matrix identified a lead candidate, VLPdEm5. This construct integrates the ESCRT cassette, an optimized furin cleavage site, and a heterologous H1N1 transmembrane-cytoplasmic domain to support efficient particle assembly and proper display of conformational neutralizing epitopes. Delivered via lipid-nanoparticle-encapsulated mRNA (mRNA-LNP), VLPdEm5 drives efficient in situ virus-like particle (VLP) self-assembly, eliciting rapid, high-titer binding antibodies with broad cross-reactivity across six major LASV lineages, robust germinal center B-cell activity, and memory T-cell responses. Crucially, VLPdEm5 mRNA-LNP provides dose-dependent protection against pseudovirus challenge in vivo. This work establishes an effective LASV vaccine candidate and validates an ESCRT-engineered mRNA-VLP platform broadly applicable to non-budding viral envelope proteins.

Original abstract (English)
Dengue and Lassa fever co-circulate in Nigeria, yet few models examine their co-infection alongside behavioural and clinical interventions. This study develops a deterministic compartmental model of dengue–Lassa fever co-infection that couples human, mosquito and rodent dynamics and distinguishes educated from uneducated susceptible individuals. We derive the basic and effective reproduction numbers using the next-generation operator approach, and establish local and global stability of the disease-free equilibrium via a Lyapunov function, together with global stability of the endemic equilibria using a Goh–Volterra function. Numerical simulations confirm the analytical results and show that public education, reduced human–vector contact and treatment of co-infected cases lower infection levels.

Authors: Rashid Bundu Kpaka, Lawrence Sao Babawo, Umaru Bangura, Prince Tongor Mabey, Ahmed Vandi
Original abstract (English)
This study examined the factors influencing the effectiveness of institutional and community support systems, focusing on guidance and counselling services in secondary schools and Community Engagement and Involvement (CEI) in Lassa fever control in Sierra Leone. The guidance and counselling component employed a mixed-methods cross-sectional design involving 128 respondents from selected secondary schools in Pujehun District. Data were collected through questionnaires and interviews and analysed using descriptive statistics, multiple linear regression, and qualitative analysis. Findings revealed inadequate counselling infrastructure, weak professional capacity, and substantial barriers, including inadequate materials (74.2%), high pupil–counsellor ratios (71.8%), lack of trained counsellors (69.4%), socioeconomic constraints (68.0%), and limited technology (67.2%). Monitoring and follow-up showed the strongest association with career development (β = 0.165, p = .073), although no individual availability or professional-capacity variable significantly predicted career development (p > .05). Training coverage was higher in Lower Bambara (73.0% vs. 55.4%; χ2 = 4.4, p = .037), while co-production was substantially greater (63.5% vs. 28.6%; χ2 = 17.8, p < .001). Lower Bambara also recorded higher effectiveness (median 13.5 vs. 9.0; p < .001), trust (median 13.0 vs. 11.0; p < .001), surveillance cooperation, and timely case reporting. Qualitative findings highlighted trusted local intermediaries, bidirectional communication, and co-production as important mechanisms. The findings demonstrate that formal availability of services or structures alone does not guarantee effectiveness. Sustainable outcomes require adequately trained personnel, sufficient resources, institutional support, regular and responsive implementation, meaningful participation, and strong local ownership. Strengthening professional capacity, infrastructure, monitoring systems, community co-production, trusted communication, and institutional support is therefore essential for improving both educational counselling and community-based public health interventions in Sierra Leone.

Ebola virus, Marburg virus, Lassa virus, and Crimean–Congo hemorrhagic fever virus contribute substantially to the global burden of these diseases. These pathogens employ diverse transmission routes, including vector-borne transmission, zoonotic spillover, and direct exposure to infected bodily fluids. Climate change, land use alterations, urbanization, population growth, and increased human–animal interactions may reshape pathogen ecology and create new opportunities for viral emergence and transmission. International travel and interconnected human populations further complicate outbreak containment. This review examines the pathophysiological mechanisms, epidemiological patterns, and major outbreaks associated with VHFs, with particular emphasis on how environmental change, ecological disruption, and infectious disease dynamics interact. We also evaluate the roles of global health organizations in strengthening disease surveillance, coordinating outbreak responses, and promoting international preparedness. Furthermore, we discuss the importance of integrated surveillance systems, cross-sectoral collaboration, and climate-informed public health strategies in reducing the risks associated with emerging and recurring VHF outbreaks. By integrating virological, ecological, epidemiological, and public health perspectives, this review highlights critical knowledge gaps and identifies priorities for improving early detection, outbreak preparedness, and coordinated responses to future VHF threats.

研究人员构建了一个确定性人-啮齿动物耦合传播模型,将人类分为易感者、临时保护者、暴露者、无症状感染者、有症状感染者和康复者,啮齿动物分为易感者和感染者。模型拟合2025年NCDC每周拉沙热系列数据,估计夸拉州年度负担,计算基本再生数并分解传播路径贡献,开展敏感性分析和最优控制模拟,比较意识宣传、啮齿动物控制及联合干预三种策略相对于年度病例目标的效果,并进行成本效果分析。研究得出基本再生数为2.5000,啮齿动物到人溢出传播贡献97.84%,是主要阈值通路;单独意识宣传使病例降至113.4例(减少31.00%)但未达目标,单独啮齿动物控制降至29.6例(减少82.00%)仍高于16例目标,联合干预降至7.8例和1.4例死亡(减少95.27%),达到目标;所需准备成本估计为88.3万美元,涵盖干预实施和剩余病例管理。该研究为夸拉州拉沙热防控提供了情景化的干预优先级和预算参考,强调了啮齿动物控制作为核心策略的必要性以及联合干预实现年度目标的可行性。
