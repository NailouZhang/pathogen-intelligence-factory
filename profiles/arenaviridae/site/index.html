Clinical Evaluation of the PANDAA Lassa Virus RT-PCR Assay in Sierra Leone

摘要中文翻译摘要 拉沙病毒的分子检测在诊断中受到病毒广泛遗传多样性和流行病学设置中有限实验室能力的困扰。RealStar(C)拉沙病毒RT-PCR试剂盒2.0是一种常用的商业双靶标检测方法,但其双靶标格式需要分别进行扩增反应,增加了试剂使用和工作流程的复杂性。我们使用来自塞拉利昂的94份存档血清样本,对单反应、自适应的PANDAA拉沙病毒RT-PCR检测方法与双靶标RealStar(C)拉沙病毒RT-PCR试剂盒2.0的诊断性能进行了评估。其中79份样本具有有效的配对结果,我们报告了PANDAA检测的阳性百分比一致率为78.12%(95% CI 61.25%-88.98%),阴性百分比一致率为93.62%(95% CI 82.84%-97.81%),总体一致率为87.34%(95% CI 78.24%-92.98%);Cohen's kappa值为0.73(95% CI 0.58-0.89)。PANDAA检测显示出显著的一致性,并提供了一种潜在高效的单孔格式,但需要进一步优化和前瞻性评估。

Clinical Evaluation of the PANDAA Lassa Virus RT-PCR Assay in Sierra Leone

Authors: Donald Samuel Grant, Caitlin R. Thompson, John Demby Sandi, Mariaam Manjia, Tiangay Mariama Patience Sallay Kallon, Mohamed Suffian Kamara, Ibrahim Umaru Fofanah, Thomas Edwards, Tom E Fletcher, Rachel Louise Byrne
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Molecular diagnosis of Lassa fever is complicated by extensive Lassa virus genetic diversity and limited laboratory capacity in endemic settings. The RealStar(C) Lassa Virus RT-PCR Kit 2.0 is a commonly used commercial dual-target assay for LASV detection, but its two-target format requires separate amplification reactions, increasing reagent use and workflow complexity. We present the diagnostic evaluation of the single-reaction, adaptive PANDAA Lassa Virus RT-PCR assay with the dual-target RealStar(C) Lassa Virus RT-PCR Kit 2.0 using 94 archived serum samples from Sierra Leone. 79 of these samples had valid paired results, and we report a positive percent agreement of 78.12% (95% CI 61.25%-88.98%), negative percent agreement of 93.62% (95% CI 82.84%-97.81%), and an overall agreement of 87.34% (95% CI 78.24%-92.98%); Cohen k; was 0.73 (95% CI 0.58-0.89). PANDAA showed substantial agreement and offers a potentially efficient single-well format, but further optimisation and prospective evaluation are needed.

Question and background
The study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of a new single-reaction RT-PCR assay for Lassa virus in Sierra Leone, where molecular diagnosis is complicated by the virus's genetic diversity and limited laboratory capacity.
Design and population
The study conducted a diagnostic evaluation of the PANDAA Lassa Virus RT-PCR assay using 94 archived serum samples from Sierra Leone, with 79 samples having valid paired results.
Core methods
The PANDAA assay was compared to the dual-target RealStar(C) Lassa Virus RT-PCR Kit 2.0 using 94 archived serum samples from Sierra Leone, with agreement metrics calculated for both assays.
Main results
The PANDAA assay demonstrated a positive percent agreement of 78.12% (95% CI 61.25%-88.98%), a negative percent agreement of 93.62% (95% CI 82.84%-97.81%), and an overall agreement of 87.34% (95% CI 78.24%-92.98%) with the RealStar(C) assay, and a Cohen's kappa of 0.73 (95% CI 0.58-0.89).
Interpretation and novelty
The PANDAA assay showed substantial agreement with the dual-target RealStar(C) assay and offers a potentially efficient single-well format, although further optimisation and prospective evaluation are needed.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings suggest that the PANDAA assay could improve diagnostic efficiency in Lassa fever surveillance and management in Sierra Leone, where laboratory resources are limited.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study used archived samples, which may limit the generalisability of results, and the evidence is moderate due to the lack of prospective evaluation and potential biases in sample selection.

Diagnostic accuracy of a single-reaction, pan-lineage Lassa virus RT-PCR at two Nigerian laboratories: a retrospective and prospective cohort study

Authors: Nelson Akin Adedosu, Suleiman Y. Alhaji, Johnson Etafo, Idris I. Abduljalail, Ruth I. Aderibigbe, Anita O. Atu, Olufunke Bosede Gbenga-Ayeni, Bawa A. Mikail, Ronke I. Uzuajemeh, Aisha M. Yarima
Original abstract (English)
Background Lassa virus (LASV) intra- and inter-lineage nucleotide diversity has made it challenging to develop a single-reaction RT-PCR assay that can detect all viral variants with equal sensitivity. We evaluated PANDAA(R) LASV, a single-reaction pan-lineage RT-PCR assay, at two Nigerian Lassa fever laboratories against the RealStar(R) Lassa Virus RT-PCR Kit 2.0, the current diagnostic standard in Nigeria, which requires two separate reactions per patient sample to mitigate against false negative results. Methodology and Principal Findings We conducted a diagnostic accuracy study using retrospectively (n = 200) and prospectively (n = 100) plasma samples that had been collected from patients meeting the standard case definition of suspected Lassa fever for testing by real-time PCR at Abubakar Tafawa Balewa University Teaching Hospital in Bauchi and Federal Medical Centre in Owo. PANDAA LASV(R) sensitivity was 98.6% (138/140; 95% CI 94.9-99.8%), specificity 99.4% (155/156; 96.5-100%), and accuracy 99.0% (293/296; 97.1-99.8%). The positive likelihood ratio was 153.8 (21.8-1084.9) and the negative likelihood ratio 0.01 (0.00-0.06). PANDAA Ct values correlated significantly with the reference assay L gene target (Spearman's {rho} = 0.80; p < 0.0001) and GPC target ({rho} = 0.69; p < 0.0001). Three confirmed discordant results occurred at high Ct values near assay detection limits. Performance was consistent across sites with overlapping confidence intervals for sensitivity and specificity and no difference in median Ct values for any assay target between laboratories. Ct values were significantly lower in patients [≥] 45 years (p = 0.008). Conclusions and Significance PANDAA(R) LASV demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy to detect Lassa virus RNA compared to the dual-reaction reference assay. Reducing the operational burden from two PCR reactions per sample to a single PANDAA(R) LASV reaction while maintaining equivalent sensitivity and specificity could have a direct impact on labour and reagent costs, and turnaround time. Clinical validation against additional LASV lineages is an immediate priority.

Question and background
The study aimed to address the challenge of developing a single-reaction RT-PCR assay capable of detecting all Lassa virus variants with equal sensitivity due to its intra- and inter-lineage nucleotide diversity.
Design and population
A retrospective and prospective cohort study was conducted using plasma samples from patients with suspected Lassa fever at two Nigerian laboratories: Abubakar Tafawa Balewa University Teaching Hospital in Bauchi and Federal Medical Centre in Owo.
Core methods
The study compared the diagnostic accuracy of PANDAA(R) LASV, a single-reaction pan-lineage RT-PCR assay, with the RealStar(R) Lassa Virus RT-PCR Kit 2.0, the current diagnostic standard in Nigeria. Ct values were analyzed for correlation with reference assay targets, and performance was assessed across sites.
Main results
PANDAA(R) LASV demonstrated a sensitivity of 98.6% (138/140; 95% CI 94.9-99.8%), specificity of 99.4% (155/156; 96.5-100%), and accuracy of 99.0% (293/296; 97.1-99.8%). Three confirmed discordant results occurred at high Ct values near assay detection limits.
Interpretation and novelty
The study concluded that PANDAA(R) LASV has high diagnostic accuracy compared to the dual-reaction reference assay, maintaining equivalent sensitivity and specificity while reducing the operational burden.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings suggest that adopting PANDAA(R) LASV could reduce labor and reagent costs and improve turnaround time for Lassa fever diagnosis in Nigerian laboratories.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study had a limited sample size and relied on existing specimens, which may affect generalizability. However, performance was consistent across sites with overlapping confidence intervals, indicating moderate evidence strength.

Original abstract (English)
Lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV) is a rare cause of meningoencephalitis in humans. Although LCMV is a rodent zoonosis with a global distribution, no UK cases in humans have been reported in published literature since the 1950s, and testing is rarely performed. LCMV meningoencephalitis is self-limiting in people who are immunocompetent, but infection in hosts who are immunocompromised causes severe disease. We report a presentation of LCMV meningoencephalitis in a patient with relapsed multiple myeloma receiving teclistamab. The patient presented with a 5-month history of progressive neurological symptoms, including hearing loss, ataxia, headache, and weight loss. Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analysis showed lymphocytic pleocytosis, markedly elevated protein concentration, and hypoglycorrhachia. Extensive microbiological and autoimmune investigations were negative, and empirical antimicrobial and antituberculosis therapies did not prevent clinical deterioration. Metagenomic next-generation sequencing of CSF identified LCMV, and a history of domestic rodent exposure was subsequently identified. Combination antiviral therapy with ribavirin, remdesivir, and molnupiravir was initiated. Molnupiravir was later substituted for favipiravir, and favipiravir dosing was guided by serum and CSF therapeutic drug monitoring. The patient showed sustained neurological improvement, with clearance of LCMV from CSF and blood within 2 months. Antiviral therapy continued for 6 months. The patient regained functional independence and returned to work, with only mild residual symptoms. In addition to reporting a novel occurrence of LCMV infection in a patient on teclistamab, the case highlights the presence of LCMV in the UK and the utility of metagenomic next-generation sequencing for pathogen identification in undiagnosed encephalitis. Finally, the case provides preliminary evidence supporting combination antiviral therapy in persistent LCMV infection.
