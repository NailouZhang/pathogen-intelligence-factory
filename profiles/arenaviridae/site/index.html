尼日利亚河流州两家三级医院的 拉沙热 暴发:临床谱、医疗保健相关感染、接触者调查和公共卫生应对

Lassa fever outbreak across two tertiary hospitals in Rivers State, Nigeria: clinical spectrum, healthcare-associated infection, contact investigation and public health response
作者： Chizaram Anselm Onyeaghala, Victoria Eno Gomba, Hope Avundaa, Mary Adanma Alex-Wele, Chioma Chinyere Nwabunike, Lekie Sigalo, Ifeoma Nwadiuto, Golden Chukwuemeka Owhonda, Omosivie Maduka, Datonye Dennis Alasia
摘要中文翻译摘要背景 拉沙热 (LF) 仍然是尼日利亚的主要公共卫生威胁,其结果受到及时的 诊断 和 治疗 以及潜在的医疗保健相关的 传播 的影响。我们在 2024 年 暴发 期间对尼日利亚河流州的两家三级医院进行了确诊的 LF 病例的特征分析和接触者调查。方法 我们对 2024 年 1 月 23 日至 8 月 13 日在哈科特港大学教学医院 (UPTH) 和河流州立大学教学医院 (RSUTH) 发现的 LF 确诊病例进行了描述性的两中心病例系列研究。病例通过拉沙病毒实时逆转录聚合酶链反应 (RT-PCR) 进行确诊。临床、流行病学、实验室、治疗 和结果数据从医院和公共卫生记录中获得。接触者调查包括风险分层、21 天主动 监测、有症状接触者的临床评估和 RT-PCR 检测,以及对符合条件的高风险接触者进行利巴韦林暴露后预防 (PEP)。分析是描述性的。结果 确定了 7 例实验室确诊病例:在 UPTH 指标病例的高危医护人员接触者中,有 5 例是社区相关感染,2 例是医疗保健相关感染。患者年龄为 21-61 岁,其中 4 名是男性。5 例患者出现胃肠道和出血表现(71.43%),3 例患者出现 AKI 和神经系统表现(42.86%)。存活5例,死亡2例,病死率为28.57%。这两例死亡均发生在使用利巴韦林治疗之前的社区相关指示病例中。联系人 监测 检测到两种与医疗保健相关的感染,其临床病程较轻。在 UPTH,确定了 106 名主要联系人;42 人为高危人群,其中 31 人出现急性发热性疾病,2 人确诊为继发性 LF。所有 42 名高危接触者均接受了利巴韦林 PEP。在 RSUTH,八名高风险接触者受到监控;一名患者接受了 PEP,没有人出现继发感染。一名患有严重肾病的幸存者需要进行六次血液透析,并在 18 天后出院。结论 LF 暴发 表现出显着的临床异质性,从轻微的医疗保健相关感染到严重的多系统疾病和死亡。接触者调查和前瞻性 监测 能够检测与医疗保健相关的继发感染,并促进有针对性的 PEP。加强早期识别、医护人员保护、实验室确认、联系 监测 和及时 治疗 应仍然是尼日利亚 LF 准备和 暴发 响应的优先事项。

Lassa fever outbreak across two tertiary hospitals in Rivers State, Nigeria: clinical spectrum, healthcare-associated infection, contact investigation and public health response

Authors: Chizaram Anselm Onyeaghala, Victoria Eno Gomba, Hope Avundaa, Mary Adanma Alex-Wele, Chioma Chinyere Nwabunike, Lekie Sigalo, Ifeoma Nwadiuto, Golden Chukwuemeka Owhonda, Omosivie Maduka, Datonye Dennis Alasia
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Lassa fever (LF) remains a major public health threat in Nigeria, with outcomes influenced by timely diagnosis and treatment and by potential healthcare-associated transmission. We characterised confirmed LF cases and conducted contact investigations at two tertiary hospitals in Rivers State, Nigeria, during the 2024 outbreak. Methods We conducted a descriptive two-centre case series of confirmed LF cases identified at the University of Port Harcourt Teaching Hospital (UPTH) and Rivers State University Teaching Hospital (RSUTH) from January 23 to August 13, 2024. Cases were confirmed by real-time reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) for Lassa virus. Clinical, epidemiological, laboratory, treatment, and outcome data were obtained from hospital and public health records. Contact investigations included risk stratification, 21-day active surveillance, clinical assessment and RT-PCR testing of symptomatic contacts, and ribavirin post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) for eligible high-risk contacts. Analysis was descriptive. Results Seven laboratory-confirmed cases were identified: five community-associated and two healthcare-associated infections among high-risk healthcare-worker contacts of the UPTH index case. Patients were aged 21–61 years, and four were male. Five patients had gastrointestinal and haemorrhagic manifestations (71.43%), while three had AKI and neurological manifestations (42.86%). Five patients survived, and two died, giving a case fatality proportion of 28.57%. Both deaths occurred in community-associated index cases before therapeutic ribavirin could be administered. Contact surveillance detected the two healthcare-associated infections, which had mild clinical courses. At UPTH, 106 primary contacts were identified; 42 were high-risk, of whom 31 developed acute febrile illness, and two were confirmed with secondary LF. All 42 high-risk contacts received ribavirin PEP. At RSUTH, eight high-risk contacts were monitored; one received PEP, and none developed secondary infection. One survivor with severe renal disease required six haemodialysis sessions and was discharged after 18 days. Conclusions The LF outbreak demonstrated substantial clinical heterogeneity, ranging from mild healthcare-associated infection to severe multisystem disease and death. Contact investigation and prospective surveillance enabled detection of healthcare-associated secondary infections and facilitated targeted PEP. Strengthening early recognition, healthcare-worker protection, laboratory confirmation, contact surveillance, and timely treatment should remain priorities for LF preparedness and outbreak response in Nigeria.

Retaining Participants in a Prospective Epidemiological Study Involving Blood Draws Through Sustained Engagement: Lessons from Enable 1.0 Incidence Study on Lassa Fever Infection in Abakaliki, Southeast Nigeria

Authors: Cosmas Kenan Onah, Onyinyechukwu Uzoamaka Oka, Chijioke Vitalus Iloke, Nkechi Violet Nwachukwu, Marycynthia Nnenna Otta, Olaedo O. Nnachi, Christian Obasi Akpa, Victoria Chioma Azuogu, Robinson Chukwudi Onoh, Emeka Ogah Onwe
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Participant retention is critical in longitudinal studies involving blood draws. Enable 1.0 Incidence Study on Lassa Fever was conducted to estimate Lassa virus infection and disease to inform outbreak preparedness and vaccine research. This nested analysis aimed to explore community engagement strategies employed to retain participants. Methods Enable 1.0 was a multi-site, multi-country, prospective cohort study (December 2020–December 2023) aiming to estimate the incidence of Lassa virus infection and symptomatic Lassa fever disease. In Abakaliki Nigeria, the study tracked Lassa virus seroconversion among 1,000 healthy participants across 10 communities in three Local Government Areas. Community Liaison Officers, village guides, and field workers sustained engagement through fortnightly visits and sensitization meetings. Fieldworkers completed a semi-structured Google Forms questionnaire documenting their experiences and observations across 713 participants, including participant reception, retention threats, and their engagement strategies. The data were analyzed descriptively using IBM-SPSS Statistics Version-25. Results Overall, the study achieved a 90.5% participant retention rate over the 24-month follow-up period across the full cohort of 1,000 participants. Based on fieldworker reports covering 713 participants, 91.4% (652/713) completed the study, while 8.6% (61/713) did not. Reported retention threats (102/713; 14.3%) included inadequate compensation (52/713; 7.3%), family pressure (48/713; 6.7%), and fear of blood rituals (37/713; 5.2%). Reasons for non-completion were relocation (23/713; 3.2%), refusal (15/713; 2.1%), family opposition (6/713; 0.8%), and death (6/713; 0.8%). Key retention strategies included engaging village guides (439/713; 61.6%), incentives (293/713; 41.1%), family support (152/713; 21.3%), and involvement of traditional leaders (151/713; 21.2%). Despite rumours related to blood rituals, sustained community engagement and unscheduled home visits supported by reassurance from the study technical team helped dispel misinformation, with 88.2% (629/713) of participants remaining warmly receptive to fieldworkers throughout the study. Conclusion The Enable 1.0 study demonstrated that high participant retention is achievable in resource-limited high-risk settings when structured community engagement and rapid response mechanisms are integrated into study implementation. Future studies should embed engagement structures, institutionalize rapid responses to rumours, and prioritize transparent communication to strengthen retention and public trust.

Original abstract (English)
In chronic infection, T cell clones recognizing the same epitope are variably expanded, with some clones evolving to become dominant, while others are lost. Clonal expansion and changes in the repertoire reflect both T cell receptor-intrinsic (TCR-intrinsic) and -extrinsic factors, yet the individuality of TCR repertoires makes it difficult to disentangle how TCR sequence shapes clonal behaviors. To address this, we exploited the observation that TCRs can be grouped into clusters based on sequence similarity. Abstract Infection-induced anorexia is a conserved sickness response that contributes to host defense and tolerance1-3. TOX and PD-1 are required for the maintenance of CD4 T cell precursors during chronic infection.

Authors: Jacqueline Berner, Gustavo P de Almeida, Siran Lin, Miriam Kuhlmann, Lara V Donhauser, Ann-Katrin Gerullis, Madlaina von Hoesslin, Christine Wurmser, David B Blumenthal, Anna M Schulz
Original abstract (English)
In contrast with the well-established mechanisms that maintain exhausted CD8 T cells, our knowledge about the differentiation and in particular the molecular control of CD4 T cells in chronic infections is limited. Using the lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV) infection model, we identified that CXCR5+TCF-1+ CD4 T cells, found mainly in secondary lymphoid organs, serve as precursors for the CD4 population. These cells transition into terminally differentiated cells and combine T follicular helper (TFH) cell features (IL-21 expression) with T helper 1 (TH1) cell characteristics (CXCR6 expression and IFN-γ and TNF secretion). In the CXCR5+TCF-1+ population, CD62L+ cells exhibit the highest re-expansion potential in adoptive transfer settings. All subsets express TOX and PD-1, which ensure the maintenance of the CD4 T cell population. Absence of either factor reduces the number of CD62L+ precursors and impairs their further differentiation. Overall, we define a hierarchical framework for CD4 T cell maintenance in chronic infection and reveal common mechanisms that sustain both exhausted CD4 and CD8 T cell responses.

Characterizing the Biological Roles of the Arenavirus Exoribonuclease (exoN) in a Tri-Segmented Pichinde Virus System

Authors: Brigitte H. Flannery, Qin-Feng Huang, Hinh Ly, Yu-Ying Liang
Original abstract (English)
Arenaviruses, such as Lassa virus (LASV) and Junin virus (JUNV), cause severe and often lethal hemorrhagic fevers, for which there are no FDA-approved vaccines or effective therapeutics. The viral nucleoprotein (NP) contains a 3′-5′ exoribonuclease (exoN) domain to suppress type I interferon (IFN-I) induction. However, the precise biological roles of the NP exoN during infection remain poorly characterized for highly pathogenic arenaviruses due to biosafety constraints and challenges in generating viable exoN mutants. Utilizing a recombinant tri-segmented Pichinde virus (PICV) vector (rP18tri) suitable for BSL-2 research, we generated two exoN-deficient GFP reporter viruses, (NPm1)-G and (NPm2)-G, by mutating key catalytic residues, and inserted JUNV NP as a transgene within the mutant backbones. All recombinant viruses replicated efficiently in IFN-deficient Vero cells. In IFN-competent A549 cells, all viruses except for the wild-type reporter virus rP18tri-G failed to undergo multicycle replication, consistent with their differential inhibition of IFN-I responses. In contrast to rP18tri-G, which effectively suppressed IFN-I induction throughout the infection time course, exoN-deficient viruses induced robust early IFN-I responses, whereas expressing JUNV NP as a transgene suppressed early IFN-I induction at 16 hpi but failed to sustain suppression at later time points. Further examination during the first round of viral replication in A549 cells demonstrated that the absence of exoN activity is associated with markedly reduced viral gene expression and decreased infectious particle production. Together, these data demonstrate an essential role for NP exoN in mediating IFN-I suppression and suggest its biological functions in viral gene expression and infectious particle production within IFN-competent cells, highlighting its potential as a broad-spectrum therapeutic target.

Lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus: an overview of a neglected neurotropic zoonosis

Authors: Silvia Cammisa, Valentina Valeriani, Bernadett Pályi, Anita Koroknai, Silvia Meschi, Fabrizio Maggi, Francesca Colavita
Original abstract (English)
Lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV), a rodent-borne mammarenavirus, is endemic worldwide and under-appreciated although it has neurotropism and potential for serious clinical disease. LCMV is commonly carried by the house mouse ( Mus musculus ), thus being distributed globally in urban and rural areas. Transmission occurs primarily through inhalation of aerosolised excreta from infected rodents or contaminated secretions, with additional routes including solid organ transplantation, rarely, and vertical transmission. In humans, LCMV infection may vary from an inapparent or self-limiting febrile disease to more severe diseases such as aseptic meningitis or meningoencephalitis especially in immunocompromised patients. Congenital infections have been reported to cause chorioretinitis, intracranial calcifications, a variety of neurologic malformations, intellectual disability, neurodevelopmental problems, and death of the foetus. To date, 88 congenital cases of LCMV infection have been documented in the literature, and 5 donor-derived transplant-associated clusters involving 17 recipients have been reported worldwide. The objective of this narrative review is to provide a comprehensive and up-to-date overview of LCMV. Specifically, the review summarizes current knowledge on its virological characteristics, epidemiology, pathogenesis, clinical manifestations, diagnosis, treatment, and prevention. Particular attention is given to congenital infection, transplant-associated disease, and the evolving epidemiology of LCMV, with emphasis on its public health relevance within a One Health framework. Laboratory approaches and diagnostic challenges are also discussed, including relevant biosafety considerations. Finally, the review highlights persisting knowledge gaps and identifies priority areas for future research, surveillance, and public health action.

Despite its historical role as a primary recognized cause of human aseptic meningitis, clinical detections have remained sparse in modern surveillance records, leading to diagnostic underestimation. The synthesis highlights how this mammarenavirus presents an expansive clinical spectrum, stretching from self-limiting febrile episodes to severe meningoencephalitis, serious congenital malformations, and fatal donor-derived infections among solid organ transplant recipients. At the molecular level, lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus is classified within the Arenaviridae family and genus Mammarenavirus, forming pleomorphic, enveloped virions measuring 110 to 130 nanometers in diameter with a characteristic sandy interior created by embedded host ribosomes. Its helical nucleocapsid encases an ambisense, single-stranded RNA genome partitioned into two segments designated short and large. The short segment encodes the nucleoprotein, which orchestrates viral transcription and replication, and the glycoprotein precursor cleaved into envelope subunits GP1 and GP2 plus a stable signal peptide. The large segment encodes the matrix zinc-binding protein and an RNA-dependent RNA polymerase. Cellular attachment is heavily governed by the interaction between GP1 and host alpha-dystroglycan, though binding affinities diverge widely across strains; high-affinity variants such as Clone 13, Traub, and WE54 demonstrate distinct tissue tropism and viral persistence in animal models, whereas low-affinity variants such as Armstrong, E350, and WE2.2 rely on alternative attachment factors, including heparan sulfate proteoglycans. The epidemiological maintenance of lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus relies fundamentally on the house mouse, Mus musculus, which sheds immense viral quantities across nasal secretions, saliva, milk, semen, urine, and feces when infected prenatally due to immune tolerance. Secondary rodent species, such as hamsters, rats, and guinea pigs, are also susceptible to infection, although their capacities to sustain prolonged maintenance cycles remain less established. Human transmission takes place through inhalation of aerosolized rodent excreta, handling of contaminated dust, food contamination, or direct animal bites, alongside vertical and donor-derived pathways. Lineages IV and V have been recorded in wild rodents within the Caucasus and Spain, while the divergent Dandenong virus was detected during an Australian transplant cluster. Current European epidemiological investigations reveal an extensive disparity between active viral circulation within reservoir communities and documented human clinical cases, a discrepancy largely driven by absent systematic surveillance, nonspecific symptom profiles, and the scarcity of routine diagnostic panels. Seroepidemiological research from Hungary exemplifies this gap: a retrospective analysis of seventy-four cerebrospinal fluid samples from individuals with central nervous system infections of unidentified origin identified confirmed infections in 2.7 percent of cases, while a subsequent national study spanning 2017 to 2023 documented twenty-three acquired cases and two serologically confirmed congenital infections. Similarly, a retrospective investigation in Piedmont, Italy, evaluating 371 serum samples from patients suspected of rodent-borne infections uncovered an IgG seroprevalence rate of 2.7 percent, demonstrating prior exposure despite absent clinical diagnoses. Seroepidemiologic studies have also recorded exposure in pregnant cohorts in Philadelphia, where IgG seropositivity reached 2.4 to 2.7 percent, alongside seropositivity reaching 13 percent in Austrian zoo workers, 36 percent in Croatian rodents, and 37.5 percent in Slovakian rodents. The mechanisms governing human neuropathogenesis remain incompletely understood and rely primarily on experimental observations gathered from adult mouse and neonatal rat models rather than direct human clinical investigations. Following initial peripheral transmission and systemic replication, the virus accesses the central nervous system, likely via endothelial infection and subsequent disruption of the blood-brain barrier. In adult rodent models, acute central nervous system disease is fundamentally driven by host immunopathology rather than direct cytolytic damage caused by viral replication. Specifically, virus-specific CD8+ T lymphocytes release potent chemokines that trigger massive infiltrations of inflammatory monocytes and neutrophils into meningeal tissues, precipitating vascular leakage and severe intracranial pathology. In neonatal rat paradigms, experimental findings reveal that viral strain characteristics dictate distinct cellular affinities; strain E350 preferentially infects glial structures, whereas WE2.2 and Clone 13 preferentially target neuronal populations. Clinically apparent infections in immunocompetent hosts typically follow a mild or moderate course, though a notable portion of patients experience a characteristic biphasic trajectory marked by initial systemic illness followed by a neurological phase after a temporary symptom-free window. When aseptic meningitis develops, lumbar puncture reveals marked lymphocytic pleocytosis, with white cell counts extending from one hundred to several thousand cells per microliter and lymphocytes constituting greater than 80 percent of the cellular population. Mild to moderate hypoglycorrhachia is frequently documented, occasionally declining to fifteen milligrams per deciliter, or 0.83 millimoles per liter. A smaller subset of patients progresses to meningoencephalitis characterized by altered mental status, seizures, or focal deficits, typically presenting with normal neuroimaging or nonspecific inflammatory patterns. In sharp contrast to the self-limiting course seen in immunocompetent individuals, donor-derived transmission in solid organ transplantation results in catastrophic clinical disease marked by rapid progression, multi-organ failure, and exceptionally high mortality. Worldwide surveillance has documented five distinct transplant clusters encompassing seventeen organ recipients who received kidneys, livers, or lungs from donors harboring unrecognized, transient viral infections. In the United States clusters recognized in 2003 and 2005, eight organ recipients contracted the pathogen, resulting in seven fatalities within nine to seventy-six days post-transplantation; epidemiological tracing in the 2005 event linked the donor to an infected domestic pet hamster carrying an identical lineage I strain. Subsequent transplant transmissions in Massachusetts in 2008 and nationally in 2011 caused four additional patient deaths. In Australia, the novel Dandenong lineage caused the deaths of three female organ recipients within four to six weeks following transplantation from an infected donor. Congenital transmission represents another devastating clinical manifestation of lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus, with eighty-eight confirmed fetal cases recorded in published medical literature since the first description in England in 1955. Maternal exposure during pregnancy, which frequently manifests as a transient or asymptomatic febrile episode, permits transplacental viral dissemination into the developing fetal central nervous system, particularly during early gestational trimesters. A comprehensive clinical review by Pencole and colleagues evaluating sixty-eight congenital cases demonstrated that manifestations are dominated by chorioretinitis in 84 percent of cases, hydrocephalus in 53 percent, microcephaly in 31 percent, ventriculomegaly in 16 percent, and periventricular calcifications in 16 percent. Extracerebral complications include fetal growth restriction, hydrops fetalis, cardiomegaly, ascites, and anemia. Case records, such as an infection following a mouse bite at six weeks of gestation leading to extensive bilateral ventriculomegaly and termination at twenty-two weeks, underscore the critical necessity of including the virus within differential diagnoses for unexplained TORCH-negative congenital neuropathies. Establishing a reliable diagnosis demands a strategic multimodal approach tailored to the infection stage, as clinical signs overlap substantially with other infectious entities and commercially approved tests remain exceedingly rare. Molecular diagnosis using reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction serves as the primary method during early acute viremia and neuroinvasive phases, targeting conserved sequences within the nucleoprotein, large protein, or short genomic segment. A recently validated quantitative assay targeting the short segment demonstrated an analytical limit of detection of 5.6 genome copies per microliter across all five viral lineages. Serological evaluation utilizing enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays and indirect immunofluorescence assays evaluates IgM and IgG antibodies, though single IgM determinations remain merely presumptive and require confirmation via paired sera demonstrating significant titer elevations. Diagnosticians must also navigate cross-reactivity with related mammarenaviruses and recognize that antibody responses are frequently absent in immunosuppressed transplant recipients. Culturing replication-competent virus in BHK-21, L-929, or Vero cells remains limited to research facilities due to weak cytopathic effects and specialized BSL-3 or ABSL-3 containment requirements. Therapeutic options for human infection remain strictly supportive, as no antiviral agents or protective human vaccines have demonstrated definitive efficacy in controlled clinical trials. For uncomplicated meningitis cases, clinical management focuses on analgesia, hydration, and monitoring, while transplantation-associated disease requires urgent reduction of pharmacological immunosuppression accompanied by individualized salvage regimens involving the broad-spectrum guanosine analogue ribavirin. Preclinical evaluations have identified several direct-acting antiviral candidates, including the nucleoside analogues favipiravir, 4′-fluorouridine, and its orally available prodrug VV251, along with cap-dependent endonuclease inhibitors and monoclonal neutralizing antibodies targeting viral surface glycoproteins. Because therapeutic measures remain restricted, public health strategies emphasize primary prevention through comprehensive rodent control programs, safe disposal of household infestations using wet cleaning techniques to suppress airborne aerosols, and the utilization of personal protective equipment. Furthermore, enhanced One Health epidemiological surveillance and rigorous donor exposure questionnaires are imperative to mitigate transmission risks across vulnerable obstetric and organ transplant populations worldwide.
