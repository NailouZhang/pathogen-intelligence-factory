从发热伴血小板减少综合征康复的猫高滴度恢复期血清保护SFTSV感染的小鼠。

High-titer convalescent serum from cats recovered from severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome protects SFTSV-infected mice.
作者： Hirohisa Mekata, Mari Yamamoto, Takeshi Kawaguchi, Tamaki Okabayashi
摘要中文翻译发热伴血小板减少综合征(发热伴血小板减少综合征)是一种由发热伴血小板减少综合征病毒(SFTSV)引起的新兴疾病,与人类和猫的高死亡率相关。目前尚无抗病毒药物或治疗药物可用于预防或治疗猫的发热伴血小板减少综合征。我们假设一些从自然感染中康复的猫会发展出高中和抗体滴度,可能作为被动免疫治疗的恢复期血清来源。我们测量了六名人类发热伴血小板减少综合征康复者的恢复期血清以及14只自然感染后康复的猫的恢复期血清中80%斑块减少中和试验(PRNT 80)滴度,并评估了这些恢复期猫血清在SFTSV感染、干扰素受体缺陷的AG129小鼠中的治疗效果。一些康复的猫表现出高PRNT 80滴度。在感染后早期被动给予高滴度恢复期猫血清可保护SFTSV感染的小鼠。在感染后第2或第3天给予单次剂量可完全保护小鼠,而感染后第4天给予则导致40%的存活率。在重复剂量实验中,早期治疗与对照组相比,在感染后第6天表现出更低的血液病毒RNA水平,存活的小鼠在感染后第13天也显示出较低的病毒RNA水平。另外三只康复猫的血清也改善了存活率。总体而言,这些发现表明一些康复的猫可以发展出高中和抗体滴度,其血清在SFTSV感染的小鼠模型中具有治疗效果,支持进一步评估被动免疫治疗作为猫发热伴血小板减少综合征的治疗策略。

High-titer convalescent serum from cats recovered from severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome protects SFTSV-infected mice.

Authors: Hirohisa Mekata, Mari Yamamoto, Takeshi Kawaguchi, Tamaki Okabayashi
Original abstract (English)
Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is an emerging tick-borne zoonosis caused by severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (SFTSV) and is associated with high case-fatality rates in humans and cats. No vaccines or antiviral drugs are currently available for the prevention or treatment of feline SFTS. We hypothesized that some cats that recover from naturally acquired SFTS develop high neutralizing antibody titers and may serve as a source of convalescent serum for passive immunotherapy. We measured 80% plaque-reduction neutralization test (PRNT 80 ) titers in convalescent sera from six human SFTS survivors and 14 cats that had recovered from naturally acquired SFTS, and then evaluated the therapeutic efficacy of convalescent cat serum in SFTSV-infected, interferon receptor-deficient AG129 mice. Some SFTS-recovered cats exhibited high PRNT 80 titers. Passive administration of high-titer convalescent cat serum early after infection protected SFTSV-infected mice. A single dose given at 2 or 3 days post-infection completely protected mice, whereas treatment at 4 days post-infection resulted in 40% survival. In the repeated-dose experiment, early treatment was associated with lower blood viral RNA levels than controls at 6 dpi, and surviving mice also showed low viral RNA levels at 13 dpi. Sera from three additional SFTS-recovered cats also improved survival. Collectively, these findings indicate that some SFTS-recovered cats can develop high neutralizing antibody titers and that their sera can exert therapeutic effects in an SFTSV-infected mouse model, supporting further evaluation of passive immunotherapy as a treatment strategy for feline SFTS.

Demographic decomposition of changes in notifications of Japanese spotted fever, severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome, and scrub typhus in Japan, 2014-2016 versus 2022-2024

Authors: Satoshi Kutsuna
Original abstract (English)
Notifications of several tick- and mite-borne infections in Japan have risen, and an increasing share of patients are aged 70 and over. Both the growth of the older population and change in age-specific notification rates can produce that pattern, while national and local population decline works in the opposite direction. This study decomposes the change in average annual notifications between 2014-2016 and 2022-2024 into three additive contributions - national population size, age composition across the published age classes, and age-class-specific notification rates - for Japanese spotted fever, severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome and scrub typhus, using the same population series, comparison periods and decomposition rule for all three. The decomposition averages over all permutations of the factors (Shapley-Shorrocks), so no separate interaction term remains and the result does not depend on substitution order. Contributions are reported in cases per year and per 100 baseline cases, alongside a fixed-rate standardised scenario, an eight-region four-factor decomposition, sex-specific analyses, scenario analyses for composition within the open 70-and-over class, a supplementary Poisson interval and a leave-one-year-out range. Only published aggregate statistics are used. The contributions are standardisation quantities, not causal effects, preventable case counts or infection counts, and the protocol fixes in advance how they may and may not be described. Scrub typhus is included as a disease with a different notification trend and is explicitly not a negative control. This registration is made before bulk retrieval of the source tables for all years; the information already viewed is enumerated rather than claiming that no data have been seen.

Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is an emerging tick‐borne zoonosis in East Asia, yet the contribution of animals to virus maintenance and spillover remains incompletely characterized. This review synthesizes current evidence of SFTS virus (SFTSV) infection across domestic livestock, companion animals, wildlife, and captive species, focusing on host range, geographic distribution, diagnostics, and reports of viral RNA and virus isolation. Available data indicate that SFTSV exposure and infection occur across a broad range of animal hosts, supporting a multi-host ecological framework rather than a simple single-reservoir interpretation. Domestic ruminants frequently exhibit high seroprevalence and may function as sentinels of enzootic activity, while occasional virus isolation from cattle demonstrates that viable virus can be recovered from naturally infected livestock, although this does not establish reservoir or amplification competence. Companion animals are considered a potential interface for interspecies transmission, as some studies have documented severe clinical disease, high viral loads, and cat-associated human transmission events supported by varying levels of epidemiological and genomic evidence. Studies in wildlife further document exposure and natural infection across diverse taxa, although their specific roles in viral maintenance remain unresolved. Interpretation of these findings depends heavily on the diagnostic modality: serology primarily indicates prior exposure, RNA detection supports current or recent infection, and virus isolation demonstrates that viable virus was present in the sampled material. Integrating animal infection data within an ecological framework reveals major knowledge gaps related to reservoir competence, infectious shedding, phylogenetic connections among animal, tick, and human cases, and implications for prevention. The review underscores the need for One Health surveillance that incorporates clinical data from companion animals and applies evidence‐based interpretation to improve recognition of animal–human interfaces associated with potential spillover risk.

News Brief
Chinese researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Wuhan Institute of Virology, University of Science and Technology of China, and Kunming Institute of Zoology have published a study in PNAS describing a new bispecific antibody, N2D2‐Fc‐N2C1, that fully protects mice and ferrets from severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (SFTSV) in preclinical trials. The antibody, derived from two nanobodies targeting different regions of the virus’s Gn glycoprotein, neutralizes the virus by blocking attachment and fusion and acts synergistically. In IFNAR‐/‐ mouse models and a clinical‐grade aged ferret model, a low dose of 5 mg/kg given either preventively or one day after infection prevented disease and reduced viral load. The authors highlight the antibody’s advantage over conventional antibody cocktails, noting its single‐molecule format simplifies production and pharmacokinetics. While the results are promising, clinical trials are still needed to assess safety and efficacy in humans.
