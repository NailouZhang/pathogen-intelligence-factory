作者： Lingfang Zhou, Cunguo Chen, Danping Cui

Independent predictors of time from symptom onset to discharge among patients with severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome: a retrospective cohort study

Authors: Lingfang Zhou, Cunguo Chen, Danping Cui
Original abstract (English)
Background Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is a tick-borne bunyaviral infection with heterogeneous recovery trajectories and high case fatality. Most prior studies use in-hospital mortality as the primary endpoint; few quantify independent predictors of recovery duration among surviving patients. This study aimed to identify clinical and laboratory factors independently associated with total time from symptom onset to hospital discharge to guide risk stratification and targeted intervention. Methods We performed a retrospective cohort analysis of 43 consecutive laboratory-confirmed SFTS survivors discharged from Taizhou Hospital of Zhejiang Province (July 2019–April 2025). Demographics, laboratory values, and organ involvement data were extracted from electronic medical records. The primary outcome was total days from initial symptom onset to meeting standardized discharge criteria. Univariate correlation and group comparisons screened candidate variables; backward stepwise multiple linear regression was applied to identify independent predictors after multicollinearity diagnosis via variance inflation factor (VIF). Model performance was assessed using adjusted coefficient of determination (adjusted R 2 ). All statistical tests were two-sided (α=0.05). Results The mean total recovery duration was 16.58 ± 7.86 days. Univariate analysis demonstrated significant correlations between longer discharge time and three variables: prolonged platelet decline duration (r=0.6577, P<0.0001), delayed hospital admission after symptom onset (r=0.7441, P<0.0001), and presence of cardiac/circulatory involvement (P = 0.0002). Multivariate regression confirmed three independent predictors: platelet decline duration (β=0.63, P = 0.0001), cardiac/circulatory involvement (β=5.33, P = 0.0003), and days from onset to hospitalization (β=1.41, P<0.001). The final model had an adjusted R 2 of 0.7762, explaining 77.62% of variance in recovery time; all included variables had VIF<2.5 without severe multicollinearity. Conclusions Prolonged platelet decline duration, cardiovascular organ injury, and delayed hospital admission after symptom onset independently prolong hospitalization duration among discharged SFTS survivors. Dynamic monitoring of platelet counts and cardiovascular biomarkers enables risk stratification. Early hospital presentation, targeted platelet support, and cardioprotective management may shorten disease courses and optimize medical resource utilization for SFTS patients.

Question and background
This study aimed to evaluate how different baseline risk specifications affect the estimation of neighbor-associated components in endemic–epidemic surveillance models, using severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome as a biological negative-control setting.
Design and population
The study analyzed weekly notifications of severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome across 47 Japanese prefectures from 2013 to 2025, fitting negative-binomial endemic–epidemic models under five area-baseline specifications.
Core methods
Negative-binomial endemic–epidemic models were fitted under five area-baseline specifications, ranging from a single national intercept to prefecture-specific effects, using aggregated national surveillance data.
Main results
Under a single national intercept, the neighbor-associated fraction for severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome was 0.149 (95% confidence interval 0.120–0.180), which fell to below 0.002 under prefecture-specific baselines.
Interpretation and novelty
The findings show that the estimated neighbor-associated fraction is highly dependent on the baseline risk specification, and that genuine coupling can only be recovered when the fitted spatial operator aligns with the generating one.
Scientific and public-health significance
These results highlight the importance of carefully specifying baseline risk and spatial weights in surveillance models to avoid misinterpreting statistical dependence as actual transmission.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study's results are limited by the use of aggregated data and the assumption that spatial operators are correctly aligned, with moderate confidence due to the absence of direct transmission evidence.

Question and background
This study aimed to investigate the prevalence of co-infection with Rickettsia monacensis in patients with severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) in Korea, as co-infections between SFTSV and R. monacensis remain poorly characterized despite their shared ecological niches.
Design and population
The study was conducted in Korea between 2024 and 2026, analyzing clinical samples from 129 patients diagnosed with SFTS. The focus was on identifying co-infections with Rickettsia monacensis in these patients.
Core methods
Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and sequencing were used to detect SFTSV and Rickettsia monacensis. One-step reverse-transcription PCR was performed for SFTSV, while rickettsial nested PCR targeting outer membrane protein A (ompA) was used for R.
Main results
Among the 129 SFTS cases, nine patients were found to be co-infected with Rickettsia monacensis, resulting in a prevalence rate of 7.9%. Phylogenetic analysis showed that the seven sequences identified clustered closely with R.

The findings indicate that co-infection with Rickettsia monacensis is a notable occurrence in SFTS patients in Korea, highlighting the need for further investigation into the clinical implications of such co-infections.
Scientific and public-health significance
These results emphasize the importance of considering co-infections in the differential diagnosis of tick-borne febrile illnesses and may inform improved diagnostic and management strategies for SFTS in Korea.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its reliance on molecular testing and the potential for false negatives, but the prevalence rate of 7.9% is based on direct testing of clinical samples, providing moderate confidence in the findings.

A retrospective cohort study at Taizhou Hospital of Zhejiang Province, published on November 10, 2026, analyzed recovery durations among 43 surviving patients with severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) who were discharged between July 2019 and April 2025. The study identified three clinical factors that independently predicted prolonged recovery: time from symptom onset to hospital admission, duration of platelet decline, and cardiac or circulatory system involvement. These factors collectively accounted for 77.62% of the variance in recovery time, as determined by a backward stepwise multiple linear regression model. The mean recovery duration was 16.58 ± 7.86 days, with a median of 15 days. SFTS is an acute bunyavirus infection with case fatality rates ranging from 6% to 30% across regions. The study highlights the importance of these factors in understanding hospital resource use and rehabilitation needs, as no licensed vaccine or targeted antiviral medication exists for SFTS. The research was approved by the hospital's ethics committee and used de-identified medical records without direct patient contact.

Prior clinical research has primarily concentrated on all-cause in-hospital mortality as a binary primary endpoint, identifying advancing age, coagulopathy, and multiple organ injuries as key prognostic factors for fatal outcomes. The authors proposed distinct pathophysiological mechanisms to explain how these three independent factors influence clinical recovery trajectories. Severe fever with thrombocytopenia virus directly targets and infects vascular endothelial cells, triggering upregulated expression of adhesion molecules and the extensive release of systemic inflammatory mediators that elevate microthrombosis risk and vascular permeability. Myocardial impairment is driven by direct viral myocardial invasion causing mitochondrial dysfunction, alongside cytokine storm-mediated myocardial contractile suppression mediated by elevated circulating interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha. The resulting systemic hypotension, subclinical heart failure, and microvascular ischemia impair circulatory perfusion and substantially delay physiological convalescence. Simultaneously, viral replication suppresses bone marrow megakaryopoiesis while consuming circulating platelets through microvascular thrombi. Because circulating platelets actively participate in inflammatory signaling, microcirculatory perfusion, and the delivery of bioactive growth factors vital for tissue regeneration, extended platelet depletion impedes organ repair, establishing a bidirectional pathophysiological loop that prolongs inpatient rehabilitation and delays clinical recovery. The investigators highlighted several methodological limitations that necessitate a cautious interpretation of their analytical findings. The study utilized a single-center retrospective design with a sample size restricted to 43 surviving patients, limiting statistical power to identify weaker biomarkers and restricting generalizability across geographic regions with differing demographics and viral transmission intensities. Relying on patient recall for symptom onset timing introduced potential retrospective memory bias, while data completeness depended on variable electronic clinical documentation. Furthermore, key virological and immunological markers—including viral load, serial cytokine measurements, and lymphocyte subpopulation profiling—were not systematically collected. Crucially, the authors emphasized a notable methodological constraint: platelet decline duration was defined as the time until platelet counts recovered to 100 × 10^9 per liter, which was itself an explicit prerequisite criterion for hospital discharge. News Brief
A study published on November 9, 2026, by Bioengineer.org highlights the potential expansion of tick habitats transmitting Severe Fever with Thrombocytopenia Syndrome (SFTS) in eastern China due to climate shifts. Conducted between 2019 and 2024 in Jiangsu Province, the research analyzed tick distribution and climate-driven changes in habitat suitability. The study found that Haemaphysalis longicornis accounted for 89.1% of collected ticks, while Haemaphysalis flava made up 9.8%. Future projections under the SSP245 scenario suggest a 336.5% increase in high-suitability areas for Haemaphysalis longicornis between 2021 and 2040, and a 640.3% increase between 2041 and 2060. For Haemaphysalis flava, the increases were 91.4% and 118.8% respectively. These findings indicate a significant shift in tick habitats, which could increase the risk of SFTS transmission. The study emphasizes the importance of integrating climate-sensitive vector surveillance into SFTS prevention strategies within a One Health framework.

Ticks represent critical vectors of zoonotic pathogens across East Asia, playing a key role in the transmission of severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome. Despite the public health importance of these vectors, comprehensive evidence detailing their spatial distribution, seasonal activity, and climate-driven habitat suitability has remained limited, particularly at the provincial scale. To address this knowledge gap, researchers Hongliang Chu, Zhiwei Cheng, Zhiming Wu, Yihan Lu, and Ye Yao analyzed surveillance records of free-living ticks collected throughout Jiangsu Province from 2019 to 2024. The authors coupled field monitoring with an optimized MaxEnt modeling framework to characterize environmental determinants and assess projected climate-driven changes in habitat suitability. Original source text (English)
Researchers in China found a newly identified tick-borne virus in 10.4 percent of 3,163 patients they tested. The virus, called Asian longhorned tick nairovirus (ALTNV), also turned up in ticks collected from 15 provinces. Patients infected with ALTNV alone recovered. Among 38 patients also infected with the virus behind severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS), seven died. The tick species that carried ALTNV most often, the Asian longhorned tick, has spread across parts of the United States. The Chinese study tested only patients and ticks in China, so it does not show whether the virus is present in U.S. ticks. Searching for a missing cause The work was led by researchers at the State Key Laboratory of Pathogen and Biosecurity in Beijing, with Xiao-Ai Zhang as first author. They reported it in September 2026 in a letter titled “ A Zoonotic Orthonairovirus from Febrile Patients in China .” Their starting point was a gap in diagnosis. More than 30 percent of patients clinically diagnosed with SFTS test negative for Dabie bandavirus (DBV), the virus that causes the disease. The team screened 3,163 patients with fever and a history of tick exposure . They looked for ALTNV genetic material or IgM antibodies, which the immune system makes early in an infection. Among patients who had tested negative for DBV, 15.1 percent tested positive for ALTNV. ALTNV belongs to the genus Orthonairovirus in the family Nairoviridae . Its proteins share less than 80 percent of their amino acids with other species in that genus. Mild alone, worse with SFTS The researchers described 56 patients with ALTNV alone. Fatigue affected 84 percent of them, and 80 percent had gastrointestinal symptoms. Low platelet counts, known as thrombocytopenia, appeared in 38 percent. Raised levels of the liver enzymes called aminotransferases appeared in 36 percent. All of these patients recovered without lasting effects. In the 38 patients carrying both ALTNV and DBV, coinfection was linked to more severe illness. Respiratory symptoms affected 61 percent of them, compared with 38 percent of patients with DBV alone. Kidney impairment affected 84 percent of the coinfected patients, compared with 66 percent. Seven of them, or 18.4 percent, died. The two viruses circulate in the same areas and share a tick vector. The authors call for better tests to tell these tick-borne viruses apart where SFTS is common. Ticks from 15 provinces The team also tested 47,428 ticks gathered across 15 Chinese provinces. ALTNV genetic material turned up in 1.29 percent of them. The Asian longhorned tick, Haemaphysalis longicornis , carried the virus most often, at 1.75 percent. In laboratory experiments, the species passed ALTNV to mice. Outside Asia, this tick can reduce production in dairy cattle by 25 percent in some regions of New Zealand and Australia, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) noted in 2018. A tick spreading in the U.S. New Jersey was the first U.S. state to report the tick, on a sheep in August 2017, according to the CDC. A single female can reproduce without mating. As a result, hundreds or thousands of ticks can build up on one animal or in one area. Ben Beard, Ph.D., was then deputy director of the CDC’s Division of Vector-Borne Diseases. “The full public health and agricultural impact of this tick discovery and spread is unknown,” Beard said in 2018. By October 2025, the U.S. Department of Agriculture had documented the tick in 21 states, with Kansas the most recent. There, it was found on a dog in Franklin County, according to the Kansas Department of Agriculture and the Kansas Department of Health and Environment ( KDHE ). The ticks have been collected from New York south to Georgia and west to Missouri. They are not competent vectors of the bacterium behind Lyme disease , the USDA’s Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service ( APHIS ) states. Tick surveillance programs In cattle, the species can transmit Theileria orientalis Ikeda, a parasite responsible for bovine theileriosis. U.S. ticks passed it to cattle in the laboratory, and it has been detected in ticks collected in the field. In 2025, the Connecticut Agricultural Experiment Station (CAES) reported the first evidence in the U.S. of a longhorned tick infected with Ehrlichia chaffeensis . This bacterium causes human monocytic ehrlichiosis. Dr. Goudarz Molaei is a research scientist and medical entomologist at the CAES and an adjunct associate professor at the Yale School of Public Health . “A rigorous program in mitigating risks should include expanded tick surveillance programs, strict measures to inspect animals, plants, and other imported materials at ports of entry, accurate and proper identification of exotic ticks and pathogens, effective eradication and prevention measures, and the ability to test exotic ticks for both native and nonnative pathogens,” Molaei said. In 2019, an Asian longhorned tick in Virginia was found to be infected with Bourbon virus, the Kansas agencies noted.
