Complex exposure-response relationships between meteorological factors and severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome risk.

Authors: Guangju Mo, Chun-Yue Ai, Meng Shang, Sheng-Ping Dou, Wen-Yu Wang, Hao-Qiang Ji, Huai-Ping Zhu, Qi-Yong Liu
Original abstract (English)
OBJECTIVE: Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is an emerging tick-borne infectious disease influenced by meteorological factors. This study aimed to characterize the complex exposure-response relationships between multiple meteorological factors and SFTS risk in Anhui Province, China. METHODS: Monthly SFTS cases and meteorological data from 2011 to 2023 were analyzed. Candidate lag structures were evaluated using negative binomial generalized additive models (NB-GAM), followed by Bayesian kernel machine regression (BKMR) to assess the joint and individual effects of meteorological factors. Pairwise interactions were quantified using posterior BKMR interaction contrasts and pooled across cities using random-effects meta-analysis. Regional subgroup analyses were also performed. RESULTS: A total of 5,715 SFTS cases were reported, with 84.74% occurring among farmers, and eight cities accounted for 95.56% of all cases. The lag01 showed the best overall model performance and was selected for BKMR analyses. BKMR revealed heterogeneous joint and individual exposure-response patterns across cities, with average temperature and atmospheric pressure showing prominent contributions. However, formal interaction analyses provided no clear evidence of pairwise interactions, and all ten pooled interaction contrasts had 95% confidence intervals including zero. Regional subgroup analysis suggested a stronger joint meteorological effect in the Jianghuai hilly area [0.27 (95% CI 0.03-0.50)] than in the Southern Mountains Region [0.03 (95% CI -0.08-0.15)]. CONCLUSIONS: Meteorological factors showed complex joint exposure-response relationships with SFTS risk, with substantial geographic variation across Anhui Province. Although no clear pairwise interactions were identified, regional differences in joint meteorological effects highlight the importance of considering local ecological and climatic conditions in SFTS early warning and prevention strategies.

Authors: Xue-Qing Wang, Xiao-Man Zhong, Jing-Jing Hao, He-Bing Guo, Ning-Ning Yin, Jing-Yuan Liu, Lin Pu
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is a life-threatening tick-borne viral disease with high mortality and limited treatment options. Understanding its transmission routes is essential for effective prevention and control. Case presentation We report a case of a 55-year-old woman from Dalian, Liaoning Province, China, who developed high fever, malaise and diarrhea five days after being bitten by a Siberian weasel ( Mustela sibirica ). A diagnosis of SFTS was made on day 12 after SFTS virus was detected in peripheral blood by metagenomic next-generation sequencing. Despite timely antiviral and supportive treatment, the patient rapidly progressed to multiple organ dysfunction and had a poor prognosis. She had no known exposure to ticks or other animals, raising strong suspicion of SFTSV transmission through the weasel bite. Conclusion This is a case of SFTS potentially associated with a Mustela sibirica bite, suggesting a possible but unconfirmed zoonotic transmission route. These findings highlight the urgent need for surveillance of animal hosts in endemic regions to better understand transmission pathways.

Authors: Shiori Egashira, Rokusuke Yoshikawa, Shoko Nogusa, Aya Yamaguchi, Yoshikuni Onodera, Nao Jonai, Fumihiko Takeshita, Jiro Yasuda, Etsushi Kuroda
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is a tick-borne disease caused by Bandavirus dabieense , also known as the SFTS virus (SFTSV). SFTS’ fatality rate is 10%–27%, and SFTS cases and the distribution of outbreaks are increasing, but there is no approved vaccine to prevent SFTS. Here, we examined the immunogenicity and efficacy of three lipid nanoparticle-mRNA (LNP-mRNA) vaccine candidates encoding SFTSV glycoprotein N (Gn), glycoprotein C (Gc), or nucleocapsid protein (NP) (LNP-mRNA-Gn, LNP-mRNA-Gc, and LNP-mRNA-NP, respectively) in mice. Immunization with LNP-mRNA-Gn, LNP-mRNA-Gc, or LNP-mRNA-NP alone completely protected Ifnar1 – / – mice from lethal SFTSV challenge. LNP-mRNA-Gn and LNP-mRNA-Gc also induced SFTSV-neutralizing activity in Ifnar1 – / – mice, whereas LNP-mRNA NP did not. The efficacy of LNP-mRNA-NP was abrogated by the in vivo depletion of CD4 + cells, but not CD8 + cells. These results highlight three promising candidates for effective vaccines against SFTS, each possessing a distinct protective mechanism.

The study was a preclinical investigation examining three lipid nanoparticle-mRNA (LNP-mRNA) vaccine candidates encoding SFTSV glycoprotein N (Gn), glycoprotein C (Gc), or nucleocapsid protein (NP) in mice. The study was conducted in a laboratory setting using Ifnar1 – / – mice as the host.

Original abstract (English)
Introduction Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS), caused by the SFTS virus (SFTSV), is a highly fatal tick-borne zoonosis that poses a substantial public health threat. Although livestock husbandry environments represent important ecological settings for viral maintenance and circulation, molecular surveillance within these settings remains insufficient. Methods We conducted an annual systematic entomological and molecular survey from March to October between 2023 and 2025 in South Korea, targeting 204 environments adjacent to cattle, horse, deer, goat, and sheep farms. Results Among 101,006 field-collected ticks, Haemaphysalis longicornis was the most abundant species (90.62%), followed by Haemaphysalis flava (8.20%) and Ixodes nipponensis (1.10%). Molecular screening of the SFTSV S-segment revealed 65 positive pools, corresponding to a minimum infection rate (MIR) of 0.06%, with the highest MIR observed in cattle-associated tick populations (0.09%). Phylogenetic analysis demonstrated that all identified strains in this study clustered within the Japanese/Korean clade of genotype B2. Six out of seven contemporary strains shared 100.00% nucleotide identity and were identical to historical domestic human isolates from 2014, demonstrating high longitudinal sequence conservation within the analyzed partial S-segment region. Concurrently, detection of SFTSV strain 24CC401 in I. nipponensis larvae provided indirect field-based evidence consistent with potential transovarial persistence. This larval isolate displayed a distinct molecular profile with a 0.65% nucleotide divergence and two nonsynonymous mutations (G129E and V158E). Discussion Our findings indicate that specific genotype B2 lineages were detected in livestock-adjacent environments, suggesting a potential ecological association with livestock farming. Although the observed sequence conservation may support continued evaluation of conserved S-segment regions for molecular surveillance, the functional significance of the mutations identified in the larval isolate remains to be determined. Reduced serum albumin levels may potentially influence the pharmacokinetic characteristics of highly protein-bound drugs; however, therapeutic drug monitoring of isavuconazole was unavailable in this case. Invasive pulmonary aspergillosis (IPA) is one of the most common secondary fungal infections in SFTS. Studies indicate that the incidence of IPA in SFTS patients is approximately 30%, with significantly increased mortality rates observed in patients co-infected with Aspergillus species. 13 In this case, right lung lesions improved after voriconazole treatment, whereas left lung lesions continued to progress and showed poor response to broad-spectrum antibacterial therapy. Ultimately, Rhizopus microsporus was identified in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid using metagenomic next-generation sequencing (mNGS), consistent with breakthrough mucormycosis. Recent studies suggest that prolonged exposure to broad-spectrum antibiotics may disrupt intestinal and respiratory microbiota homeostasis through the gut–lung axis. 14 , 15 This may have contributed to the development of breakthrough mucormycosis in this patient. In addition, persistent immunosuppression following SFTSV infection may further promote invasive growth of Mucorales. Although breakthrough mucormycosis has been widely reported in COVID-19-associated fungal infections, it remains relatively rare in SFTS. 16 In this case, early sputum culture only detected Aspergillus fumigatus, while subsequent NGS analysis of lavage fluid identified Rhizopus microsporus, highlighting the limited sensitivity of conventional sputum culture for deep pulmonary Aspergillus infections. For SFTS patients, particularly those with severe thrombocytopenia, progressive pulmonary lesions, or poor response to voriconazole therapy, breakthrough mycotic infections should be suspected; mNGS aids in early etiological diagnosis. This patient had a pre-existing chronic hepatitis B background and had experienced SFTSV infection, multiple infections, and prolonged anti-infective therapy, leading to a progressive decline in hepatic reserve function. After approximately one month of isavuconazole treatment, the patient developed significant jaundice, elevated transaminase levels, coagulation disorders, with total bilirubin rising to 217.0 μmol/L and PT and APTT markedly prolonged, indicating severe liver injury accompanied by pronounced coagulation abnormalities and a trend toward progression to liver failure. The patient’s HBV-DNA levels remained consistently low, with no evidence of viral reactivation or other definitive factors of liver injury such as biliary obstruction. Although the patient had previously received voriconazole and broad-spectrum antibiotics, these agents had been discontinued before the onset of the marked hepatic deterioration. In addition, nebulized amphotericin B had been discontinued, and isavuconazole was the only ongoing antifungal agent at the time of hepatic deterioration. Following discontinuation of isavuconazole and initiation of supportive therapies including artificial liver support, liver function and coagulation parameters gradually improved. To further evaluate the causal relationship between isavuconazole and liver injury, this study employed the RUCAM scoring system for assessment. 17 The RUCAM score was 8, indicating a probable causal relationship between isavuconazole and liver injury. This assessment was supported by the temporal association, improvement after drug discontinuation, exclusion of alternative etiologies, and previously reported hepatotoxicity of isavuconazole. Notably, the patient’s liver function gradually recovered after discontinuation of isavuconazole and initiation of artificial liver support therapy. Given the need for continued antifungal treatment and the limited therapeutic alternatives, isavuconazole was subsequently reintroduced under close monitoring of liver function and coagulation parameters. No recurrence of significant liver injury or further severe deterioration of liver function was observed, while the pulmonary lesions were largely resolved. Although the absence of recurrent liver injury upon rechallenge does not completely exclude an association with isavuconazole, This case illustrates the potential role of cautious rechallenge in highly selected patients when continued antifungal therapy is necessary, therapeutic alternatives are limited, and the potential benefits are judged to outweigh the risks, provided that close monitoring is implemented.
