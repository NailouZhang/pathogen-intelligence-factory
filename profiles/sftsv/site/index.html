Droplet digital PCR-based nucleic acid amplification test for improved molecular and clinical diagnosis of severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome patients

Authors: Yujin Baek, Minchae Koo, Yong Shin, Chang-Seop Lee, Il-Hwan Kim
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is caused by SFTS virus (SFTSV) and is characterized by high mortality and non-specific clinical manifestations, which make early and accurate molecular diagnosis essential. This study aims to investigate whether reverse transcription droplet digital PCR (RT-ddPCR) provides improved diagnosis of SFTS compared to existing molecular diagnostic platforms. Detection limits of RT-ddPCR, reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR), and reverse transcription nested PCR (RT-nested PCR) were determined using RNA extracted from metrologically validated SFTSV nucleic acid reference material. Then, diagnostic performance metrics of each PCR method were compared using RNA from 29 SFTS patients and 29 disease controls with scrub typhus. Statistical analyses of key laboratory parameters were performed to identify markers significantly correlated with SFTSV load, as defined by RNA copy number, in early SFTS patients. RT-ddPCR showed the lowest detection limit of 3.82 copies/μL among the three PCR methods. The sensitivity of RT-ddPCR was 82.75% in clinical samples, which was higher than that of RT-qPCR at 72.41%. The specificity of RT-ddPCR was 100% compared to 89.66% for RT-nested PCR. Overall, RT-ddPCR demonstrated the highest diagnostic performance among the three PCR methods, with an accuracy of 91.38% and an area under the curve value of 0.91. A strong correlation between viral RNA loads and laboratory parameters was observed in the early stage of SFTS, particularly with high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, aspartate aminotransferase, alanine aminotransferase, lactate dehydrogenase, and activated partial thromboplastin time. Our findings demonstrate that a novel molecular diagnosis method using RT-ddPCR shows improved diagnostic performance for detecting SFTSV RNA from SFTS patients and potentially contributes to accurate early diagnosis and enhanced clinical diagnosis. IMPORTANCE Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is a high-mortality tick-borne infection caused by SFTS virus (SFTSV), and reliable molecular diagnosis is essential for patient care in endemic regions. Existing PCR-based assays for SFTS show limited sensitivity and specificity, particularly in patients with suspected or early-stage disease. Here, we describe the development and analytical validation of a reverse transcription droplet digital PCR (RT-ddPCR) assay for the detection and quantification of SFTSV RNA from patient sera. By using metrologically validated viral nucleic acid reference materials and clinical samples, this assay demonstrates significantly improved sensitivity and overall diagnostic performance compared with conventional reverse transcription quantitative PCR and reverse transcription nested PCR. RT-ddPCR can provide clinical microbiology laboratories with a useful tool to enhance early and reliable molecular diagnosis of SFTS.

Question and background
This study was needed to characterize the evolving epidemiological patterns of severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) in China, as its incidence has risen steadily since 2009 with expanding geographic scope.
Design and population
This retrospective descriptive study included clinically diagnosed and laboratory-confirmed SFTS cases from the China Information System for Disease Control and Prevention (CISDCP) between 2010 and 2024.
Core methods
Joinpoint regression was used to analyze incidence trends, and case fatality rate (CFR) differences were assessed by chi-squared or Fisher’s exact tests, with temporal trends evaluated by linear regression.
Main results
During 2010–2024, 30 provinces reported 34,055 cases, with an incidence rate showing a fluctuating upward trend (AAPC: 43.28%; 95% CI: 43.23%–43.33%; P < 0.01). The average CFR was 4.79%, increasing with age to 9.15% among those aged 85 years and above.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted the findings as indicating a marked increase in reported incidence and considerable geographical expansion of SFTS, with the rapid rise in cases in provinces like Anhui suggesting a need to reevaluate ecological and social transmission factors.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings inform surveillance priorities by highlighting the need to monitor geographic expansion and elderly mortality risk, and to reevaluate ecological and social factors driving transmission in newly affected areas.
Limitations and evidence strength
The evidence strength is moderate due to the retrospective descriptive design and reliance on surveillance data, which may have limitations in case ascertainment and reporting completeness.

Macrogenomic analysis showcases the diversity of tick RNA viruses in Mentougou, Beijing, China

Authors: Qiangqiang Shi, Qinqin Song, Xuan Liu, guoyong mei, Chen Gao, HaiJun Du, Zhiqiang Xia, Mi Liu, J H Song, Lifeng Zhang
Original abstract (English)
Background Ticks are the second most significant vector of human pathogens worldwide, with 911 documented species globally and a broad distributed across China. Currently, over 160 tick-borne viruses (TBVs) have been identified, several of which pose substantial threats to human health, such as Dabie bandavirus, Jingmen tick virus, Alongshan virus, Songling virus, Beiji nairovirus, and Langya henipavirus, raising increasing global attention. Despite their significance, the diversity of TBVs in Beijing remains poorly characterized. Methods In this study, we conducted metagenomic sequencing on tick samples collected from Mentougou District, Beijing. The obtained reads were subjected to quality control, de novo assembly, and viral sequence identification, followed by phylogenetic and evolutionary analyses. Results Our results identified 19 distinct viral species spanning 12 families, including Hepelivirales,Solemoviridae,Mymonaviridae,Nodaviridae,Permutotetraviridae,Phenuiviridae,Rhabdoviridae,Tombusviridae,Totiviridae,Peribunyaviridae,Flaviviridae,Nodaviridae,Tymoviridae,Tombusviridae . Among these, six novel viruses from five virus families were discovered. A potential pathogen, Tick jingmen-like virus, was also detected in the selected pathogens. These findings underscore the remarkable diversity of RNA viruses harbored by ticks in Mentougou District. Conclusions Our research findings reveal a previously unrecognized diversity of tick-borne viruses in the Mentougou District, Beijing, and provide essential baseline data for informing future surveillance strategies and guiding prevention and control of tick-borne diseases in the Beijing metropolitan area.

Tick alert near livestock farms as SFTS fatality rate hits 18.5%

News Brief
South Korea is issuing heightened tick alerts for livestock workers as summer temperatures increase tick activity. The Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency has urged workers near barns and pastures to wear protective clothing and use repellents following confirmed cases of Severe Fever with Thrombocytopenia Syndrome (SFTS). Original source text (English)
Published : July 29, 2026 - 11:00:00 As rising summer temperatures drive up tick activity, livestock farmers working in and around barns and pastures face heightened risk. Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome, or SFTS, has no approved vaccine or treatment, making prevention — through proper work clothing and repellents — the only reliable defense. The Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency on Wednesday urged workers near livestock barns and pastures to wear long-sleeved clothing and boots, secure their cuffs and trouser hems, and apply tick repellents according to label instructions. South Korea is home to a variety of hard ticks, including the Asian longhorned tick, distributed across forests, grasslands and other outdoor environments. These ticks can transmit not only SFTS but also the pathogens behind Lyme disease, anaplasmosis and babesiosis. SFTS is a zoonotic disease transmitted through the bite of an infected tick. Both humans and livestock can develop symptoms including high fever, chills, muscle pain, vomiting and diarrhea, and severe cases can be fatal. According to the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency, 2,065 SFTS cases were reported in South Korea between 2013 and 2024, resulting in 381 deaths — a cumulative fatality rate of 18.5%. With no approved vaccine or treatment available, avoiding tick bites remains the most critical preventive measure. Livestock farms are particularly vulnerable because barns and grazing areas often border grasslands and brush where ticks thrive. Ticks in tall grass latch onto people and animals to feed, so workers in these areas should minimize exposed skin. Before heading out, workers should put on long-sleeved work clothes and boots and fasten cuffs and trouser hems tightly. Tick repellents should be applied at the recommended dosage and frequency. During work, sitting or lying directly on grass should be avoided, as should contact with wild animals. After finishing work, workers should carefully check their bodies and clothing for attached ticks. Grass around livestock barns should be cleared regularly, and livestock should also be inspected for ticks. Anyone who develops symptoms such as high fever, vomiting or diarrhea within two weeks of outdoor work should seek medical attention immediately and inform the doctor of any time spent working near barns or in grassy areas. Since 2023, the quarantine agency has been collecting and testing ticks at 76 sites near livestock farms across the country, monitoring the distribution of tick-borne pathogens year-round. No SFTS virus genetic material has been detected in ticks collected so far this year. However, the agency is stepping up regional surveillance as tick activity peaks during the summer months. The number of ticks testing positive for SFTS virus genes was 28 in 2023, 32 in 2024 and five last year. "We will continue to monitor hard ticks and tick-borne pathogens near livestock farms nationwide and promptly share information needed for prevention and disease control," agency chief Choe Jeong-rok said.
