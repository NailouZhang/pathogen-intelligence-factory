Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus: immune evasion, therapeutic landscape, and vaccine prospects

Authors: Amin Ullah, Mei Yang, Lili Li, Jian Shang
Original abstract (English)
Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (SFTSV) is an emerging tick-borne bunyavirus that poses a growing public health threat. National surveillance data from China (2010-2023) report an overall case fatality rate of approximately 4.8%. However, hospital-based cohorts of severe or critically ill patients report substantially higher rates, ranging from 12% to 30%. Despite its designation as a WHO priority pathogen in 2017, no vaccine has been approved, although a first-in-human Phase I/II trial of an mRNA vaccine candidate was initiated in 2025, and favipiravir, the only authorized antiviral (Japan, 2024), remains of debated clinical benefit, leaving a widening gap between mechanistic understanding and clinical translation. This gap is rooted in the virus’s ability to dismantle the very immune pathways on which vaccines and antivirals depend. The non-structural protein NSs sequesters TBK1, IRF3, and STAT1/STAT2 into viroplasm-like structures, while the nucleoprotein NP induces autophagy-mediated degradation of cGAS and MAVS, collectively diminishing type I interferon responses, impairing B-cell and T-cell immunity, and permitting the dysregulated cytokine storm that is associated with multi-organ failure. These same mechanisms explain why vaccine candidates that perform well in immunocompromised (IFNAR−/− or STAT2−/−) mouse models have not translated to immunocompetent or aged populations, and why antiviral efficacy collapses at high viral loads or late treatment initiation. Unlike prior reviews that treat pathogenesis, antivirals, and vaccines as separate topics, this review integrates them into a single framework linking viral immune evasion to the failure modes of current countermeasures, and identifies the specific obstacles that must be overcome before clinical translation: missing correlates of protection, immunocompromised animal models, and reassortment-driven antigenic diversity. We conclude by outlining priority directions, including AI-assisted antigen design, host-directed and combination therapies, standardized animal models, and integrated One Health surveillance.

Explainable machine learning integrating virological and immune-inflammatory biomarkers for in-hospital mortality prediction in severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome

Authors: Shu-Wen Ding, Ruize Ma, Yan-Li Xu, Ling Lin, Zhi-Hai Chen
Original abstract (English)
Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS), characterized by an excessive inflammatory response and impaired adaptive immunity, is associated with a high risk of mortality. This study aims to establish and validate a machine learning (ML)-based predictive model integrating clinical and immune-inflammatory biomarkers for in-hospital mortality in patients with SFTS. A total of 238 patients, including 177 survivors and 61 non-survivors, were divided by stratified random sampling into a training cohort ( n = 167) and an internal validation cohort ( n = 71) at a 7:3 ratio. Features were selected from the intersection of Boruta, Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO), and random forest recursive feature elimination (RF-RFE). Model hyperparameters were optimized exclusively within the training cohort using repeated stratified five-fold cross-validation, with the internal validation cohort reserved for final evaluation. Model performance was then assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), calibration plots, and decision curve analysis (DCA). SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) was employed to improve model interpretability. To facilitate individualized risk estimation, the selected model was presented as a nomogram and incorporated into an interactive web-based calculator. The final logistic regression model incorporated four predictors: SFTSV RNA load, CD3+CD4+ T-cell count, IL-6, and PCT. In the internal validation cohort, the area under the ROC curve was 0.841 (95% CI: 0.736–0.930). Based on the maximum Youden index in the training cohort, the optimal cutoff probability was established at 0.2053. When this cutoff was applied to the validation cohort, the model yielded a sensitivity of 0.944 (95% CI: 0.742–0.990) and specificity of 0.698 (95% CI: 0.565–0.805). The model also demonstrated a Brier score of 0.139 (95% CI: 0.094–0.189) and potential net clinical benefit. We present an interpretable predictive model based on viral, immune, and inflammatory biomarkers for predicting in-hospital mortality in patients with SFTS. A multicenter prospective evaluation is highly recommended prior to its clinical use.

Original abstract (English)
Small-mammal-associated zoonoses such as haemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome, scrub typhus, leptospirosis and severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome are common notifiable infections in north-east Asia, but their burden in the region is poorly understood. Human cases are counted by separate surveillance systems with different case definitions, while infection in the small mammals and ectoparasites that maintain these pathogens is reported in a largely separate literature. The two are rarely measured in the same place and period, and the inputs a burden estimate needs are not reported consistently. This systematic review brings the two together for pathogens grouped into five transmission guilds. For the Republic of Korea, we extract all reported human, host and vector estimates for the four guilds other than flea-borne pathogens, and pool prevalence where studies are comparable. For each pathogen and country or area, we record which burden inputs are reported and which are missing. We also describe studies that measured infection in animals and disease in people in the same area. The expected outputs are pooled prevalence estimates for the Republic of Korea, an evidence map for the rest of the region, an inventory of missing burden inputs, and a minimum set of reporting items for future studies. The review is a first step towards a regional burden estimate.
