Development of anti-Gn monoclonal antibodies for magnetic nanoparticle-coupled lateral flow detection of severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus

Authors: Yu-Chih Chang, Hiroshi Shimoda, Yumiko Yamada, Day-Yu Chao, Marla Anggita, Ken Maeda, Ching‐Yu Tseng, Md. Din Islam, Tsai Ching-Hsiu, Jae U. Jung
Original abstract (English)
Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (SFTSV) is an emerging tick-borne zoonotic pathogen associated with high mortality in humans and silent circulation in animals, highlighting the need for rapid, field-deployable diagnostics. Current molecular and serological methods remain dependent on specialized infrastructure, limiting their use in resource-limited settings. Here, we developed a magnetic nanoparticle-enhanced lateral flow assay targeting the SFTSV glycoprotein Gn (Gn-LFA) for rapid antigen detection. A panel of anti-Gn monoclonal antibodies was generated and systematically characterized for isotype, epitope recognition, binding affinity, and viral capture efficiency. A monoclonal antibody exhibiting strong Gn recognition and antigen-capturing activity was selected as the detection antibody and conjugated to magnetic nanoparticles, while three additional antibodies were used as capture antibodies on the test line to improve antigen recognition. The Gn-LFA detected recombinant Gn proteins produced in both mammalian and plant expression systems and demonstrated compatibility across sera from multiple host species. The assay detected as little as 20 ng of recombinant Gn protein or approximately 5 × 10 5 FFU of SFTSV within 45 min without requiring specialized instrumentation. Collectively, this study provides proof of concept for a rapid and low-cost approach to SFTSV antigen detection and supports its further development for potential applications in resource-limited settings and One Health surveillance.

Question and background
Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is associated with substantial mortality, and shock with cardiac involvement is an important contributor to fatal outcomes. However, biomarker correlates, temporal trajectories, and early warning signals of clinically adjudicated cardiogenic shock (CS) in SFTS remain incompletely defined.
Design and population
We retrospectively analyzed 775 laboratory-confirmed SFTS patients admitted to Nanjing Second Hospital from April 2020 through December 2024. The requirement for informed consent was waived because of the retrospective design and anonymized data collection.
Core methods
Time to first clinically adjudicated CS after admission was analyzed using univariable Cox regression, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), and multivariable Cox regression. Dynamic trajectories of 6 key biomarkers over the first 14 days were assessed using locally estimated scatterplot smoothing, and restricted cubic spline models were used to characterize continuous exposure-response relationships.
Main results
Clinically adjudicated CS occurred in 143 patients (18.5%); among 164 in-hospital deaths, 99 (60.4%) occurred in patients with CS. Biomarker trajectory analyses showed sustained increases in inflammatory, cardiac, renal, and coagulation markers in patients who developed CS, whereas lipid markers showed a time-dependent decline.
Interpretation and novelty
In SFTS, clinically adjudicated CS clusters early after admission and reflects multisystem dysregulation involving inflammatory, cardiac, renal, coagulation, and lipid pathways.
Scientific and public-health significance
A six-biomarker panel may support early recognition of high-risk patients and help define a clinically relevant surveillance window during the first week of hospitalization.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is retrospective, and external validation and more objective cardiac assessment are needed before routine implementation. The evidence is moderate due to the observational nature and potential for confounding factors.

Original abstract (English)
Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS), caused by Dabie bandavirus, is associated with high mortality among intensive care unit (ICU) patients; however, the integrated proteomic–metabolomic mechanisms underlying fatal outcomes remain poorly characterized. This prospective cohort study enrolled 68 patients with SFTS, who were admitted to the ICU and classified them as survivors (n = 33) or non-survivors (n = 35) according to their 28-day clinical outcomes. Integrated analysis revealed that the four tryptophan metabolites were associated with 20 differentially expressed proteins, six of which showed robust associations within outcome subgroups. Collectively, tryptophan metabolic disturbances coupled with inflammatory proteomic features may characterize fatal severe SFTS, and the identified metabolite–protein signatures may serve as promising prognostic biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets in critically ill patients.

The work addresses a persistent gap in the response to SFTS: while molecular diagnostics such as reverse transcription PCR are sensitive and reliable, they depend on thermocyclers, trained personnel and cold-chain logistics that are often unavailable in the rural, agricultural settings where exposure to infected ticks is most likely. SFTSV belongs to the genus Bandavirus in the family Phenuiviridae and was first identified in China in 2009 after outbreaks of a mysterious febrile illness characterized by severe thrombocytopenia, leukopenia and, in a substantial fraction of cases, multi-organ failure. Because human infections often begin with nonspecific fever and gastrointestinal complaints, clinicians need a rapid differential diagnosis, particularly in areas where other tick-borne diseases such as scrub typhus and spotted fever rickettsiosis co-circulate. The new study targeted the Gn glycoprotein, one of the two mature subunits derived from the viral envelope precursor protein. Gn sits on the virion surface alongside its partner Gc and plays a central role in receptor binding and entry, making it an attractive antigen for immunodiagnostic capture. This characterization step is critical in lateral flow development because the assay depends on a carefully choreographed sandwich: one antibody, labeled with a visible reporter, binds the antigen in the sample, and a second antibody, immobilized in a thin line on the nitrocellulose membrane, catches the resulting complex so that it accumulates as a readable band.
