Preclinical evaluation of NHC and molnupiravir against severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus.

Authors: Hirohisa Mekata, Mari Yamamoto, Akatsuki Saito
Original abstract (English)
Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is an emerging tick-borne zoonotic disease caused by severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (SFTSV). The disease can be severe or fatal in companion animals. In veterinary medicine, no specific treatment has been established for SFTS, and management is limited to supportive care. To investigate the antiviral potential of molnupiravir against SFTSV, we evaluated the activity of its ribonucleoside metabolite, β-D-N 4 -hydroxycytidine (NHC), in feline- and canine-derived cells and the effects of oral molnupiravir on survival and blood viral RNA loads in a lethal mouse model. NHC inhibited the production of infectious SFTSV in all four cell lines tested, with plaque assay-based 50% effective concentrations of 0.33-0.97 μM. In SFTSV-infected mice lacking type I and type II interferon receptors, oral molnupiravir treatment (100 mg/kg twice daily for 3 days), initiated at 24, 48, or 72 h post-infection, delayed mortality and reduced median blood viral RNA loads. However, only one of six mice in each treated group survived to 12 days post-infection. At 6 days post-infection, median blood viral RNA loads in treated groups were 0.57%-1.59% of the median load in vehicle controls, with significant reductions when treatment was initiated at 24 or 48 h. In a separate experiment, extending treatment to 5 or 7 days did not demonstrate a clear additional survival benefit. These findings demonstrate antiviral activity in companion animal-derived cells and delayed mortality in an interferon-receptor-deficient mouse model. Further studies are required to establish therapeutic efficacy in naturally infected cats.

Platelet-derived extracellular vesicles aggravate macrophagic pyroptosis in Dabie bandavirus infection

Platelet-derived extracellular vesicles aggravate macrophagic pyroptosis in Dabie bandavirus infection

Authors: Yanjun Zhang, Ji He, Ni Yuxi, Yanfu Wang, Lufeng Shi, Yumo Zhou, Yilei Ma, Lingxuan Su, Yin Chen, Lei Fang
Original abstract (English)
Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is an emerging tick-borne infectious disease causing remarkably increased morbidity and mortality in East Asia. Thrombocytopenia is a hallmark pathogenic event in SFTS, clinical feature and an independent predictor of multi-organ failure and poor clinical outcomes. Although Dabie bandavirus (DBV) can bind to and replicate within platelets, the contribution of activated platelets to SFTS pathogenesis remains elusive. Here, we identify platelet-derived extracellular vesicles induced by DBV (DPEVs) as critical mediators of immune dysregulation. DBV-activated platelets released abundant extracellular vesicles that directly induced macrophage pyroptosis and markedly amplified virus-driven inflammatory responses. Proteomic and functional analyses demonstrated that DPEVs reprogram macrophages toward a pro-inflammatory and pyroptosis-prone state. Compared with vesicles generated by physical disruption, DPEVs displayed enhanced immunostimulatory capacity, indicating stimulus-dependent functional specialization. Mechanistically, DPEVs disrupted mitochondrial integrity, promoted mitochondrial reactive oxygen species production, thereby triggering the downstream pyroptotic program. In addition, DPEVs were capable of incorporating viral components, suggesting a potential dual role in both inflammatory amplification and viral dissemination. Collectively, these findings define a previously unrecognized pathogenic axis linking platelet activation, extracellular vesicle signaling, mitochondrial injury, and macrophage pyroptosis during DBV infection. DPEVs act as a key amplifier of inflammation and provide a mechanistic link between thrombocytopenia and disease severity in SFTS, highlighting their potential value as both biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

Authors: Qu Cheng, Qi Li, Minghui He, Xiang Xu, Yufei Yan, Junhua Tian, Zhihang Peng
Original abstract (English)
Background The Asian longhorned tick, Haemaphysalis longicornis, is the primary vector of severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS), an emerging infectious disease rapidly spreading in Asia with a case-fatality rate of up to 30%. Understanding the seasonal activity of H. longicornis is critical for elucidating SFTS epidemiology and improving risk prediction. Methods We compiled databases of life stage-specific seasonal activity data for H. longicornis, as well as laboratory-derived developmental durations under different temperature conditions, from the published literature. We then developed an empirical model integrating laboratory observations with field temperature data to predict interstadial and full-generation developmental durations across 20 locations with observed tick seasonality, seven SFTS transmission hotspots, and three locations without reported H. longicornis presence. Finally, we inferred the cohort structures underlying observed H. longicornis seasonal patterns by integrating observed seasonality with model-predicted developmental durations. Results Despite substantial variation in annual mean temperature (7.3–17.4°C), the seasonal activity of H. longicornis was largely synchronous across locations, with nymphs peaking in spring, adults in summer, larvae in fall, and no detectable activity during winter, potentially attributable to the substantially higher overwinter survival of nymphs than of other life stages. In contrast, human SFTS cases exhibited diverse seasonal patterns, with peaks occurring in May, July, or October depending on the location, suggesting that variation in human-tick contact patterns may play an important role in shaping the observed seasonality of human SFTS. The predicted full-generation developmental duration was 1–2 years in locations with observed H. longicornis seasonality or reported human SFTS cases, but < 1 year in locations without known H. longicornis presence under most examined scenarios. Inferred cohort structures showed that a single seasonal activity peak may comprise multiple overlapping cohorts. For example, the summer adult peak from May to August likely consists of overwintered adults for the early part, and newly matured adults that fed as nymphs in the spring for the latter part. Conclusions These findings highlight the need to consider human-tick contact patterns and location- and month-specific temporal scales when modelling human SFTS risk.

A novel 17-plex multi-pathogen detection for rapid and accurate diagnosis of tick-borne diseases

Authors: Jie Fu, Li-Yan Fu, Qi Chen, Zheng-Yuan Su, Xu-Hua Guan, Xuan Yao, Jin-Feng Xiong, Jin Qian, Yao-Hui Fang, Fēi Dèng
Original abstract (English)
In recent years, anthropogenic ecological disruption has escalated tick-borne zoonoses; ticks transmit taxonomically diverse pathogens with overlapping clinical signs, challenging precise diagnosis and increasing risk of simultaneous detection in pooled samples. Despite advancements, current sequencing technologies, such as Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS), Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) assays, and antibody-based detection methods, are hampered by limitations including extended processing times and limited detection throughput. These constraints significantly hinder their effectiveness in high-throughput pathogen screening for animal or patient samples. To address these challenges, we developed an innovative multi-detection method based on the Multi-analyte Suspension Assay (MASA), enabling rapid and accurate diagnosis of a comprehensive panel of 17 vector-borne pathogens. This 17-plex panel comprises Rickettsia spp., Anaplasma phagocytophilum, Borrelia recurrentis, Babesia spp., Borrelia burgdorferi, Rickettsia prowazekii, Rickettsia heilongjiangensis, Orientia tsutsugamushi, Coxiella burnetii, Leptospira, Bartonella spp., Candidatus Ehrlichia erytraense, Hantaan Virus (HTNV), Seoul Virus (SEOV), Crimean-Congo Hemorrhagic Fever Virus (CCHFV), Severe Fever with Thrombocytopenia Syndrome Virus (SFTSV), and Tick-Borne Encephalitis Virus (TBEV). By combining this novel method with traditional techniques, including nested PCR and Real-Time Quantitative Reverse Transcription PCR (qRT-PCR), we achieved accurate detection of pathogens such as Rickettsia spp., Anaplasma phagocytophilum, and SFTSV in tick samples. Notably, the technique revealed the co-existence of multiple pathogens within the same sample group, highlighting its ability in identifying co-detection of pathogens. Additionally, in suspected cases of tick-borne pathogen infection in febrile patients, the method quickly identified positive results for SFTSV and Rickettsia spp. These preliminary results suggest the potential of this method as a promising screening tool for clinical diagnostics, although larger-scale validation is warranted. This method provides a preliminary technical candidate approach for the development of more sensitive field-deployable detection tools.

Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome has mortality rates ranging between 10 and 30 percent, lacks established antiviral therapies, and poses grave risks of nosocomial transmission to healthcare workers exposed to infectious blood or bodily secretions. Scrub typhus, in contrast, has a case fatality below 1 percent and responds rapidly to empirical antimicrobial regimens such as doxycycline.
Response, status and uncertainty
The study's findings were based on a retrospective cohort and de-identified medical records. The authors outlined methodological limitations, including partially non-contemporaneous sampling periods and evolving diagnostic access. The clinical scoring tool was validated using a penalized logistic regression model trained on 16 variables, achieving a high area under the curve (0.989) and strong sensitivity and specificity at certain thresholds.
