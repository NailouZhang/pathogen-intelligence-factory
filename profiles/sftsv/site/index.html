Mutations that positively affect Bandavirus glycoprotein function in VSV-vectored vaccines

Authors: Raegan J. Petch, Philip Hicks, Jonna B. Westover, Brian B. Gowen, Paul Bates
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (SFTSV) and Heartland bandavirus (HRTV) are emerging tick-borne bandaviruses. They have high case fatality rates (10%), and no FDA-approved vaccines exist for disease prevention. SFTSV and HRTV are therefore identified as priority pathogens. A recombinant vesicular stomatitis virus (rVSV) vaccine, which replaces the original VSV glycoprotein with the SFTSV glycoproteins, shows early promise for SFTSV as it induces strong immune responses that are protective against lethal challenge. However, rVSV-SFTSV is highly attenuated in cell culture, which may be due to incompatibility between the assembly sites of SFTSV (the Golgi and endoplasmic reticulum-Golgi intermediate compartment [ERGIC]) and those of VSV (the plasma membrane). In this study, we identify a noncanonical coatomer complex I (COPI)-binding motif found in the cytoplasmic tail of SFTSV glycoproteins and demonstrate that an amino acid substitution in this motif (K1071A) inhibits binding to COPI. This mutation results in increased surface expression of SFTSV glycoproteins, improved incorporation onto VSV virions, and enhanced replication of rVSV-SFTSV in vitro . A mutation at a homologous site (K1074A) in HRTV yields similar results, and rVSV-HRTV K1074A exhibits increased replication in vitro and in vivo . We show that vaccination with rVSV-HRTV K1074A results in improved induction of neutralizing antibody responses in immunocompetent C57BL/6 mice, and neutralizing antibodies elicited by vaccination are protective when administered to severely immunocompromised mice via passive transfer. Overall, our study identifies a mutation that improves the efficacy of the rVSV-SFTSV vaccine candidate and introduces the first vaccine candidate directly addressing HRTV infections. IMPORTANCE Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (SFTSV) and Heartland bandavirus (HRTV) are emerging tick-borne viruses with high fatality rates. FDA-approved vaccines and antiviral drugs are unavailable but critically needed. We identify an important mutation in the SFTSV glycoprotein that disrupts a previously unreported COPI-binding site. The mutation improves the efficacy of the previously described recombinant vesicular stomatitis virus vaccine candidate for SFTSV (rVSV-SFTSV). We also develop an rVSV-HRTV vaccine and show potent induction of neutralizing antibodies and protection from lethal challenge. This is the first study directly addressing the lack of vaccines specifically targeting HRTV.

问题与背景
本研究旨在强调华东地区耳螨病的诊断和管理挑战,强调需要提高初级保健机构的认识和应对能力。
设计与对象
这是在华东地区三所三级医院:青岛大学附属医院(青岛)、山东第一医科大学附属山东省立医院(济南)和复旦大学眼耳鼻喉医院(上海)进行的多中心回顾性病例系列研究。该研究包括三例外耳道蜱虫叮咬病例,这最初给基层医院的诊断和管理带来了挑战。
核心方法
通过使用“蜱”、“耳螨病”、“外耳道异物”和“耳朵异物”等关键词搜索电子病历来识别病例。使用标准化表格提取临床数据,并由昆虫学专家在立体显微镜下检查蜱标本。形态学鉴定基于盾板、头状体、腿分段和口器结构(下体和螯肢)。
主要结果
病例1为一名70岁女性,腹部被误认为是鼓膜蜱虫,外耳道内可见暗红色粪便。取出后内窥镜检查显示鼓膜完整,取出后可见活跃、充血的蜱虫。记录了蜱虫腿部运动的视频记录。
解释与创新
这些病例表明,耳螨病并不是一种孤立的临床罕见病,而是中国东部农村和城市地区反复出现的问题。这凸显了加强医生教育和公众意识的必要性,以改善早期识别和患者治疗结果。
科研与公卫意义
研究结果表明公共卫生和初级保健教育之间存在潜在差距,使其成为该地区公共卫生基础设施的相关问题。他们还强调了解“同一个健康”范式在解决 蜱媒 疾病方面的重要性。
局限与证据强度
该研究因其回顾性设计和缺乏蜱鉴定的分子方法而受到限制,这将蜱的分类限制在科或可能的属水平。由于这些限制,证据是中等的。

Question and background
This study aimed to highlight the diagnostic and management challenges of otoacariasis in East China, emphasizing the need for improved recognition and response in primary care settings.
Design and population
This was a multicenter retrospective case series conducted at three tertiary hospitals in East China: the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University (Qingdao), Shandong Provincial Hospital Affiliated to Shandong First Medical University (Jinan), and Eye & ENT Hospital of Fudan University (Shanghai). The study included three cases of tick bites in the external auditory canal that initially posed diagnostic and management challenges at primary hospitals.
Core methods
Cases were identified by searching electronic medical records using keywords such as 'tick,' 'otoacariasis,' 'external auditory canal foreign body,' and 'ear foreign body.' Clinical data were extracted using a standardized form, and tick specimens were examined under stereomicroscopy by entomology specialists. Morphological identification was based on the scutum, capitulum, leg segmentation, and mouthpart structures (hypostome and chelicerae).
Main results
Case 1 involved a 70-year-old woman whose tick abdomen was mistaken for the tympanic membrane, with dark red feces observed in the external auditory canal. Post-removal endoscopy revealed an intact tympanic membrane, and the active, engorged tick was visualized after removal. A video recording of tick leg movement was documented.
Interpretation and novelty
The cases illustrate that otoacariasis is not an isolated clinical rarity but a recurring issue in East China, spanning both rural and urban settings. This highlights the need for enhanced physician education and public awareness to improve early recognition and patient outcomes.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings suggest a potential gap in public health surveillance and primary care education, making it a relevant concern for the region's public health infrastructure. They also emphasize the importance of understanding the One Health paradigm in addressing tick-borne diseases.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its retrospective design and the lack of molecular methods for tick identification, which restricted classification to the family or probable genus level. The evidence is moderate due to these constraints.
