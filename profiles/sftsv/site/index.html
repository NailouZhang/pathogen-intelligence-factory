Infection Control and Nursing Management in Severe Fever with Thrombocytopenia Syndrome Wards: A single-center experience from a tertiary hospital in China

Authors: Ling Wang, Lanlan Long, Xiu Li, Zihan Wang, Xiaoyun Zhang, Guang Chen, Di Wu, Li Tan
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Severe Fever with Thrombocytopenia Syndrome (SFTS) is an acute zoonotic disease caused by Dabie Bandavirus. Severe cases have a high mortality rate and pose a risk of human-to-human transmission, posing a serious challenge to clinical infection control and nursing management. This study aims to explore key aspects of infection control measures and nursing management in SFTS wards, summarize clinical experience, and provide a scientific basis for optimizing SFTS patient management. Methods : A retrospective analysis was conducted on the clinical nursing data of 512 SFTS patients admitted to the Department of Infectious Diseases at Tongji Hospital, Tongji Medical College of Huazhong University of Science and Technology, from June 2022 to October 2025. The clinical application effects of ward infection control practices (including layout isolation, personnel protection, and environmental disinfection) and nursing management measures (including personnel training, disease monitoring, complication prevention, nutritional support, and psychological nursing) were systematically summarized. Results : Through the implementation of an infection control strategy that combines standard precautions and contact isolation, long with refined nursing management based on disease staging, our department achieved zero nosocomial infection events related to SFTS and zero accidents, including patient falls, bed falls, and suicides over the past three years. The "Technical Guidelines for Prevention and Control of Fever with Thrombocytopenia Syndrome (2024 Edition)" published by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention in 2024 further validated the scientific validity of this practice. Conclusions : Strict infection control measures and systematic nursing management are key to ensuring medical safety in SFTS wards. Future research should focus on conducting multi-center prospective studies, establishing standardized nursing pathways based on disease severity grading, and effectively extending inpatient management to post-discharge follow-up to promote the continuous optimization of SFTS nursing practices.

Question and background
Severe Fever with Thrombocytopenia Syndrome (SFTS) is an acute zoonotic disease caused by Dabie Bandavirus with high mortality and human-to-human transmission risk, posing challenges to clinical infection control and nursing management. The study aimed to explore infection control measures and nursing management in SFTS wards to provide a scientific basis for optimizing patient management.
Design and population
A retrospective analysis was conducted on the clinical nursing data of 512 SFTS patients admitted to the Department of Infectious Diseases at Tongji Hospital, Tongji Medical College of Huazhong University of Science and Technology, from June 2022 to October 2025.
Core methods
The clinical application effects of ward infection control practices, including layout isolation, personnel protection, and environmental disinfection, were systematically summarized alongside nursing management measures such as personnel training, disease monitoring, complication prevention, nutritional support, and psychological nursing.
Main results
Through the implementation of an infection control strategy combining standard precautions and contact isolation with refined nursing management based on disease staging, the department reported zero nosocomial infection events related to SFTS and zero accidents, including patient falls, bed falls, and suicides over the past three years.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted the findings as demonstrating that strict infection control measures and systematic nursing management are key to ensuring medical safety in SFTS wards. They highlighted the validation of these practices by the "Technical Guidelines for Prevention and Control of Fever with Thrombocytopenia Syndrome (2024 Edition)" published by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings support the optimization of SFTS patient management through standardized infection control and nursing protocols, which may inform hospital infection prevention strategies and clinical care pathways for SFTS in tertiary healthcare settings.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its single-center retrospective design, lack of a control group, and absence of quantitative outcome metrics beyond zero-event reporting, which constrains generalizability and strength of evidence. The evidence strength is assessed as moderate due to these methodological constraints.

中国山东长角血蜱双性和孤雌种群的遗传分化和微生物组分化

Genetic divergence and microbiome differentiation between bisexual and parthenogenetic populations of Haemaphysalis longicornis in Shandong, China
作者： Hang-Li Su, Zhao-An Sheng, Shi-Yao Ding, Zhi‐Tao Wang, Lian-Qi Yu, Can-Can Bu, Wenxiang Lv, Peng Cheng, Li-Juan Liu, Yu Guo
摘要中文翻译摘要 背景长角蜱 Haemaphysalis longicornis 是 发热伴血小板减少综合征病毒 (SFTSV) 和其他人畜共患病原体的重要 媒介。这种蜱表现出独特的双重繁殖策略——双性繁殖和专性孤雌生殖——这可能会影响其种群动态、遗传结构和微生物组组成。然而,这两种繁殖类型之间的空间分布、遗传分化和微生物群落变异仍然知之甚少,特别是在蜱虫和 发热伴血小板减少综合征 的高流行地区山东省。方法 采集山东省14个地级市的长角雉标本525份。线粒体 COX1 基因测序用于评估遗传多样性、单倍型分布、群体分化 (Fst) 和人口统计历史。此外,还进行了 16S rRNA 基因测序,以表征双性与孤雌群体中的细菌群落组成、多样性、共现网络和功能潜力(PICRUSt2、FAPROTAX)。结果 鉴定出 33 个单倍型,形成两个截然不同的进化枝:进化枝 A(双性)主要分布在内陆山区,进化枝 B(单性生殖)主要局限于沿海地区。双性恋种群表现出较高的遗传多样性和基因流,而孤雌生殖种群则表现出多样性降低和明显的孤立性。微生物组分析显示,孤雌蜱以柯克斯体为主(相对丰度约 80%),并拥有一个简化的、高度互利的共生网络(无负边缘,模块性 = 0.59)。相比之下,双性蜱拥有更加多样化的微生物群,具有复杂的模块化网络(模块化= 0.74),包括竞争性相互作用。功能预测表明,孤雌生殖群体的致病性相关途径丰富,而双性群体的环境感知和营养循环功能丰富。结论 生殖模式是 H 遗传分化和微生物组分化的主要驱动因素。孤雌生殖群体依赖于专门的 Coxiella 主导的共生,并表现出遗传多样性降低,而双性群体则保持灵活、有弹性的 宿主 微生物系统。这些发现为了解这种重要蜱的生态适应和病原体 传播 潜力提供了一个新的框架,并强调了对生殖类型特异性 监测 和控制策略的需求。

Genetic divergence and microbiome differentiation between bisexual and parthenogenetic populations of Haemaphysalis longicornis in Shandong, China

Authors: Hang-Li Su, Zhao-An Sheng, Shi-Yao Ding, Zhi‐Tao Wang, Lian-Qi Yu, Can-Can Bu, Wenxiang Lv, Peng Cheng, Li-Juan Liu, Yu Guo
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background The longhorned tick, Haemaphysalis longicornis , is an important vector of severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (SFTSV) and other zoonotic pathogens. This tick exhibits a unique dual reproductive strategy—bisexual reproduction and obligate parthenogenesis—which may influence its population dynamics, genetic structure, and microbiome composition. However, the spatial distribution, genetic differentiation, and microbial community variation between these two reproductive types remain poorly understood, particularly in Shandong Province, a high-endemic region for both the tick and SFTS. Methods We collected 525 H. longicornis specimens from 14 prefecture-level cities across Shandong Province. Mitochondrial COX1 gene sequencing was used to assess genetic diversity, haplotype distribution, population differentiation ( Fst ), and demographic history. Additionally, 16S rRNA gene sequencing was performed to characterize bacterial community composition, diversity, co-occurrence networks, and functional potentials (PICRUSt2, FAPROTAX) in bisexual versus parthenogenetic populations. Results Thirty-three haplotypes were identified, forming two deeply divergent clades: Clade A (bisexual) predominantly in inland mountainous areas, and Clade B (parthenogenetic) largely confined to coastal regions. Bisexual populations exhibited higher genetic diversity and gene flow, whereas parthenogenetic populations showed reduced diversity and pronounced isolation. Microbiome analysis revealed that parthenogenetic ticks were dominated by Coxiella (∼80% relative abundance) and possessed a simplified, highly mutualistic co-occurrence network (no negative edges, modularity = 0.59). In contrast, bisexual ticks harboured a more diverse microbiota with a complex, modular network (modularity = 0.74) including competitive interactions. Functional predictions indicated that parthenogenetic populations were enriched for pathogenicity-related pathways, while bisexual populations were enriched for environmental sensing and nutrient cycling functions. Conclusions Reproductive mode is a major driver of both genetic differentiation and microbiome divergence in H. Parthenogenetic populations rely on a specialised Coxiella -dominated symbiosis and exhibit reduced genetic diversity, whereas bisexual populations maintain a flexible, resilient host-microbe system. These findings provide a novel framework for understanding the ecological adaptation and pathogen transmission potential of this important tick, and highlight the need for reproductive-type-specific surveillance and control strategies.

Question and background
The study aimed to investigate the genetic divergence and microbiome differentiation between bisexual and parthenogenetic populations of Haemaphysalis longicornis in Shandong, China, as these ticks are important vectors of severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (SFTSV) and other zoonotic pathogens.
Design and population
The study was conducted in Shandong Province, China, across 14 prefecture-level cities, where 525 Haemaphysalis longicornis specimens were collected to compare the genetic and microbiome characteristics of bisexual and parthenogenetic populations.
Core methods
Genetic diversity and population differentiation were assessed using mitochondrial COX1 gene sequencing, while microbiome composition, diversity, co-occurrence networks, and functional potentials were analyzed through 16S rRNA gene sequencing and PICRUSt2 and FAPROTAX tools.
Main results
Thirty-three haplotypes were identified, forming two clades: Clade A (bisexual) in inland mountainous areas and Clade B (parthenogenetic) in coastal regions. Bisexual populations showed higher genetic diversity and gene flow, while parthenogenetic populations had reduced diversity and pronounced isolation. Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted that reproductive mode significantly influences both genetic differentiation and microbiome divergence in Haemaphysalis longicornis, offering a novel understanding of the ecological adaptation and pathogen transmission potential of this tick.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings suggest that reproductive-type-specific surveillance and control strategies are necessary for managing the spread of SFTSV and other pathogens associated with Haemaphysalis longicornis.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its focus on genetic and microbiome data without direct pathogen transmission analysis, and the evidence is moderate due to the absence of statistical validation for the observed microbiome differences.

问题与背景
该研究解决了 媒介 传播的病毒感染引起的日益严重的公共卫生问题,由于环境和社会变化,这些病毒感染的地理范围和 媒介 强度正在增加。该研究旨在了解这些病毒如何随着时间的推移操纵 宿主 先天免疫以支持其复制和 宿主。

科研与公卫意义
这些发现提供了对发热伴血小板减少综合征病毒中免疫逃避和发病机制机制的见解,这可以为宿主导向的抗病毒药物疗法的开发提供信息,并改进监测和媒介传播疾病的预防策略。

该研究的局限性在于其只关注单一代表性病毒,这可能会限制其普遍性。然而,证据是强有力的,因为它是基于对目标病毒背景下的免疫相互作用和病毒复制动态的直接观察。

Temporal Rewiring of Innate Immunity by Vector-Borne Viruses for Host-Directed Antiviral Therapy

Authors: Eunji Kim, Andreas S. Baur, Jung‐Hyun Lee
Original abstract (English)
The geographic range and outbreak intensity of vector-borne viral infections are increasing as climate, land use, urbanization, and human mobility reshape vector ecology and human exposure. Despite their growing importance to public health, effective antiviral and preventive options remain limited for many emerging and reemerging vector-borne viruses due to viral genetic diversity, rapid evolutionary capacity, sporadic outbreak patterns, and economic constraints. While these viruses differ in taxonomy, genome organization, vector specificity, tissue tropism, and clinical manifestations, they exploit a shared vulnerability in host antiviral defense, particularly the timing of innate antiviral immunity to support viral replication, immune evasion, inflammatory dysregulation, and disease progression. Rather than simply suppressing antiviral defense, vector-borne viruses can delay early viral nucleic acid sensing, attenuate interferon induction or responsiveness, and extend the initial phase for viral replication. As viral burden increases and infected tissues undergo stress or damage, delayed immune activation can shift toward excessive inflammatory amplification, contributing to disease-specific pathology. In this study, we examine this temporal rewiring of innate antiviral immunity in representative vector-borne viruses such as dengue virus, chikungunya virus, and severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus and propose that understanding these conserved host dependencies may lead to broader, stage-specific, adaptable antiviral strategies that complement conventional virus-directed approaches.

Question and background
The study addresses the growing public health concern posed by vector-borne viral infections, which are increasing in geographic range and outbreak intensity due to environmental and societal changes. The research aims to understand how these viruses manipulate host innate immunity over time to support their replication and pathogenesis.
Design and population
The study examines the temporal rewiring of innate antiviral immunity in representative vector-borne viruses, including severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus, to explore conserved host dependencies that could inform antiviral strategies. It focuses on the mechanisms by which these viruses interact with host immune responses.
Core methods
The study uses a combination of molecular and immunological approaches to analyze how vector-borne viruses, such as severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus, alter the timing of innate immune responses. It investigates viral strategies that delay early nucleic acid sensing, attenuate interferon induction, and extend the initial phase of viral replication.
Main results
Vector-borne viruses, including severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus, can delay early viral nucleic acid sensing, attenuate interferon induction or responsiveness, and extend the initial phase for viral replication. As viral burden increases, this delayed immune activation can shift toward excessive inflammatory amplification, contributing to disease-specific pathology.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpret these findings as evidence of a conserved mechanism by which vector-borne viruses manipulate host innate immunity over time. This understanding may lead to broader, stage-specific, adaptable antiviral strategies that complement conventional virus-directed approaches.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings provide insights into the mechanisms of immune evasion and pathogenesis in severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus, which could inform the development of host-directed antiviral therapies and improve surveillance and prevention strategies for vector-borne diseases.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its focus on a single representative virus, which may restrict generalizability. However, the evidence is strong as it is based on direct observations of immune interactions and viral replication dynamics in the context of the target virus.
