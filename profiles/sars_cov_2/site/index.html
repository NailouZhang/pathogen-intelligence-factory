Immunogenicity and Safety of Coadministration of AS01E-Adjuvanted Respiratory Syncytial Virus Prefusion F Protein Vaccine and a COVID-19 mRNA Vaccine in Adult s Aged ≥50 Years: A Phase 3, Randomized, Noninferiority Trial.

Authors: Marc Bonten, Brandon Essink, Nikita Hanning, Isabel Leroux-Roels, Cristina Masuet-Aumatell, Silvina Natalini Martinez, Hiwot Amare Hailemariam, Ghassem Jandari, Paulia Kwo Ton Ge Jua, Charlotte Servais
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: This trial evaluated coadministration of the AS01E-adjuvanted respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) prefusion F protein-based vaccine (adjuvanted RSVPreF3) and an Omicron XBB.1.5-based coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) mRNA vaccine in ≥50-year-olds. METHODS: This phase 3, open-label, multicenter trial randomized ≥50-year-olds 1:1 to coadministration (Co-Ad group) or sequential administration approximately 1 month apart (Control group) of adjuvanted RSVPreF3 and the COVID-19 mRNA vaccine. Primary objectives were to demonstrate noninferiority of humoral immune responses at 1 month postvaccination in terms of RSV-A, RSV-B, and severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) neutralizing titers, with the upper limit of the 95% CI of adjusted geometric mean titer (GMT) group ratios (Control/Co-Ad) ≤1.50. Secondary objectives included assessment of reactogenicity up to 4 days and safety up to 6 months after vaccination. Noninferiority was achieved for RSV-A (GMT ratio: 1.12 [95% CI: .97-1.28]) and RSV-B (GMT ratio: 1.08 [95% CI: .94-1.23]) and marginally missed for SARS-CoV-2 Omicron XBB.1.5 (GMT ratio: 1.31 [95% CI: 1.13-1.51]). Within 4 days postvaccination, the most frequently reported solicited events were administration-site pain, myalgia, and fatigue. Most solicited events were of mild-to-moderate intensity, and the overall median duration was ≤3 days and comparable between groups. Unsolicited and serious adverse events were generally balanced between groups. CONCLUSIONS: Adjuvanted RSVPreF3 coadministered with a COVID-19 mRNA vaccine maintained an acceptable safety profile in ≥50-year-olds. The marginal miss of the noninferiority criterion for SARS-CoV-2 does not suggest clinically relevant interference. Therefore, the results support the coadministration of these vaccines. Immunogenicity and Safety of PHH-1V COVID-19 Vaccine as a Heterologous Booster in Adolescents.

Authors: Ignasi Esteban Riera, Raúl Pérez-Caballero, Silvina Natalini Martínez, Miriam González-Amores, Laura Roig Soria, Laia Bernad, Santiago Cavero, Benjamin Trinité, Elena Aurrecoechea Pumar, Roc Pomarol Moyà
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: PHH-1V, a protein-based, non-mRNA, bivalent, adjuvanted COVID-19 vaccine, has demonstrated strong immunogenicity and favorable safety as a booster in adults. This study evaluated PHH-1V as a heterologous booster in adolescents. METHODS: HIPRA-HH-3 was a phase IIb, open-label, multicenter, noninferiority trial assessing the immunogenicity and safety of PHH-1V in 240 healthy adolescents (≥ 12 to < 18 years) primed with BNT162b2. Immunogenicity (N = 90) was assessed by measuring neutralizing antibodies against SARS-CoV-2 variants and total binding antibodies in adolescent participants vs young adults (≥ 18 to ≤ 25 years) (N = 81) from a previous trial, who had also been boosted with PHH-1V. T-cell responses at day 14 post booster were also evaluated. Antibody durability and adverse events (AEs) were assessed through day 336. RESULTS: Neutralizing titers-against Omicron BA.1 (primary endpoint), WH1, Beta, and Delta variants-and total binding antibodies were significantly higher in adolescents than in young adults (P < .001), demonstrating noninferiority (margin < 1.5). T-cell responses against BA.1 and Beta also increased significantly (P < .001). Cross-variant antibody titers remained above baseline (day 0) through day 336. AEs were mostly mild/moderate, primarily injection-site pain and headache within 28 days. No related serious AEs were reported. CONCLUSIONS: As a heterologous booster in adolescents, PHH-1V is safe and elicits cellular and sustained humoral responses with cross-variant immune recognition, which support the use of PHH-1V in adolescent vaccination strategies. Data in younger and specific risk groups are still needed.

Question and background
This study aimed to evaluate the immunogenicity and safety of PHH-1V as a heterologous booster in adolescents, building on its demonstrated effectiveness in adults.
Design and population
HIPRA-HH-3 was a phase IIb, open-label, multicenter, noninferiority trial involving 240 healthy adolescents (≥ 12 to < 18 years) who had previously received BNT162b2 as a prime vaccine.

These results provide evidence for the effectiveness and safety of PHH-1V in adolescents, which can inform vaccination strategies and contribute to broader public health efforts against SARS-CoV-2.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its focus on a specific age group and the absence of data on younger populations or specific risk groups, which may affect generalizability. The evidence is strong, with statistically significant results and a well-defined noninferiority margin.

Boosting hybrid immunity with intranasal live-attenuated or intramuscular mRNA vaccination prevents SARS-CoV-2 XBB.1.5 transmission

Authors: Jana Kochmann, Nico Joel Halwe, G. Tuba Barut, Nadine Ebert, Volker Thiel, Anca Dorhoi, Donata Hoffmann, Martin Beer, Jacob Schön, Björn Corleis
Original abstract (English)
Rapid antigenic evolution of SARS-CoV-2 and waning immunity have reduced antibody-mediated neutralization, enabling breakthrough infections and sustained transmission despite widespread immunity. This highlights the need for variant-adapted booster vaccines. However, pre-existing immunity may influence the magnitude and specificity of immune responses induced by variant-adapted booster vaccination. Using the Syrian hamster model, we previously described a safe and efficient mucosal vaccination approach using live attenuated viruses (LAV) based on a "One-to-Stop (OTS)" codon modification strategy. Whether OTS vaccines are suitable as effective booster vaccines in the presence of pre-existing mucosal immunity remained unaddressed. Here, we modeled pre-existing hybrid immunity in Syrian hamsters using intramuscular bivalent mRNA vaccination followed by SARS-CoV-2 infection and subsequently administered heterologous intranasal OTS-LAV or intramuscular mRNA XBB.1.5 booster vaccination. Both booster strategies significantly increased XBB.1.5-binding- and neutralizing antibodies systemically and at mucosal sites. In contrast, booster vaccination left SARS-CoV-2 specific T cell frequencies unchanged. While all hybrid-immune animals were protected from disease following XBB.1.5 challenge infection, virus transmission was completely blocked only after booster vaccination and was associated with the absence of infectious virus in nasal samples. Collectively, using OTS-LAV we provide a proof of concept that intranasal LAV vaccination can effectively boost pre-existing hybrid immunity and restrict viral transmission. These findings support the use of OTS-LAV as a heterologous booster strategy against SARS-CoV-2 and demonstrate that variant-adapted booster vaccination can efficiently limit transmission, a strategy highly desired for tackling emerging respiratory infections.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract Sepsis survivors often suffer from chronic immune dysfunction, leading to frequent rehospitalization and recurrent sepsis. Vaccinations are effective in preventing common infections in sepsis survivors. However, vaccination behavior in this vulnerable population remains unknown. Prospective interviewer-administered survey performed between 04/2023 and 04/2024 among sepsis survivors in Germany. Participants were recruited from the Mid-German Sepsis Cohort (MSC) 6 to 8 years after initial inclusion. We assessed risk perception, cues to action and barriers for vaccination against influenza, pneumococci and SARS-CoV-2. Predictors for the vaccination status of each of the three infections were identified by logistic regression analyses. Among 174 survivors, vaccination rates against influenza and pneumococci rose from 57.3 to 70.7% and from 31.4 to 60.5% 3 months to 8 years post-sepsis, respectively. A total of 135 patients (77.6%) had full vaccination coverage against SARS-CoV-2 at the time of the survey. Higher age and an intermediate vs. low education increased the odds for influenza vaccination. No associations between sociodemographic indicators and the vaccination status against pneumococci and SARS-CoV-2 were found. Vaccination counselling by a general practitioner increased the odds for vaccination between 5.5-fold (influenza) and 3.2-fold (pneumococci) in the multiple regression analyses. The review of general vaccination status was associated with a 4.7-fold increase of influenza vaccination rates and 3.1-fold increase of SARS-CoV-2 vaccination rates. Among patients with at least one missing vaccination (n = 92), impaired mobility (16.8%), long waiting times in the practice (14.8%) and fear of infection in the practice (14.7%) were the most relevant barriers. There are considerable vaccination gaps in sepsis survivors. Pro-active vaccination counselling and checks of vaccination status by general practitioners may offer a great potential to increase vaccination rates and thus should be considered as an important element of sepsis aftercare.
