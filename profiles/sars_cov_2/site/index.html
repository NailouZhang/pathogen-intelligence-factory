Effects of COVID-19 vaccination type and vaccination-infection interval on viral clearance dynamics in Omicron BA.5 patients at Guangzhou Fangcang shelter hospitals: A propensity score matched multicenter retrospective cohort study

Authors: Zhen-Jie Jiang, Qian Wang, Xue-Tao Kong, Xiao-Ling Lin, Dong-Yu Ma, Fa-Quan Chen, 蔡秋芝, Qing-Wen Sun, Zhi-Gang Deng, Zeng Huiqing
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT The Omicron BA.5 subvariant exhibited pronounced immune evasion and was associated with widespread breakthrough infections. Unlike settings where mRNA vaccines predominated, China mainly used inactivated vaccines, with the adenovirus type 5-vectored vaccine Ad5-nCoV also administered at scale. Real-world evidence on post-infection viral clearance associated with these platforms remains limited. This multicenter retrospective cohort study enrolled 16,533 mild or asymptomatic Omicron BA.5 cases from two Fangcang shelter hospitals in Guangzhou, China, from Novernber 5 to December 8, 2022. The primary objective was to compare viral-clearance time between unvaccinated and vaccinated participants. The secondary objective was to compare inactivated-vaccine recipients, or and Ad5-nCoV recipients, containing regimens in a propensity score-matched cohort; the last-dose-to-infection interval analysis was exploratory. Propensity score matching was performed using age, sex, marital status, occupation, and province as covariates to improve between-group comparability. All patients underwent standardized daily RT-PCR testing containing both N and ORF1ab genes, with Ct values > 35 defined as negative. The outcome was time to viral clearance, defined as two consecutive negative nasopharyngeal swabs ≥ 24 hours apart. In the overall cohort, vaccinated patients had significantly shorter shedding duration (9.05 vs... 10.42d; P < .001) compared with unvaccinated patients. The matched cohort included 228 unvaccinated, 228 inactivated vaccine recipients, and 114 Ad5-nCoV recipients. Median time to viral clearance differed across groups with 9.42, 8.56, and 8.48d, respectively. Prior vaccination was associated with shorter viral clearance but with no significant difference observed between inactivated vaccine and Ad5-nCoV recipients among the matched regimens.

Question and background
The study aimed to investigate the effects of different vaccination types and the interval between vaccination and infection on viral clearance dynamics in patients infected with the Omicron BA.5 subvariant of SARS-CoV-2.
Design and population
This multicenter retrospective cohort study enrolled 16,533 mild or asymptomatic Omicron BA.5 cases from two Fangcang shelter hospitals in Guangzhou, China, from November 5 to December 8, 2022. The study compared viral clearance time between unvaccinated and vaccinated participants, with a secondary objective to compare inactivated vaccine recipients and Ad5-nCoV recipients.
Core methods
Propensity score matching was used to ensure comparability between groups, with age, sex, marital status, occupation, and province as covariates. Patients underwent standardized daily RT-PCR testing targeting both N and ORF1ab genes, with Ct values > 35 defined as negative. Viral clearance was defined as two consecutive negative nasopharyngeal swabs ≥ 24 hours apart.
Main results
In the overall cohort, vaccinated patients had significantly shorter shedding duration (9.05 vs 10.42 days; P < .001) compared with unvaccinated patients. The matched cohort included 228 unvaccinated, 228 inactivated vaccine recipients, and 114 Ad5-nCoV recipients. Median time to viral clearance differed across groups with 9.42, 8.56, and 8.48 days, respectively.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted that prior vaccination was associated with shorter viral clearance time, but there was no significant difference observed between inactivated vaccine and Ad5-nCoV recipients among the matched regimens. This study provides novel insights into the impact of vaccination type on viral clearance dynamics in Omicron BA.5 patients.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings can inform public health strategies for vaccine selection and timing in the context of Omicron BA.5 infections, particularly in regions where inactivated vaccines are predominantly used.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its retrospective design and potential for confounding factors, although propensity score matching was used to improve comparability. The evidence is moderate in strength due to these limitations.

问题与背景
由于持续的职业暴露,医护人员(HCWs)仍面临严重急性呼吸综合征冠状病毒2(严重急性呼吸综合征冠状病毒2)感染的高风险。然而,关于印度医护人员中再感染动态的证据仍然有限。
设计与对象
在印度11家三级医疗机构中,于2021年12月至2023年3月期间开展了一项多中心前瞻性研究。所有实验室确诊严重急性呼吸综合征冠状病毒2感染的医护人员被纳入研究,包括回顾性纳入(在4周内)和前瞻性纳入(8个月随访)。
核心方法
再感染通过流行病学标准定义,并在有配对样本的情况下通过全基因组测序确认。使用标准化表格收集了社会人口学、临床、暴露、免疫学和实验室数据。估计了再感染的发病率,并进行了双变量分析以确定与再感染相关的因素。
主要结果
Of 1,598 HCWs screened, 1,334 (83.4%) were enrolled and 1,331 (83.3%) completed follow-ups. Thirteen participants developed confirmed SARS-CoV-2 reinfection, yielding a cumulative incidence of 9.7 per 1,000 individuals and an incidence density of 2.97 per 100,000 person-days (95% CI: 1.73-5.11). The median interval between infection episodes was 193 days (IQR: 183-211). All reinfection cases were symptomatic but mild, with no hospitalizations or requirement for oxygen therapy.

Reinfection with Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus-2 (SARS CoV-2) among healthcare workers in India.

Authors: Aparna Mukherjee, Tanu Anand, Kh Jitenkumar Singh, Lokesh Sharma, Poonam Sharma Velamuri, Aanchal Satija, Nikita Birhman, Kripa Josten, Pragya D. Yadav, Rima R Sahay
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Healthcare workers (HCWs) remain at heightened risk of Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus-2 (SARS-CoV-2) infection due to sustained occupational exposure. The emergence of the Omicron variant, characterized by increased transmissibility and immune escape, had led to a rise in reinfections despite prior infection and vaccination. However, evidence on reinfection dynamics among HCWs in India remains limited. METHODS: A multicentric prospective cohort study was conducted across 11 tertiary care hospitals in India between December 2021 and March 2023. HCWs with laboratory-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection were enrolled retrospectively (within 4 weeks prior) and prospectively (8-month follow-up. Reinfection was defined using epidemiological criteria and confirmed by whole-genome sequencing wherever paired samples were available. Socio-demographic, clinical, exposure, immunological, and laboratory data were collected using standardized case report forms. Reinfection incidence was estimated, and bivariate analysis was performed to identify factors associated with reinfection. RESULTS: Of 1,598 HCWs screened, 1,334 (83.4%) were enrolled and 1,331 (83.3%) completed follow-ups. Thirteen participants developed confirmed SARS-CoV-2 reinfection, yielding a cumulative incidence of 9.7 per 1,000 individuals and an incidence density of 2.97 per 100,000 person-days (95% CI: 1.73-5.11). The median interval between infection episodes was 193 days (IQR: 183-211). All reinfection cases were symptomatic but mild, with no hospitalizations or requirement for oxygen therapy. Whole-genome sequencing identified predominant circulation of Delta and Omicron lineages, with complete paired genome sequences confirming reinfection with genetically distinct. Although reinfection was more frequent among HCWs working in COVID-19 care areas (OR = 1.93; 95% CI: 0.63-5.95) and those aged ≥60 years (OR = 2.28; 95% CI: 0.29-18.15), these associations were not statistically significant. CONCLUSIONS: SARS-CoV-2 reinfection occurred among HCWs during the Omicron-dominant period, with moderate incidence and predominantly mild clinical outcomes. Occupational exposure and recent contact history may contribute to reinfection risk. These findings underscore the importance of sustained infection prevention strategies and continued vaccination efforts to mitigate disease severity in high-risk populations.

Changes in age-specific patterns of household transmission of SARS-CoV-2 over six epidemic waves in 2020–2022: a population-based study in Japan
作者： Masahiro Shirata, Isao Ito, Shotaro Aso, Taisuke Jo, Yu Hidaka, Yumiko Ibi, Kensuke Nishioka, Norihiro Jikuya, Hideo Yasunaga, Toyohiro Hirai

Insights from SARS-CoV-2 nanopore genomic surveillance in Edo State, Nigeria

Authors: Cyril Oshomah Erameh, Ehizojie Emua, Emanuele Gustani‐Buss, Joseph Okoeguale, Alexandru Tomazatos, Racheal Omiunu, Sarah Ryter, Precious Olachukwu Njigha, Ganiyu Igenegbale, Obehiaghe Benedicta Okogbenin
Original abstract (English)
Abstract In response to the COVID-19 pandemic, local SARS-CoV-2 nanopore sequencing capacity was established at Irrua Specialist Teaching Hospital (ISTH), a Lassa fever reference centre based in Edo State, Nigeria. This was enabled through training of local staff in Nigeria and Guinea in both laboratory and bioinformatic SARS-CoV-2 sequencing protocols. Between March 2020 and December 2023, the laboratory at ISTH conducted 29,643 diagnostic tests for SARS-CoV-2, of which 4,575 (15.4%) were positive. Between April and December 2022, 410 SARS-CoV-2 positive samples were selected and retrospectively sequenced in 19 runs. From these, 213 near-complete (> 93%) viral genomes were generated and submitted to GISAID. Phylogenetic and phylogeographic analyses supported that Nigeria served simultaneously as a source and recipient of SARS-CoV-2 lineages, driving global spread of the AY.36 Delta variant and receiving Omicron sublineages (BA.1.1 and BQ.1) from North America and Europe. Here we show that a pre-existing long-term laboratory capacity development program for Lassa virus, a high-risk pathogen, facilitated the successful integration of novel workflows for a new emerging pathogen.

Electrochemiluminescence as a Transformative Diagnostic Tool for SARS-CoV-2 Variant Detection: Implications for Future Pandemic Preparedness

Authors: Jarid du Plooy, Kefilwe Vanessa Mokwebo, Keagan Pokpas, Samantha Fiona Douman
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Over the past two decades, the three major betacoronavirus outbreaks, SARS-CoV (2002), MERS-CoV (2012), and SARS-CoV-2 (2019), have resulted in millions of infections and deaths globally. SARS-CoV-2 has displayed rapid mutations, enhancing its transmissibility, immune evasion, and resistance to existing vaccines and diagnostics. For these reasons, it has been named the most notorious viral outbreak in modern history. Due to the ever-mutating Spike (S) protein, key interactions with bioreceptors have been disrupted, making conventional detection methods more challenging. To minimize viral spread and improve diagnostic accuracy, innovative biosensing technologies are critical. As such, electrochemiluminescence (ECL) offers a powerful biosensing platform for SARS-CoV-2 variant detection by combining the high sensitivity of optical detection with electrochemistry. Its advantages include culture-independent detection, low background noise, a broad dynamic range, and the ability to detect low viral concentrations. Recent advances in multiple disciplines, such as novel luminophore development, nanotechnology, lab-on-a-chip miniaturization, and AI-driven machine learning, have further enhanced its diagnostic powers. Furthermore, smartphone-integrated ECL platforms enable rapid, point-of-care testing, improving disease surveillance and outbreak response. By uniting AI with ECL biosensing, the diagnostic system can refine variant classification and predict emerging mutations, making ECL a promising candidate for the next generation of pandemic diagnostics. Despite the many advantages, there is limited research that has explored ECL for SARS-CoV-2 variant detection, which is addressed in this review. This review highlights ECL’s potential to become a gold-standard technique for detecting rapidly evolving pathogens, offering a robust and accessible solution for future infectious disease monitoring.

Authors: Gamuchirai M. Kavhumbura, Wonderful Choga, Sikhulile Moyo, Alltalents T. Murahwa, Vinie Kouamou, Chiratidzo Ellen Ndhlovu, Azure T. Makadzange, Moyo Sikhulile, Tariro Makadzange
Original abstract (English)
Genomic surveillance of severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) depends on sequence quality and reliable specimen linkage. We characterised lineage distribution, genome quality and clinical-data linkage in 731 genomic records from Zimbabwe dated November 2022 to January 2024. Consensus sequences were analyzed using Nextclade, and genomic metadata were linked to clinical records by exact identifiers. Clinical data were available for 697 records; median age was 37 years (interquartile range, 27–48), and 71.4% were female. Fifty-nine Pango lineage assignments and 12 Nextclade clade labels were observed. XBB.1.5 descendants accounted for 443/731 records (60.6%); the most frequent exact assignments were XBB.1.5 (36.4%) and XBB.1.5.28 (17.4%). Quality-control categories were good for 24.9%, mediocre for 42.1% and bad for 33.0%. Identical aligned consensus sequences occurred in 186 pairs involving 372 records (50.9%). Five BA.2.86-group assignments had unresolved date–lineage conflicts. XBB.1.5 descendants were the most frequently assigned group in this historical dataset. Heterogeneous sequence quality, uneven sampling and unresolved record relationships constrain temporal interpretation and preclude national prevalence estimates. The findings highlight the importance of documented specimen linkage and reproducible analysis in retrospective genomic surveillance.

Mental health was evaluated using validated scales for distress (DT), perceived stress (PSS-4), depression (PHQ-2), anxiety (GAD-2), and loneliness (R-UCLA-3). Analyses included group comparisons and binary logistic regression.
Consensus and conclusions
Medical students reported a more positive perception of the MSLE in later survey waves during the transition from pandemic to post-pandemic medical education. However, these improvements were not matched by a uniform recovery in medical students' well-being. Higher age, more advanced study years, greater perceived stress, and perceived lack of practical experience were associated with lower odds of positive MSLE.
Controversies and gaps
The study found that while positive MSLE perceptions increased over time, the changes were not statistically significant after correction for multiple comparisons. The proportion of students reporting decreased study motivation was significantly lower in the final wave, but this was not consistent across all demographic groups. The evidence also highlights varying patterns in mental health indicators, with distress peaking in summer 2022 and anxiety in summer 2023, suggesting unresolved issues in student well-being.
Research and practice implications
Medical schools are encouraged to strengthen students' learning environments by ensuring learning opportunities, mentoring, and mental health support throughout training. The study suggests a need for targeted interventions to address the mental health challenges faced by students, particularly those in more advanced study years and those experiencing loneliness or distress.

A number of countries have stopped reporting or changed their frequency of reporting COVID-19 case and death counts to WHO. An outcome of these differences in reporting is that WHO may receive daily data from some countries, while other countries may only report data to WHO once every 14 days. In addition, countries differ in how they choose to report statistics; some countries provide their data attributed to specific dates while others who report less frequently may group data from 7 days into a single statistic attributed to a week in their reporting. As of 25 August 2023, WHO declared that it is no longer necessary for Member States to report daily counts of cases and deaths to WHO and requested strengthening of weekly reporting. Reported data is still available attributed to specific dates (daily data) here as a download. This dashboard presents the same statistics as weekly figures in charts in order to mitigate against the visual misinterpretation of data. Should daily data have been presented here, many countries would show zero counts for multiple consecutive days due in part to the differences in how they choose to report. While weekly intervals do not completely mitigate against this, the approach reduces the risk that some dashboard users might infer zero cases or deaths when lack of data is often due to reporting differences. A number of countries have stopped reporting or changed their frequency of reporting COVID-19 case and death counts to WHO. An outcome of these differences in reporting is that WHO may receive daily data from some countries, while other countries may only report data to WHO once every 14 days. In addition, countries differ in how they choose to report statistics; some countries provide their data attributed to specific dates while others who report less frequently may group data from 7 days into a single statistic attributed to a week in their reporting. As of 25 August 2023, WHO declared that it is no longer necessary for Member States to report daily counts of cases and deaths to WHO and requested strengthening of weekly reporting. Reported data is still available attributed to specific dates (daily data) here as a download. This dashboard presents the same statistics as weekly figures in charts in order to mitigate against the visual misinterpretation of data. Should daily data have been presented here, many countries would show zero counts for multiple consecutive days due in part to the differences in how they choose to report. While weekly intervals do not completely mitigate against this, the approach reduces the risk that some dashboard users might infer zero cases or deaths when lack of data is often due to reporting differences.
