Evaluating a multi-vaccine respiratory immunization strategy involving SARS-CoV-2, pneumococcal, and influenza vaccines in adults: A randomized, open-label, multicenter, controlled, non-inferiority Phase IV trial in China.

Authors: Xuanwen Shi, Shasha Han, Cong Wu, Xuejuan Wang, Jiao Huang, Wei Chen, Xiaoqin Liu, Sheng Wei, Jun Liu, Xiang Guo
Original abstract (English)
Co-administration of vaccines targeting multiple respiratory pathogens may enhance coverage and streamline adult immunization programs. We conducted a randomized, multicentre, open-label Phase IV trial registered at Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2100046227) in China to evaluate the immunogenicity and safety of co-administering an inactivated SARS-CoV-2 vaccine (WIBP-CorV) with the 23-valent pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine (PPV23) and a quadrivalent inactivated influenza vaccine (IIV4). A total of 1,404 adults aged ≥ 18 year, including equal numbers of adults aged 18-59 year and ≥60 year, were randomized 1:1:1 to receive WIBP-CorV + PPV23 on Day 0 followed by WIBP-CorV + IIV4 on Day 28, WIBP-CorV alone on Days 0 and 28, or PPV23 on Day 0 followed by IIV4 on Day 28. Co-administration preserved humoral immune responses across vaccine platforms. Non-inferiority in geometric mean titers or concentrations was demonstrated for SARS-CoV-2 neutralizing antibodies and pneumococcal serotype-specific IgG responses. Seroconversion rates were non-inferior to those following separate administration for all pneumococcal serotypes, though results were less consistent for SARS-CoV-2 (after the second dose) and influenza A(H1N1). Age-stratified analyses showed age- and strain-specific non-inferiority findings that should be interpreted cautiously. Co-administration demonstrated an acceptable safety profile, with only slightly higher rates of mild, transient local reactions and no vaccine-related serious adverse events (SAEs). These findings support the feasibility of co-administering WIBP-CorV with PPV23 and IIV4 while maintaining immunogenicity and safety in adults represented in this study. Trial registration The study was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR) under registration number ChiCTR2100046227.

Clinical effects of third doses of mRNA COVID-19 vaccines among hemodialysis patients: a nationwide retrospective cohort study.

Authors: Ajin Cho, Seon A. Jeong, Yu Bin Seo, Hayne Cho Park, Do Hyoung Kim, Young‐Ki Lee, Korean Society of Nephrology Disaster Preparedness and Response Committee
Original abstract (English)
Background: Evidence on the effectiveness of third-dose messenger RNA (mRNA) coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) vaccination during the Omicron-dominant period in Asian hemodialysis (HD) patients remains limited. We evaluated the clinical effectiveness of a third mRNA vaccine dose against severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) infection, hospitalization, and mortality in Korean HD patients. Methods: Using the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency-National Health Insurance Service database, we conducted a nationwide cohort study of HD patients aged ≥18 years who received two or three doses of BNT162b2 or mRNA-1273 before January 16, 2022. Study outcomes were SARS-CoV-2 infection, hospitalization, and death. Cox proportional hazards models were used to assess the associations between third-dose vaccination and clinical outcomes. Results: Among 66,389 HD patients (mean age, 65.7 ± 12.9 years; 60.5% male), 55,125 (83.0%) received three vaccine doses and 11,264 (17.0%) received two doses. Receipt of three doses was associated with a significantly lower risk of SARS-CoV-2 infection (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR], 0.58; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.56-0.60). Among 17,886 patients with SARS-CoV-2 infection, third-dose vaccine recipients had a lower risk of hospitalization (aHR, 0.65; 95% CI, 0.62-0.70) and 30-day mortality (aHR, 0.35; 95% CI, 0.30-0.41) compared with those who received two doses. Receipt of mRNA-1273 as the third dose was associated with lower observed risks of infection and hospitalization than receipt of BNT162b2. Conclusion: A third dose of an mRNA COVID-19 vaccine was associated with lower risks of SARS-CoV-2 infection, hospitalization, and mortality among HD patients during the Omicron-dominant period. These findings support the clinical importance of booster vaccination in this high-risk population.

An interpretable machine learning model integrating mucosal and cellular immunity to predict SARS-CoV-2 reinfection: a multicenter cohort study.

Authors: Jinqiu Ran, Wanying Zhang, Hui Huang, Lejun Gong, Shengxia Yin, Yuxin Chen, Aofan Wang, Huijin Min, Liguo Zhu, Jinjin Chu
Original abstract (English)
SARS-CoV-2 reinfection remains a significant public health challenge that requires accurate risk assessment across populations. Using prospectively collected data from two independent cohorts, we developed and externally validated an interpretable machine learning model for predicting symptomatic SARS-CoV-2 reinfection. The cohort comprised 1,156 adults from Wuxi and Nantong, China, with a median follow-up duration of 10 months. Reinfection occurred in 128 participants (11.1%). The model integrated eighteen clinical and immune features, and ultimately identified spike-specific nasal secretory immunoglobulin A (sIgA) and spike-specific CD4 + central memory T cells (T CM ) as key predictors. Developed using the extreme gradient boosting (XGB) algorithm, the prediction model achieved an AUC of 0.835 in the internal validation cohort and demonstrated strong discrimination in the external validation cohort. SHAP analysis revealed that higher nasal spike-specific sIgA was associated with lower risk, while spike-specific CD4 + T CM cells exhibited a non-linear association with reinfection. This interpretable immune-based model provides a framework for immune-informed risk stratification, enabling better prediction and management of SARS-CoV-2 reinfection risk in settings with heterogeneous population immunity.

Positivity rates were highest among those aged 85 years and over, at 13.4%. The report highlights that influenza activity remained stable, with ED attendances for influenza-like illness increasing slightly. Vaccine uptake data for the 2026 to 2027 season showed lower rates among certain groups, such as those aged 65 and over, and those in clinical risk groups, but higher uptake among pregnant women. The report also mentions that data quality issues affected the availability of influenza vaccination data in weeks 41 and 42, which were corrected for week 43. Testing policy changes over time may influence positivity rates, and comparisons should be made cautiously. The report is based on data up to week 42, ending on 18 October 2026.

National flu and COVID-19 surveillance report: 22 October 2026 (week 43) - GOV.UK

News Brief
The National Flu and COVID-19 Surveillance Report for 22 October 2026 (week 43) provides an overview of respiratory virus activity in England. It notes that SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) activity showed mixed trends, with low circulation levels and a slight increase in emergency department (ED) attendances for COVID-19-like illness. The number of confirmed acute respiratory infection (ARI) incidents increased compared to the previous week, with a rolling 7-day positivity rate of 8.9% in hospital settings. Positivity rates were highest among those aged 85 years and over, at 13.4%. The report highlights that influenza activity remained stable, with ED attendances for influenza-like illness increasing slightly. Vaccine uptake data for the 2026 to 2027 season showed lower rates among certain groups, such as those aged 65 and over, and those in clinical risk groups, but higher uptake among pregnant women. The report also mentions that data quality issues affected the availability of influenza vaccination data in weeks 41 and 42, which were corrected for week 43. Testing policy changes over time may influence positivity rates, and comparisons should be made cautiously. The report is based on data up to week 42, ending on 18 October 2026.

A number of countries have stopped reporting or changed their frequency of reporting COVID-19 case and death counts to WHO. As of 25 August 2023, WHO declared that it is no longer necessary for Member States to report daily counts of cases and deaths to WHO and requested strengthening of weekly reporting. Should daily data have been presented here, many countries would show zero counts for multiple consecutive days due in part to the differences in how they choose to report.
