炎症性肠病患者中严重急性呼吸综合征冠状病毒2 mRNA 疫苗的纵向免疫原性与抗TNF治疗及年龄的关系:一项前瞻性队列研究研究

Longitudinal SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccine immunogenicity in inflammatory bowel disease in relation to anti-TNF therapy and age: a prospective cohort study
作者： Wolfram Mayr, Astrid I. Knell, Anna K. Boehm, Michael Jäger, Lilly Ennemoser, Andrea Griesmacher, Rosa Bellmann-weiler, Robert Koch, Herbert Tilg, Wilfried Posch
摘要中文翻译
查看研究七要素
问题与背景
接受免疫调节治疗的炎症性肠病(IBD)患者可能对疫苗接种的反应较差。本研究旨在评估IBD患者在12个月内的严重急性呼吸综合征冠状病毒2 mRNA 疫苗的免疫原性。
设计与对象
本研究为单中心前瞻性队列研究研究,追踪了接受奥地利国家计划下mRNA 疫苗接种的IBD成人患者。所有53名参与者均为无感染史且两次接种,随访时间为3、6和12个月。
核心方法
两剂mRNA 疫苗疫苗间隔28天接种,第三剂在第一剂后6-12个月接种。在基线和接种后3、6、12个月时,测量了S1-RBD IgG、针对野生型、Delta、Omicron BA.1和BA.2的活病毒50%中和滴度(NT50)以及IFN-γ ELISpot反应。使用基于排名的纵向模型和调整年龄和性别的混合效应模型分析了抗TNF治疗和年龄的相关性。
主要结果
All 53 twice-vaccinated, infection-naive patients seroconverted at 3 months. The geometric mean (GM) S1-RBD IgG peaked at 3 months (465.7 BAU/ml) and fell by 6 months (124.8 BAU/ml). At 6 months, the adjusted geometric mean ratio versus other therapy was 0.31 (95% CI 0.20-0.49) for S1-RBD IgG and 0.22 (0.12-0.41) for wild-type NT50. Each additional year of age was associated with 2.4% lower wild-type NT50 (0.976 per year, 95% CI 0.962-0.990).
解释与创新
在相对较年轻的队列研究中,抗TNF治疗和较高年龄与较低且持续时间较短的体液免疫反应相关,而细胞免疫反应似乎相对保持稳定。这些探索性关联需要在具有临床终点的更大规模队列研究中得到验证。
科研与公卫意义
这些发现强调了对接受抗TNF治疗的IBD患者监测疫苗反应的必要性,并突出了年龄对免疫原性潜在影响的重要性。它们还表明,尽管体液免疫反应减弱,细胞免疫可能仍保持强大。
局限与证据强度
该研究受限于单中心设计和相对较小的样本量。证据强度中等,因为它是基于观察性队列研究研究,且没有临床终点。

Longitudinal SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccine immunogenicity in inflammatory bowel disease in relation to anti-TNF therapy and age: a prospective cohort study

Authors: Wolfram Mayr, Astrid I. Knell, Anna K. Boehm, Michael Jäger, Lilly Ennemoser, Andrea Griesmacher, Rosa Bellmann-weiler, Robert Koch, Herbert Tilg, Wilfried Posch
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) on immunomodulatory therapy may respond less well to vaccination. We followed humoral and cellular responses to SARS-CoV-2 vaccination over 12 months. METHODS: In this single-centre, prospective cohort study, adults with IBD received mRNA vaccine under the Austrian national programme. Two doses were administered 28 days apart and a third dose 6-12 months after the first. S1-RBD IgG, live-virus 50% neutralisation titres (NT50) against wild-type, Delta, Omicron BA.1 and BA.2, and IFN-γ ELISpot responses were measured at baseline and after 3, 6 and 12 months. Associations with anti-TNF therapy and age were analysed with rank-based longitudinal models and with mixed-effects models adjusted for age and sex. RESULTS: All 53 twice-vaccinated, infection-naive patients seroconverted at 3 months. Geometric mean (GM) S1-RBD IgG peaked at 3 months (465.7 BAU/ml) and fell by 6 months (124.8 BAU/ml). At 3 months, GM NT50 values were lower against BA.1 (9.0) than against wild-type (24.2) or Delta (26.9), and 13 patients (24.5%) never reached a wild-type NT50 of 1:16. Anti-TNF therapy (32/53, 60.4%) was significantly associated with faster waning. At 6 months, adjusted geometric mean ratio versus other therapy was 0.31 (95% CI 0.20-0.49) for S1-RBD IgG and 0.22 (0.12-0.41) for wild-type NT50. Each additional year of age was associated with 2.4% lower wild-type NT50 (0.976 per year, 95% CI 0.962-0.990). In 33 thrice-vaccinated patients, intercurrent infection (15/33) raised S1-RBD IgG 4.6-fold more than the third dose alone, without a measurable gain in neutralisation. In the adjusted model, IFN-γ ELISpot responses did not differ between treatment groups. DISCUSSION: Anti-TNF therapy and higher age within a relatively young cohort were associated with lower and shorter-lived humoral responses, while cellular responses appeared comparatively preserved. These exploratory associations need confirmation in larger cohorts with clinical endpoints.

Design and population
This single-centre, prospective cohort study followed adults with IBD who received mRNA vaccine under the Austrian national programme. All 53 participants were infection-naive and twice-vaccinated, with follow-up at 3, 6 and 12 months.

Interpretation and novelty
Anti-TNF therapy and higher age within a relatively young cohort were associated with lower and shorter-lived humoral responses, while cellular responses appeared comparatively preserved. These exploratory associations need confirmation in larger cohorts with clinical endpoints.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings inform the need for monitoring vaccine responses in IBD patients on anti-TNF therapy and highlight the potential impact of age on immunogenicity. They also suggest that cellular immunity may remain robust despite reduced humoral responses.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its single-centre design and relatively small sample size. The evidence is moderate as it is based on an observational cohort study with no clinical endpoints.

Genome-wide within-host evolution during prolonged SARS-CoV-2 infection in a person with advanced HIV infection

Authors: Karina Perlaza, Ishaan Dureja, Diane V. Havlir, Vivek Jain, Jeffrey D. Whitman, Monica T. Dayao, Bryan Castillo-Rojas, Julia Rosecrans, Melanie Ott, Carina Marquez
Original abstract (English)
Within-host evolution during prolonged SARS-CoV-2 infection can be extensive in immunocompromised individuals, but the consequences of mutations that emerge and change in frequency over time remain difficult to predict. We characterized an XBB.1.5.10 infection lasting nearly 90 days in a person with advanced human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection. Genome-wide tracking of allele frequencies in samples collected on study days 43, 48, and 70 revealed a Spike N-terminal domain (NTD) deletion spanning residues 138-145 at frequencies of 33%, 80%, and 96%, respectively. A receptor-binding domain (RBD) deletion-insertion spanning residues 373-378 was detected on days 43 and 48 but not on day 70, while nsp12 C464Y was detected at approximately 8% on day 48 and 86% on day 70. We reconstructed representative Spike and non-Spike mutation profiles from the longitudinal samples and tested their effects on cell entry and viral RNA replication. The representative Spike construct for days 43 and 48 increased entry relative to XBB.1.5. Comparison with a matched construct lacking the receptor-binding domain deletion-insertion showed that the deletion-insertion enhanced entry, although it was not detected in the day 70 sample. The day 48 non-Spike profile supported the highest RNA replication among the tested replicons. Together, allele-frequency tracking revealed both transient mutations and mutations detected at progressively higher frequencies across the sampled time points. Functional testing showed that increased entry or RNA replication in vitro did not necessarily predict persistence in vivo.

Recombinant Variants of SARS-CoV-2 Identified in the South of Western Siberia During the COVID-19 Pandemic

Authors: Anton P. Devyaterikov, Natalia V. Palyanova, Andrey Yu. Palyanov, Andrey B. Komissarov, Ivan A. Sobolev, Alexander M. Shestopalov
Original abstract (English)
Recombination is one of the mechanisms driving SARS-CoV-2 evolution and can generate genomes with novel combinations of characteristics inherited from parental lineages. We analyzed a total of 1963 genomes available SARS-CoV-2 genome sequences collected in the south of Western Siberia between March 2020 and 5 May 2023. Initial screening with Nextclade identified five potentially recombinant genomes. The consensus assemblies were re-examined by the originating laboratories and the absence of co-infection was confirmed. Recombinant origin was assessed with RecombinHunt and independently evaluated with 3SEQ and RDP5. EPI_ISL_11742584 was excluded because of numerous undetermined nucleotides. For EPI_ISL_15919275 the laboratory of origin did not confirm the reproducibility of the build. EPI_ISL_20438394 contained BA.2.35-like and XBB.3.1-like components but the signal did not survive correction for multiple comparisons. Recombination was confirmed for two sequences. EPI_ISL_17317906 presumably originated from CR.1-like and XBB.1.15-like lineages and was assigned to the recombinant lineage XBV. EPI_ISL_16287253 carried a BA.2-derived segment on a BA.1-derived background. These findings indicate that automated classification should be followed by statistical validation, verification of genome assembly quality and the confirmed absence of co-infection. Incomplete and uneven genomic surveillance may still limit reconstruction of the exact origins of rare recombinant variants.

Original abstract (English)
Background: /Objectives: The global rollout of COVID-19 vaccines faced significant implementation challenges: limited adult immunization infrastructure, particularly in low- and middle-income countries; rapidly shifting viral epidemiology driven by emerging variants; and widespread vaccine misinformation that fueled hesitancy. The review assessed intervention effectiveness across diverse population groups and settings and identified lessons to inform the design of adult vaccination programs and future outbreak response. Methods: Between March 2022 and July 2024, we performed a systematic search in PubMed, Scopus, and Global Index Medicus, for COVID-19 demand and uptake studies published after December 2020, with no language or geographic restrictions. Studies were categorized into nine intervention approaches, based on an adapted communication and service-delivery taxonomy. Interventions operated at individual, systems, and structural levels. These findings have relevance beyond COVID-19, offering transferable lessons for adult immunization programs and future outbreak preparedness.

新闻简报The National Flu and COVID-19 Surveillance Report for 8 October 2026 (week 41) provides an overview of the health situation in England up to week 40. It notes that influenza activity increased, with ED attendances for ILI above seasonally expected levels, while SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) activity rose slightly, circulating at low levels. RSV activity remained low. The report highlights that vaccine uptake for the 2026-2027 season is lower in certain age groups, including those aged 65 and over, and individuals in clinical risk groups. However, uptake among pregnant women is comparable. The findings are based on data from the Second Generation Surveillance System (SGSS) and other sources, using statistical methods like MEM and MSD to assess activity levels. The report cautions that results may change as more data becomes available, particularly for older adults and pregnant women. The data is subject to interpretation due to potential shifts in testing policies and practices over time.
