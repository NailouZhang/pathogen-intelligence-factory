在黎巴嫩引入监测污水严重急性呼吸综合征冠状病毒2

Introducing SARS-CoV-2 wastewater surveillance in Lebanon
作者： Mirna Bou Hamdan, Mariam Mroweh, Farouk F. Abou Hassan, Aline Germani, Jocelyn DeJong, Fadi El‐Jardali, Nada M. Melhem, On behalf of CAEP Study Group, Fadi El-Jardali, CAEP Study Group

问题与背景
The study aimed to detect and sequence SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater from an area in Lebanon that hosts vulnerable groups of Lebanese residents and refugees. Wastewater surveillance (WWS) gained global attention during the COVID-19 pandemic as an early warning system to continuously monitor SARS-CoV-2 and its evolving variants.
设计与对象
2023年2月20日至5月31日期间,从18个检查井中收集了188份污水样本。本研究聚焦于一个包含易感人群的黎巴嫩区域。收集的样本中有18%为严重急性呼吸综合征冠状病毒2阳性。
核心方法
进行了病毒RNA提取、逆转录聚合酶链反应(RT-PCR)和基因组测序。本研究使用从检查井中收集的污水样本检测和对严重急性呼吸综合征冠状病毒2进行测序。
主要结果
3月份检测到Omicron BA.2(33.33%)与B.1.1类似亚系的重叠,包括C.26(16.67%)、B.1.1.207(16.67%)、B.1.1.214(16.67%)和C.2.1(16.67%)。4月份,尽管Omicron的传播率下降至12.5%,但仍检测到BA.1和BA.2的重组株(XM)。在国家病例激增之前,污水样本在大多数月份显示出更高的阳性率。
解释与创新
这些发现表明,污水监测可以提供严重急性呼吸综合征冠状病毒2传播趋势的早期信号。这些结果突出了在资源有限的环境中实施WWS的重要性与可行性,以补充流行病学的监测。
科研与公卫意义
本研究为利用污水监测监测黎巴嫩严重急性呼吸综合征冠状病毒2趋势提供了证据。它强调了WWS在资源有限地区对公共卫生干预措施的潜在价值,并补充临床数据以监测严重急性呼吸综合征冠状病毒2在人群/社区层面的趋势。
局限与证据强度
由于研究地点居民缺乏细分数据集和病例特定的严重急性呼吸综合征冠状病毒2报告,限制了我们对污水与国家临床数据之间进行相关性和比较分析的能力。由于数据可用性的限制以及研究的观察性质,证据强度中等。

Introducing SARS-CoV-2 wastewater surveillance in Lebanon

Authors: Mirna Bou Hamdan, Mariam Mroweh, Farouk F. Abou Hassan, Aline Germani, Jocelyn DeJong, Fadi El‐Jardali, Nada M. Melhem, On behalf of CAEP Study Group, Fadi El-Jardali, CAEP Study Group
Original abstract (English)
UNLABELLED: Wastewater surveillance (WWS) gained global attention during the COVID-19 pandemic as an early warning system to continuously monitor SARS-CoV-2 and its evolving variants. The aim of this study is to detect and sequence SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater from an area in Lebanon that hosts vulnerable groups of Lebanese residents and refugees. A total of 188 wastewater samples were collected from 18 manholes between 20 February 2023 and 31 May 2023. Viral RNA extraction, RT-PCR, and genomic sequencing were performed. Eighteen percent of collected samples were SARS-CoV-2-positive. Omicron BA.2 was the only circulating variant in February. An overlap between Omicron BA.2 (33.33%) and B.1.1-like sublineages, including C.26 (16.67%), B.1.1.207 (16.67%), B.1.1.214 (16.67%), and C.2.1 (16.67%) was detected in March. In April, while Omicron circulation decreased to 12.5%, a BA.1 and BA.2 recombinant (XM) was detected. The lack of disaggregated data sets and case-specific SARS-CoV-2 reports from the study site residents limited our ability to perform correlation and comparative analyses between wastewater and national clinical data. However, wastewater samples showed higher positivity rates during most of the months prior to national surges of cases. These findings underscore the importance of incorporating WWS in public health interventions to complement clinical and epidemiologic surveillance. IMPORTANCE: Wastewater surveillance (WWS) is a valuable environmental surveillance tool that facilitates the real-time detection of circulating viruses and emerging variants. The COVID-19 pandemic has led to significant acceleration in the use of WWS as an early warning system for detecting SARS-CoV-2 and monitoring community transmission. This is the first study introducing SARS-CoV-2 community-based WWS in Lebanon. The findings of this study highlight the importance and feasibility of implementing WWS in a limited-resource setting to complement epidemiological surveillance in monitoring trends of SARS-CoV-2 at the population/community level.

Real-world safety outcomes of COVID-19 mRNA vaccine: A result from Korea post-marketing surveillance

Authors: Sang Hoon Han, Jang Hee Hong, Sook In Jung, Mo Ji-Hun, Juwon Woo, Jacob Lee, Ji-Hun Mo
Original abstract (English)
The COVID-19 pandemic has caused a substantial global health burden, highlighting the need for post-authorization safety evaluation in real-world settings. BNT162b2 is a widely used mRNA COVID-19 vaccine, and, in addition to the original strain-based formulation, BA.1-, BA.4/5-, XBB.1.5-, and JN.1-adapted formulations were introduced sequentially. This study aimed to evaluate the real-world safety profile of the BNT162b2 product family using post-marketing surveillance data collected in Korea. Data collection was conducted from 50 sites. Solicited adverse events (AEs) were collected for 7 days, and unsolicited AEs for 28 days after vaccination. AEs occurring beyond Day 28 were also collected if reported during routine follow-up, without restriction on the collection period. Of 6,343 enrolled participants, 6,251 were included in the safety analysis set. Overall AEs were reported in 1,640 participants (26.2%) and adverse drug reactions (ADRs) in 1,610 participants (25.8%). Serious AEs occurred in 2 participants (<0.1%), and serious ADRs in 1 participant (<0.1%); no unexpected serious ADRs were identified. Solicited AEs were predominantly mild reactogenicity events, most commonly injection site pain, myalgia, fatigue, and headache. The overall safety profile of the BNT162b2 product family was generally manageable in this predominantly adult population, with very few serious ADRs and no unexpected serious ADRs identified. However, due to limited systematic capture of adverse events of special interest (AESIs) and underrepresentation of pediatric participants, AESI-related findings and pediatric safety conclusions should be interpreted with caution. These findings provide complementary real-world evidence regarding the safety of BNT162b2-adapted vaccines in Korea.

Modeling the cost-effectiveness of NVX-CoV2705 and the public health impact of vaccination for the prevention of seasonal COVID-19 in the US

Authors: Lindsey Fox, Kyle Paret, Maureen P. Neary, José Bartelt-Hofer, William L. Herring, Charalampos Valmas, Karla Therese L. Sy, Katie A. Sharff, Stefan Gravenstein
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Older adults and those with comorbidities have increased risk of severe disease and mortality from COVID-19. This analysis estimated the cost-effectiveness of COVID-19 vaccination versus no vaccination in the US and potential impact of increased vaccine coverage. RESEARCH DESIGN AND METHODS: A Markov-based model was updated using the latest epidemiology and vaccine reactogenicity data. Cases, costs, and outcomes for individuals aged 18-64 with ≥1 comorbidity and ≥65 years were estimated for NVX-CoV2705 (Nuvaxovid) versus no vaccination from a third-party payer perspective. Scenario analyses considered the societal perspective and analogous comparisons for BNT162b2 (Comirnaty), mRNA-1273 (Spikevax), and mRNA-1283 (mNEXSPIKE). Results were scaled for public health impact analyses. RESULTS: Base-case results associated NVX-CoV2705 with fewer cases (0.0447), hospitalizations (0.0037), and deaths (0.0004) per person on average. NVX-CoV2705 yielded an ICER of $9,840/QALY gained, reflecting fewer QALYs lost (0.0039) alongside increased direct costs ($38.09) per person. Differences for mRNA vaccines were driven by price and reactogenicity. Increasing vaccine coverage by 5-15 percentage points resulted in 114-341 hospitalizations and 11-33 deaths averted per 1,000,000 individuals. CONCLUSIONS: NVX-CoV2705 was predicted to be cost-effective over no vaccination. This analysis included a more granular approach to modeling reactogenicity events, an important consideration in vaccine cost-effectiveness analyses.

Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) causes COVID-19, a highly transmissible acute respiratory infection that can result in severe pneumonia and death. Many details of SARS-CoV-2 infection are not fully understood, including the cell biology and host-virus interactions involved in coronavirus assembly and release, in which the Envelope (E) structural protein is instrumental. Deletion of E in other coronaviruses has been shown previously to either attenuate or abrogate infection. To determine the role of E in SARS-CoV-2 virus production and infectivity, we produced a reporter SARS-CoV-2 with or without the E gene deleted using a bacterial artificial chromosome. Replication of ΔE SARS-CoV-2 was attenuated in Vero E6 cells expressing human ACE2 and TMPRSS2 and in human epithelial cell lines. Electron and immunofluorescence microscopy and virology assays showed that ΔE SARS-CoV-2 increased cell surface expression of Spike (S) glycoprotein, leading to reduced S incorporation into ΔE SARS-CoV-2 particles and promotion of increased cell-to-cell transmission that evades neutralizing antibody inhibition. In addition to validating the role of E in retention of S in the ER-Golgi intermediate complex (ERGIC), our results showed that a lack of E led to reorganization of the ERGIC and Golgi during SARS-CoV-2 infection. SARS-CoV-2 lacking Envelope transmitted primarily via cell fusion and evaded neutralizing antibodies. In addition, the absence of Envelope resulted in the reorganization of the cell secretory compartment during SARS-CoV-2 infection. A better understanding of how Envelope influences SARS-CoV-2 replication could guide directed design of novel therapeutics for treatment of COVID-19 patients, as well as the potential for pan-coronavirus protection against future coronavirus outbreaks.

Authors: Eric M. Foote, Ellen L. Bouchard, Elizabeth F. Baylis, Shiffen Getabecha, Phacharee Arunleung, Maria Salas, Gautham Gautham, Christina Morales, Debra A. Wadford, Kathleen R. Jacobson
Original abstract (English)
Background: Testing arriving international travelers for COVID-19 paired with whole-genome sequencing (WGS) may identify new introductions of SARS-CoV-2 variants of concern in California, enabling a timely public health response. Traveler genomic surveillance may be a way to improve gaps in worldwide disease genomic surveillance. Objective: This paper aimed to describe a pilot surveillance program to identify SARS-CoV-2 variants in arriving international travelers at San Francisco International Airport (SFO). Methods: The California Department of Public Health implemented a pilot program at SFO to voluntarily test arriving international travelers for COVID-19 from June 2021 to November 2021 to identify SARS-CoV-2 variants. Arriving international travelers were offered self-collect COVID-19 antigen tests upon arrival at SFO. Travelers with positive COVID-19 antigen tests or with COVID-19 symptoms were recommended to self-collect and mail an additional specimen for diagnostic molecular testing. WGS of SARS-CoV-2 was performed on RNA extracts from positive tests to identify variants. Results: In total, 2.1% (19,008/892,558) of all arriving international travelers at SFO from 133 different countries of departure enrolled in the program. The COVID-19 test positivity among this group was 0.7% (33/5038), and the weekly test positivity range, 0% to 1.5%, was lower than the weekly test positivity across the state of California during the same time frame. Arriving international travelers departing from Turkey (3/145, 2.1%), Mexico (7/602, 1.2%), and the United Kingdom (6/626, 1.0%) had the highest test positivity. Of the 14 positive molecular tests, 10 (71.4%) were sequenced, and all were found to belong to the SARS-CoV-2 Delta B.1.617.2 lineage, the most prevalent SARS-CoV-2 variant in California and worldwide at the time. Conclusions: Voluntary testing of arriving international travelers paired with WGS was operationally and logistically feasible. Although the number of sequenced positive COVID-19 specimens in this program was limited, the lessons learned from this pilot informed improvements to subsequent iterations of traveler viral genomic surveillance in California and highlighted the potential value of traveler viral genomic surveillance to support early identification and characterization of emerging infectious diseases entering a region.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract The effectiveness of travel restrictions in mitigating the spread of novel viral variants remains understudied, particularly in regions with sparse genomic surveillance. We evaluated the efficacy of these restrictions in Western Asia, where countries sequenced less than one percent of SARS-CoV-2 cases in 2021, focusing on Iraq’s travel ban on India and the tourist travel restriction. We generated 535 SARS-CoV-2 whole-genome sequences from clinical samples collected in Duhok, Iraq, between June 2021 and March 2022, encompassing the Delta and Omicron variant waves. Using discrete Bayesian phylogeographic modeling, we reconstructed spatiotemporal viral spread patterns to determine whether travel restrictions delayed the introduction of the variants into the country, postponed community transmission onset, or altered the geographic sources of viral introductions over time. We found that the Delta variant was introduced into Iraq three months before the targeted travel ban was enacted, predominantly from Western and Southern Asia, whereas the Omicron variant was introduced despite the tourist travel restriction already being in place. We also show that both variants had established community transmission within the country despite travel restrictions, rendering these measures ineffective. Analysis of travel restrictions on a global scale reveals similar patterns of limited efficacy in mitigating the introductions of novel SARS-CoV-2 variants, challenging the usefulness of such measures.

Background Multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children (MIS-C) associated with SARS-CoV-2 frequently involves acute myocardial dysfunction. However, the prevalence of persistent left ventricular (LV) systolic dysfunction at longer-term follow-up remains uncertain. We therefore performed a systematic review and meta-analysis to estimate the prevalence of persistent LV systolic dysfunction at ≥6 months after MIS-C. Methods We included observational cohort studies reporting longitudinal echocardiographic follow-up in children and adolescents aged <21 years with SARS-CoV-2–associated MIS-C.
