尼帕病毒和单克隆抗体介绍:理解地方性和高风险国家的观点以指导需求预测

Nipah vaccine and monoclonal antibody introduction: Understanding endemic and at-risk country perspectives to inform demand forecasting
作者： Katelyn A. Dinkel, Anika Ruisch, Ishani Mathur, Rebakaone T. Bowe, Peter Windus, Justice Nonvignon, Damian G. Walker, Andrew A. Torkelson

Nipah vaccine and monoclonal antibody introduction: Understanding endemic and at-risk country perspectives to inform demand forecasting

Authors: Katelyn A. Dinkel, Anika Ruisch, Ishani Mathur, Rebakaone T. Bowe, Peter Windus, Justice Nonvignon, Damian G. Walker, Andrew A. Torkelson
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Nipah virus (NiV) is a zoonotic pathogen in the Henipavirus genus that causes severe, often fatal disease in humans. The World Health Organization and the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations (CEPI) have designated NiV as a priority pathogen with pandemic potential. While CEPI is funding research on medical countermeasures, including vaccines and monoclonal antibodies (mAbs), there remains limited understanding of how at-risk countries will use them. This study aims to address this gap through targeted interviews and demand forecasting for 10 countries: Australia, Bangladesh, Cambodia, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam. Methodology/Principal Findings Interviews with country and global stakeholders gathered data on regional sentiment, financing, and affordability. Profiles were developed to summarize each country’s perceived outbreak risk and their likely use of countermeasures. Demand was estimated by integrating these perspectives into models of likely country-level responses to three NiV outbreak scenarios: Expected, Amplified, and Extended. In the Expected scenario, countries focused on reactive use with global annual demand ranging from approximately 800 to 5,000 doses for vaccine-only, mAb-only, and combination interventions. Ring strategies were preferred given outbreak size and infrequency. Demand was highest for vaccine-only and combination interventions, with lower demand for mAbs-only due to affordability concerns and less familiarity in Bangladesh and India. In Amplified and Extended scenarios, countries expanded their responses to include preventive campaigns for healthcare workers and rural populations. These campaigns would require approximately 410,000 and 6,300,000 doses, respectively, if introduced in all 10 countries. Conclusions/Significance This study has revealed existing sentiments regarding Nipah disease and interventions and modeled demand across three outbreak scenarios. Given global annual demand based on current epidemiology, Nipah countermeasure markets are unlikely to be commercially viable solely on routine country-financed demand. Instead, market health will require external financing and stockpile mechanisms to maintain demand between sporadic outbreaks. Author Summary Nipah virus (NiV), which causes severe and often fatal disease in humans, is recognized by the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations (CEPI) as a priority pathogen with pandemic potential. In response, CEPI has supported the development of vaccines and monoclonal antibodies (mAb). However, limited evidence exists on how at-risk countries will deploy these medical countermeasures. We interviewed key decision-makers across 10 at-risk countries, along with global experts on NiV, to better understand disease prioritization, financing and affordability considerations, and countermeasure preferences. Interview findings informed the development of a demand forecast in response to Nipah outbreaks of varying intensity. At expected outbreak levels, countries focused their response on reactive strategies given outbreak size and infrequency. As outbreaks grew in size, countries considered expanding their response to include preventive campaigns targeting healthcare workers and at-risk, rural populations. This study provides new insights into country-level perspectives on NiV and medical countermeasures and quantifies expected demand across three outbreak scenarios and 10 countries. Our findings can provide a foundation for the strategic development of an international Nipah countermeasure stockpile, designed to address episodic NiV outbreaks and enhance pandemic preparedness.

在ReGlyco Binder Design Filter框架中整合糖基化进行从头蛋白设计

Integrating glycosylation in de novo protein design with ReGlyco Binder Design Filter
作者： O. Singh, E. Fadda
摘要中文翻译基于人工智能(AI)的3D蛋白质结构预测方法正在革新结构生物学,使用户能够迅速获得任何分子结构和蛋白质-蛋白质相互作用(PPI)的3D视角。尽管目前各种方法存在固有的局限性,算法的持续改进以及开放获取(OA)网络服务器、软件包和数据库的广泛可用性,必将加速生物制药的发现。在此背景下,开发针对特定靶点的蛋白质结合体从头设计计算流程尤其令人振奋。目前,这些过程仍然相当低效且昂贵,能够快速输出数千种设计,但实际产出却寥寥无几。本文展示如何通过在生物制剂的3D从头设计流程中显式地将糖基化作为筛选条件,显著提高效率并降低实验室成本,而所需的额外计算资源却极少。作为概念验证,我们使用了GlycoShape数据库和ReGlyco工具(https://glycoshape.org)来筛选由Adaptyv Bio于2025年10月发起的开放竞赛中针对高度糖基化的尼帕病毒糖蛋白(NiV-G)(https://proteinbase.com/competitions/adaptyv-nipah-competition)设计的结合体。通过ReGlyco对1,201个选定设计进行筛选,可以在双核CPU上约3小时内消除20%的确认非结合体。通过旋转体搜索进行优化后,可在实验前标记11%的非结合体,并从预测的3D复合物中捕获所有但5个确认的结合体。可用的替代结合构象进一步提高了这一评分。我们还提供了一个开放获取的colab笔记本演示(https://colab.research.google.com/github/Ojas-Singh/GlycoShape-Resources/blob/main/colab/ReGlyco_Binder_Design_filter.ipynb)以说明我们的工作流程。在该演示中,用户可以通过将GlycoShape资源与来自蛋白质设计研究所(IDP)的RFdiffusion3(RFD3)流程结合,设计针对人类促红细胞生成素(hEPO)的“迷你结合体”。
查看研究七要素
问题与背景
本研究旨在解决当前针对尼帕病毒设计蛋白质结合体方法的低效和高成本问题,因为尼帕病毒是一种重要的公共卫生问题,其潜在能力可导致严重疾病。
设计与对象
本研究使用计算流程进行针对尼帕病毒的蛋白质结合体从头设计,重点在于通过将糖基化作为筛选条件来提高效率。
核心方法
方法包括使用GlycoShape数据库和ReGlyco工具筛选针对尼帕病毒糖蛋白的开放竞赛结果,以及通过旋转体搜索进行优化以在实验前识别非结合体。
主要结果
通过ReGlyco筛选1,201个选定设计,可以在双核CPU上约3小时内消除20%的确认非结合体。通过旋转体搜索进行优化后,可在实验前标记11%的非结合体,并从预测的3D复合物中捕获所有但5个确认的结合体。可用的替代结合构象进一步提高了这一评分。
解释与创新
作者将这些结果解释为表明,在从头设计流程中整合糖基化作为筛选条件可以显著提高效率并降低实验室成本,所需的额外计算资源极少,这是蛋白质结合体设计领域的一种新颖方法。
科研与公卫意义
这些发现可以为开发更高效、更具成本效益的方法提供信息,用于针对尼帕病毒设计蛋白质结合体,这对生物制药发现以及尼帕病毒感染的治疗具有重要意义。
局限与证据强度
本研究的局限性在于其依赖于单一竞赛数据集,并且没有对所有结果进行直接实验验证,这可能会影响研究结果的普遍适用性。证据强度中等。

Integrating glycosylation in de novo protein design with ReGlyco Binder Design Filter

Authors: O. Singh, E. Fadda
Original abstract (English)
Artificial Intelligence (AI)-based methods for 3D protein structure prediction are revolutionising structural biology, rapidly giving users a 3D perspective on any molecular architecture and protein-protein interaction (PPI) virtually on-demand. Regardless of the inherent limitations of the various methods to date, the continuous improvement of the algorithms, and the broad availability of open access (OA) web servers, software packages and databases are bound to accelerate biopharmaceuticals’ discovery. Within this context, the development of computational pipelines for the de novo design of target-specific protein binders is especially exciting. As it stands, these processes are still rather inefficient and expensive, rapidly outputting thousands of designs relatively quickly, which translate into meagre yields. Here we show how the explicit integration of glycosylation as a filter in the 3D de novo design pipeline of biologics can significantly improve efficiency and reduce laboratory costs with minimal additional computational resources. As a proof-of-concept, we used the GlycoShape database and ReGlyco tools (https://glycoshape.org) to filter the results of an open competition launched by Adaptyv Bio in October 2025 for the design of binders as inhibitors against the heavily glycosylated Nipah virus glycoprotein (NiV-G) (https://proteinbase.com/competitions/adaptyv-nipah-competition). Screening of the 1,201 selected designs in block with ReGlyco allows users to eliminate 20% of the confirmed non-binders from the pool in approximately 3 hours on a dual-core CPU. Refinement with a rotamer search enabled flags 11% of non-binders prior to experiment and catches all but 5 confirmed binders from the given predicted 3D complexes. The availability of alternative binding poses improves this score. We complement this analysis with a demo OA colab notebook (https://colab.research.google.com/github/Ojas-Singh/GlycoShape-Resources/blob/main/colab/ReGlyco_Binder_Design_filter.ipynb) to illustrate our workflow. In this demo users can design ‘mini binders’ against human erythropoietin (hEPO) by integrating GlycoShape resources with the RFdiffusion3 (RFD3) pipeline from the Institute for Protein Design (IDP).

Question and background
The study aimed to address the inefficiency and high cost of current methods for designing protein binders against Nipah virus, which is a significant public health concern due to its potential to cause severe disease.
Design and population
The study involved the use of computational pipelines for de novo design of protein binders targeting Nipah virus, with a focus on improving efficiency through the integration of glycosylation as a filter.
Core methods
The methods included the use of the GlycoShape database and ReGlyco tools to filter results from an open competition for binder design against Nipah virus glycoprotein, as well as refinement with a rotamer search to identify non-binders prior to experimental validation.
Main results
Screening of the 1,201 selected designs using ReGlyco allowed the elimination of 20% of confirmed non-binders in approximately 3 hours on a dual-core CPU, and refinement with a rotamer search flagged 11% of non-binders before experiment, catching all but 5 confirmed binders from the predicted 3D complexes.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted these results as demonstrating that integrating glycosylation as a filter in the de novo design pipeline significantly improves efficiency and reduces laboratory costs with minimal additional computational resources, which is a novel approach in the field of protein binder design.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings can inform the development of more efficient and cost-effective methods for designing protein binders against Nipah virus, which has important implications for biopharmaceutical discovery and the treatment of Nipah virus infections.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its reliance on a single competition dataset and the absence of direct experimental validation for all results, which may affect the generalizability of the findings. The evidence is moderate in strength.

News Brief
Clinical trials for a vaccine against the Nipah virus are set to begin in Japan, as announced by the University of Tokyo. The trials are scheduled to start in April 2026 in Belgium, following recent outbreaks of the virus in India that began in January 2026. The Nipah virus is a zoonotic infection transmitted from animals to humans, primarily through large fruit bats. It is highly fatal and can cause severe damage to the nervous and respiratory systems. Currently, there is no approved effective vaccine against the Nipah virus globally. If successful, this vaccine could help prevent future outbreaks and reduce epidemiological risks. The development is a confirmed initiative, with no further details on the trial's progress or outcomes provided in the evidence.
