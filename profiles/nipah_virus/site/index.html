Information nudging, attitude, and protective behaviours against Nipah virus in India, 2026: a national experimental survey

Authors: Si-Jia Wang, Li-Yan Zhou, Kun-Yue Xing, Zhi-Yu Zhang, Shun-Zhe Wu, Qiao Liu, Min Liu, Hong-Liang Zhang, Sri Harsha Kota, Li-Yuan Tao
Original abstract (English)
Background: Nipah virus (NiV) poses a significant public health threat due to its high fatality, yet public readiness for medical countermeasures remains understudied. We aimed to evaluate the associations of perceived severity and benefits information nudges with the dimensions of the Health Belief Model (HBM), identify highly responsive populations, and assess whether changes in HBM dimensions mediate the associations between information nudges and protective behavioural intentions in India. Methods: We conducted a nationwide experimental web survey among Indian adults between 6 and 18 February 2026. The survey contained an embedded information nudge module in which participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: a perceived severity nudge, a perceived benefits nudge, or no nudge. We assessed changes in HBM dimensions using analysis of covariance. Six predictive models were evaluated to identify subpopulations highly susceptible to cognitive shifts. Mediation analyses were performed to estimate the indirect effects of nudges on intentions to promptly seek medical care, receive vaccination, and accept treatment. Results: Our sample comprised 1,124 participants randomly assigned to receive a perceived severity nudge (n = 373), a perceived benefits nudge (n = 376), or no nudge (n = 375). Compared with no nudge, the severity nudge significantly increased perceived severity (adjusted mean difference (AMD) = 0.17; 95% confidence interval (CI) = 0.05, 0.30; P = 0.005) and cues to action (AMD = 0.20; 95% CI = 0.09, 0.32; P < 0.001), while the benefits nudge significantly increased cues to action (0.30; 95% CI = 0.19, 0.41; P < 0.001). Predictive modelling (extreme gradient boosting: area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) = 0.717; logistic regression: AUROC = 0.789) identified baseline health beliefs and residence outside prior outbreak regions as dominant contributors to nudge responsiveness. Mediation analyses indicated significant indirect effects of the severity nudge on treatment intention via perceived severity (effect estimate (β) = 0.016; 95% CI = 0.002, 0.032), as well as the perceived benefits nudge via cues to action on both vaccination intention (β = 0.024; 95% CI = 0.001, 0.054) and treatment intention (β = 0.028; 95% CI = 0.002, 0.055). Neither information nudge significantly influenced promptly seeking medical care. Conclusions: Targeted information nudges could alter health beliefs and influence vaccination and treatment readiness through distinct psychological pathways.

Deterministic–stochastic fractional-order modeling and optimal control of Nipah virus in hypertensive nephrosclerosis

Authors: Muhammad Aon Raza, Saima Rashid, Muhammad Imran
Original abstract (English)
Nipah virus is a zoonotic virus that causes severe respiratory and neurological disease and is spread through pigs, bats or contaminated food. This research develops a fractional-order mathematical model to investigate the co-infection dynamics of hypertensive nephrosclerosis and Nipah virus infection. The model is kept positive and bounded to guarantee biological reality, and the disease-free equilibrium E p l is studied. The basic reproduction number R 0 l p < 1 implies extinction of the infection while R 0 l p > 1 implies small outbreaks developing into epidemics. These results are backed up by bifurcation analysis, global stability analysis and existence and uniqueness proofs for the solution. Real Malaysian co-infection data was used to parameterise the model, and simulations were carried out for both deterministic and stochastic fractional systems. The fractional-order parameter has significant impact on the infection rates and convergence behaviour. The memory effects and nonlocal dynamics are described by using piecewise Caputo–Fabrizio derivatives. Stochastic perturbations are considered to model random environmental effects. The parameters were changed to highlight the role of vaccination and stochasticity in disease extinction. The amalgamation of Caputo–Fabrizio fractional calculus and stochastic modelling, supported by a novel numerical scheme, offers a robust and flexible approach to simulate co-infection dynamics under uncertainty. The framework is further validated by PRCC sensitivity analysis and MCMC parameter estimation. Optimal control strategies provide to control the co-infection spread with economic feasibility. To summarize, the model provides useful insights into the interaction between hypertensive nephrosclerosis and Nipah virus transmission, supporting the design of effective and economically feasible control strategies.

Development of a Pan-Proteome Multi-Epitope Vaccine Candidate Against Nipah Virus Using Reverse Vaccinology and Immunoinformatics Approaches

Authors: M. A. Khan, Md. Nahian, Bithi Akter, Tabassum Basher, Tanjum Ahmed Nodee, Irin Aminur Asha, Mostafizur Rahaman Sany, Sanjida Akter Sathy, Farzana Sharmin Shila, Mohammad Ariful Islam
Original abstract (English)
Nipah virus (NiV) is a high-consequence zoonotic pathogen for which no licensed human vaccine is currently available. This study applied a consensus-guided pan-proteome immunoinformatics framework to design and prioritize a multi-epitope vaccine candidate using sequence information from all nine NiV proteins. A total of 901 NiV protein sequences were retrieved, of which 842 passed quality filtering and were processed through clustering, multiple-sequence alignment, and protein-specific consensus generation. Consensus-derived fragments were screened for cytotoxic T-lymphocyte, helper T-lymphocyte, and B-cell epitopes, followed by multi-criterion filtering based on antigenicity, allergenicity, toxicity, conservancy, class-specific immune-response properties, and human-proteome similarity. Ten epitope-selection strategies were combined with two adjuvant candidates and three construct architectures to generate 60 vaccine constructs. Shortlisted candidates were subsequently evaluated for physicochemical properties, HLA population coverage, structural characteristics, conformational B-cell epitopes, TLR3 docking, molecular dynamics, immune-response simulation, and expression-oriented optimization. VC033_V6_BD_L03 construct was prioritized as the lead candidate and showed combined predicted population coverage of 99.78% worldwide and 99.96% in South Asia. Docking against human TLR3 structure 1ZIW yielded a ClusPro score of -1624.8. During a 100-ns molecular-dynamics simulation, the complex underwent early conformational reorganization followed by lower late-phase structural variation and persistent interchain hydrogen bonding. MM/GBSA analysis yielded a mean interaction-energy estimate of -20.52, 15.42 kcal/mol. C-ImmSim predicted coordinated humoral and cellular responses, while codon optimization generated a 1,539-bp expression sequence compatible with virtual cloning into pET-28a(+). These analyses prioritize VC033_V6_BD_L03 as a consensus-derived, pan-proteome NiV multi-epitope vaccine candidate for experimental evaluation of expression, immunogenicity, safety, and protective efficacy.

Original abstract (English)
Henipaviruses are negative-strand RNA viruses in the Paramyxoviridae family with currently five species: Cedar, Ghanaian bat, Hendra, Mojang and Nipah henipaviruses. They are naturally harbored by pteropid fruit bats (flying foxes) and microbats of several species found throughout the tropics and subtropics. Henipavirus outbreaks in humans have occurred in northern Australia and Southeast Asia since the mid-1990s. Hendra virus (HeV) has been reported nearly annually since 1994 in the eastern states of Australia, while Nipah virus (NiV) outbreaks in humans were first reported in 1998-99 in Malaysia and Singapore and are reported almost annually in India and Bangladesh. The broad geographical distribution of the henipavirus natural animal reservoir, the high overall NiV and HeV case fatality rate in humans as well as the growing evidence of multiple routes of virus transmission, including human-to-human transmission, are causes of serious concern for henipaviruses to become epidemic or pandemic, and have turned NiV and HeV viruses into high priority pathogens of the World Health Organization (WHO). Despite the recognized high epidemic and pandemic potential of henipaviruses, no safe and effective vaccines nor therapeutics are currently available for human use. This PhD project addresses this need and aims to discover novel small molecule antivirals directly acting against henipaviruses and preferably with pan-paramyxovirus activity. To this end, the PhD project has the following aims: 1. Set up and perform a high throughput phenotypic screening assay with Cedar virus (CedV), a non-pathogenic henipavirus that can serve as a surrogate for BSL-4 NiV/HeV. Confirm and validate henipavirus antiviral activity of hit compounds in multiple complementary assays. In addition, compounds will be assessed for activity against HeV and NiV by partner institutes and for broader-spectrum anti-paramyxovirus activity. In parallel, they will be used to isolate virus variants that show phenotypic resistance to the respective compounds in a first step to unravel their precise molecular mechanism of action. Establish the first CedV infection model in mice or hamster to allow future evaluation of the efficacy of small molecules in vivo.

Endothelial protein C receptor is an entry receptor for Langya virus

Authors: Young-Jun Park, Jaewon Song, Risako Gen, Cameron Stewart, Sanghyun Lee, David Veesler
Original abstract (English)
Langya virus (LayV) was recently discovered in infected individuals with febrile illness and classified as a Parahenipavirus, a group of poorly understood pathogens. LayV is distantly related to Nipah henipavirus which causes recurring outbreaks with case fatality rates reaching up to 90%. No vaccines or therapeutics are approved for use in humans for any parahenipavirus or henipavirus despite their broad geographic distribution and pandemic potential. Here, we discovered that human endothelial protein C receptor (EPCR) is an entry receptor for LayV and that the tetrameric LayV attachment glycoprotein (G) binds EPCR avidly. We reveal the molecular basis of LayV G recognition of the EPCR receptor and provide a blueprint of the determinants of receptor ortholog usage. We found that human and shrew EPCR orthologs promote cell entry of LayV and of the closely related Mojiang pseudoviruses. These results along with the detection of several Parahenipaviruses in shrews set these pathogens apart from Henipaviruses, which use Pteropid fruit bats as reservoir hosts. Our data support future surveillance efforts, by enabling prediction of EPCR utilization among newly discovered Parahenipaviruses, and the development and evaluation of countermeasures targeting this emerging group of human pathogens.

Its handling of the first outbreak in 2018 and subsequent occurrences has earned global recognition. However, that hard-won reputation is now under serious challenge following allegations that expired doses of the antiviral drug Remdesivir were administered to Nipah patients between 2023 and 2025, during the previous LDF regime. The UDF government has constituted a medical board to ascertain whether the expired drugs contributed to the six Nipah deaths during this period, triggering a sharp backlash from the LDF and a rebuttal from health experts. The controversy offers a much-needed reality check for Keralam’s celebrated public healthcare system. It raises serious questions about inventory management, treatment protocols and administrative accountability. The state government procured 3,000 vials of Remdesivir in June 2021, with an expiry date of May 30, 2023. Data from Kozhikode Medical College show that 102 of these vials were used in 2023-24, 34 in 2024-25 and 10 in 2025-26. Health Minister K Muraleedharan, who flagged the possible lapses, said that the packages carried an expiry date but no manufacturing date, and some stock was labelled ‘For clinical trial use’. Dismissing the suspicion, critical care specialist Dr A S Anoop Kumar said none of the six Nipah fatalities between 2023 and 2025 had received Remdesivir from the medical college, as all were diagnosed only after death. Attributing deaths to expired medication without indisputable evidence would be irresponsible. Nipah is an exceptionally dangerous infection, and the effectiveness of drugs remains far from conclusive. But that does not make the questions any less serious. Why was the medical college retaining out-of-date medication? Was any of it administered to patients? Any lapse in procurement, storage or inventory management must be identified and responsibility fixed. If this leads to a better treatment protocol, that would be a bonus for a state battling recurring outbreaks. However, accountability must not become a pretext for discrediting a system built over decades. Nor should a serious public health issue degenerate into a political slugfest. Keralam’s healthcare system must remain open to scrutiny precisely because its achievements are worth protecting. The priority must be to establish facts, address systemic deficiencies and strengthen public confidence, not to score political points.
