W730A 扰乱 尼帕病毒 聚合酶抑制剂口袋中的分子识别:BMS-986205 和 ERDRP-0519 的动力学

W730A PERTURBS MOLECULAR RECOGNITION IN THE NIPAH VIRUS POLYMERASE INHIBITOR POCKET: DYNAMICS OF BMS-986205 AND ERDRP-0519
作者： Vihaan Agrawal
摘要中文翻译色氨酸残基通过空间体积、分散、芳香接触和吲哚-NH 相互作用来形成蛋白质-配体识别。我们使用 W730A 来探究这些特征的丧失如何改变 尼帕病毒 聚合酶抑制剂口袋,并将 BMS-986205 与结构结合抑制剂 ERDRP-0519 进行比较。100 种化合物的筛选提供了选择背景,同时配对对接定义了每个配体与 WT 和 W730A 聚合酶的显式溶剂分子动力学的起始姿势。我们分析了配体位移、蛋白质和口袋运动、相互作用指纹、几何事件占用和 MM-GBSA 终点估计。BMS-986205/W730A 显示出与其特定案例参考姿势相比的最低平均位移 (1.86 Å)。ERDRP-0519 在两种受体状态下产生了更负的原始 MM-GBSA 估计值。W730A 还以配体依赖性方式改变了静态接触注释和残基水平终点术语。我们观察到去除 W730 吲哚化学后,两种化学型的局部分子识别发生明显变化。因此,我们建议 BMS-986205 作为 W730A 依赖性口袋重塑的结构探针,并为后续实验定义接触和结合假设。

该研究探讨了 尼帕病毒 聚合酶抑制剂袋中的 W730 残基如何影响两种 抗病毒药物 化学型(BMS-986205 和 ERDRP-0519)的分子识别。其目的是阐明去除 W730 的吲哚化学对配体结合的结构和能量影响。

W730A PERTURBS MOLECULAR RECOGNITION IN THE NIPAH VIRUS POLYMERASE INHIBITOR POCKET: DYNAMICS OF BMS-986205 AND ERDRP-0519

Authors: Vihaan Agrawal
Original abstract (English)
Tryptophan residues shape protein-ligand recognition through steric volume, dispersion, aromatic contacts, and indole-NH interactions. We used W730A to probe how loss of these features alters the Nipah virus polymerase inhibitor pocket and compared BMS-986205 with the structure-bound inhibitor ERDRP-0519. A 100-compound screen supplied selection context, while paired docking defined starting poses for explicit-solvent molecular dynamics of each ligand with WT and W730A polymerase. We analyzed ligand displacement, protein and pocket motion, interaction fingerprints, geometry-event occupancies, and MM-GBSA endpoint estimates. BMS-986205/W730A showed the lowest mean displacement from its case-specific reference pose (1.86 Å). ERDRP-0519 produced more-negative raw MM-GBSA estimates in both receptor states. W730A also altered static contact annotations and residue-level endpoint terms in a ligand-dependent manner. We observed distinct changes in local molecular recognition for the two chemotypes after removal of W730 indole chemistry. We therefore propose BMS-986205 as a structural probe of W730A-dependent pocket remodeling and define contact and binding hypotheses for experimental follow-up.

Question and background
The study addressed how the W730 residue in the Nipah virus polymerase inhibitor pocket influences molecular recognition of two antiviral chemotypes, BMS-986205 and ERDRP-0519. It aimed to clarify the structural and energetic consequences of removing W730’s indole chemistry on ligand binding.
Design and population
The research used computational modeling to compare wild-type and W730A mutant Nipah virus polymerase inhibitor pockets in complex with BMS-986205 and ERDRP-0519. A 100-compound screen provided selection context, and paired docking defined starting poses for explicit-solvent molecular dynamics simulations.
Core methods
Explicit-solvent molecular dynamics simulations were performed for each ligand with wild-type and W730A polymerase. Analyses included ligand displacement, protein and pocket motion, interaction fingerprints, geometry-event occupancies, and MM-GBSA endpoint binding estimates.
Main results
BMS-986205 in the W730A mutant showed the lowest mean displacement from its case-specific reference pose (1.86 Å). ERDRP-0519 produced more-negative raw MM-GBSA binding estimates in both wild-type and W730A receptor states. W730A altered static contact annotations and residue-level endpoint terms in a ligand-dependent manner.
Interpretation and novelty
The findings indicate that W730A-dependent remodeling of the inhibitor pocket differentially affects the two chemotypes, with BMS-986205 showing greater structural stability in the mutant. The authors propose BMS-986205 as a structural probe for W730A-dependent pocket remodeling and define testable contact and binding hypotheses.
Scientific and public-health significance
The study provides mechanistic insights into Nipah virus polymerase inhibitor resistance and may guide the design of antiviral agents resilient to W730A-mediated pocket remodeling. It also offers experimentally testable hypotheses for optimizing inhibitor binding and specificity.
Limitations and evidence strength
The evidence is limited to in silico analyses without experimental validation, which constrains the strength of conclusions about biological relevance. The study’s reliance on computational predictions introduces uncertainty regarding real-world binding dynamics and therapeutic efficacy.

One Health Insights From Pteropus medius: Nipah Virus Spillover, Microbiota, and Antimicrobial Resistance.

Authors: Punam Chowdhury, Shah Md Tanvir Khan, Sajal Roy, Md Shohel Al Faruk
Original abstract (English)
Pteropus medius is a major reservoir of Nipah virus (NiV), a zoonotic pathogen responsible for recurrent fatal encephalitis outbreaks in Bangladesh. Human infections are primarily associated with consuming raw date palm sap contaminated with bat excreta. Beyond viral spillover, growing evidence suggests that bat-associated microbiota and guano are potential reservoirs of antimicrobial resistance (AMR), contributing to the environmental dissemination of resistant bacteria and resistance genes. This narrative literature review examined the relationship among NiV spillover, bat microbiota, and AMR within a One Health framework. A structured literature search was conducted using PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, and Google Scholar to identify relevant peer-reviewed studies published between 2006 and 2025. The reviewed evidence indicates that Pteropus medius populations harbor clinically important resistant bacteria, including ESBL-(Extended-Spectrum Beta-Lactamase) producing Escherichia coli, Salmonella spp., and methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus spp. In addition, recent studies indicate that bat roosts near agricultural lands, wastewater discharge sites, and peri-urban settlements may facilitate bidirectional exchange of ARGs among wildlife, livestock, and humans. Habitat fragmentation, wastewater contamination, urbanization, agricultural intensification, and increased human-wildlife interactions were identified as major drivers facilitating both NiV spillover and AMR dissemination. Overall, the literature demonstrates a significant ecological association among anthropogenic environmental disturbance, zoonotic spillover risk, and the emergence of antimicrobial resistance in bat-associated systems. These findings highlight the importance of integrated One Health surveillance and environmental management strategies to mitigate future zoonotic and AMR threats in Bangladesh.

Research and practice implications
Integrated One Health surveillance and environmental management strategies are recommended to mitigate future zoonotic and AMR threats in Bangladesh, emphasizing the need for comprehensive monitoring of bat-associated microbiota and environmental contamination.
