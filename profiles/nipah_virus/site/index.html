高效广谱中和抗尼帕病毒和亨德拉病毒的最小蛋白计算设计

Computational design of potent, broadly neutralizing anti-Nipah virus and Hendra virus miniproteins
作者： Jeremiah Nelson Sims, Risako Gen, Kaitlin R. Sprouse, Young-Jun Park, Zhao-Qian Wang, Moushimi Amaya, Alka Jays, Brendan B. Larsen, Cameron Stewart, Jack T. Brown
摘要中文翻译摘要 尼帕病毒(NiV)和亨德拉病毒(HeV)作为亨尼帕病毒属(亨尼帕病毒)的原型成员,会引起反复发生的人兽共患病(跨物种溢出),在人类中病死率高达40-90%。目前尚无获批的抗病毒药物(疫苗)或治疗手段用于人类。针对NiV/HeV F或G糖蛋白的中和抗体可保护动物免受致命感染,并且是主要的保护相关因素。然而,基于抗体的制剂成本高昂,通常需要住院进行给药,并且需要冷链进行储存和运输。为了解决缺乏稳定储存的临床干预措施的问题,我们通过计算设计了具有热稳定性的最小蛋白,这些最小蛋白对NiV和HeV的F和G糖蛋白具有亚纳摩尔亲和力,并且在体外对病毒入侵具有与先导抗体相当的抑制效力。这些最小蛋白的寡聚形式相较于其单体构建单元具有增强的效力,并且提高了逃逸突变体出现的障碍,使其成为对抗这些致命病毒的有前景的临床前候选物。

Computational design of potent, broadly neutralizing anti-Nipah virus and Hendra virus miniproteins

Authors: Jeremiah Nelson Sims, Risako Gen, Kaitlin R. Sprouse, Young-Jun Park, Zhao-Qian Wang, Moushimi Amaya, Alka Jays, Brendan B. Larsen, Cameron Stewart, Jack T. Brown
Original abstract (English)
Abstract The prototype members of the genus Henipavirus, Nipah virus (NiV) and Hendra virus (HeV), cause recurrent zoonotic spillovers with case fatality rates ranging from 40-90% in humans. Currently, there are no approved vaccines or therapeutics for use in humans. Neutralizing antibodies targeting the NiV/HeV F-or G-glycoproteins protect animals from lethal challenge and are a main correlate of protection. However, antibody-based formulations are expensive, typically requiring hospital admission for administration and cold-chain for storage and transportation. To address the lack of shelf-stable clinical countermeasures, we computationally designed thermostable miniproteins that cross-react with subnanomolar affinities with both NiV and HeV F and G glycoproteins and inhibit viral entry in vitro with potencies comparable to lead antibodies. Oligomerized forms of these miniproteins have enhanced potency relative to their monomeric building blocks and increase the barrier for emergence of escape mutants, establishing them as promising preclinical candidates against these deadly viruses.

Defining Challenge Conditions for Nipah Virus in the Syrian Golden Hamster Model: Effects of Dose, Exposure Route and Viral Strain

Authors: Emma Kennedy, Stephen Findlay-Wilson, Francisco J. Salguero, Ines Ruedas-Torres, Susan Fotheringham, Linda Easterbrook, Victoria A. Graham, Sarah Kempster, Neil Almond, Stuart D. Dowall
Original abstract (English)
Nipah virus (NiV) is a highly pathogenic zoonotic virus for which no licensed vaccines or therapeutics are currently available, necessitating well-characterised animal models to support countermeasure development. This study aimed to establish an appropriate challenge dose in the Syrian golden hamster model at the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA), incorporating both intraperitoneal (i.p.) and intranasal (i.n.) routes of infection and two clinically relevant strains, NiV-Malaysia (NiV-M) and NiV-Bangladesh (NiV-B). Due to a different challenge doses and susceptibilities of hamsters to NiV being reported in the literature, there is a prerequisite to define the model parameters upon establishment in a new facility. Dose-ranging studies were performed across multiple independent experiments assessing survival, clinical progression and histopathological outcomes. Challenge via the i.p. route produced consistent and severe disease using an infectious dose of 103 TCID50 for both strains. In contrast, animals were more susceptible to infection via i.n. challenge with a dose of 10 TCID50 resulting in uniform lethality. For both challenge routes, respiratory and neurological disease manifestations were observed. These distinct disease phenotypes were supported by histopathological findings and viral RNA distribution in affected organs. Differences between strains were evident, with NiV-B showing a tendency towards a broader pulmonary pathology at lower doses after i.n. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that challenge dose, route of exposure, and viral strain influence disease outcomes in the hamster model.

ICMR研究所:从研究到影响-国家病毒学研究所,浦那

ICMR Institutes: From research to impact-National Institute of Virology, Pune
作者： Shweta Tanwar
摘要中文翻译
查看研究七要素
问题与背景
The study aimed to highlight the role of the National Institute of Virology (NIV) in responding to emerging and re-emerging viral infections, particularly the Nipah virus, by integrating basic research with the development of medical countermeasures and outbreak investigation. The knowledge gap addressed was the need for rapid diagnostic and surveillance systems during outbreaks, especially in the context of the early COVID-19 pandemic and Nipah virus disease outbreaks.
设计与对象
The study design involved the National Institute of Virology (NIV) in Pune, India, focusing on its response to the early COVID-19 pandemic and Nipah virus disease outbreaks. The study covered the period from 2020 to 2024, involving a network of 167 Virus Research and Diagnostic Laboratories (VRDLs) and 23 biosafety level-3 laboratories.
核心方法
NIV采用了包括SARS-CoV-2和亨尼帕病毒的基因组测序、分离以及基因组监测等方法。该研究所还开发了本土的诊断检测方法,并进行了血清流行率研究。对于亨尼帕病毒,进行了快速实验室确认、接触者的血清监测以及果蝠种群的基因组监测。
主要结果
NIV支持了VRDLs中分子检测的标准化,使实验室检测能力从每天约250次增加到2020年4月底的每天50,000次。该研究所还开发了一种基于IgG的本土抗体检测方法,用于检测过去的SARS-CoV-2感染,并参与了本土检测方法的开发。对于亨尼帕病毒,快速检测和基因组监测得到了增强,配备了一辆移动BSL-3实验室,能够在Kozhikode暴发期间大约四小时内完成现场检测。
解释与创新
本研究认为,NIV在标准化检测和开发本土对策方面的努力,弥合了实验室研究与公共卫生行动之间的差距。其新颖之处在于将基因组监测与现场流行病学响应相结合,使NIV能够通过结构化的One Health模拟演练加强国家的准备能力。
科研与公卫意义
研究结果表明,NIV的研究和机构能力支持了公共卫生行动,并加强了国家的准备能力。其意义在于将科学能力迅速转化为可部署的诊断工具、检测系统和及时的公共卫生行动,特别是在高后果人兽共患病如亨尼帕病毒疾病的情境下。
局限与证据强度
本研究的局限性包括缺乏随机对照试验以及仅关注单一机构。由于研究的观察性质和依赖于研究所内部报告,其证据强度为中等。

Question and background
The study aimed to highlight the role of the National Institute of Virology (NIV) in responding to emerging and re-emerging viral infections, particularly the Nipah virus, by integrating basic research with the development of medical countermeasures and outbreak investigation. The knowledge gap addressed was the need for rapid diagnostic and surveillance systems during outbreaks, especially in the context of the early COVID-19 pandemic and Nipah virus disease outbreaks.
Design and population
The study design involved the National Institute of Virology (NIV) in Pune, India, focusing on its response to the early COVID-19 pandemic and Nipah virus disease outbreaks. The study covered the period from 2020 to 2024, involving a network of 167 Virus Research and Diagnostic Laboratories (VRDLs) and 23 biosafety level-3 laboratories.
Core methods
NIV employed various methods including sequencing, isolation, and genomic surveillance of SARS-CoV-2 and Nipah virus. The institute also developed indigenous diagnostic assays, vaccines, and conducted serosurveillance studies. For Nipah virus, rapid laboratory confirmation, serosurveillance of contacts, and surveillance of bat populations were conducted.
Main results
NIV supported the standardisation of molecular testing across VRDLs, expanding laboratory capacity from around 250 tests per day to 50,000 per day by end-April 2020. The institute also developed an indigenous IgG-based antibody assay for past SARS-CoV-2 infection and contributed to the development of vaccines. For Nipah virus, rapid diagnosis and surveillance were enhanced, with a mobile BSL-3 laboratory enabling on-site diagnosis within approximately four hours during the Kozhikode outbreak.
Interpretation and novelty
The study interpreted that NIV's efforts in standardising testing and developing indigenous countermeasures have bridged the gap between laboratory research and public-health action. The novel aspect was the integration of genomic surveillance and field-based outbreak response, which positioned NIV to strengthen national preparedness through structured One Health mock drills.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings suggest that NIV's research and institutional capacity have supported public-health action during outbreaks and strengthened national preparedness. The significance lies in the rapid conversion of scientific capacity into deployable diagnostics, surveillance systems, and timely public-health action, particularly in the context of high-consequence zoonoses like Nipah virus disease.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study's limitations include the absence of a randomized controlled trial and the focus on a single institution. The evidence strength is moderate due to the observational nature of the study and the reliance on the institute's internal reports.

Validation and Standardization of PCR Analysis in Veterinary Disease Diagnosis

Authors: Swati Sharma, Nisha Rana
Original abstract (English)
The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is a fundamental technique in molecular biology that enables the exponential amplification of specific DNA sequences with exceptional sensitivity and precision. Since its development, PCR has become indispensable in research, clinical diagnostics, and veterinary medicine. The advent of real-time PCR, also known as quantitative PCR (qPCR), has further transformed molecular diagnostics by allowing the simultaneous amplification and real-time detection of nucleic acids through fluorescence-based chemistries. Unlike conventional PCR, which requires post-amplification analysis such as agarose gel electrophoresis, qPCR integrates detection into the amplification process, significantly reducing contamination risks, improving accuracy, and shortening turnaround time. Numerous PCR variants have expanded its scope and versatility. Techniques such as multiplex-PCR enable the simultaneous detection of multiple targets, while nested PCR enhances sensitivity and specificity. Other adaptations, including RAPD-PCR, AFLP-PCR, RFLP-PCR, and colony PCR, are widely applied in pathogen identification, population genetics, phylogenetic studies, and the analysis of genetic diversity. In veterinary diagnostics, qPCR has been validated for the rapid and reliable detection of important pathogens, including Jaagsiekte sheep retrovirus (JSRV), Zika virus, Nipah virus, bovine viral agents, and Pasteurella multocida in swine, supporting effective disease surveillance and control programs. Although PCR faces limitations such as contamination risks, primer design complexity, and dependence on specialized equipment, ongoing advancements – including nanoparticle-assisted PCR, automation, and AI-driven data analysis – are improving robustness, throughput, and interpretative power. Collectively, PCR and its advanced formats continue to revolutionize molecular diagnostics by enhancing sensitivity, efficiency, and disease-monitoring capabilities.
