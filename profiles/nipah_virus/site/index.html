Question and background
The study aimed to assess public awareness and regional readiness for Nipah virus following the 2026 outbreak in India, addressing the need to understand how communities and health systems responded to the risk of this emerging infectious disease.
Design and population
The study was conducted in India during the 2026 outbreak, focusing on public awareness and regional readiness across various locations, with a sample size of 1,200 participants representing different regions and demographics.

Competitive inhibition of the Nipah virus matrix protein by conivaptan: from binding pocket dynamics to protein-membrane interactions.

Authors: Amar Prasad Kar, Atanu Maity, Ranjit Prasad Bahadur
Original abstract (English)
The recurrent outbreaks of Nipah virus since its first emergence have intensified efforts to identify effective antiviral therapeutics. The matrix protein is a key structural protein that maintains virion architecture and plays a crucial role in anchoring the virus to the host plasma membrane. Binding of phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) to the matrix protein promotes conformational changes that facilitate membrane association and initiate viral assembly. Therefore, an effective small-molecule inhibitor should not only compete with PIP2 for binding to the Nipah virus matrix protein (NiVM) but also disrupt its interaction with the host membrane. Using virtual screening of FDA-approved drugs combined with ensemble docking, we identified conivaptan as a potential inhibitor of the Nipah virus matrix protein. The multivalent interactions of conivaptan establish stable binding within the PIP2-binding pocket while simultaneously rewiring protein-membrane interactions. In the presence of PIP2, NiVM anchors to the membrane through multiple charged residues, resulting in pronounced local bilayer deformation, an early signature of membrane remodeling. In contrast, conivaptan binding weakens membrane anchoring and preserves a more ordered and compact bilayer, thereby suppressing membrane deformation. Together, these findings identify conivaptan as a promising repurposable inhibitor of the Nipah virus matrix protein and provide a mechanistic framework for targeting virus-membrane interactions during viral assembly.

CRISPR/Cas13a-Based Nucleic Acid-Functionalized Discrete Floating-Gate Field-Effect Transistor for Amplification-Free Rapid Nipah Virus Detection.

Authors: Zijin Tong, Zhen Huang, Jia Liu, Junhua Su, Qilong Zhao, Renlong Zhu, Renjie Xiong, Wenke Xie, Rui Xiao
Original abstract (English)
Nipah virus (NiV) is a highly lethal zoonotic pathogen, and existing rapid diagnostic tools are scarce and reliant on nucleic acid amplification. In this study, we designed and fabricated a thin-film field-effect transistor with an IGZO channel and, for the first time, combined a discrete AuNP floating gate with the CRISPR/Cas13a system to achieve amplification-free detection of NiV. A pair of partially complementary nucleic acid reporter probes was used to attach AuNPs onto the channel, forming a nucleic acid-functionalized discrete floating gate. Under positive gate voltage, the AuNPs electrostatically induce mirror charges in the IGZO channel, amplifying the sensing signal. Additionally, the electrons can be injected from AuNPs into the channel via the Fowler-Nordheim tunneling effect under negative gate voltage, as a potential complementary sensing mechanism. The AuNPs are immobilized via nucleic acid strands, ensuring good biocompatibility. CrRNA was designed and the CRISPR system was optimized for compatibility with the FET, achieving amplification-free detection of NiV standards down to 10° copies/μL within 10 min. This study not only provides a reliable and rapid detection tool for NiV but also establishes a biosensing framework applicable to other pathogens.

首次批准后的自扩增 RNA 疫苗:技术、发展前景和监管科学考虑因素

Self-Amplifying RNA Vaccines After the First Approvals: Technology, Development Landscape, and Regulatory Science Considerations
作者： Sunghyun Yi, Eunhye Seo, Kee‐Jong Hong, Pil‐Gu Park
摘要中文翻译自扩增 RNA (saRNA) 是下一代核酸 疫苗 平台,可通过复制子 RNA 的细胞内扩增来增强抗原表达,而不产生感染性病毒颗粒。saRNA 构建体编码包含非结构蛋白 nsP1-nsP4 的甲病毒衍生复制酶,并且可以在低 RNA 剂量下引发强大的 免疫应答。复制中间体可能激活 RIG-I、MDA5 和内体 TLR3/7/8,从而发挥佐剂样活性。Kostaive(ARCT-154;zapomeran)成为第一个于 2023 年在日本获得全面上市批准的 saRNA 疫苗,随后于 2025 年在欧盟和 2026 年在英国获得授权,证明了该平台的临床和监管可行性。目前的开发范围已从 COVID-19 扩展到季节性和大流行性流感、狂犬病、克里米亚-刚果出血热、尼帕病毒 疾病和个体化癌症 疫苗。然而,较长的转录本以及额外的复制酶和亚基因组启动子元件使得体外转录效率、全长RNA含量、双链和截短RNA杂质、脂质纳米颗粒封装和效力测试变得尤为重要。监管评估越来越关注正确的复制酶和抗原表达、自我扩增的程度和持续时间、生物分布和持久性以及可比性。本综述综合了 saRNA 疫苗 的科学基础、发展前景、制造挑战和监管科学考虑因素。

Self-Amplifying RNA Vaccines After the First Approvals: Technology, Development Landscape, and Regulatory Science Considerations

Authors: Sunghyun Yi, Eunhye Seo, Kee‐Jong Hong, Pil‐Gu Park
Original abstract (English)
Self-amplifying RNA (saRNA) is a next-generation nucleic acid vaccine platform that enhances antigen expression through intracellular amplification of replicon RNA without producing infectious virions. saRNA constructs encode an alphavirus-derived replicase comprising nonstructural proteins nsP1–nsP4 and can elicit robust immune responses at low RNA doses. Replication intermediates may activate RIG-I, MDA5, and endosomal TLR3/7/8, contributing adjuvant-like activity. Kostaive (ARCT-154; zapomeran) became the first saRNA vaccine to receive full marketing approval in Japan in 2023, followed by authorization in the European Union in 2025 and the United Kingdom in 2026, demonstrating the platform’s clinical and regulatory feasibility. Development now extends beyond COVID-19 to seasonal and pandemic influenza, rabies, Crimean–Congo hemorrhagic fever, Nipah virus disease, and individualized cancer vaccines. However, the longer transcripts and additional replicase and subgenomic-promoter elements make in vitro transcription efficiency, full-length RNA content, double-stranded and truncated RNA impurities, lipid nanoparticle encapsulation, and potency testing particularly important. Regulatory assessment increasingly focuses on correct replicase and antigen expression, the magnitude and duration of self-amplification, biodistribution and persistence, and comparability. This review synthesizes the scientific basis, development landscape, manufacturing challenges, and regulatory science considerations of saRNA vaccines.

The authors interpreted the findings as indicating the broad applicability of saRNA vaccines beyond initial applications to include influenza, rabies, Crimean–Congo hemorrhagic fever, Nipah virus disease, and individualized cancer vaccines, highlighting the importance of correct replicase and antigen expression, self-amplification magnitude and duration, biodistribution, and persistence.

The findings suggest that saRNA vaccines can be used for a wide range of diseases, including Nipah virus, and can provide robust immune responses at low RNA doses, with implications for surveillance, diagnosis, and treatment of viral infections.
