利用预测蛋白质建模研究尼帕病毒糖蛋白的结构特征

Structural Aspects of Nipah Virus Glycoproteins Using Predictive Protein Modeling
作者： Urmi Roy
摘要中文翻译尼帕病毒是一种通过病毒传播的人兽共患病,主要发生在东南亚国家。尼帕病毒(NiV)感染具有潜在致命性,因为它可能导致致命的脑炎,目前尚无疫苗或治疗性治疗。高度致病性的NiV属于副粘病毒科,其天然储存宿主是宿主的果蝠。尽管NiV在美国尚未被检测到,但最近在阿拉巴马州坎普希尔发现了一种特征上与尼帕病毒相似且与NiV同属亨尼帕病毒类群的坎普希尔病毒(CHV)。本文利用现有的蛋白质结构数据库描述了这些病毒糖蛋白的结构,并探讨它们是否与其他病毒糖蛋白存在进化联系。同时,还研究了不同国家中发现的某些相似糖蛋白。通过基于时间的分子动力学(MD)模拟,确定了这两种NiV糖蛋白的结构-功能关系。研究分析了NiVG与ephrinB2受体以及NiVF与单克隆抗体(Fab66)复合物之间的界面配体-受体相互作用。结果表明,融合前NiVF(在此情况下为截断形式)的较高基础灵活性展示了病毒进入诱导所需的构象亚稳性,而NiVG-受体界面则呈现为一个刚性且稳定的中和抗体靶标。通过系统发育分析,探讨了这些病毒糖蛋白可能的进化关系,以及与相关新兴人兽共患病病毒的联系。这些结构研究的主要动机是,这些结果可能有助于未来针对NiV的治疗性药物开发和设计。

Structural Aspects of Nipah Virus Glycoproteins Using Predictive Protein Modeling

Authors: Urmi Roy
Original abstract (English)
Nipah is a virus-borne zoonotic disease that mostly occurs in Southeast Asian countries. Nipah virus (NiV) infection is potentially lethal as it can cause fatal encephalitis, and as of now, there are no vaccines or therapeutic treatments for this disease. The highly pathogenic NiV belongs to the paramyxovirus family, and the natural host of NiV is fruit bats of the Pteropodidae family. Though NiV has not been detected in the US, the Camp Hill virus (CHV), which is characteristically similar to Nipah and shares the henipavirus group with NiV, was recently identified in Camp Hill, Alabama. The present paper describes the structures of these viral glycoproteins using the available database of protein structures, and examines if they have an evolutionary link to other viral glycoproteins. Certain similar glycoproteins found in different countries are also explored. The structure–function relationships for these two NiV glycoproteins are determined using time-based molecular dynamics (MD) simulation. The interfacial ligand–receptor interactions between the NiVG–ephrinB2 receptor and NiVF–monoclonal antibody (Fab66) complexes are examined. The results indicate that the higher baseline flexibility of pre-fusion NiVF (truncated in this case) demonstrates conformational metastability necessary for viral entry induction, whereas the NiVG-receptor interface presents a rigid, stable target for neutralizing antibodies. Possible evolutionary relationships of these viral glycoproteins, along with related emerging zoonotic viruses, are examined through phylogenetic analyses. A primary motivation for these structural studies is that these results might contribute to future therapeutic developments and drug designs to effectively combat NiV.

Question and background
This study was needed to better understand the spatial dynamics of Nipah virus transmission, which are not adequately captured by purely temporal compartmental models. The research aimed to formulate a spatial reaction-diffusion model that incorporates host, environmental, and spatial interactions.
Design and population
The study employed a two-dimensional reaction–diffusion model to describe transmission among human, fruit, and fruit-bat populations, including pathways for fruit-mediated and cross-species infections, demographic processes, recovery, mortality, immunity loss, and spatial transport. The model was calibrated using cumulative reported cases from Bangladesh over the period 2001–2024.
Core methods
The model parameters were calibrated using bounded nonlinear least-squares estimation applied to a nonspatial count-based model. Consistent scaling relations were then used to convert the calibrated parameters into density-based quantities for the spatial system. A Crank–Nicolson Lie-splitting finite-difference scheme was developed for numerical approximation with first-order temporal and second-order spatial accuracy.
Main results
The calibrated parameters were converted into density-based quantities for the spatial system. Simulations initiated by normalized multiple-Gaussian hotspots showed diffusion-driven broadening of localized outbreaks, substantial decay of infected-human and infected fruit-bat densities, and comparatively persistent infection in the fruit compartment.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted the findings as demonstrating the role of fruit-mediated infection in sustaining spatial persistence of Nipah virus. The study's novelty lies in its data-informed spatial description of Nipah virus transmission, which clarifies the dynamics of the disease across different populations.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings can inform surveillance and risk assessment strategies for Nipah virus by highlighting the importance of spatial factors in disease spread. They also provide insights into the potential for fruit-mediated transmission to maintain endemicity in certain regions.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study assumes non-negative uniformly bounded solutions and equal diffusion coefficients within each population group, which may limit the generalizability of the results. The evidence is moderate, as the model is based on calibrated parameters and simulations, with no direct clinical or epidemiological data reported.

Original abstract (English)
The growing threat of emerging and drug-resistant viruses demands efficient approaches for predicting virus-inhibitor interactions. However, most existing computational models treat viruses as categorical identifiers and fail to capture sequence-level biological information. Here, we assembled a data set of 50,711 compound-virus activity pairs spanning 178 viral species and explored protein language models as a strategy for viral representation learning. Using embeddings generated by ESMC-600M, we introduce a virtual virus representation in which viruses are encoded as sequence-derived biological entities rather than as taxonomic labels. These viral representations are integrated with molecular features to model compound-virus interactions. Notably, virtual virus representations consistently outperformed conventional taxonomy-based encodings, while ablation studies highlighted viral sequence information as a key contributor to the model performance. Case studies on Nipah virus and hantaviruses further demonstrated the ability of DeepMolVir to identify candidate antiviral scaffolds and support cross-family drug repurposing. Collectively, our findings establish protein-language-model-based viral representation learning as an effective strategy for sequence-aware antiviral activity prediction and provide a foundation for computational antiviral discovery.
