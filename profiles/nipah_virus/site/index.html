致死性 尼帕病毒 感染的免疫控制:宿主 巨噬细胞的关键作用

Immune control of lethal Nipah virus infection: the critical role of host macrophages
作者： Ilona Ronco, Kévin P. Dhondt, Mathieu Iampietro, Marie Moroso, Candice Babarit, Thibaut Larcher, Olivier Reynard, Branka Horvat
摘要中文翻译摘要 尼帕病毒 (NiV) 是亨尼帕病毒属高致病性人畜共患副粘病毒,在东南亚引起人类 暴发 复发,致死率为 40-75%。与这种新兴病毒相关的免疫发病机制仍然知之甚少,免疫应答 的不同成分在控制感染中的作用仍然未知。与许多其他哺乳动物物种相比,野生型小鼠由于有效的 I 型干扰素反应而对 NiV 感染具有抵抗力。我们分析了各种免疫细胞群在控制 NiV 感染中的作用,并证明了 T 淋巴细胞的关键作用,但 B 淋巴细胞和 NK 细胞都不是原始细胞。此外,通过注射氯膦酸盐脂质体来消耗小鼠体内的吞噬细胞导致其对NiV感染的易感性急剧增加。此外,使用 CD169-DTR 转基因小鼠,我们在此表明​​ CD169 + 巨噬细胞的特异性消耗导致小鼠对 NiV 攻击的敏感性增加,突出了该细胞群的重要作用。最后,在感染 NiV 的非洲绿猴中(与严重的人类尼帕病非常相似),病毒诱导的 I 型和 II 型干扰素产生,随后血液和肺部的 CD169 + 细胞显着增加,其中一些被 NiV 感染。由于 CD169 + 巨噬细胞已被描述在各种感染中先天性免疫和适应性免疫之间的十字路口发挥着重要作用,因此这些结果也揭示了它们在 NiV 感染期间的潜在决定作用,这可能有助于更好地了解这种高度致命的传染病。

研究结果表明,针对 CD169 + 巨噬细胞可能是 NiV 感染的潜在治疗策略,并可能有助于开发更好的诊断工具和预防措施。

该研究受到使用小鼠作为模型的限制,可能无法完全代表人类疾病。小鼠研究结果需要在人体研究中得到验证。

Immune control of lethal Nipah virus infection: the critical role of host macrophages

Authors: Ilona Ronco, Kévin P. Dhondt, Mathieu Iampietro, Marie Moroso, Candice Babarit, Thibaut Larcher, Olivier Reynard, Branka Horvat
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Nipah virus (NiV) is highly pathogenic zoonotic paramyxovirus of Henipaviru s genus that causes recurrent human outbreaks in South-East Asia, with a lethality of 40-75%. Immunopathogenesis associated to this emerging virus is still poorly understood and the role of different components of the immune response in the control of infection remains unknown. In contrast to many other mammalian species, wild type mice are resistant to NiV infection, due to an efficient type-I interferon response. We have analysed the role of various immune cell populations in the control of NiV infection and demonstrated the critical role of T lymphocytes but neither B lymphocytes nor NK cells were primordial. In addition, the depletion of phagocytes in mice by injection of clodronate liposomes resulted in dramatical increase of their susceptibility to NiV infection. Moreover, using CD169-DTR transgenic mice we show here that the specific depletion of CD169 + macrophages results in an increased susceptibility of mice to NiV challenge, highlighting the important role of this cell population. Finally, in NiV-infected African green monkeys, which closely resemble severe human Nipah disease, the production of virus-induced type-I and -II interferons was followed with an important increase of CD169 + cells in the blood and lungs, with some of them being NiV-infected. As CD169 + macrophages have been described to play an important role at the crossroads between innate and adaptive immunity in various infections, these results reveal their potentially determining role during NiV infection as well, which may help to better understand this highly lethal infectious disease.

Deterministic–stochastic fractional-order modeling and optimal control of Nipah virus in hypertensive nephrosclerosis

Authors: Muhammad Aon Raza, Saima Rashid, Muhammad Imran
Original abstract (English)
Nipah virus is a zoonotic virus that causes severe respiratory and neurological disease and is spread through pigs, bats or contaminated food. This research develops a fractional-order mathematical model to investigate the co-infection dynamics of hypertensive nephrosclerosis and Nipah virus infection. The model is kept positive and bounded to guarantee biological reality, and the disease-free equilibrium E p l is studied. The basic reproduction number R 0 l p < 1 implies extinction of the infection while R 0 l p > 1 implies small outbreaks developing into epidemics. These results are backed up by bifurcation analysis, global stability analysis and existence and uniqueness proofs for the solution. Real Malaysian co-infection data was used to parameterise the model, and simulations were carried out for both deterministic and stochastic fractional systems. The fractional-order parameter has significant impact on the infection rates and convergence behaviour. The memory effects and nonlocal dynamics are described by using piecewise Caputo–Fabrizio derivatives. Stochastic perturbations are considered to model random environmental effects. The parameters were changed to highlight the role of vaccination and stochasticity in disease extinction. The amalgamation of Caputo–Fabrizio fractional calculus and stochastic modelling, supported by a novel numerical scheme, offers a robust and flexible approach to simulate co-infection dynamics under uncertainty. The framework is further validated by PRCC sensitivity analysis and MCMC parameter estimation. Optimal control strategies provide to control the co-infection spread with economic feasibility. To summarize, the model provides useful insights into the interaction between hypertensive nephrosclerosis and Nipah virus transmission, supporting the design of effective and economically feasible control strategies.

Competitive inhibition of the Nipah virus matrix protein by conivaptan: from binding pocket dynamics to protein–membrane interactions

Authors: Amar Prasad Kar, Atanu Maity, Ranjit Prasad Bahadur
Original abstract (English)
The recurrent outbreaks of Nipah virus since its first emergence have intensified efforts to identify effective antiviral therapeutics. The matrix protein is a key structural protein that maintains virion architecture and plays a crucial role in anchoring the virus to the host plasma membrane. Binding of phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) to the matrix protein promotes conformational changes that facilitate membrane association and initiate viral assembly. Therefore, an effective small-molecule inhibitor should not only compete with PIP2 for binding to the Nipah virus matrix protein (NiVM) but also disrupt its interaction with the host membrane. Using virtual screening of FDA-approved drugs combined with ensemble docking, we identified conivaptan as a potential inhibitor of the Nipah virus matrix protein. The multivalent interactions of conivaptan establish stable binding within the PIP2-binding pocket while simultaneously rewiring protein-membrane interactions. In the presence of PIP2, NiVM anchors to the membrane through multiple charged residues, resulting in pronounced local bilayer deformation, an early signature of membrane remodeling. In contrast, conivaptan binding weakens membrane anchoring and preserves a more ordered and compact bilayer, thereby suppressing membrane deformation. Together, these findings identify conivaptan as a promising repurposable inhibitor of the Nipah virus matrix protein and provide a mechanistic framework for targeting virus-membrane interactions during viral assembly.

Vertical Transmission of Nipah Virus in an Experimentally Infected Nonhuman Primate: A Brief Report and Description of Histopathological Lesions.

Authors: Declan D Pigeaud, Natalie S Dobias, Antoinette Lona, Christopher C Broder, Robert W Cross, Thomas W Geisbert, Karla A Fenton
Original abstract (English)
Nipah virus (NiV) is a highly pathogenic henipavirus that causes severe, often fatal disease in humans and animals. African green monkeys have been used extensively to investigate NiV disease and are the only nonhuman primate model that accurately recapitulates human NiV pathogenesis. Here, we describe histopathological lesions in the reproductive tissues of an incidentally pregnant female African green monkey that succumbed to NiV disease. We employed multiplex immunohistochemistry techniques to profile specific cell populations that identified NiV antigen in both maternal and fetal cells of the placenta, which has not been previously described in nonhuman primate models of NiV disease.

Characterization of Nipah Virus Strains Malaysia and Bangladesh in a Hamster Aerosol-Exposure Model

Authors: Christina L. Gardner, Lisa Pieterse, Lauren E. Panny, Joshua D. Shamblin, Ashley E. Piper, Elaine S. Cerchin, William A. Baskerville, Sarah B. Sulkosky, Crystal W. Burke
Original abstract (English)
Nipah virus (NiV) is an enveloped, single-stranded, negative-sense virus that causes severe respiratory distress and neurological disease in humans. While NiV disease is associated with high mortality rates (40–70%), no FDA-approved medical countermeasures (MCM) against NiV currently exist. Phylogenetic analyses have clustered isolates from Malaysia (NiV-M) and Bangladesh (NiV-B) into two distinct circulating strains associated with unique transmission routes, clinical manifestations, and lethality rates in humans. Predominantly spread by zoonotic transmission, NiV-M yields lower case-fatality rates, mild respiratory symptoms, and severe neurological manifestations. In contrast, NiV-B showcases higher case-fatality rates, marked respiratory disease, and human-to-human transmission via respiratory secretions. NiV hamster models utilizing aerosol, oronasal, intranasal, intracranial, and intraperitoneal inoculations exist. However, aerosol infection of NiV-B in hamsters remains poorly understood, while direct strain comparisons using aerosolization as an inoculation route have yet to be performed. To establish a Syrian golden hamster model comparing NiV strain-specific disease presentations and pathologies following aerosol exposure for downstream MCM characterization, we infected SGHs with a range of NiV-M and NiV-B aerosol inocula, determined the median lethal dose (LD50) and survival curves, and further elucidated both histopathological changes and viral antigen distribution within the brain and lungs. We found increased NiV-M lethality, respiratory distress, and neurological disease, in addition to more severe pathologies and viral signal within both the brain and lungs compared to NiV-B-infected animals. Our study serves as the first to compare aerosol exposure of NiV-B versus NiV-M in a hamster model, which can subsequently be used for crucial strain-specific MCM characterizations.

Original abstract (English)
Respiratory viral infections continue to pose a substantial global health challenge, with a pressing need for effective treatment options. The rapid mutation rate of RNA viruses presents a significant risk of escape from existing treatments. Here, we harness the rapid adaptability and high sequence specificity of the RNA-targeting CRISPR-Cas13 nuclease technology to target and cleave the genome of different RNA viruses, disrupting replication and impairing propagation. We identified potent CRISPR-RfxCas13d/crRNA (Cas13d/crRNA) combinations that can efficiently restrict viral replication in both pre- and post-viral exposure treatment settings. Hence, the Cas13d/crRNA antiviral method holds promise as a potential therapeutic approach against severe viral infections caused by both (+) and (−) RNA viruses. Furthermore, the multi-locus targeting of viruses by Cas13d could offer a possibility to prevent virus breakthrough and increase the therapeutic arsenal against many emerging RNA viruses in the future.

Targeted strategies of containment are the best approach to protect against outbreaks of Nipah virus, according to new modelling research from the London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine (LSHTM). A historical analysis of outbreaks in India and Bangladesh showed that most Nipah virus outbreaks die out with low transmissibility. However, some cases can develop into dangerous outbreaks. In such cases, the researchers argue that targeted quarantines and contact tracing are the best approach for containment. The findings could also inform other public health initiatives against future Nipah virus outbreaks as well as the drive to develop new vaccines. Published in PLoS Neglected Tropical Diseases, the researchers collected reports of Nipah virus outbreaks from 2001 to 2026 in Bangladesh and India, and modelled these data to understand how the virus spreads. It is the first analysis of Nipah virus’ spread, compiling all known outbreaks in India. Nipah virus was first identified in 1998 in an outbreak among pig farms in Maylasia, with the virus spreading only from animals to humans. After its initial outbreak in South East Asia, Nipah virus spread to India and Bangladesh in 2001 where outbreaks have been reported almost every year since. These findings are timely, as Nipah virus has been classified a ‘priority disease’ by the World Health Organization (WHO) due to its potential to develop into an epidemic. This calls for more research and development to improve our understanding of the virus, developing tests, and treatments against Nipah virus like vaccines. The variants of Nipah virus in India and Bangladesh are unique, as they are the only versions identified so far that can be spread directly between humans. Studying these variants are vital to understand how Nipah virus could develop into, and public health strategies to prevent, a future pandemic. As a disease that has recently emerged, there is less research on Nipah virus. Sol Kim, PhD student and lead author at LSHTM, said: "Our work analyses the historical Nipah outbreaks in Bangladesh and India to measure how many people each patient infects, how unevenly that spread is shared, and how quickly new cases follow. This work is part of our efforts on epidemic and pandemic preparedness against Nipah virus, which is considered a high-priority pathogen in public health.” The model’s results found that it takes, on average, 13 days for the virus to pass from one person to another.. However, Nipah virus spreads unevenly, meaning that most people with an infection will be unlikely to spread it to other people. But a small proportion of those infected will unwittingly act as ‘super-spreaders’ to others, and drive most of the growth in Nipah virus infections during an outbreak. This fact is very important in guiding public health efforts about Nipah virus outbreaks to control its spread. As infections spread unevenly in specific cases of high clusters, the authors recommend targeted measures such as contact tracing for affected areas and quarantines specific to affected families or neighbourhoods, rather than blanket approaches like lockdown quarantines. Alongside containment, these findings could support the development of new vaccines. The uneven dispersion will need to be considered when designing clinical trials for Nipah virus vaccine candidates, and the best strategy for delivering vaccines during a potential epidemic.
