Variable transmission efficiency of mammalian origin HPAI D1.1 H5N1 strains in ferrets

Authors: Grace E. Quirk, Michelle N. Vu, Valerie Le Sage, Kaitlyn Bushfield-Thomason, Hanh Dung Nguyen, Seema S. Lakdawala, Kaitlyn Bushfield
Original abstract (English)
AbstractHighly pathogenic avian influenza H5N1 2.3.4.4b genotype D1.1 lineage continues to predominate in the United States wild bird population and has spilled over into dairy cattle three independent times. To assess the transmission risk of this sublineage, we performed direct-contact transmission experiments for three distinct D1.1 strains in ferrets. Two of these strains were isolated from humans and one from a lethal cat infection. We found that only one human isolate (A/NV/10/2025) was able to transmit efficiently between ferrets. Compared to the other strains, this isolate harbored the mammalian adaptive PB2 D701N mutation, suggesting this mutation may be a candidate contributing to enhanced transmission efficiency of D1.1 as opposed to the PB2 E627 K substitution present in the lethal cat isolate. Based on these data we conclude that the transmission fitness of D1.1 strains is modest but special attention should be paid to emergence of adaptation at the PB2 701 position.

Question and background
The study aimed to evaluate the transmission efficiency of mammalian-origin HPAI D1.1 H5N1 strains in ferrets, as these strains have been increasingly detected in wild bird populations and have spilled over into dairy cattle. The research sought to understand the potential zoonotic risk and transmission dynamics of these strains.
Design and population
The study involved direct-contact transmission experiments conducted with three distinct D1.1 H5N1 strains in ferrets. Two of the strains were isolated from human infections, while the third was obtained from a lethal cat infection.
Core methods
Transmission experiments were performed using direct contact between ferrets to assess the ability of the D1.1 H5N1 strains to spread. The strains were tested in a controlled setting to determine their transmission fitness.
Main results
Only one human isolate, A/NV/10/2025, was found to transmit efficiently between ferrets. The other strains, including the lethal cat isolate, did not demonstrate efficient transmission.
Interpretation and novelty
The findings suggest that the mammalian adaptive PB2 D701N mutation may enhance transmission efficiency in D1.1 H5N1 strains, distinguishing them from the PB2 E627K substitution present in the cat isolate.
Scientific and public-health significance
The results highlight the importance of monitoring specific mutations in HPAI D1.1 strains for their potential to increase transmission efficiency, which has implications for zoonotic surveillance and risk assessment.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited to ferret models and does not account for other potential hosts or environmental factors. The evidence is moderate, as it is based on controlled experimental conditions rather than real-world data.

Co-circulation of H5N1 and H5N6 High Pathogenicity Avian Influenza Viruses in Migratory Birds at a Coastal Wintering Site in China

Authors: Xinyu Hu, Haiyang Xiang, Wenhao Wang, Yunyi Kong, Hongrui Liang, Min Li, Yaqi Miao, Huan Han, Zhongxing Sun, Mingyuan Yu
Original abstract (English)
Since 2020, H5Nx high Pathogenicity avian influenza viruses (HPAIVs) belonging to hemagglutinin (HA) clade 2.3.4.4b have undergone widespread dispersal in wild birds, posing substantial threats to global poultry populations and mammals worldwide. In this study, we report the co-circulation of clade 2.3.4.4b H5N1 and H5N6 HPAIVs associated with mortality events in Anseriformes and Charadriiformes at a coastal wintering site in Fujian Province, China. H5N1 and H5N6 viruses were identified in 4 species of waterfowl (Bean Goose, Eastern Spot-billed Duck, Eurasian Wigeon, and Eurasian Curlew). Phylogenetic analysis indicated that these H5N1 and H5N6 isolates were clade 2.3.4.4b strains circulating within Eurasia and lacked known mammalian adaptation markers in key proteins. The infected birds exhibited severe systemic multi-organ pathology, with the most pronounced damage observed in the lungs. H5N6 and H5N1 viruses caused lethal injury in BALB/c mice, with H5N1 leading to systemic infection. Environmental sampling also revealed the presence of H5N1 HPAIVs alongside low-pathogenicity avian influenza viruses (LPAIVs) such as H3N1 and H6N1. Our findings highlight the complex and dynamic transmission patterns of H5 HPAIV in wild bird populations and the substantial threat they pose to mammals, underscoring the critical importance of sustained surveillance for high pathogenicity avian influenza in wild birds.

Mammalian H5N1 cases predict online discourse volume and emotion during the 2024–2025 H5N1 outbreak

Authors: Luizza Dalla Pria, Katherine J Koebel, Amelia Nelson, Renata Ivanek
Original abstract (English)
Abstract The surge of highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) infection in animals across the United States since 2024 has renewed pandemic concern, making public perception a critical determinant of effective risk communication. We analyzed 42,957 Reddit comments (January 2024–December 2025) to characterize the patterns in online discourse during the outbreak. Topic modeling identified themes of public health, pandemic comparisons, and food safety. Sentiment analysis identified fear as the dominant emotional response, while anger was the most event-reactive, correlating with reported cases in cats, poultry, and wild mammals. Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average with Exogenous Regressors (ARIMAX) models with blocked cross-validation identified reported cases in domestic cats and dairy cattle as predictors of weekly Reddit engagement, reducing forecasting error by 31% over baseline. Mammalian cases thus were associated with early-warning signals for public reaction during the 2024–2025 outbreak, offering a one-week window for proactive risk communication.

Original abstract (English)
The antiviral innate immune response is a major species barrier that emerging viruses must overcome to infect and transmit efficiently in new hosts. In humans, the interferon-stimulated MX1 gene encodes the antiviral MX1 protein, which restricts zoonotic influenza A viruses (IAVs). Resistance to MX1 is considered a key feature of human-adapted IAVs and a potential determinant of zoonotic and pandemic risk. However, MX1-mediated restriction and viral escape have mostly been investigated in cell lines and animal models, which incompletely recapitulate virus-host interactions in the human airway epithelium, representing the primary site of IAV replication. MX1 knockout in hAEC ALI cultures had only a minor effect on the replication kinetics of the human-adapted H1N1pdm09 strain. By contrast, MX1 knockout markedly impaired interferon lambda-mediated restriction of the zoonotic bovine H5N1/Texas and avian H7N9/Anhui strains, resulting in viral titers up to 3-4 logs TCID50 higher than in MX1-competent cultures. These findings provide direct evidence that MX1 restricts zoonotic IAVs in primary human airway epithelium. Our study establishes a biologically relevant platform for assessing the sensitivity of emerging IAVs to a key antiviral host factor and thereby informs evaluations into their zoonotic and pandemic risk.

A cross-sectional serological study conducted between 2018 and 2025 around Poyang Lake, Jiangxi Province, China, found varied antibody reactivity to avian influenza virus subtypes among poultry-exposed individuals. The study included 2,437 serum samples from backyard poultry keepers, their household members, and live poultry market workers in Nanchang County and Duchang and Wuning counties. Antibody positivity was observed for H5N6 (4.43%), H9N2 (4.39%), H7N9 (2.74% from 2018–2020), and H3N8 (1.44% from 2021–2025). The findings do not confirm subtype-specific human infections or local circulation of the viruses. The National Influenza Center performed hemagglutination-inhibition assays, and the authors recommend continued surveillance combined with virological data for risk assessment. The study notes that sample numbers varied across subtypes, locations, and years, limiting direct comparisons. The results are based on antibody reactivity, not clinical diagnoses, and the article is presented in an accepted, citable version that may undergo further edits before publication.
