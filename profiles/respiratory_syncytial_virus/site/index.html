呼吸道合胞病毒(RSV)诱导的ADAM17活性控制上皮屏障完整性和促进合胞体形成

Respiratory syncytial virus-induced ADAM17 activity controls maintenance of epithelial barrier integrity and promotes syncytium formation
作者： Robert Meineke, Marie-Christin Knittler, Rajarshee Tagore, Nele Engling, Martin Ludlow, Albert D. M. E. Osterhaus, Guus F. Rimmelzwaan, Osterhaus ADME
摘要中文翻译摘要 呼吸道合胞病毒 (RSV) 感染导致人类肺组织上皮损伤、屏障破坏以及融合体的形成;然而,涉及的调控 宿主 防御机制仍尚未明确。ADAM17 是一种多功能的分离酶,参与炎症性肺病,被视为病毒感染期间的潜在治疗靶点。在此,我们展示 RSV 感染通过 PKC- 和 ERK 依赖性信号激活 ADAM17,提高其表面水平和分离酶活性。遗传性敲除或药物抑制 ADAM17 减少了 RSV 诱导的融合体形成、病毒进入后的传播以及病毒滴度。这表明 ADAM17 是一种 宿主 因子,促进病毒传播。ADAM17 缺失同时加重了 RSV 诱导的上皮损伤,表现为细胞间结合丧失、屏障功能受损、间隙通透性增加以及细胞毒性增强。RSV 诱导的 ADAM17 活性促进了多种上皮生长因子受体 (EGFR) 配体的释放。使用重组表皮生长因子受体配体部分恢复了 ADAM17 缺失细胞的屏障功能。与这些功能效应一致,ADAM17 缺失导致与上皮分化相关的转录程序丧失,并上调炎症基因的表达。这些发现表明 ADAM17 在 RSV 感染中发挥双重作用。它促进融合体病毒传播,同时通过自激素样 EGFR 信号通路保护上皮完整性。因此,ADAM17 活性代表了病毒传播与组织保护之间的生物权衡,表明治疗性 ADAM17 抑制可能降低病毒传播,但会加剧上皮损伤。在 呼吸道合胞病毒 (RSV) 感染期间,气道上皮必须维持屏障完整性,同时应对病毒诱导的融合体形成和组织损伤。目前尚不清楚哪些 宿主 因子在组织保护与病毒复制及传播之间取得平衡。在此,我们展示 宿主 细胞金属蛋白酶 ADAM17 在 RSV 感染时被激活。尽管 RSV 诱导的 ADAM17 活性促进融合体形成和病毒传播,它同时调控上皮屏障完整性的维持、细胞存活的保护以及抑制超炎症信号。通过表明 ADAM17 同时控制病毒传播和上皮屏障韧性,本研究揭示了一个核心权衡:同一被 RSV 利用于传播的 宿主 因子对于保护气道组织至关重要。因此,针对 抗病毒药物 疗法的 ADAM17 靶向面临重大挑战,因为阻断病毒传播可能削弱重要的上皮屏障防护。

Respiratory syncytial virus-induced ADAM17 activity controls maintenance of epithelial barrier integrity and promotes syncytium formation

Authors: Robert Meineke, Marie-Christin Knittler, Rajarshee Tagore, Nele Engling, Martin Ludlow, Albert D. M. E. Osterhaus, Guus F. Rimmelzwaan, Osterhaus ADME
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) infection causes epithelial injury, barrier disruption, and formation of syncytia in human lung tissue; however, the regulatory host defense mechanisms involved remain poorly defined. ADAM17 is a multifunctional sheddase implicated in inflammatory lung disease and is considered a potential therapeutic target during viral infections. Here, we show that RSV infection induces ADAM17 activity through PKC- and ERK-dependent signaling, increasing surface ADAM17 levels and sheddase activity. Genetic loss or pharmacological inhibition of ADAM17 reduced RSV-induced formation of syncytia, post-entry viral spread, and viral titers. This identifies ADAM17 as a host factor that supports viral dissemination. ADAM17 deficiency simultaneously exacerbated RSV-induced epithelial injury, characterized by loss of intercellular cohesion, impaired barrier function, increased paracellular permeability, and enhanced cytotoxicity. RSV-induced ADAM17 activity promoted the release of multiple epithelial growth factor receptor (EGFR) ligands. Supplementation with recombinant amphiregulin partially restored barrier function in infected ADAM17-deficient cells. Consistent with these functional effects, ADAM17 deficiency was associated with the loss of epithelial differentiation-associated transcriptional programs and increased expression of inflammatory genes. These findings indicate that ADAM17 plays a dual role in RSV infection. It facilitates syncytial viral spread, while preserving epithelial integrity through autocrine EGFR signaling. Thus, ADAM17 activity represents a biological trade-off between viral dissemination and tissue protection, indicating that therapeutic ADAM17 inhibition may reduce viral spread at the cost of aggravating epithelial damage. IMPORTANCE During respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) infection, the airway epithelium must maintain barrier integrity while responding to virus-induced syncytium formation and tissue damage. It is poorly understood which host factors balance tissue preservation against viral replication and spread. Here, we demonstrate that the host cellular metalloproteinase ADAM17 is activated upon RSV infection. While RSV-induced ADAM17 activity promotes syncytium formation and viral spread, it concurrently controls the maintenance of epithelial barrier integrity, preserves cell survival, and inhibits hyperinflammatory signaling. By demonstrating that ADAM17 has a dual mode of action controlling both viral propagation and epithelial barrier resilience, this work uncovers a central trade-off: the same host factor exploited by RSV for dissemination is essential for protecting airway tissue. Consequently, targeting ADAM17 for antiviral therapy presents major challenges, as blocking viral spread may compromise vital epithelial defenses.

Molecular detection of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) in hospitalized children at the University Hospital Center of Brazzaville, Republic of the Congo

Authors: Moyen Nanikaly, Yvette Ngakengni Neli, Audrey Ombele Moïse, M. Anicet Boumba L., Bidounga Joël, Makaya Celeste, F. Pouki S., Moyen Engoba, Okobo Rachel, Nguimbi Etienne
Original abstract (English)
Epidemiological Respiratory viral infections are among the most common diseases responsible for morbidity and mortality among children in sub-Saharan Africa, with respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) being a major contributor. This situation highlights the urgent need for rapid and accurate diagnostic tools. The objective of this study was to detect RSV among hospitalized children under five years of age with acute respiratory infections at the University Hospital Center of Brazzaville in the Republic of the Congo. A total of 81 nasopharyngeal swab samples were collected from children aged ≤5 years who were admitted to the Pediatric Intensive Care Unit and Neonatology Department of the University Hospital Center of Brazzaville with acute respiratory infections between August and December 2023. Respiratory viruses were identified using rapid antigen detection assays and reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). Rapid antigen tests detected SARS-CoV-2 in 2 cases (2.5%), whereas RSV and influenza A and B were not detected. In contrast, RT-qPCR identified RSV in 4 samples (4.9%). All RSV-positive patients were male, aged over 28 days, and hospitalized in the pediatric intensive care unit. The main clinical signs observed were respiratory distress (100%), cough (75%), fever (50%) and rhinorrhea (50%). These findings confirm the circulation of RSV among hospitalized children at the University Hospital Center of Brazzaville and highlight the superior sensitivity of RT-qPCR over rapid antigen tests for RSV detection. Key words: Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), children, rapid antigen test, reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR), Brazzaville.

Abstract Background Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV) is a leading cause of severe respiratory infections, particularly pneumonia, in children, especially those under the age of five. The incidence of severe RSV pneumonia has risen in recent years, underscoring the need for an effective predictive tool. Therefore, the development and validation of a model to predict the severity of RSV pneumonia in pediatric patients is essential. Methods A retrospective analysis was conducted involving 180 children with confirmed RSV pneumonia, categorized into severe (n = 62) and non-severe (n = 118) groups. Clinical, laboratory, and imaging data were collected at admission. Features selection was performed using LASSO regression and the Boruta algorithm. DCA demonstrated that the RF model provided a significant net benefit, indicating its clinical utility for early intervention. SHAP analysis revealed that respiratory rate and shortness of breath were the most influential features in predicting severe RSV pneumonia. Original abstract (English)
Interferons (IFN) induce cellular proteins with antiviral activity. One such protein is Interferon Stimulated Gene 15 (ISG15). ISG15 is conjugated to target cellular proteins through ISGylation to confer antiviral activity and can regulate cellular processes associated with infectious, inflammatory and neurodegenerative diseases, and cancer. Apart from ISGylation, unconjugated free ISG15 is also released from cells during various conditions, including virus infection. The role of extracellular ISG15 during virus infection is poorly understood. Additionally, it was unknown whether extracellular ISG15 can activate IFN independent ISGylation via activation of alternative cellular signaling pathway. We have used IFN and IFN signaling incompetent epithelial cells infected with human respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) or treated with purified human ISG15 protein. Additional studies were conducted with IFN competent cells treated with IFN signaling inhibitors, along with extracellular ISG15 blocking antibody and immunofluorescence analysis to demonstrate the role of extracellular ISG15 during virus infection. The involvement of integrin-Focal Adhesion Kinase (FAK) pathway during this event was investigated by using integrin-FAK pathway inhibitors and mutant ISG15 protein (generated based on in silico modeling) that failed to interact with integrin. To demonstrate post-translational regulation, protein chase experiment was performed with protein synthesis inhibitor treated cells. We have identified an alternative non-canonical ISGylation route initiated by extracellular ISG15 and mediated by integrin-FAK signaling, in contrast to the classical IFN-driven ISGylation. We show that extracellular ISG15 acts as a soluble antiviral factor that triggers IFN independent ISGylation and restricts virus infection in epithelial cells. Specifically, extracellular ISG15 engages with cell surface integrins via its RGD-like motif to activate the integrin-FAK pathway, resulting in IFN-independent ISGylation. Furthermore, in the absence of ISG15 transcriptional upregulation, extracellular ISG15 enhances the post-translational stability of basal intracellular ISG15 protein to support ISGylation. Thus, our studies have identified extracellular ISG15 as an antiviral factor that promotes non-canonical IFN independent ISGylation through integrin-FAK signaling and post-translational stabilization of intracellular ISG15 protein. Because ISG15 and ISGylation influence diverse infectious, inflammatory, and degenerative conditions, this alternative ISGylation route has broad implications for antiviral immunity, disease resolution, and development of host-directed therapeutic strategies for various diseases.

Original abstract (English)
Background Following the lifting of pandemic-era restrictions, China experienced a sharp resurgence in pediatric respiratory infections; however, post-pandemic epidemiological and climatic data on lower respiratory tract infections (LRTIs) from the temperate continental climate of northern China remain scarce. Methods In this retrospective, single-center surveillance study, we characterized the post-pandemic epidemiology of six respiratory viruses in children hospitalized with LRTI in Tianjin and assessed their pathogen-specific, lagged climatic associations. Question and background
Older adults with comorbidities experience substantial morbidity from respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), yet evidence from low- and middle-income countries on functional RSV immunity, particularly the relationship between humoral and cellular immune responses remain limited.
Design and population
We conducted a cross-sectional study among adults aged ≥ 60 years at a tertiary care center in New Delhi, India. Individuals attending outpatient clinics or admitted for non-acute, non-respiratory conditions were eligible. Enrolled individuals were found to be RSV seropositive in all age groups.
Core methods
We conducted a cross-sectional sero-immunologic study between January and December 2024 at a tertiary care center in New Delhi, India. Majority of the samples from older adults failed to produce IL-2, IFN-γ or TNF-α in CD4+/CD8+ cells upon stimulation with RSV peptide pool.
Main results
The magnitude of neutralizing antibody titers was lower in older adults with comorbidities compared to young healthy adults and we found a positive correlation between binding IgG and neutralizing antibody titers. Neutralizing activity was significantly lower in older adults with chronic liver disease (p = 0·009) and malignancy (p = 0·04). RSV-specific T-cell responses were significantly lower in older adults than in healthy young adults, with median IFN-γ ELISpot values of 8 and 68 spot-forming units (SFU) per million cells, respectively (p = 0·0068).
Interpretation and novelty

Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a leading cause of lower respiratory tract disease, but the contribution of long non‐coding RNAs (lncRNAs) to RSV‐induced epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT)‐associated changes remains unclear. We used transcriptomic screening in RSV‐exposed BEAS‐2B cells to identify candidate lncRNAs and then examined SRGAP2‐AS1 in cell and mouse models. Expression was measured by RT‐qPCR, and loss‐ and gain‐of‐function experiments were combined with RNA immunoprecipitation, co‐immunoprecipitation, Western blot analysis, and TCID 50 assays. SRGAP2‐AS1 was increased in RSV‐infected BEAS‐2B cells and mouse lung tissue. Silencing SRGAP2‐AS1 reduced viral replication, lowered IL‐6 and IL‐1β mRNA, increased IFN‐β mRNA, and attenuated EMT‐associated marker changes in RSV‐infected BEAS‐2B cells. SRGAP2‐AS1 was predominantly cytoplasmic and enriched in PTBP1 immunoprecipitates. Reciprocal co‐immunoprecipitation showed that PTBP1 and ZEB1 were present in the same endogenous complex, and ZEB1 abundance changed with SRGAP2‐AS1 gain and loss of function. ZEB1 knockdown produced parallel changes in viral RNA, immune‐related transcripts, and EMT‐associated markers. Ectopic ZEB1 expression reversed the E‐cadherin change caused by SRGAP2‐AS1 depletion, supporting a downstream role for ZEB1 in the epithelial arm of the EMT‐associated phenotype. These findings support regulatory relationships among SRGAP2‐AS1, PTBP1, and ZEB1 during RSV infection. The causal order of the viral and immune‐related changes, and the in vivo relevance of the EMT‐associated phenotype, remain unresolved.

Punicalin targets RSV glycoprotein and fusion protein at non-overlapping sites to prevent viral escape.

Authors: Yingcai Xiong, Jingyi Ming, Zhaowei Zhang, Jiru Zhang, Xiaoqing Zhou, Biyu Cao, Guang Li, Yuqing Zhu, Zepeng Yang, Junxi Song
Original abstract (English)
Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) causes substantial respiratory disease, while treatment is limited by drug resistance. Molecular docking of a 3,880-compound natural-product library identifies punicalin (PUN) as a dual-target RSV entry inhibitor. PUN inhibits the evaluated RSV-A and RSV-B strains, including a clinical isolate, and retains activity against strains resistant to ziresovir, ribavirin, nirsevimab, and palivizumab. After 20 passages under PUN selection, the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC 50 ) increases 3.88-fold, remaining below the predefined >10-fold resistance threshold. Mechanistic analyses suggest that PUN interacts with the G protein cystine-noose region involving N178, N179, and W183 to inhibit attachment and with an F protein region involving V243, T245, and P246 to stabilize prefusion F and inhibit membrane fusion. These regions do not overlap known resistance hotspots. In RSV-infected mice, nebulized PUN provides sustained respiratory exposure, reduces lung viral loads and pathology, and attenuates inflammatory responses.

Regional inequality in infant RSV morbidity and mortality burden in the era of expanded RSV passive immunisation: a global projection modelling study

Authors: Shao-Long Ren, Bingbing Cong, Jing Zou, Ling Guo, Harish Nair, Erin G. Sparrow, Daniel R. Feikin, You Li, Shaolong Ren, Erin Sparrow
Original abstract (English)
Background Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a major cause of acute lower respiratory infection (ALRI) in infants, with low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) bearing a disproportionately high burden. While novel RSV prophylactic products including long-acting monoclonal antibodies and a maternal vaccine are being rolled out for protecting the general infant population in high-income countries, implementation in LMICs is only expected by 2028, potentially exacerbating health inequities. We aimed to assess regional inequality in infant RSV disease burden with and without the implementation of these interventions. Methods We re-analysed the previous global RSV disease burden dataset in infants by WHO regions and World Bank Income Classification for the year of 2019 as a baseline, using generalised linear mixed-effects models. We developed a static deterministic model that explicitly accounted for the varied risks for RSV admission by birth month and chronological age in month and projected the annual RSV-associated ALRI hospital admissions and RSV-attributable deaths under two scenarios: a no-implementation scenario that assumed the same hospital admission and mortality rates to the 2019 baseline, and a June-2025 product use scenario that was based on country-level implementation status of novel RSV prophylactic products as of June 2025; the two scenarios were then compared to estimate the impact of the current implementation of novel prophylactic products. We further calculated the ratio of RSV-attributable deaths between the highest and lowest burden quintiles (Q5-Q1 ratio) to assess inequality under the two scenarios. Findings At baseline, the African Region had the highest incidence of RSV-associated ALRI (117.4 per 1000 person-years, 95% uncertainty range, UR: 60.0–229.8) and highest RSV-attributable mortality rate (0.97 per 1000 person-years, 95% UR: 0.76–1.31) while having the lowest RSV-associated ALRI hospital admission rate (12.6 per 1000 person-years; 95% UR: 7.5–21.3); the Eastern Mediterranean Region had the highest hospital admission rate (29.9 per 1000 person-years, 95% UR: 14.2–63.2). The regional variations in incidence, hospital admission, and mortality rates persisted when further stratifying the above estimates by country income level. As of June 2025, nirsevimab and maternal RSVpreF had been licensed in 54 and 64 countries, respectively, with 16 and 12 countries introducing them into their national immunisation programmes. A total of 4,641,000 doses of nirsevimab and 1,955,000 doses of RSVPreF were estimated to be administered annually under the June-2025 product use scenario globally. Administration of both products was projected to jointly avert 91,000 (95% UR: 75,000–118,000) RSV-associated ALRI hospital admissions and 300 (95% UR: 210–470) RSV-attributable deaths in infants annually, corresponding to 3.7% (95% UR: 2.7–4.9) and 0.4% (95% UR: 0.3–0.7) of the global admissions and deaths, respectively. Approximately 90% of averted hospital admissions and over 70% of averted deaths in infants occurred in high-income countries (81,000 [95% UR: 66,000–108,000] and 220 [95% UR: 140–390], respectively), corresponding to reductions of 33.2% (95% UR 31.0–35.9) and 29.1% (95% UR 22.6–35.2); by contrast, the relative reduction of admissions and deaths in middle-income countries was less than 5%, and no reduction was expected in low-income countries. Globally, substantial inequality was observed in the RSV-attributable overall mortality burden under the no-implementation scenario, with the Q5-Q1 ratio of RSV-attributable deaths estimated at 18.3 (95% UR: 11.7–27.5). The June-2025 product use scenario was associated with a possible slight increase in global inequality, with the Q5-Q1 ratio rising to 20.8 (95% UR: 13.0–31.7). Interpretation The global RSV burden in 2019 varied substantially by region and income level, with the African Region exhibiting the highest incidence and mortality but the lowest hospitalisation rates. The current implementation of novel RSV prophylactic products, characterised by widespread licensing and use in high-income countries and lack of availability in LMICs, particularly the African Region, is projected to yield only modest reductions in the global infant RSV disease burden. Critically, inequitable access to these products may exacerbate existing inequalities in RSV mortality burden. These findings underscore the urgent need for targeted strategies to accelerate the introduction and scale-up of infant RSV immunisation programmes in LMICs. Original abstract (English)
Abstract Pregnancy and early infancy are periods of heightened vulnerability, with SARS-CoV-2 and influenza infections potentially linked to adverse pregnancy outcomes, including preterm birth and stillbirth. Maternal vaccination provides direct protection to mothers by active immunisation and to infants by passive immunisation. This scoping review mapped and described published literature on maternal vaccination against COVID-19, influenza, pertussis, and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). It includes information on the outcome domains studied and if timing of vaccination was examined, with the aim of identifying evidence gaps and supporting decision-makers in choosing priority areas for subsequent systematic review topics. A comprehensive literature search across multiple databases of studies published from January 2000 to October 2025 identified 635 publications (540 publications of primary studies, 95 evidence syntheses). Studies on COVID-19 (260 publications of studies, 45 reviews), influenza (161 publications of studies, 29 reviews), pertussis (113 publications of studies, 20 reviews), and RSV (20 publications of studies, 11 reviews) were analysed. Available evidence on COVID-19, influenza, and pertussis vaccination on efficacy, effectiveness, safety and immunogenicity outcomes is considerable. RSV vaccination evidence is limited. Updated systematic reviews would be helpful to clarify the optimal timing of COVID-19 and influenza vaccination and the effectiveness and safety of the coadministration of influenza and pertussis vaccines.

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is the leading cause of lower respiratory tract infection (LRTI) and hospitalization in infants and young children worldwide, with the highest disease burden occurring in otherwise healthy term infants during the first 6 months of life. The recent introduction of long-acting monoclonal antibodies and maternal RSV vaccination has fundamentally changed preventive strategies against severe RSV disease. Evidence from randomized clinical trials and large real-world studies consistently demonstrates that a single dose of Nirsevimab provides season-long protection against RSV-associated LRTI and reduces hospitalization rate across diverse infant populations, while maintaining an excellent safety profile. Emerging data also support its effectiveness in routine clinical practice, although important uncertainties remain regarding long-term epidemiological effects, viral evolution, resistance, comparative effectiveness with maternal immunization, and optimal implementation across different healthcare settings. Current evidence supports Nirsevimab as a major advance in infant RSV prevention. However, continued surveillance, comparative effectiveness studies, and equitable global implementation will be essential to maximize its long-term public health impact. This narrative review summarizes current evidence on Nirsevimab, with emphasis on its clinical efficacy, safety, real-world effectiveness, resistance, implementation challenges, cost-effectiveness, and its role alongside maternal RSV vaccination.

Nirsevimab for RSV Prevention in India: Economic Viability and Implementation Pathways for National Introduction: A Review

Authors: Gaurav Gupta
Original abstract (English)
Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) causes substantial morbidity and mortality in Indian infants, with an estimated 800,000–1.2 million annual hospitalizations. Nirsevimab, a long-acting monoclonal antibody providing single-dose seasonal protection, has recently become available in India but remains cost-prohibitive for public health implementation. To evaluate the economic viability of Nirsevimab introduction in India and propose evidence-based implementation pathways aligned with national epidemiology and health system capacity. A structured review of RSV disease burden, Nirsevimab clinical efficacy, and economic evidence was undertaken. An India-specific cohort decision-analytic model was constructed using WHO-recommended cost-effectiveness thresholds. Deterministic and probabilistic sensitivity analyses were performed, and phased implementation pathways were developed, incorporating supply considerations, program readiness, and integration with existing immunization platforms. The manuscript was prepared in accordance with the Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles (SANRA) guidelines. At current market prices (₹40,000–₹80,000 per dose), universal Nirsevimab would consume 9–17% of India’s public health expenditure while exceeding accepted cost-effectiveness thresholds. Economic modeling indicates an economically justifiable price of approximately ₹6,300 per dose. At ₹4,000 per dose, national introduction could prevent approximately 640,000 hospitalizations and 6,800 deaths annually while remaining highly cost-effective. Sensitivity analyses demonstrate robustness across plausible epidemiologic and pricing scenarios. Nirsevimab can become a cost-effective RSV prevention strategy in India if differential pricing reduces per-dose cost to ₹4,000–₹6,000. A phased introduction prioritizing high-burden regions and high-risk infants offers a pragmatic pathway for national adoption.

News Brief
The National Institute for Communicable Diseases (NICD) has announced the end of the 2026 respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) season, which began in week 11 (9 March 2026) and peaked in week 25 (15 June 2026). 2026 respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) season has ended. RSV is a leading cause of bronchiolitis and lower respiratory tract infections (LRTI) in young children and can cause severe illness in infants. It is highly contagious, spreads primarily through respiratory droplets, and reinfections can occur. Risk factors for severe RSV-associated disease include prematurity, congenital heart disease, chronic lung disease of prematurity, neurological conditions, immunodeficiency, lack of breastfeeding, HIV infection or exposure, and age under 6 months. Over the past decade, excluding the COVID-19 pandemic years 2020 and 2021, the RSV season in South Africa has typically begun around mid-February, with onset ranging from late January to late February. During the past 2 years, the start of the season was delayed compared to previous seasons. The 2026 season began in week 11 (week starting 9 March 2026), when the RSV detection rate among children aged <5 years enrolled in inpatient pneumonia surveillance at public hospitals exceeded the seasonal threshold and was sustained above the threshold for two consecutive weeks (Figure 1). RSV activity subsequently increased and peaked in week 25 (week starting 15 June 2026) at the moderate activity level, with the season ending in week 34 (week starting 17 August 2026). Message to clinicians Although the 2026 RSV season has ended, sporadic RSV infections may continue to occur outside the defined seasonal period. Clinicians should therefore continue to consider RSV in the differential diagnosis of patients presenting with respiratory illness, particularly young infants and children at increased risk of severe disease. Prevention of RSV Measures to reduce the spread of RSV and other respiratory viruses remain important throughout the year, even after the defined RSV season has ended. These include avoiding close contact with people who are ill, regular handwashing, avoiding touching the mouth, eyes and nose, and covering coughs and sneezes appropriately. Individuals experiencing respiratory symptoms should stay at home where possible to reduce transmission to others. During the RSV season, additional RSV-specific preventive options are available for groups at increased risk of severe disease. Palivizumab, which requires monthly administration during the RSV season, has been available in South Africa for some time, although its high cost may limit its use. Long-acting monoclonal antibodies are also available in the private sector for infant protection, while maternal RSV vaccination is available in the private sector to protect infants. Introduction of maternal RSV vaccination into the public sector is being considered but is not currently available. As these interventions are targeted to specific groups and, in some instances, timed in relation to the RSV season, general measures to reduce respiratory virus transmission remain important throughout the year. Medscape AI News & Perspective Tools & Reference CME/CE More Video Events Specialties Topics RegisterLog In Medscape News CanadaFeatures Low RSV Vaccine Coverage Leaves Seniors at Risk Evra Taylor September 29, 2026 Respiratory virus season will begin soon in Canada, but recent government data indicate low respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) vaccine coverage among older Canadians. Only 34% of Canadians aged 75 years or older reported being vaccinated, along with only 21% of those aged 60-74 years with chronic conditions. A recent update from the National Advisory Committee on Immunization (NACI) strongly recommends vaccination for everyone aged 75 years or older and for patients aged 65-74 years who are at increased risk for severe disease. But the provinces are free to determine which vaccination programs to fund. RSV is a common respiratory virus that can cause serious complications, including hospitalization, ICU admission, and death, in older adults and patients with certain medical conditions. Historically, RSV has been seen as a disease affecting children younger than 24 months. This is because it was first recognized in the pediatric population, and most of the surveillance, prevention, and public awareness were focused on children. But globally, the disease is associated with roughly 336,000 hospitalizations and 14,000 in-hospital deaths each year in adults aged 65 years or older, according to a systematic review. The first RSV vaccine for patients aged 60 years or older received Health Canada approval in August 2023. Older Adults and RSV Risk “For older adults, particularly those with underlying health conditions, RSV is not simply a bad cold,” Ian Culbert, executive director of the Canadian Public Health Association, told Medscape News Canada. “It can cause pneumonia, exacerbate chronic heart and lung disease, lead to hospitalization and intensive care stays, and even result in death. We definitely need to broaden the way we talk about RSV. It certainly remains an important pediatric infection, but it’s also an important cause of serious illness in older adults and adults with certain chronic conditions.” RSV causes 5%-8% of symptomatic respiratory infections in adults aged 60 years or older. Patients in their eighties have particularly high rates of infection, and the virus becomes more prevalent with age, Culbert explained. The benefits of the vaccine for older adults are clear, according to Isaac Bogoch, MD, infectious disease specialist at Toronto General Hospital in Toronto. “The emerging data for the RSV vaccine deployed in older Canadians appear to be pretty darn good,” Bogoch told Medscape News Canada. “Real-world experience is emulating the trials being conducted, and the vaccine is reducing the risk of hospitalization with ICU stays. The nice thing about this vaccine is its very long lifespan: It probably has decent efficacy for two or even more seasons,” he added. When patients are admitted to the hospital for a respiratory illness such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) exacerbation, heart failure, or community-acquired pneumonia, it’s an opportune time to discuss their risk factors for severe infection and protective measures that will reduce their risk for further hospitalizations related to RSV or other infections, Bogoch noted. Lack of Provincial Coverage Affects Access Clinicians play an important role in talking to older patients about RSV vaccination. In surveys, patients often say that they weren’t told about the vaccination option. But a recommendation from a clinician doesn’t necessarily translate into access to the vaccine. Most provinces and territories have limited or no universal public funding for routine adult RSV vaccination outside of specific high-risk groups. Despite the NACI recommendation, public funding varies wildly across various regions and residential settings, and coverage is nuanced. British Columbia, Ontario, and Prince Edward Island cover adults aged 75 years or older, for example. British Columbia has a free, time-limited vaccination program starting in fall 2026 for Indigenous patients aged 60 years or older. Alberta pays for the vaccine for those aged 70 years or older; residents of continuing care or senior supportive living aged 60 years or older; and First Nations, Métis, and Inuit patients aged 60 years or older. “The problem is how sick people can get and the duration of the illness. It affects their lifespan going forward,” said Jobin Varughese, MD, family physician and assistant dean of primary care education at Toronto Metropolitan University in Toronto. “The people most at risk are those with COPD, bronchitis, emphysema, or even frequent viral colds — so is anyone over age 60 years in a long-term care facility or retirement setting where they’re at close quarters with other elderly patients.” With age, lungs lose a lot of their plasticity and their ability to fight off infection. With a decreased immune response, it’s critical that seniors be vaccinated, Varughese explained. “A person’s access to a recommended vaccine should not depend so heavily on where they live in Canada. By investing in vaccines, we can take the pressure off hospitals and emergency rooms, especially during respiratory virus season,” concluded Culbert. Culbert, Bogoch, and Varughese reported having no relevant financial relationships. American Lung Association logo Each year, respiratory viruses cause hundreds of thousands of hospitalizations, which significantly impact a person's work, school and family time. According to the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention's (CDC) Respiratory Virus Hospitalization Surveillance Network: There was an estimated 32 million illnesses, 390,000 hospitalizations and 24,000 deaths during the 2025-2026 influenza season. From October 1, 2025, through July 18, 2026, as many as 2.3 million COVID-19 outpatient visits and 240,000 COVID-19 hospitalizations. From October 1, 2025, through June 6, 2026, there have been an estimated 2.8 million RSV outpatient visits and as many as 340,000 RSV hospitalizations. "Fortunately, vaccines are available and effective in keeping people healthy, active and out of the hospital. Vaccines are especially important for the 35 million individuals in the United States with lung disease, like asthma or COPD, because respiratory infections can worsen existing lung disease symptoms," said Harold Wimmer, president and CEO of the American Lung Association. "As families look ahead to the winter and the holiday season, the best thing they can do to protect their health is to make sure everyone is up to date on their recommended vaccinations." The Lung Association encourages individuals to speak to a healthcare provider about receiving the following recommended vaccines to prevent severe illness this respiratory virus season, which typically peaks from October through March: Flu: Everyone ages six months and older is recommended to get a flu shot annually. COVID-19: Everyone six months and older or their caregiver should discuss COVID-19 vaccination with a healthcare provider. RSV: Adults ages 50 to 74 who are at an increased risk of developing severe RSV illness, like adults living with chronic lung or heart disease, and all adults ages 75 or older are recommended to get a single RSV vaccine if they have not received one previously. Additional doses are not recommended at this time. Story Continues For the millions of people in the U.S. living with asthma, respiratory viruses like flu, COVID-19 and RSV can place added stress on the lungs even when asthma is mild or well controlled. Respiratory viruses are the leading cause of asthma flare-ups, or exacerbations, accounting for up to 80% of asthma attacks, according to the CDC. Adults with asthma are more likely to develop pneumonia after getting sick with the flu than people who do not have asthma. In addition, respiratory illnesses pose a serious risk to individuals with lung cancer because their body, including their lungs, is already vulnerable from cancer and its treatments, such as radiation and surgery. Many people with lung cancer may have weakened immune systems, meaning that they may not be able to easily fight off viral infections which can quickly become severe and lead to complications like pneumonia or hospitalization. People living with COPD are also at increased risk of serious complications from respiratory viruses. Respiratory infections are a leading cause of COPD flare-ups, or exacerbations, which can worsen symptoms such as shortness of breath, coughing and excess mucus production. These flare-ups can take weeks or longer to recover from and increase the risk of hospitalization and other serious complications. Educating adults about vaccine-preventable disease is essential to reducing severe illness and hospitalizations. Raising awareness about surveillance trends during the 2026-2027 fall and winter respiratory virus season.
