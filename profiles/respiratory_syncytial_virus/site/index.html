Enhanced respiratory syncytial virus surveillance of young children admitted to the Sydney Children's Hospitals Network, 2019-2023.

Authors: Gemma L Saravanos, Kathryn Meredith, Shirley Wong, Rebecca Burrell, Adam Bartlett, Nusrat Homaira, Philip N Britton, Rama Kandasamy
Original abstract (English)
OBJECTIVES: In this study, we aimed to comprehensively describe the clinical characteristics, management and outcomes of children with respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) disease admitted to the Sydney Children's Hospitals Network (SCHN), determine the full burden of severe disease and identify population-specific risk factors for intensive care unit (ICU) admission. STUDY TYPE: Multi-centre, retrospective cohort study conducted over 5 years. METHODS: The study was conducted at two tertiary paediatric hospitals in metropolitan New South Wales, Australia, before implementation of government-funded immunisation programs against RSV in children. Participants were children aged <2 years hospitalised with laboratory-confirmed RSV from 1 January 2019 to 31 December 2023, comprising all ICU admitted, and a random sample of non-ICU admitted patients. The main outcome measures were characteristics and incidence rate of RSV hospitalisations and ICU admissions among children aged <2 years. RESULTS: Over a 5-year period, 4414 children were hospitalised with laboratory-confirmed RSV, of whom 11.3% (499/4414) were admitted to ICU. The median age was younger in the ICU cohort (n = 499) compared with the non-ICU sample (n = 1000; 3.3 vs 7.1 months, P < 0.0001). Factors associated with higher odds of ICU admission in multivariate logistic regression were decreasing month of age (OR 1.10, 95% CI 1.06-1.11), prematurity (OR 2.17, 95% CI 1.61-2.91), Indigenous identity (OR 4.10, 95% CI 2.30-7.56) and cardiac disease (OR 3.39, 95% CI 2.06-5.70). The average hospitalisation incidence rate was 1615 per 100,000 (95% CI 1565-1666), with higher rates among children aged <1 year, 2141 per 100,000 (95% CI 2061-2224) compared with those aged 1-2 years, 1088 per 100,000 (95% CI 1030-1148). CONCLUSIONS: RSV immunisation programs are expected to substantially reduce the burden of hospitalisation among Australian children in their first year of life. Children who are Indigenous, were born prematurely or have heart disease are at greatest risk of severe disease and may benefit from extended preventative programs.

Under pressure: lessons learnt from rapid implementation of the respiratory syncytial virus maternal vaccine and infant nirsevimab in Sydney, Australia.

Authors: Isabella Townshend, Kate Griew, Rosemary A Burke, Vendula Blaya-Novakova, Rebekah Dashwood, Sarah Everett, Leena Gupta, Vendula Blaya‐Nováková
Original abstract (English)
OBJECTIVE: To share lessons learnt from the Sydney Local Health District (SLHD) rollout of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) prevention products, and present data on immunisation uptake for mothers and babies in two maternity hospitals. TYPE OF SERVICE: Rollout of the RSV maternal vaccine and the injectable, long-acting monoclonal antibody nirsevimab for newborn infants to protect them from RSV infection and hospitalisation. METHODS: Deidentified summary data from the electronic medical record on RSV immunisation status in two SLHD maternity hospitals were analysed descriptively as counts and proportions. The Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research was used as a lens to reflect on the rollout process. RESULTS: RSV vaccination coverage in women giving birth in two SLHD hospitals improved from 4% in the first month of rollout (February 2025) to a range of 70-74% from June to September 2025. As antenatal coverage improved, the number of live born babies requiring nirsevimab decreased. Among eligible newborns, 36% of families declined nirsevimab, with reason for refusal not recorded. Documentation of RSV immunisation status improved as the rollout progressed. LESSONS LEARNT: Engaging multidisciplinary leaders and clinicians is critical for a coordinated response and program rollout success. Leveraging existing education structures, staff meetings and using online prerecording can optimise clinician education. New programs take time to embed into routine clinical practice, and programs that involve delivering multiple immunisation products for distinct groups require clear messaging and systemic safeguards to prevent errors. Integrating immunisation into medical record workflows as standard practice can support program adoption and uptake.

Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: The concept of immunity debt due to reduced circulation of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) in adults is controversial. We questioned whether adults lost protective antibodies when RSV was not circulating in Houston during the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) pandemic. METHODS: We studied 100 adults (mean age, 49 years; range, 21-82 years) from a longitudinal cohort: 50 SARS-CoV-2 seronegative and 50 SARS-CoV-2 infected. They were followed up from March 2020 to April 2021, when RSV was not in circulation. Three serum samples were collected per adult during the observation period and were tested for neutralizing antibodies (NtAbs) against 4 RSV/A and RSV/B strains, fusion protein (F) site competitive antibodies (CAs), and natural killer cell antibody-dependent cell cytotoxicity (NK ADCC). Antibody kinetics and decay were analyzed. RESULTS: Seronegative and infection groups exhibited similar geometric mean NtAb titers against RSV/A and RSV/B strains, warranting a single combined cohort. The NtAb titers against the RSV strains during the 3 visits were stable over a mean of 309 days. Comparable antibody kinetic stability and decay rates were observed for F site CAs to sites Ø, II, and IV. NK ADCC activity was detected in 29 participants with no correlation to neutralizing or F site CAs. CONCLUSIONS: We detected remarkable stability in protective antibody levels in adults against RSV when it was not circulating in Houston. Immunity debt could not be ascribed to the adult population in our cohort. Original abstract (English)
Background: The relationship between recent viral infection and subsequent susceptibility to, or protection against, other viruses remains unclear, particularly across ages and symptom status. Methods: A prospective cohort study enrolled participants aged 6 months to 49 years in Washington and Oregon, USA, 2022-2024. Weekly nasal swabs were tested for SARS-CoV-2, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and influenza A/B; additional swabs were collected during symptomatic illness and were also tested for parainfluenza (PIV) 1-4, endemic human coronaviruses (hCOV), adenovirus, human metapneumovirus, human rhinovirus (hRV), and enteroviruses. Question and background
The study aimed to identify the etiological profile of pathogens responsible for severe acute respiratory infection (SARI) other than SARS-CoV-2 in a predominantly pediatric cohort, addressing the need for better diagnostic tools in resource-limited settings.
Design and population
A retrospective observational study was conducted at a tertiary care hospital in North India from September to December 2021, analyzing 117 patients admitted with SARI during the COVID-19 pandemic.
Core methods
Multiplex PCR and conventional microbiological culture were used to detect respiratory pathogens. The study cohort comprised 103 children and 14 adults. Pathogen detection was performed using the Fast-Track Diagnostics Respiratory Pathogens 21 Plus assay, and statistical analysis was conducted using Fisher’s exact test.
Main results

HRSV was the predominant respiratory pathogen, and bacterial sepsis was the principal determinant of mortality. The ViVI Score demonstrated excellent discrimination for mortality, with a cutoff of ≥30 showing strong association.

The findings suggest that the ViVI Score can be a useful tool for clinical triage and respiratory pathogen surveillance in resource-limited settings, and HRSV is a significant cause of severe respiratory illness in children.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its retrospective design and the small sample size, which may affect generalizability. The high mortality rate in patients with culture-confirmed sepsis (60%) highlights the need for better diagnostic and treatment strategies.

Cost‐effectiveness analysis of the mRNA‐1345 RSV vaccine for older adults in Italy.

Authors: Mariia Dronova, Camille Moyon, Lukasz Pyrek, Katherine A. Hicks, Zi-Yi Xiao, Filippo Rumi, Chiara de Waure, Stefan Michael Scholz, Parinaz K. Ghaswalla
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Introduction Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is an important cause of respiratory disease in older adults and adults with chronic medical conditions, contributing substantially to the healthcare burden in Italy. The availability of effective RSV vaccines provides an opportunity to reduce RSV-related morbidity, mortality, and healthcare costs in populations at high risk of severe disease. This study evaluates the potential public health impact and cost-effectiveness of vaccination using mRNA-1345 administered as a single dose compared with no vaccination in Italian high-risk adults aged 60-74 years and all adults aged ≥75 years. Methods A static decision-analytic model was developed to project clinical and economic outcomes over a 5-year time horizon. Economic outcomes were evaluated from the Italian National Health Service (Servizio Sanitario Nazionale, SSN) perspective. Model inputs were informed by the most recent Italian epidemiological, clinical, and economic evidence, supplemented by published international data when necessary. Deterministic, probabilistic, and scenario analyses were conducted to assess the impact of uncertainty in model inputs and assumptions on the study results. Results Vaccination with mRNA-1345 in high-risk adults aged 60-74 years and all adults aged ≥75 years was projected to avert over 19,800 hospitalizations, 4,000 emergency department visits, 381,000 outpatient visits, 6,000 RSV-attributable deaths, and 212,000 antibiotic prescriptions compared with no vaccination over a 5-year period. The total incremental cost of €1,143 million and the additional 47,477 QALYs gained resulted in an ICER of €24,078, which was below the commonly referenced willingness to-pay range of €33,000- 40,000 per QALY gained. Sensitivity analyses confirmed robustness of the analysis results. Conclusions Vaccination with mRNA-1345 is a cost-effective strategy for the prevention of RSV in high- risk adults aged 60-74 years and all adults ≥75 years in Italy and has the potential to provide substantial public health benefits. Key Summary Points Why carry out this study? The clinical and economic burden of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) in high-risk and older adults is significant. RSV vaccines have received regulatory authorization in Europe, and national recommendations or immunization programs for older adults have been introduced in several countries including Germany, France, Spain and the United Kingdom. In Italy, RSV vaccination for older adults has not yet been implemented as a uniform national program, however such a program has been endorsed by several scientific societies. The study objective was to estimate the potential public health impact and cost- effectiveness of vaccination using mRNA-1345 administered as a single dose compared with no vaccination in Italian high-risk adults aged 60-74 years and all adults aged ≥75 years, in the 5-year period following vaccination. What was learned from the study? Vaccination of high-risk adults aged 60-74 years and all adults aged ≥75 years with mRNA- 1345 was projected to prevent more than 19,800 hospitalizations, 4,000 ED visits, 381,000 outpatient visits, 6,000 RSV-attributable deaths, and 212,000 antibiotic prescriptions (up to 21% reduction across outcomes), in comparison to no vaccination, over a 5-year period. This analysis suggests the mRNA-1345 vaccine is a cost-effective strategy for the prevention of RSV in high-risk adults in Italy and has the potential for substantial public health benefit. Our findings provide evidence to inform national immunization policy and reimbursement decisions, and support the implementation of an RSV vaccination program for high-risk adults aged 60-74 years and all adults aged ≥75 years in Italy. They found that using a single-dose vaccination strategy could prevent over 19,800 hospitalizations and more than 6,000 deaths over the following 5 years. Although vaccination requires investments, the health benefits are estimated to be large enough for mRNA-1345 to provide good value for money for the Italian healthcare system.

Original abstract (English)
Background: Reliable and scalable molecular testing is essential for risk assessment and monitoring of respiratory viruses with epidemic or pandemic potential. The AveloMask Kit (Avelo) is a non-invasive sampling device that collects exhaled breath aerosols (XBA) using a high-performance filter, which can be released into a stabilizing buffer for nucleic acid preservation. Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background: Community pharmacists in France have been authorized to prescribe and administer influenzae vaccines since 2019, and of all vaccines on the immunization schedule for individuals aged 11 years and older in August 2023. A few studies have explored patients’ and pharmacists’ perceptions of vaccination in community pharmacies, but their contribution to vaccination schedule adherence under routine conditions remains limited in France. The aim of this study was to evaluate the implementation of pharmacy-based vaccination in the greater Paris Region (Île-de-France (IDF) region), France. Methods: We analysed the vaccines (excluding COVID-19 vaccines) prescribed and administered by healthcare professionals in the greater Paris Region (Ile de France, IDF) over a three-year period (2022-2024), using the database of the National Health Data System (Système National des Données de Santé, SNDS). Authors: Elizabeth M La, Jonathan Graham, Mei Grace, Eleftherios Zarkadoulas, Neha Shah
Original abstract (English)
OBJECTIVES: Adults aged ≥60 years and adults with certain health conditions are at increased risk for severe respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) disease, which can have a substantial clinical burden. Residents in long-term care settings often have multiple risk factors, including age ≥60 years, underlying health conditions, and/or frailty, compounding risk for severe RSV disease. This study evaluated the public health impact of adjuvanted RSV prefusion F3 (RSVPreF3) vaccination on averted RSV disease burden. DESIGN: Public health impact analysis using a static, multicohort Markov model. SETTING AND PARTICIPANTS: United States (US) adults aged ≥60 years residing in long-term care settings. METHODS: Adjuvanted RSVPreF3 vaccination vs no vaccination was simulated over 5 years using real-world RSV vaccination uptake data. Other model inputs have been previously validated and reported. Authors: Tanvir Ahmed, Kamaljeet Kaur, Elizabeth Myers, Selah Willis, Chase Crimmins, Yue Zhang, Jessica A. Plante, Kenneth Plante, Rachael M. Jones, Darrah K. Sleeth
Original abstract (English)
Airborne transmission of respiratory viruses in healthcare settings is difficult to assess, particularly with respect to the size and viability of virus-containing aerosols. Most field studies rely on molecular detection, which limits inference about the infectiousness of virus. In this study we collected size-resolved airborne samples in a dental clinic and hospital during the 2024-2025 respiratory virus season using a BC (BioCascade)-BioSpot VIVAS (Viable Virus Aerosol Sampler). We analyzed these samples by RT-PCR and culture-based assays for influenza A, influenza B, SARS-CoV-2, and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). Viral RNA was detected in 11.1% of the 81 sampling events, with the highest positivity (25%) observed in pulmonary unit patient rooms. When influenza and SARS-CoV-2 RNA were detected, all of these detections occurred in the 4-10 μm size fraction, whereas all RSV RNA was detected in the <1.5 μm size fraction. Cell culture assays confirmed the presence of viable influenza in the samples that were positive by RT-PCR. Plaques were only observed for influenza A viral positive samples at the ‘neat’ or undiluted rate, whereas RSV and SARS-CoV-2 did not produce cytopathic effects in the cell culture assays. These findings highlight virus-specific differences in aerosol size distributions and the challenges of recovering viable virus from field-collected bioaerosols in healthcare environments.

Abstract Background Hajj is one of the world's largest annual mass gatherings and provides favorable conditions for respiratory virus transmission because of intense crowding and extensive international travel. Contemporary post-pandemic molecular surveillance data remain limited. This study investigated the prevalence, determinants, and characteristics of respiratory virus detections among pilgrims during the 2026 Hajj season. Methods A cross-sectional study was conducted among adult domestic and international pilgrims over an extended peri-Hajj period (before, during and after Hajj) in the year 2026. Participants completed a structured questionnaire and provided combined nasopharyngeal and oropharyngeal swab specimens for multiplex real-time RT-PCR detection of common respiratory viruses. A subset of positive samples underwent genomic sequencing for viral subtype and variant characterization. Logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with laboratory-confirmed respiratory viral infection. Diabetes mellitus, chronic lung disease and smoking were independently associated with increased odds of respiratory virus detection. Conversely, receipt of pre-travel health advice, influenza vaccination and regular facemask use were independently associated with reduced odds of respiratory virus detection. Conclusions Respiratory virus transmission remains an important public health concern during Hajj. Strengthening pre-travel health advice, promoting influenza vaccination, and reinforcing personal preventive measures may reduce respiratory viral transmission during Hajj and other international mass gatherings.

A systematic review was conducted following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, searching four databases up to October 2025 for observational studies on RSV, PIV, and hMPV LRTI in adults undergoing chemotherapy for hematological malignancies; study selection, data extraction, and quality assessment were performed independently by two reviewers using the Newcastle‐Ottawa Scale, and a narrative synthesis was undertaken.
Consensus and conclusions
Twelve studies comprising 4,841 patients were included; mortality rates for RSV were comparable to those for influenza and PIV/hMPV (0–25% for RSV versus 0–16% for PIV, 1.4% for hMPV, and 2.2–33.3% for influenza); ICU admission rates varied but overlapped across all viruses, indicating no clear superiority of RSV in severity.
Controversies and gaps
Data on mechanical ventilation were scarce and absent for PIV and influenza; evidence for prolonged hospital stay was limited to a single small‐cohort study of PIV; overall heterogeneity in ICU admission rates and limited sample sizes highlight the need for more robust data.
Research and practice implications
Prospective studies are urgently required to validate the potential morbidity burden of RSV and to guide optimized management and resource allocation for patients with hematological malignancies.

Original abstract (English)
Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) remains a leading cause of lower respiratory tract infection and hospitalization in infancy worldwide. Historically, prevention relied on monthly palivizumab administration for selected high-risk infants, despite evidence that a substantial proportion of severe RSV disease occurs in previously healthy term infants. Original abstract (English)
Vaccination can reduce antimicrobial resistance (AMR) by preventing infections that trigger antibiotic treatment, lowering transmission of resistant vaccine-targeted strains and reducing population antibiotic selection pressure. This scoping review mapped global primary evidence published from 1999 to 2025 on vaccination, antibiotic use and microbiological resistance. The review followed Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) guidance and was reported using PRISMA-ScR and PRISMA-S. PubMed was searched in June 2026 for records published from 1999 to 2025; Europe PMC, WHO Global Index Medicus, Google Scholar and backward/forward citation searching were retained as supplementary discovery and verification routes. Comparative human primary studies were eligible when vaccination was linked to antibiotic prescribing, dispensing or consumption, or to phenotypic or genotypic resistance. A prespecified term-based prioritization step was followed by detailed title/abstract and full-record assessment. One reviewer conducted initial screening and a second author verified all inclusions and full-record exclusions. Data were charted by vaccine, population, design, outcome and effect estimate, and design-appropriate JBI domains informed interpretation. The PubMed strategy returned 7,433 records; nine additional PubMed-indexed studies were identified through citation chaining. Seventy-four primary studies were included: 35 addressed antibiotic use and 39 direct resistance-related outcomes. Influenza vaccination generally reduced respiratory antibiotic exposure, although null trials and seasonal heterogeneity prevented a universal effect. Pneumococcal conjugate vaccines produced modest reductions in all-cause use, larger syndrome-specific reductions and marked declines in resistant vaccine-type disease. Quantitative examples included 8% vaccine effectiveness against outpatient antimicrobial purchases, a 14.7% indirect reduction in otitis-related antimicrobial purchases and declines in nonsusceptible invasive pneumococcal disease from 43.9 to 3.2 per 100,000 children aged < 5 years in long-term US surveillance. An investigational maternal respiratory syncytial virus vaccine reduced infant antimicrobial prescribing by 12.9%; rotavirus, COVID-19 and typhoid studies extended the pathway beyond influenza and pneumococcus. Serotype replacement and ecological confounding limited inference for total AMR burden. Vaccination is an upstream component of antimicrobial stewardship, with the strongest evidence for reduced influenza-associated prescribing and resistant vaccine-type pneumococcal disease. Vaccine programmes should be integrated with diagnostic and prescribing stewardship and sustained serotype, genomic and antimicrobial-consumption surveillance.
