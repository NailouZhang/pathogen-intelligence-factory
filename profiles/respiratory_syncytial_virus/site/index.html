Subtype-specific differences in susceptibility to monoclonal antibodies and vaccines among contemporary RSV-A and RSV-B isolates

Authors: Paula Eillanny Silva Marinho, Dibya Ghimire, João Vitor Tsai Silva, Jamie Devlin, SunYoung Kwon, Gordon C. Adams, Patricia A. Jorquera, Jeremy Luban, Jacob E. Lemieux
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) continues to circulate at high levels despite the introduction of new monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) and vaccines targeting the pre-fusion F (pre-F) protein. We analyzed the viral genome sequences of 133 RSV clinical samples collected during the 2022–2023 and 2023–2024 seasons and selected representative isolates for phenotypic testing. We selected four RSV-A and four RSV-B replication-competent, sequence-verified stocks and then assessed them for replication kinetics in vitro and neutralization sensitivity with a panel of F-targeting mAbs and polyclonal sera using a rabbit vaccination model. Isolates retained sensitivity to all mAbs tested. We detected no mutations in mAb binding sites in the isolates tested. RSV-A isolates were slightly less susceptible to multiple mAbs than RSV-B isolates. Antigenic cartography revealed a separation of antibody responses by subtype: RSV-A isolates clustered together and aligned with lower neutralization by antibodies such as MPE8 and 101F, whereas RSV-B isolates formed a distinct cluster associated with higher mAb susceptibility. In a rabbit vaccination model, RSV-A-only sera efficiently neutralized all RSV-A isolates and the RSV-B1 reference strain but showed diminished activity against contemporary RSV-B isolates. RSV-B-only sera displayed balanced neutralization across both subtypes. Combined RSV-A + RSV-B immunization produced uniformly strong responses to all isolates, suggesting that multivalent exposure may overcome subtype-specific antibody polarization. Collectively, our results demonstrate consistent antigenic divergence of RSV subtypes and underscore the importance of using contemporary isolates, rather than legacy reference strains, to guide F-directed immunoprophylaxis and vaccine design. IMPORTANCE Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) remains a major cause of lower respiratory tract infections. Although new monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) and vaccines have been approved in recent years, the impact of circulating genetic diversity on these therapeutics is incompletely understood. In this study, we demonstrate how recent RSV isolates respond to a panel of mAbs and whether sera from rabbits vaccinated with mRNA vaccines effectively neutralize these isolates. We observe that RSV-A isolates are slightly more resistant to neutralization by mAbs than RSV-B isolates and that a bivalent vaccine elicits broader neutralizing responses than the monovalent vaccine. This study provides insights into how viral diversity may influence antibody-mediated protection and suggests that recent RSV-B isolates may respond differently from the laboratory-adapted strains commonly used in research, an important consideration for the design of next-generation vaccines and antibody therapeutics.

Guillain-Barré syndrome temporally associated with respiratory syncytial virus vaccination

Authors: Saeed Hreiz, Mohamad Abu Zaher, Varghese-Kurien Thyparambil, Katherine Bettany
Original abstract (English)
An older woman developed acute progressive limb weakness associated with pain, paraesthesia and falls 2 weeks after receiving a respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) vaccine. Initial examination and imaging suggested alternative diagnoses, including cervical spondylotic disease and cerebrovascular pathology and early neurophysiology was non-diagnostic. During admission, she deteriorated with urinary retention, areflexia and type 2 respiratory failure requiring intubation and ventilation and was commenced on intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG). Cerebrospinal fluid analysis later demonstrated albumin-cytologic dissociation and repeat nerve conduction studies confirmed an acute inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy consistent with Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS). She improved following intensive care and IVIG, with partial recovery of limb strength and functional status and was discharged to a rehabilitation facility. This case illustrates a rare but important possible association between RSV vaccination and GBS; highlights the diagnostic challenges of early presentations with non-specific symptoms and inconclusive investigations; and emphasises the need for serial assessment and early escalation of care to optimise patient outcomes.

Question and background
This study aimed to investigate a potential association between respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) vaccination and Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS), highlighting the need to understand rare adverse events following immunization.
Design and population
A case report describes an older woman who developed acute progressive limb weakness and other neurological symptoms 2 weeks after receiving an RSV vaccine, with initial investigations failing to provide a definitive diagnosis.
Core methods
The patient underwent initial examination, imaging, and neurophysiology tests, which were inconclusive, and later cerebrospinal fluid analysis and repeat nerve conduction studies were performed to confirm the diagnosis of GBS.
Main results
Cerebrospinal fluid analysis revealed albumin-cytologic dissociation, and repeat nerve conduction studies confirmed acute inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy consistent with GBS.
Interpretation and novelty
The case suggests a possible link between RSV vaccination and GBS, although no definitive causal relationship was established, and it underscores the diagnostic challenges in early presentations of GBS.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings highlight the importance of monitoring for rare neurological complications following RSV vaccination and the need for serial assessments to improve patient outcomes.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited to a single case report, which may not be representative of broader trends, and the evidence is considered moderate due to the lack of controlled comparisons.

Authors: Ardalan Maleki Chegeni, Zahra Heydarifard, Gholam Reza Talei, Mohsen Mohammadi, Sayyad Khanizadeh
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT Background and Aims Pathogenic viruses represent a principal etiology of acute respiratory tract disease. Concurrent detection of multiple respiratory pathogens has been associated with diagnostic challenges, therapeutic complications, and unfavorable outcomes in patients with COVID‐19. This investigation aimed to characterize the frequency of concurrent SARS‐CoV‐2, influenza virus, and human respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) infections among individuals presenting with suspected COVID‐19 symptoms during the pandemic period in Lorestan Province, Iran. Methods A retrospective observational study was conducted among 196 participants who presented with suspected COVID‐19 and clinically compatible respiratory symptoms at regional healthcare facilities in Lorestan Province from July through December 2022. Nasopharyngeal specimens were obtained from all participants, maintained at 2°C–8°C during transit on ice, and subsequently subjected to nucleic acid extraction and multiplex quantitative reverse‐transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT‐PCR) analysis at the Molecular Virology Laboratory. Results Of 196 participants evaluated, positivity rates were as follows: SARS‐CoV‐2, 40.81%; influenza virus, 30.10%; and RSV, 5.10%. Dual infections were identified in the following proportions: SARS‐CoV‐2 with RSV 3.57%, SARS‐CoV‐2 with influenza virus 11.22%, and RSV with influenza 3.06%. Simultaneous detection of all three pathogens was documented in 2.04% of the study population. Conclusion This study documents the circulation of SARS‐CoV‐2, influenza virus, and RSV among symptomatic outpatients in Lorestan Province during the study period, including a limited number of dual and triple coinfections. These findings highlight the importance of continued respiratory virus surveillance and multiplex molecular testing in outpatient settings. Nevertheless, the small numbers of RSV‐positive patients, triple‐infection cases, and children younger than 6 years limit the strength of subgroup‐based inferences; therefore, age‐specific, pediatric, and symptom‐related findings should be interpreted cautiously. Further studies with larger and more diverse populations are warranted.

Authors: Karan Thakkar, Ali Shajarizadeh, Nishu Gaind, Luka Ivkovic, Gopika Balasubramanian, Rengina Kefalogianni, Jessie Zhang, Kay Choong See, Andrea Lay-Hoon Kwa, Diana Mendes
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a common virus causing severe illness in adults. RSV prefusion F protein subunit (RSVpreF) vaccine was approved by the Singapore Health Sciences Authority for adults aged 18-59 years at increased risk and for adults aged ≥ 60 years, to prevent RSV-associated lower respiratory tract disease. This study estimated the clinical and economic impact of RSVpreF vaccination for the prevention of RSV among adults in Singapore. RESEARCH DESIGN AND METHODS: A Markov model projected clinical and economic outcomes of RSV for the vaccination of adults with RSVpreF compared to no intervention. Analyses were conducted from the healthcare system perspective, with direct costs (2025 Singapore dollars [S$]) and outcomes discounted at 3% annually; scenario/sensitivity analyses tested model robustness. RESULTS: Compared to no intervention, RSVpreF vaccination would prevent 4,675 hospitalizations, 14,064 emergency department cases, and 37,631 outpatient visits over lifetime, averting S$31.92 million in direct medical costs with a gain of 2,675 quality-adjusted life years (QALYs). The RSVpreF vaccine would be cost-effective up to S$440.79/dose under a threshold of 1 × gross domestic product per capita per QALY gained (S$122,370). CONCLUSIONS: RSVpreF vaccination would substantially reduce the clinical and economic burden of RSV among adults in Singapore.

Original abstract (English)
INTRODUCTION: Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) causes significant disease in older and comorbid adults. Current UK vaccination recommendations restrict eligibility to adults ≥ 75 years, 65-74 years with chronic respiratory disease or immunosuppression, and those in care homes, but evidence on clinical burden in adults < 75 years with comorbidities is limited. This study assessed patient characteristics, healthcare resource utilization (HCRU), and mortality in adults hospitalized with RSV in England. METHODS: Population-based retrospective cohort study using linked Clinical Practice Research Datalink Aurum and Hospital Episode Statistics. Adults ≥ 60 years hospitalized with RSV between October 2014 and March 2019 were included. We defined RSV episodes as 90 days post-diagnosis. All-cause HCRU (hospitalizations, critical care admissions, outpatient attendance, primary care consultations, and prescriptions) and case fatality rates were assessed. Results were stratified by age (60-74 and ≥ 75 years), case definitions (RSV-specific, RSV-possible), and comorbidity profiles (chronic respiratory disease, immunocompromised, cardiovascular disease). RESULTS: A total of 97,712 hospitalized episodes in those aged ≥ 60 years were included in the analysis, of which 785 (0.8%) were RSV-specific cases (n = 338 aged 60-74 years, n = 447 aged ≥ 75 years). Critical care admissions were more often observed across comorbidity stratifications (13.85-22.34%) compared to those ≥ 75 years (5.03%). All-cause and RSV-related case fatality rates for RSV-specific cases were highest among those ≥ 75 years (all-cause: 19.3%; RSV-related: 10.3%). CONCLUSIONS: HCRU within RSV episodes in those aged 60-74 years across comorbidity profiles was equal to or greater than that in those aged ≥ 75 years. These findings highlight the need to prioritize consideration of comorbidity groups that could benefit from RSV vaccination.

Current UK vaccination recommendations restrict eligibility to adults ≥ 75 years, 65-74 years with chronic respiratory disease or immunosuppression, and those in care homes, but evidence on clinical burden in adults < 75 years with comorbidities is limited.
Design and population
This study assessed patient characteristics, healthcare resource utilization (HCRU), and mortality in adults hospitalized with RSV in England. Adults ≥ 60 years hospitalized with RSV between October 2014 and March 2019 were included.
Core methods
Population-based retrospective cohort study using linked Clinical Practice Research Datalink Aurum and Hospital Episode Statistics. All-cause HCRU (hospitalizations, critical care admissions, outpatient attendance, primary care consultations, and prescriptions) and case fatality rates were assessed.
Main results
A total of 97,712 hospitalized episodes in those aged ≥ 60 years were included in the analysis, of which 785 (0.8%) were RSV-specific cases (n = 338 aged 60-74 years, n = 447 aged ≥ 75 years). Critical care admissions were more often observed across comorbidity stratifications (13.85-22.34%) compared to those ≥ 75 years (5.03%). All-cause and RSV-related case fatality rates for RSV-specific cases were highest among those ≥ 75 years (all-cause: 19.3%; RSV-related: 10.3%).
Interpretation and novelty
HCRU within RSV episodes in those aged 60-74 years across comorbidity profiles was equal to or greater than that in those aged ≥ 75 years. These findings highlight the need to prioritize consideration of comorbidity groups that could benefit from RSV vaccination.
Scientific and public-health significance
The results provide critical insights into the clinical burden of RSV in older adults with comorbidities, which can inform targeted vaccination strategies.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is a retrospective cohort study, which may be subject to biases and confounding factors. The evidence is moderate in strength due to the observational nature of the study and potential limitations in data linkage and completeness.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background: Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and adenovirus are important causes of acute respiratory tract infections in children, but their clinical presentations often overlap with those of other viral and bacterial pathogens. Although the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is the reference standard for diagnosis, rapid antigen tests may provide clinically useful results in emergency settings. This study evaluated the diagnostic accuracy of the RapidForTM RSV/ADV Rapid Test Kit compared with multiplex PCR in pediatric patients with acute respiratory symptoms. Methods: This prospective diagnostic accuracy study included 302 children aged ≤ 18 years who presented to a pediatric emergency department with acute respiratory symptoms from February to May 2025. Comparative Outcomes of Influenza, SARS-CoV-2, and Respiratory Syncytial Virus Community-Acquired Pneumonia: Insights from an Italian Retrospective Cohort Study

Authors: Margherita S. Silani, Anna Stainer, Massimiliano Russo, Antonio Desai, Sofia Giuriolo, Aniello Fabio Vitale, Vincenzo Alberto Artuso, Angela Tramontano, Francesco Amati, Beatrice Clemente
Original abstract (English)
Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Viral pathogens, particularly influenza, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and SARS-CoV-2, are increasingly recognized as key contributors to CAP in hospitalized adults. This single-center, retrospective, cohort study examined and compared the clinical characteristics and outcomes of adults hospitalized with viral CAP due to influenza, SARS-CoV-2, or RSV between January 2022 and December 2023 in Milan, Italy. A total of 165 patients with laboratory-confirmed single-virus infection and clinically and radiologically confirmed pneumonia were included. Patients with SARS-CoV-2 pneumonia presented with the highest in-hospital (20.9%) and 12-month all-cause mortality (51.9%). Influenza patients, although younger, showed increased rates of cardiovascular events and ICU admissions. Bacterial coinfections were most common in influenza cases. Immunocompromised status was the second most frequent comorbidity (37.6%) after arterial hypertension. These findings underscore significant differences in clinical trajectory and long-term outcomes among major viral CAP etiologies and support the need for pathogen-specific management strategies.

The study aimed to address the need for understanding the clinical characteristics and outcomes of viral community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) due to influenza, SARS-CoV-2, and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) in hospitalized adults, given their increasing recognition as key contributors to CAP.
Design and population
A single-center, retrospective, cohort study was conducted in Milan, Italy, from January 2022 to December 2023, examining 165 adults with laboratory-confirmed single-virus infection and clinically and radiologically confirmed pneumonia.
Core methods
The study involved the comparison of clinical characteristics and outcomes of patients with influenza, SARS-CoV-2, or RSV CAP, including in-hospital and 12-month all-cause mortality rates, ICU admissions, cardiovascular events, and bacterial coinfections.

Clinical, Laboratory, and Epidemiological Characteristics of Children Hospitalized for RSV Infection: A Single-center Retrospective Descriptive Cohort Study

Authors: Mustafa Gençeli, Sena Nur Kekeç, Abdulkerim Çokbiçer, Methiye Doğan, Uğur Saraç, Hatice Uzdur, Gonca Başak Soran, Zeynep Sena Gürsoy, Erkam Başpınar, Sanaa Albakkar
Original abstract (English)
Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is one of the most common causes of lower respiratory tract infections in childhood and may be associated with substantial morbidity and mortality, particularly during infancy. This study aimed to describe the demographic, clinical, laboratory, treatment, and outcome characteristics of children hospitalized with laboratory-confirmed RSV infection at a single center in 2024. This single-center, retrospective, descriptive cohort study included 57 patients aged 0-18 years who tested positive for RSV and were hospitalized in 2024. Among the 57 patients included in the study, 52.6% were female, and the median age was 5.0 months (interquartile range: 3.0-13.0). Infants aged 0-3 months constituted the largest age group, accounting for 33.3% of the cohort. Among the 48 patients with available exposure data, household tobacco smoke exposure was documented in 39 (81.3% of those 48); exposure status was unknown for the remaining 9 of the 57 patients. A history of preterm birth was present in 19.3%, chronic disease in 3.5%, and palivizumab prophylaxis in 1.8%. RSV-A was the predominant subtype detected in 73.7% of patients. The most common presenting symptoms were cough (98.2%), fever (71.9%), and respiratory distress (71.9%). Oxygen support was required in 78.9% of patients, with high-flow nasal cannula oxygen therapy being the most frequently used modality (42.1%). Mechanical ventilation and admission to the intensive care unit were observed in 3.5% of cases. Antibiotic therapy was administered to 40.4% of patients. Fifty-six patients (98.2%) were discharged alive; one died. In this single-center hospital cohort, most patients were young infants, and oxygen support was frequently used. Although tobacco smoke exposure was frequently documented, its association with risk could not be assessed because no control group was included.

Airway epithelium and respiratory syncytial virus - effect of developmental age on responses to infection

Authors: Mary McCabe
Original abstract (English)
Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) remains a significant global health concern, particularly among vulnerable populations, such as infants and older adults, who experience the greatest burden of severe disease. Despite advances in vaccine development and monoclonal antibody prophylaxis, understanding the immune determinants underlying severe RSV disease remains incomplete. The disease is primarily driven by a dysregulated host immune response, yet the immune mechanisms involved, particularly in early-life, are still poorly understood. In this thesis, we focused on the role of innate immune responses in RSV infection, particularly during early life. Using well-differentiated paediatric nasal epithelial cell (WD-PNEC) cultures from newborns and one-year-olds, we demonstrated that innate immune responses to RSV become more robust with age, with significant differences in cytokine secretion and interferon-stimulated gene (ISG) expression between newborns and the same children at one-year-old. This work highlights the crucial role of the airway epithelium in the early immune response to RSV, which is a key factor in determining disease severity. Notably, interferon epsilon (IFNε), a lesser-characterized type I IFN, is expressed at lower levels in newborns, which may contribute to their increased susceptibility to severe disease. Further investigation of interferon-stimulated gene 15 (ISG15), an immune modulator highly expressed during RSV infection, revealed that although ISG15 knockout modestly reduced RSV spread, it did not significantly impact infectious viral release. These findings suggested that ISG15 may not be directly antiviral but could influence infection outcomes through modulation of interferon signalling. This work provides critical insights into the complex interplay of immune responses during RSV infection, emphasizing the importance of early-life immunity and offering potential avenues for therapeutic interventions aimed at enhancing innate immune responses to reduce disease severity. 新闻简报英国卫生安全局于2026年10月1日发布了一份官方统计数据报告,详细说明了截至2026年5月英格兰的母体呼吸道合胞病毒 疫苗接种 覆盖率。报告指出,2026年5月分娩的孕妇中有62.2%接受了呼吸道合胞病毒 疫苗 接种,存在地区差异。最高接种率出现在西南地区,为72.3%,而伦敦的接种率最低,为54.8%。综合照护委员会(ICBs)之间也存在显著差异,诺福克和韦内地区报告接种率为76.3%,而伯明翰和索利胡尔地区仅为41.1%。在种族群体方面,亚洲或亚洲英国-中国群体的接种率最高,为78.5%,而黑人-加勒比群体的接种率最低,为39.2%。数据来自英格兰98.7%的全科医生(GPs),反映了自2025年1月起数据质量的提升,使得对疫苗接种率的分析更具代表性。该报告作为针对婴儿和老年人呼吸道合胞病毒相关疾病的国家免疫计划的监测工具。

News Brief
The UK Health Security Agency released an official statistics report on 1 October 2026 detailing the maternal RSV vaccination coverage in England as of May 2026. The report highlights that 62.2% of pregnant women who delivered in May 2026 received the RSV vaccine, with regional variations observed. The highest uptake was recorded in the South West region at 72.3%, while London had the lowest at 54.8%. Integrated care boards (ICBs) also showed significant differences, with Norfolk and Waveney reporting 76.3% uptake and Birmingham and Solihull at 41.1%. Ethnic group disparities were noted, with the Asian or Asian British - Chinese group having the highest uptake at 78.5% and the Black - Caribbean group the lowest at 39.2%. The data, collected from 98.7% of GPs in England, reflects improvements in data quality since January 2025, allowing for a more representative analysis of vaccine coverage. The report serves as a monitoring tool for the national immunisation programme targeting RSV-related illnesses in infants and older adults.

Analysis of maternal RSV vaccine uptake Maternal uptake of the RSV vaccine is assessed monthly to monitor the reach and impact of the national immunisation programme. Given the programme’s design, uptake is defined as the proportion of pregnant women who delivered in the reporting month and had received the RSV vaccine during pregnancy. This metric serves as a proxy for vaccine coverage by infant birth month, reflecting the extent of passive immunity conferred to newborns. This monthly report presents RSV vaccine uptake for women who delivered in the twenty-first month of the RSV vaccination programme in May 2026. The monthly uptake trend among pregnant women who have delivered since the programme’s launch is presented in Figure 1. Overall, the monthly RSV maternal vaccine uptake for May 2026 was 62.2%. RSV uptake increased in the first few months of the programme and has remained steady since October 2025. Figure 1. Since the RSV maternal vaccination programme has now been running for 21 months (up to the reporting month), an increasing number of pregnant women are receiving the vaccine during their eligible window. This growing awareness and notification from gestation week 20 onwards have contributed to a gradual increase in monthly vaccine uptake from 40.5% in September 2024 to the current figure reported for May 2026. As the programme continues and more women are informed earlier in their pregnancies, monthly vaccination uptake is expected to gradually increase. Data sources and methodology Further details on the data sources, methodology, data quality, and limitations are available in the quality and methodology information (QMI) report. The maternal RSV vaccine coverage estimates are calculated using monthly GP practice data automatically submitted to ImmForm by participating GP IT suppliers and validated and analysed by the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA). The report covers women who delivered during the reporting month (1 May 2026 to 31 May 2026), excluding miscarriages and stillbirths, with uptake measured as receipt of RSV vaccination from 28 weeks of pregnancy up to delivery. To ensure complete and accurate recording of vaccination and delivery information, data is extracted using a three-month reporting lag. The May 2026 estimates are based on data from the two major GP IT suppliers, representing 98.7% of participating GP practices nationally. The June 2026 maternal RSV vaccination uptake report is scheduled to be released on 5 November 2026. Background information The RSV vaccine has been offered to pregnant women in England since September 2024 to address the significant burden of RSV-related illness, hospitalisations and deaths, particularly among infants under 6 months of age and older adults, who are at increased risk of lower respiratory tract infection (LRTI) (see the Green Book on immunisation). In 2023, the Joint Committee on Vaccination and Immunisation (JCVI) issued a full statement recommending the implementation of a maternal RSV vaccination programme. This recommendation was based on robust evidence demonstrating the safety and effectiveness of RSV vaccines in protecting infants during their most vulnerable period. The vaccine is recommended as a year-round programme to be administered from 28 weeks of gestation onwards. This timing aligns with the optimal window for passive antibody transfer to the foetus, ensuring effective protection against severe RSV-related illness. From 1 September 2024 (the programme start date), all women who are at least 28 weeks pregnant are advised to receive the vaccine as soon as possible, with vaccination continuing to be recommended for all eligible women throughout pregnancy up until delivery. Additionally, the vaccine will be reoffered for every subsequent pregnancy to maintain consistent coverage and protection. This monthly report includes vaccine uptake data for women who delivered in May 2026. Programme delivery The programme is delivered through GP and maternity services, ensuring accessible vaccination opportunities for eligible pregnant women across England. In addition, the RSV vaccine is available through the community pharmacy delivery model at approximately 37 sites across two ICBs in the East of England. It is also available at up to 200 additional community pharmacy sites across the Midlands, North West, and London commissioning regions during the 2025 to 2026 expansion phase.
