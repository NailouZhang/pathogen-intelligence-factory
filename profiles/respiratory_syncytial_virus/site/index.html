Enhanced respiratory syncytial virus surveillance of young children admitted to the Sydney Children’s Hospitals Network, 2019–2023
作者： Gemma L. Saravanos, Kathryn Meredith, Shirley Wong, Rebecca Burrell, Adam S. Bartlett, Nusrat Homaira, Philip N. Britton, Rama Kandasamy

问题与背景
本研究旨在全面描述被送往悉尼儿童医院网络的呼吸道合胞病毒(RSV)疾病患儿的临床特征、管理及预后,确定严重疾病的总体负担,并识别特定人群进入重症监护病房(ICU)的风险因素。
设计与对象
本研究是一项在澳大利亚新南威尔士州首府地区两家三级儿科医院进行的多中心、回顾性队列研究研究,历时5年,涉及4414名<2岁因实验室确诊RSV感染而住院的儿童,包括所有ICU住院患儿以及非ICU住院患儿的随机样本。
核心方法
本研究收集了<2岁儿童RSV住院和ICU住院的数据,主要结局指标为RSV住院和ICU住院的特征及发病率率。通过多变量逻辑回归分析确定了与ICU住院相关的因素。

在5年期间,共有11.3%(499/4414)的<2岁儿童因实验室确诊的RSV感染住院被转入ICU。ICU 队列研究患儿的中位年龄为3.3个月,而非ICU样本的中位年龄为7.1个月。

作者将研究结果解释为表明RSV疫苗接种计划预计会显著减轻澳大利亚儿童在出生第一年内的住院负担。
科研与公卫意义
这些发现可以为高风险群体(包括原住民儿童、早产儿和患有心脏疾病的儿童)制定针对性的预防性计划提供依据。
局限与证据强度
由于研究是在政府资助的RSV儿童疫苗接种计划实施之前进行的,因此可能限制其预测此类计划影响的能力。由于研究设计为回顾性,且存在未测量的混杂因素,证据强度中等。

Enhanced respiratory syncytial virus surveillance of young children admitted to the Sydney Children’s Hospitals Network, 2019–2023

Authors: Gemma L. Saravanos, Kathryn Meredith, Shirley Wong, Rebecca Burrell, Adam S. Bartlett, Nusrat Homaira, Philip N. Britton, Rama Kandasamy
Original abstract (English)
OBJECTIVES: In this study, we aimed to comprehensively describe the clinical characteristics, management and outcomes of children with respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) disease admitted to the Sydney Children's Hospitals Network (SCHN), determine the full burden of severe disease and identify population-specific risk factors for intensive care unit (ICU) admission. STUDY TYPE: Multi-centre, retrospective cohort study conducted over 5 years. METHODS: The study was conducted at two tertiary paediatric hospitals in metropolitan New South Wales, Australia, before implementation of government-funded immunisation programs against RSV in children. Participants were children aged <2 years hospitalised with laboratory-confirmed RSV from 1 January 2019 to 31 December 2023, comprising all ICU admitted, and a random sample of non-ICU admitted patients. The main outcome measures were characteristics and incidence rate of RSV hospitalisations and ICU admissions among children aged <2 years. RESULTS: Over a 5-year period, 4414 children were hospitalised with laboratory-confirmed RSV, of whom 11.3% (499/4414) were admitted to ICU. The median age was younger in the ICU cohort (n = 499) compared with the non-ICU sample (n = 1000; 3.3 vs 7.1 months, P < 0.0001). Factors associated with higher odds of ICU admission in multivariate logistic regression were decreasing month of age (OR 1.10, 95% CI 1.06-1.11), prematurity (OR 2.17, 95% CI 1.61-2.91), Indigenous identity (OR 4.10, 95% CI 2.30-7.56) and cardiac disease (OR 3.39, 95% CI 2.06-5.70). The average hospitalisation incidence rate was 1615 per 100,000 (95% CI 1565-1666), with higher rates among children aged <1 year, 2141 per 100,000 (95% CI 2061-2224) compared with those aged 1-2 years, 1088 per 100,000 (95% CI 1030-1148). CONCLUSIONS: RSV immunisation programs are expected to substantially reduce the burden of hospitalisation among Australian children in their first year of life. Children who are Indigenous, were born prematurely or have heart disease are at greatest risk of severe disease and may benefit from extended preventative programs.

Pulmonary ILC2 Priming as a Link Between Severe Early-Life RSV Bronchiolitis and Asthma

Authors: An V. Q. Pham, Afia Kwayie, Anh T. Q. Phan, Kent A. Willis, Diego R. Hijano, Emma Jurkovac, Stephania A. Cormier, Luan D. Vu
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Severe respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) bronchiolitis in infancy is associated with increased childhood asthma risk, but the mechanisms that sustain airway vulnerability remain unresolved. Airway samples from infants with severe RSV disease and experimental models show robust IL-33 responses and pulmonary group 2 innate lymphoid cell (ILC2) activation during acute infection. Additionally, neonatal IL-33 and allergen-rechallenge studies demonstrate that ILC2s can persist and acquire memory-like properties. These observations support, but do not establish, a model in which severe lower-airway RSV disease primes ILC2s or their tissue niche for exaggerated responses to later allergens. Beyond limited interferon control and prolonged epithelial injury, which may amplify IL-33/ST2 signaling during a developmental window when long-lived lung ILC2 populations are established, we focus on additional candidate mechanisms that could sustain this priming. These include metabolic pathways governing glycolysis, lipid utilization, iron uptake, and mitochondrial function; epigenetic mechanisms involving chromatin remodeling and histone or DNA modifications; and neuroimmune signals that regulate ILC2 activation and recall. Direct evidence that RSV induces durable, cell-intrinsic metabolic, epigenetic, or neuroimmune reprogramming of infant pulmonary ILC2s is currently lacking. Longitudinal ILC2 profiling and adoptive transfer of RSV-experienced versus control ILC2s are needed to test this model. Maternal vaccination and long-acting monoclonal antibodies now provide an opportunity to determine whether preventing severe early-life RSV disease alters airway immune programming and subsequent asthma risk.

We investigated an RSV-B outbreak among 239 children aged <5 years in Dibrugarh, Assam, during November-December 2022. RSV-B was detected in 67 (28.0%) samples by real-time RT-PCR. These findings provide evidence of RSV-B lineage circulation in Northeast India.

Recent advances enable layered prevention through maternal vaccination for infant protection, long-acting monoclonal antibodies for newborns and select toddlers, and vaccines for older adults. However, inequities in surveillance, seasonality-driven timing, and challenges in affordability and delivery—particularly in low- and middle-income countries— impede equitable uptake. This editorial underscores priority populations, synthesizes current evidence and policy directions, and outlines actionable levers in financing, procurement, and integrated maternal–child health delivery to accelerate access, making RSV prevention an urgent and achievable global health priority.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Lung transplant (LT) recipients are at high risk for severe respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) infection, but predictors of worse outcomes remain poorly defined. Methods Retrospective multicenter cohort study of adult LT recipients hospitalized with PCR-confirmed RSV infection between January 2016 and December 2019. Clinical characteristics, treatments, and outcomes within 90 days were analyzed. Logistic regression was used to identify predictors of outcomes, while Cox proportional hazards models assessed risk factors for mortality. Results 155 LT recipients were included. Median age was 61 years, RSV occurred a median of 14 months post-transplant. Risk factors for hypoxic respiratory failure were baseline oxygen use [OR 4.8, (1.29–23.93)] and lower respiratory tract symptoms [OR 3.74, (1.49–10.18)]. Ninety-day all-cause mortality was 7.7%, with mechanical ventilation independently associated with death [HR 7.79, (2.47–24.60)]. Conclusions RSV infection in LT recipients is associated with high morbidity, graft function decline, and mortality. The association between older age and severe outcomes suggests the importance of preventive strategies in this high-risk population.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a highly prevalent respiratory virus in children, and there is no effective antiviral drug therapy or vaccine available for the infant population. Monocytes are pivotal cells that produce cytokines that influence the inflammatory response. Since monocyte responses can be regulated by β2-adrenergic receptor (β2AR) agonists, the aim of this study was to examine the effect of salmeterol, a long-acting β2-adrenergic (LABA) agonist, on the activation of monocytes during RSV infection. Here, we evaluated the effects of increasing concentrations of salmeterol (1 or 10 μM) on THP-1 cell viability, viral replication, and cellular responses, including nitric oxide, TNF-α and IL-1β production, as well as p38 MAPK pathway activation by Western blot analysis following RSV infection. Results revealed that salmeterol treatment was not cytotoxic and effectively reduced RSV-induced NO production, although it had no effect on RSV replication. Additional experiments demonstrated that salmeterol could downregulate the proinflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-1β in RSV-infected THP-1 monocytes. Furthermore, these effects appeared to be related to the downregulation of phosphorylated p38 MAPK, which was significantly increased during RSV infection. Overall, these findings suggest that salmeterol could be considered an anti-inflammatory adjuvant in RSV-induced inflammatory responses.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract Introduction Quilombola communities, composed of descendants of enslaved individuals from Brazil’s colonial period, often face limited access to healthcare, which may increase their susceptibility to viral infections. Oral transmission routes, particularly through saliva, are relevant for viruses such as those in the Polyomaviridae family. Therefore, here we aimed to investigate the prevalence of HSV-1, JC (JCV), and BK polyomaviruses, and respiratory viruses (Influenza A/B, RSV, SARS-CoV-2), as well as the salivary inflammatory profile in individuals from a Quilombola community. Methods A cross-sectional study was conducted in the Quilombola community of Mangabeira, Pará, Brazil. Unstimulated saliva samples were collected from 100 individuals. BKPyV infection was associated with elevated IFN-γ ( p < 0.01). SARS-CoV-2 and HSV-1 infections were linked to pro-inflammatory cytokine profiles. No association was found between viral detection and tooth loss or the use of prosthesis. Conclusion A high prevalence of BKPyV and a salivary pro-inflammatory response were observed, highlighting potential oral health vulnerabilities in this underserved population.

Original abstract (English)

After European Medicine Agency approval of nirsevimab, a monoclonal antibody against RSV, immunization campaigns have been implemented in several European Union countries for administration to children as preventive intervention against severe RSV disease during RSV epidemic season. Monitoring its safety in routine healthcare practice is also important. We described suspected adverse drug reactions (ADRs) following nirsevimab administration in children aged 0-24 months, reported in the EudraVigilance system between 2023 and 2025. There were 1,923 ADRs, corresponding to 808 cases. In addition, pyrexia, rash, and injection site reactions were also common. This study offered systematic characterization of ADRs following administration of nirsevimab. It found that the reported ADRs are consistent with the safety results from pre- and real-world safety studies. Our study can support the continue monitoring of the real-world safety and guide the planning of analytical observational studies on the safety profile of nirsevimab.

Question and background
This study aimed to evaluate the feasibility and safety of a short IV antibiotic course (≤ 4 days) in neonates with UTI. The background highlights that UTIs are a leading cause of hospitalization in infants under 60 days, with approximately 10–20% of febrile episodes attributed to them.
Design and population
A retrospective single-center cohort study was conducted at the university Trousseau Hospital, including neonates < 28 days hospitalized for culture-proven UTI. The cohort comprised 138 patients, with 82% male and a median age of 15.5 days, stratified as low-risk or high-risk based on clinical severity, bacteremia, pyelo-ureteral dilatation, or ESBL infection.
Core methods
Demographic, clinical, laboratory, imaging, and treatment data were collected. Clinical severity was assessed based on signs of peripheral hypoperfusion, need for fluid resuscitation, or admission to intensive care. Statistical analysis included descriptive statistics for quantitative variables and the Shapiro-Wilk test for normality of distribution.
Main results
A total of 138 patients were included; 82% were male, with a median age of 15.5 days. Of these, 97 (71%) were classified as low-risk, and 95 (98%) received a short IV antibiotic course. Fever was present in 70% of patients, with 17% febrile for more than 12 hours before admission. Elevated procalcitonin (> 0.5 μg/L) was observed in 43% of patients, including 37% without risk factors for severe infection.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors concluded that short-course IV antibiotic therapy followed by oral treatment appears safe and effective for carefully selected neonates under 28 days with UTI and low risk factors for severe infection. This finding supports a more tailored approach to antibiotic duration in this age group.
Scientific and public-health significance
These findings suggest that a more tailored approach to antibiotic duration in neonates with UTI could reduce unnecessary treatment, potentially improving antibiotic stewardship and reducing healthcare costs.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is retrospective and single-center, which may limit generalizability. The evidence is moderate, as the findings are based on observational data and require prospective multicenter validation.

From Evidence to Implementation: The Added Value of Health Economics for RSV Immunisation Implementation in Low- and Middle-Income Countries

Authors: Neele Rave, Infection & Immunity, Infectieziekten onderzoek2 (Wildenbeest), Louis Bont, Joanne Wildenbeest
Original abstract (English)
Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a leading cause of acute lower respiratory infections in young children. In 2019, RSV was estimated to cause approximately 33 million acute lower respiratory infections, 3.6 million hospitalisations and more than 101,000 deaths among children under five years of age, with more than 97% of deaths occurring in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). The recent availability of a maternal RSV vaccine and long-acting monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) provides new opportunities to prevent severe disease during early infancy. This thesis investigates how epidemiological and health economic evidence can support the implementation of RSV immunisation strategies, with particular emphasis on LMICs. Epidemiological studies first examined RSV burden, seasonality and the age distribution of severe disease. In the Netherlands, RSV circulation was substantially disrupted during the COVID-19 pandemic, including an unusual summer epidemic in 2021, but subsequently returned towards its pre-pandemic winter pattern. Severe disease remained concentrated in young infants. Importantly, 6% of RSV-related hospitalisations among infants aged <1 year occurred within the first 14 days of life, highlighting the importance of timely protection after birth. The RSV GOLD III–ICU Network Study subsequently investigated severe respiratory infections among children aged <2 years across ten LMICs. During local RSV seasons, 29.0% of children admitted with severe respiratory illness tested positive for RSV. Most life-threatening RSV infections occurred among infants aged <6 months, with almost half occurring during the first three months of life, demonstrating the potential for early-life immunisation to prevent severe disease. Cost-of-illness studies in Mozambique and Nepal demonstrated that RSV also imposes a substantial economic burden. In Mozambique, mean costs increased from USD 612 for severe hospitalised disease to USD 1,161 for life-threatening disease requiring intensive care. Household costs represented approximately 43–145% of the monthly minimum wage. In Nepal, mean total costs were USD 312 for hospitalised cases and USD 664 for intensive-care cases, with substantial out-of-pocket expenditure relative to the monthly minimum wage. Cost-effectiveness analyses evaluated maternal vaccination and long-acting mAbs over 2025–2034. In Nepal, maternal vaccination at USD 5 per dose was estimated to prevent approximately 2,500 deaths and 50,000 hospitalisations, with an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio of USD 387 per disability-adjusted life year (DALY) averted. A mAb at the same price could prevent approximately 2,400 deaths and 54,000 hospitalisations at USD 486 per DALY averted. Seasonal delivery could further improve dose efficiency. Analyses in Mozambique similarly demonstrated substantial potential health gains, although cost-effectiveness remained strongly dependent on intervention price and the cost-effectiveness threshold used. Overall, this thesis demonstrates that new RSV immunisation strategies could substantially reduce clinical and economic burden, but their value and feasibility are context-dependent. Integrating local epidemiology, healthcare utilisation, costs and implementation characteristics into economic evaluations can make estimates more relevant for national decision-making. Health economic evidence can thereby support priority setting and resource allocation, while successful and equitable implementation ultimately also depends on affordable pricing, sustainable financing and health-system readiness.

Question and background
This study aimed to understand factors influencing herpes zoster (HZ) vaccination behavior and intention in a self-payment setting, addressing the gap in knowledge about how affordability and motivation interact in vaccine uptake.
Design and population
A nationwide, web-based cross-sectional survey of adults aged ≥18 years was conducted in 2025, with participants recruited through active digital distribution across online platforms. The sample included 1690 respondents categorized into three groups based on their HZ vaccination status.
Core methods
Clinical health status was assessed based on 10 chronic conditions, and perceived pain severity, impact on daily life, and concern levels were self-rated on 1–10 numeric scales. Logistic regression models were used to analyze associations between prior vaccination history and HZ vaccination behavior or intention, with six separate models constructed for each outcome.
Main results
HZ vaccination behavior was significantly associated with age ≥65 years (OR: 1.59; 95% CI: 1.12–2.25), prior HZ infection (OR: 1.62; 95% CI: 1.23–2.15), prior receipt of non-COVID-19 adult vaccines (OR: 3.32; 95% CI: 2.26–4.89), and awareness of local subsidy programs (OR: 2.30; 95% CI: 1.70–3.12).
Interpretation and novelty
The study highlights that prior receipt of non-COVID-19 adult vaccines, particularly RSV and pneumococcal vaccines, is strongly associated with HZ vaccination behavior, suggesting a potential link between health-seeking behaviors and vaccine uptake.
Scientific and public-health significance
These findings can inform public health strategies to improve HZ vaccination rates by leveraging existing vaccination behaviors and promoting awareness of subsidy programs to reduce financial barriers.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited by its reliance on self-reported data and the potential for recall bias. However, the use of logistic regression models and sensitivity analyses strengthens the evidence, supporting a moderate confidence assessment.

Question and background
The study aimed to investigate the pathophysiology of non-T2 asthma, which remains largely unexplored, and to identify mechanisms by which early respiratory viral infection contributes to its development.
Design and population
The study used a non-T2 asthma mouse model induced by early respiratory viral infection combined with allergen sensitization. The model involved trained macrophages that displayed hyperactive methionine cycling and elevated levels of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM).

Question and background
The study aimed to investigate age-related differences in diagnosis-coded hospitalizations associated with Mycoplasma pneumoniae and the burden of PICU-associated care in pediatric patients. This is important due to the potential variability in clinical manifestations and outcomes across different age groups.
Design and population
A single-center retrospective study was conducted using diagnosis-coded discharge records from a tertiary maternal and child health hospital in Hubei, China. The analytic dataset included 33,047 pediatric hospitalization records aged ≥29 days and <18 years, with 872 records associated with PICU care.
Core methods
The study used a de-identified structured inpatient discharge dataset and conducted a stratified systematic chart-validation substudy of 762 records to evaluate key discharge-coded variables. Logistic regression models were employed to estimate adjusted odds ratios (aORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for PICU-associated hospitalization, with the parsimonious model including age group, sex, and selected pathogen-related coded co-diagnoses.
Main results
In 2023, monthly records of M. pneumoniae-associated hospitalizations increased from 99 in January and 139 in February to 2,282 in November. Respiratory failure/hypoxemia was recorded in 6.6% of the 1–<12 mo group versus 1.3% of the 72–<144 mo group; coded sepsis/septicemia in 11.2% versus 5.9%; and cardiac abnormality/coded myocardial involvement in 25.0% versus 15.6%.
Interpretation and novelty
The findings highlight age-related heterogeneity in diagnosis-coded phenotypes and PICU-associated burden, suggesting that younger children have a higher likelihood of severe clinical manifestations compared to older age groups. This provides novel insights into the clinical patterns of M. pneumoniae-associated hospitalizations across different pediatric age strata.
Scientific and public-health significance
These results can inform targeted surveillance and clinical management strategies for pediatric M. pneumoniae infections, particularly in younger age groups who show a higher risk of PICU-associated care. They may also guide resource allocation and early intervention protocols in hospital settings.
Limitations and evidence strength

Question and background
The study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic utility of bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) cytokine and lymphocyte profiles for lower respiratory tract infections (LRTIs) in patients with lymphoma, addressing the need for improved diagnostic tools in this vulnerable population.

This retrospective study included 147 participants: 55 lymphoma patients with LRTIs, 52 with non-pulmonary infections (NPIs), and 40 non-lymphoma patients with LRTIs. BALF cytokines and lymphocyte subsets were analyzed using multiparameter flow cytometry, with samples diluted 1:3 prior to testing. 摘要中文翻译呼吸道合胞病毒(RSV)是导致婴幼儿和儿童急性下呼吸道感染、毛细支气管炎、肺炎和住院的主要原因之一,其中6个月以下婴儿、早产儿以及患有支气管肺发育不良、先天性心脏病、免疫抑制状况及其他基础合并症的儿童面临最严重的疾病风险。在墨西哥,现有证据表明RSV感染具有显著的临床负担,并且主要在9月至3月期间呈现季节性流行。近年来,新型预防策略的发展改变了RSV预防的方法,特别是在通过双价前融合F蛋白疫苗进行母体免疫接种以及使用长效单克隆抗体进行被动免疫预防方面。墨西哥儿科学会发布的这一立场声明回顾了目前针对儿童群体RSV感染预防策略的流行病学、临床和真实世界效果证据。在妊娠32至36周期间进行的母体疫苗接种可促进抗体通过胎盘转移,并在婴儿出生后的最初几个月内提供保护。单克隆抗体,特别是尼塞韦单抗,已被证明可显著降低下呼吸道感染、住院、重症监护病房入院以及严重RSV相关疾病的发病率,且具有良好的安全性。基于疾病负担、国家季节性特征、预防产品的可获得性以及墨西哥医疗体系的特性,墨西哥儿科学会建议采用一种互补且逐步推进的国家策略。

Original abstract (English)
Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is one of the leading causes of acute lower respiratory tract infection, bronchiolitis, pneumonia, and hospitalization among infants and young children, with the highest risk of severe disease observed in infants younger than six months, preterm infants, and children with bronchopulmonary dysplasia, congenital heart disease, immunocompromising conditions, and other underlying comorbidities.In Mexico, available evidence demonstrates a substantial clinical burden and a predominant seasonal circulation from September to March.In recent years, the development of novel preventive strategies has transformed the approach to RSV prevention, particularly through maternal immunization with a bivalent prefusion F protein vaccine and passive immunoprophylaxis with long-acting monoclonal antibodies.This position statement from the Mexican Academy of Pediatrics reviews the epidemiological, clinical, and real-world effectiveness evidence regarding currently available strategies for the prevention of RSV infection in the pediatric population.Maternal vaccination between 32 and 36 weeks of gestation promotes transplacental antibody transfer and provides protection to infants during the first months of life.Monoclonal antibodies, particularly nirsevimab, have demonstrated significant reductions in lower respiratory tract infection, hospitalization, intensive care unit admission, and severe RSV-associated disease, with a favorable safety profile.Based on disease burden, national seasonality, availability of preventive products, and the characteristics of the Mexican health care system, the Mexican Academy of Pediatrics proposes a complementary and progressive strategy

Scope and question
The review addresses the burden of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) infection in Mexican infants and young children and evaluates two preventive interventions—maternal vaccination administered at 32‐36 weeks gestation and passive immunoprophylaxis with long‐acting monoclonal antibodies such as nirsevimab—within the context of Mexico’s seasonal RSV circulation.

Consensus and conclusions
The authors conclude that maternal vaccination effectively transfers antibodies to the infant and that nirsevimab markedly reduces lower respiratory tract infection, hospitalisation, ICU admission and severe RSV disease, with a favorable safety profile, indicating broad agreement on the efficacy and safety of both strategies.

Vaccination for girls and women across the life course: Evidence‐based recommendations for clinical practice and policy

Authors: Gelila Kidane Goba, Fionnuala Mary McAuliffe, Chiara Benedetto, Méabh Ní Bhuinneáin, Suvarna Satish Khadilkar, Marisa J Labovsky, Michael Abou‐Dakn, Eytan R. Barnea, Agnaldo Lopes da Silva Filho, Elizabete Argale
Original abstract (English)
Vaccination across the life course is a cornerstone of preventive health care for girls and women. Although WHO recommends completion of essential vaccines by adolescence, global coverage remains suboptimal. Vaccine-preventable diseases-including HPV-related cancers, vaginal, vulvar, anal, oropharyngeal, influenza, pertussis, hepatitis B, herpes zoster, and pneumococcal disease-continue to impose a disproportionate burden on girls and women, particularly in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) and migrant populations. Key life stages, such as childhood (early and middle), adolescence, preconception, pregnancy, postpartum, midlife, and beyond, offer critical opportunities for immunization. Obstetricians, gynecologists, midwives, and other women's healthcare providers are uniquely positioned to integrate vaccination into routine care in all settings and influence vaccine acceptance. Strengthening vaccine advocacy within women's health care is essential to reduce preventable morbidity and mortality and advance health equity for girls and women globally. This paper presents evidence-based recommendations for equitable vaccination across the life course of girls and women, synthesizing guidance from WHO, the Strategic Advisory Group of Experts on Immunization (SAGE), the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), and national immunization technical advisory groups. Vaccines reviewed include HPV, tetanus-diphtheria-pertussis (Tdap), influenza, hepatitis B, measles-mumps-rubella (MMR), varicella, herpes zoster, pneumococcal, Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV), and COVID-19. A life-course framework is proposed, emphasizing maternal immunization, cervical cancer prevention, and integration of regular and opportune vaccination into routine obstetric and gynecologic services. Practical implementation strategies and policy recommendations are outlined to support FIGO member societies.

The review synthesizes evidence-based recommendations from WHO, SAGE, CDC, ACOG, and national immunization technical advisory groups. Consensus and conclusions
The review concludes that vaccination across the life course is essential for preventing RSV-related diseases, particularly in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) and migrant populations. It emphasizes the importance of maternal immunization, cervical cancer prevention, and integrating regular and opportunistic vaccination into routine obstetric and gynecologic services.

The review notes that while RSV is a significant concern, vaccine-preventable diseases such as HPV-related cancers, influenza, and pertussis continue to impose a disproportionate burden on girls and women. There is a lack of comprehensive data on the effectiveness of RSV vaccines in specific populations and life stages, highlighting a gap in evidence.

The review recommends practical implementation strategies and policy actions to support FIGO member societies in promoting equitable vaccination across the life course. It highlights the need for strengthening vaccine advocacy within women's healthcare to reduce preventable morbidity and mortality and advance health equity.

Authors: Elke Loots, Eva Bernaert, Elke Van den Brande, Reinout Naesens, Reinout Naessens
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Respiratory viruses such as SARS-CoV-2, influenza, and RSV place considerable pressure on healthcare systems. Inconsistent respiratory virus screening among hospitalized patients with respiratory symptoms may delay recognition of respiratory viral infections and implementation of appropriate infection prevention and control measures. OBJECTIVE: To assess the appropriateness of respiratory virus screening among hospitalized patients with respiratory symptoms and to identify factors associated with inappropriate screening. METHODS: We conducted a retrospective observational study in a 2500-bed tertiary hospital in Belgium during three periods of the 2022-2023 influenza epidemic. Medical records were reviewed for demographic and clinical characteristics, respiratory symptoms, and respiratory virus screening practices. Screening was classified as appropriate or inappropriate (delayed or absent despite indication) based on prevailing guidelines. Determinants of inappropriate screening were explored using univariable analyses. RESULTS: Of 743 hospital admissions assessed for eligibility, 345 (46%) involved patients with respiratory symptoms and constituted the primary analysis population. Respiratory virus screening was appropriate in 75% (258/345) and inappropriate in 25% (87/345) of cases. CONCLUSION: Gaps remain in appropriate respiratory virus screening among hospitalized patients with respiratory symptoms, particularly among patients who develop symptoms during hospitalization. Systematic symptom-based screening and standardized decision-support tools may help improve adherence to respiratory virus screening recommendations.
