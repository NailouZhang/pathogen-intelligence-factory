基于假病毒的中和抗体测定,用于测量针对 狂犬病病毒 的中和抗体

A pseudovirus-based neutralizing antibody assay for measuring neutralizing antibody against rabies virus
作者： Xinxin Li, Pei Hu, Yueling Chen, Weizhao Lin, Yingyin Deng, Zixia Qian, Can Xiong, Limei Sun, Jianfeng He, Chunsheng Dong
摘要中文翻译摘要 建立针对 狂犬病病毒 的人中和抗体 (NAb) 的实验室检测方法,并评估其在人血清样本中的效率。使用含有 RABV G 基因的假病毒 pXN2-RABV-G。评估了测定的精密度、稳健性、线性、稳定性、包容性和检测限。分析该方法与ELISA、快速荧光聚焦抑制试验(RFFIT)的一致性和相关性。成功开发了基于pXN2-RABV-G的伪病毒中和抗体(pVNA)检测系统,并表现出广泛的稳健性(培养基:χ 2 = 4.00,P > 0.05,细胞传代:χ 2 = 0.25,P > 0.05)、精密度(独立检测:P > 0.05)、线性下/上限(0.09至93.73 IU/mL,R 2 > 0.95),稳定性( P > 0.05)。检测限 (LOD) 为 2.63 IU/mL(95% CI:1.81–3.84),表示为可与背景信号可靠区分的最低人血清抗体水平,变异系数小于 20%。与商业ELISA和金标准RFFIT测定相比,kappa值分别为0.74和0.82,结果高度一致。线性相关分析显示这些变量之间存在很强的相关性(R 2 > 0.90)。2019年至2023年共有103名受助者参与了血清学检测和以下分析。Nab 滴度与 疫苗 剂量( P < 0.001)和最近 疫苗 时间显着相关( P < 0.001)。5剂量疫苗接种组的几何平均滴度(GMT)大于1剂量、2剂量、3剂量和4剂量组(3.15 vs 3.19 vs 6.18 vs 12.21 vs 13.40 IU/mL)。建立的用于人狂犬病 NAb检测的pVNA测定法具有与金标准RFFIT相似的检测能力,但具有降低生物安全要求、实验室活动和时间的优点,这突出了其安全性、高效性以及继疫苗接种之后筛选和监测狂犬病病毒抗体的可行性。重要性 狂犬病 是一种致命性疾病,临床上尚无有效的 治疗。疫苗接种 是常规预防的唯一方法,这使得 疫苗接种 后抗体检测至关重要。目前的标准测试需要设备齐全的实验室、生物安全措施,并且非常耗时。本研究开发了一种安全、有效的测试,用于使用假病毒技术检测 狂犬病 中和抗体。它的准确性可与金标准测试相媲美,但更容易在常规实验室中实施,这使得 疫苗接种 后的抗体能够更广泛地用于筛选和监测。这一优势解决了 狂犬病 预防中的一个关键差距,减少了对 狂犬病 无反应且面临 狂犬病 暴露风险的免疫缺陷个体的不确定性。

A pseudovirus-based neutralizing antibody assay for measuring neutralizing antibody against rabies virus

Authors: Xinxin Li, Pei Hu, Yueling Chen, Weizhao Lin, Yingyin Deng, Zixia Qian, Can Xiong, Limei Sun, Jianfeng He, Chunsheng Dong
Original abstract (English)
ABSTRACT To establish a laboratory detection assay for a human neutralizing antibody (NAb) against rabies virus and to evaluate its efficiency in human serum samples. A pseudovirus pXN2‐RABV‐G harboring the RABV G gene was applied. The precision, robustness, linearity, stability, inclusivity, and limit of detection of the assay were evaluated. The consistency and correlation of the assay with ELISA and rapid fluorescent focus inhibition test (RFFIT) were analyzed. A pseudovirus-based neutralizing antibody (pVNA) detection system based on the pXN2‐RABV‐G was successfully developed and showed broad robustness (culture media: χ 2 = 4.00, P > 0.05, cell passages: χ 2 = 0.25, P > 0.05), precision (independent detection: P > 0.05), lower/upper limit of linearity (0.09 to 93.73 IU/mL, R 2 > 0.95), and stability ( P > 0.05). The limit of detection (LOD) was 2.63 IU/mL (95% CI: 1.81–3.84), expressed as the lowest human sera antibody level reliably distinguishable from background signals, and the coefficient of variation was less than 20%. Compared with the commercial ELISA and the gold standard RFFIT assay, the kappa values were 0.74 and 0.82, respectively, and the results were highly consistent. Linear correlation analysis revealed a strong correlation between these variables ( R 2 > 0.90). A total of 103 recipients from 2019 to 2023 were included in the serological testing and the following analysis. NAb titers were significantly correlated with vaccine dose ( P < 0.001) and the most recent vaccination time ( P < 0.001). The geometric mean titer (GMT) of the 5-dose vaccination group was greater than that of the 1-dose, 2-dose, 3-dose, and 4-dose groups (3.15 vs 3.19 vs 6.18 vs 12.21 vs 13.40 IU/mL). The established pVNA assay for human rabies NAb detection has a detection capability similar to that of the gold standard RFFIT, but it has the advantages of reduced biosafety requirements, laboratory activity, and time, which highlight its safety, high efficiency, and feasibility for screening and monitoring rabies virus antibodies after vaccination. IMPORTANCE Rabies is a lethal disease with no effective clinical treatment. Vaccination is the only way to prevent it routinely, which makes post-vaccination antibody testing essential. The current standard test requires a highly equipped laboratory, biosafety measures, and is time-consuming. This study develops a safe, efficient test for detecting rabies neutralizing antibodies using pseudovirus technology. It is comparable to the gold standard test in accuracy but easier to implement in routine labs, which enables broader use in screening and monitoring antibodies post-vaccination. This advantage addresses a key gap in rabies prevention, reducing the uncertainty for individuals with immunodeficiency who are nonresponsive to vaccination and at risk of rabies exposure.

Question and background
This study aimed to establish a laboratory detection assay for measuring neutralizing antibodies (NAb) against rabies virus and to evaluate its efficiency in human serum samples. Rabies is a lethal disease with no effective clinical treatment, making post-vaccination antibody testing essential.
Design and population
A pseudovirus-based neutralizing antibody assay was developed and evaluated using 103 recipients from 2019 to 2023. The study focused on assessing the assay's performance in human serum samples.
Core methods
The assay's precision, robustness, linearity, stability, inclusivity, and limit of detection were evaluated. The consistency and correlation of the assay with ELISA and rapid fluorescent focus inhibition test (RFFIT) were analyzed. The established pVNA assay was compared with the commercial ELISA and the gold standard RFFIT assay.
Main results
The pseudovirus-based neutralizing antibody (pVNA) detection system showed broad robustness, precision, and stability. The limit of detection (LOD) was 2.63 IU/mL (95% CI: 1.81–3.84), and the coefficient of variation was less than 20%. Linear correlation analysis revealed a strong correlation between these variables (R2 > 0.90).
Interpretation and novelty
The pVNA assay demonstrated high consistency with the gold standard RFFIT assay, with kappa values of 0.82. The results indicate that the assay is accurate and has the potential to be widely used in routine labs for screening and monitoring rabies virus antibodies post-vaccination.
Scientific and public-health significance
The pVNA assay provides a safer, more efficient alternative to the current standard test, which is time-consuming and requires highly equipped laboratories. This advancement enables broader use in antibody screening and monitoring, particularly for individuals with immunodeficiency who are nonresponsive to vaccination.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study's evidence is strong, with high kappa values and strong linear correlation, but it does not address potential biases or confounding factors. The evidence is limited to the specific context of the pVNA assay and its comparison with ELISA and RFFIT.

Atypical neuroimaging in rabies encephalitis: A case report of concurrent cerebral venous sinus thrombosis in a nine-year-old patient

Authors: Djoudline Doughmi, Amal Akammar, Imad Daoudi, Said Benlamkaddem, Adnane Berdai, Meryem Boubbou, Mustapha Harandou, Mohamed Adnane Berdaï
Original abstract (English)
Human rabies is a fatal viral encephalitis that presents a profound diagnostic challenge in the absence of a known history of animal exposure. We report the case of a 9-year-old boy from Morocco who presented with fever, seizures, psychomotor agitation, hypersalivation and hydrophobia, despite no reported history of an animal bite or scratch. Brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) served as a critical diagnostic tool, strongly suggesting rabies encephalitis by revealing classic bilateral symmetric grey matter hyperintensities in the thalami and temporal lobes. Concurrently, MRI revealed an atypical and rare macrovascular finding: extensive cerebral venous sinus thrombosis of the right transverse and sigmoid sinuses, which likely accounted for additional asymmetric white matter changes. A nuchal skin biopsy analyzed via reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction confirmed the presence of rabies virus RNA. Despite aggressive neurocritical care and therapeutic anticoagulation, the patient’s condition deteriorated, and he passed away on day 9 of admission. This case underscores the dual role of MRI in acute encephalitis: guiding early etiological diagnosis of rabies and identifying concurrent, clinically significant macrovascular complications like cerebral venous sinus thrombosis.

Question and background
This study aimed to highlight the diagnostic challenges of human rabies in the absence of a known animal exposure history and to emphasize the importance of neuroimaging in identifying atypical presentations of rabies encephalitis.
Design and population
The study is a case report detailing the clinical and neuroimaging findings of a 9-year-old boy from Morocco who presented with fever, seizures, psychomotor agitation, hypersalivation, and hydrophobia without a documented history of animal bite or scratch.

The patient underwent brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), which was used to identify characteristic bilateral symmetric grey matter hyperintensities in the thalami and temporal lobes. A nuchal skin biopsy was also performed and analyzed via reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) to confirm the presence of rabies virus RNA.
Main results
MRI revealed classic bilateral symmetric grey matter hyperintensities in the thalami and temporal lobes, strongly suggesting rabies encephalitis. Additionally, MRI identified an atypical macrovascular finding of extensive cerebral venous sinus thrombosis in the right transverse and sigmoid sinuses, which likely contributed to asymmetric white matter changes.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpret that MRI plays a dual role in acute encephalitis: it aids in the early diagnosis of rabies and can also detect concurrent macrovascular complications such as cerebral venous sinus thrombosis, which may have significant clinical implications.
Scientific and public-health significance
The findings can inform the use of MRI in the diagnosis of rabies encephalitis and highlight the need to consider cerebral venous sinus thrombosis as a potential complication in patients with suspected rabies.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study is limited to a single case report, which may not be generalizable. However, the evidence is strong as it includes both neuroimaging findings and RT-PCR confirmation of rabies virus RNA, supporting the diagnosis and the observed complications.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background The Taihu Lake region, a highly urbanized area in the Yangtze River Delta, experiences a high prevalence of animal‐related injuries. Within this region, Wuxi has reported no indigenous human rabies cases for over a decade. Against China’s 2024–2025 rabies resurgence, any decline in post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) quality in zero-case areas could become a weak link that ignites epidemic resurgence. This study evaluated first-visit PEP quality in the region, explored the possible risk of what we term “prevention fatigue”—a silent decline in PEP quality in areas with no reported indigenous human rabies cases—and proposed targeted intervention strategies. Methods In this retrospective cross-sectional study, we included 40,222 first‐visit cases of animal‐related injuries at our hospital from January 2023 to December 2025. Outcomes included non-use of passive immunization in Grade III exposures, failure of standard first-visit care, and delayed presentation. Multivariable logistic regression examined associations and interaction effects. Results Grade III exposures accounted for 60.5% (24,322/40,222) of all cases. Among these, the passive immunization rate was 55.5%, and the standard first‐visit care rate was 45.3% (11,019/24,322). Young adults (15–44 years, aOR = 2.17), scratches (vs. bites, aOR = 1.20), and delayed presentation (aOR = 1.49) were independently associated with incomplete PEP delivery. Significant interactions were found for age group × injury type ( P < 0.001), animal × delayed presentation ( P < 0.001), and exposure site × delayed presentation ( P = 0.019). From 2023 to 2025, passive immunization rates among Grade III exposures increased from 52.8% to 60.1% (P for trend < 0.001), whereas standard first-visit care rates were 44.5%, 42.8%, and 48.4% across the three years (P for trend < 0.001); delayed presentation decreased from 37.9% to 31.9% (P for trend < 0.001). Conclusions Persistent gaps in PEP compliance were observed in this region with over a decade of no indigenous human rabies cases. Young adults, patients with scratch injuries and delayed consultation constituted overlapping high-risk groups. We put forward two sentinel surveillance indicators and stratified intervention strategies, stressing that Grade III scratches require the same protective management as bite wounds.

Community engagement strategies for preventing recurrent Nipah virus outbreaks in Bangladesh and India: adapting a framework for outbreak preparedness and response.

Authors: Shuma Banik, Md Zakiul Hassan, Bipin Adhikari
Original abstract (English)
The Nipah virus (NiV) infection is a highly fatal zoonotic disease with pandemic potential which has led to recurrent outbreaks in Bangladesh and India. While transmission pathways, including contaminated date palm sap and human-to-human spread, are increasingly identified, significant uncertainties remain. With no approved therapeutics or vaccines, prevention depends on addressing ecological and behavioural drivers of transmission. This viewpoint draws on selected evidence from NiV outbreaks and response to other zoonotic disease epidemics, such as Ebola, rabies, and the Marburg virus disease, we seek to foster discussion on why community engagement could be central to NiV prevention and preparedness. We highlight the relevance of community engagement through a spectrum of its intensity, which distinguishes between community-oriented, community-based, community-managed, and community-owned approaches. Adapting an existing model, we discuss how community engagement principles can be applied to tackle recurring NiV outbreaks in Bangladesh and India. By aligning interventions with sociocultural realities, community engagement can improve acceptability, enhance early detection, strengthen outbreak response, and support preparedness for future vaccine and therapeutic research. However, evidence specific to NiV remains limited and lessons from other diseases should be applied judiciously. In the absence of medical countermeasures, participatory, locally grounded approaches offer a sustainable pathway to reduce recurrent outbreaks and prevent future spillover events.

Original abstract (English)

Viral PDZ target sequences are not inherently more promiscuous than those derived from endogenous target proteins.

Authors: Cole D Masuga, Caroline Ceravolo, Elise F. Tahti, Daniel A. Pollard, Jeanine F. Amacher
Original abstract (English)
PDZ domains are one of the largest families of short linear motif (or peptide) binding domains in the human proteome. These scaffolding domains are important in signaling and trafficking pathways, such as in the formation of tight junctions or in the postsynaptic density of neurons. PDZ domains are also targeted by several pathogenic viral proteins, including human papillomavirus (HPV), influenza, hepatitis, rabies, coronaviruses, among others. Previously, we investigated the specificity determinants of modulator, or non-motif, residues for two PDZ binding motifs (or PBMs) from the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) and HPV16 E6 proteins to better understand differences in relative promiscuity between these similar sequences. To test whether viral PDZ-binding motifs are inherently more promiscuous than endogenous targets, we measured binding affinities for 7 endogenous and 7 viral PBMs across 8 well-studied Class I PDZ domains. Fluorescence anisotropy assays revealed no significant difference in relative binding affinities between viral and endogenous PBMs, indicating that viral versus endogenous origin alone does not explain PBM promiscuity. Our results were consistent with available data from high throughput holdup assays. Taken together, our data support previously reported sequence hotspots, whereby certain PBMs are recognized by large numbers of PDZ domains, likely due to a combination of widely favorable modulator residues.

Editorial: Cross-species transmission of viral infections

Authors: Alexandro Guterres
Original abstract (English)
The emergence and re-emergence of zoonotic diseases represent a formidable and often unpredictable threat to global public health, as demonstrated by the devastating impact of recent viral outbreaks. These events are increasingly driven by a complex interplay of anthropogenic activities, human-animal coexistence, and enhanced global connectivity, which provide unprecedented opportunities for pathogen spillover and genetic recombination. Understanding the intricate mechanisms of viral dynamics at the human-animal-environment interface requires a multidisciplinary "One Health" approach that integrates robust genomic surveillance with spatial and epidemiological data. This Research Topic brings together nine high-impact studies that utilize advanced molecular and metagenomic tools to decipher the evolutionary trajectories of high-risk pathogens, ranging from well-established threats like Middle East Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus (MERS-CoV) and Influenza A to underappreciated viruses in wildlife and domestic reservoirs. By mapping transmission networks and identifying novel genetic variants, these contributions provide a proactive scientific foundation for pandemic preparedness and the development of effective biosecurity policies (Figure 1).Coronaviruses continue to be a primary focal point of genomic surveillance due to their broad host range and high propensity for genomic recombination and point mutations. Han and collaborators provide a critical "cross-species transmission alert" by documenting a novel recombinant coronavirus, TigerCoV-JL1, infecting a captive Amur tiger in China (Figure 1) (Han et al., 2026). Utilizing metagenomic next-generation sequencing, the researchers identified that this strain represents a unique canine-raccoon dog recombinant, carrying genetic signals from domestic canine coronaviruses and raccoon dog lineages within the ORF1a and Spike (S) genes. This discovery highlights the susceptibility of endangered felids to viral jumps and underscores the risks inherent in semi-captive environments where high-density interfaces between carnivores facilitate complex evolutionary pathways. In parallel, the study by Pan and colleagues delves into the molecular determinants of immune evasion within the merbecovirus subgenus.By evaluating ten anti-MERS-CoV monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) against a diverse panel of pseudoviruses, they demonstrated how the V530L substitution in the MERS-CoV clade B, identified during the 2015 South Korean outbreak, drastically reduces the neutralization potency of the potent mAb m336 (Pan et al., 2025). Advanced structural modeling using AlphaFold3 revealed that this substitution induces significant steric changes in the receptor-binding domain (RBD), disrupting high-affinity hydrogen bonds between the antibody and the viral protein.These findings collectively emphasize that while large-scale recombination events expand host range, discrete molecular adaptations can compromise therapeutic efficacy, necessitating the development of multi-epitope strategies for future pandemic preparedness (Pan et al., 2025;Han et al., 2026).The role of domestic animals as "mixing vessels" for viral evolution remains a critical pillar of zoonotic risk assessment (Figure 1). Pigs, in particular, possess receptors for both avian and human influenza viruses, facilitating complex genetic reassortment, and the emergence of strains with pandemic potential. Cavicchio and associates exemplify the importance of sustained monitoring through a decade-long passive surveillance study (2013)(2014)(2015)(2016)(2017)(2018)(2019)(2020)(2021)(2022) in Northern Italy, which identified 12 distinct Swine Influenza A (swIAV) genotypes, including the first European reports of the Novel 1 and Novel 2 genotypes (Cavicchio et al., 2025). Their phylogenetic analysis revealed that genetic clustering is strongly influenced by anthropogenic factors, such as farm ownership and management practices, with documented instances of human-to-swine transmission sustaining local viral circulation and increasing the complexity of the internal gene constellations. Parallel surveillance in China by Zhao and coworkers characterized a novel triplereassortant H1N2 virus, designated as genotype G21, which acquired its HA gene from Eurasian avian-like H1N1 and its internal genes from the 2009 pandemic H1N1 (Zhao et al., 2026). Molecular analysis of this isolate identified the N31 substitution in the M2 protein, conferring adamantane resistance, and the S42 mutation in the NS1 protein, which is known to influence virulence in mammalian models. Furthermore, experimental infection in BALB/c mice confirmed moderate pathogenicity and efficient replication in the lungs and nasal turbinates, signaling its potential for further regional adaptation. Beyond respiratory threats, the study by Liu and collaborators underscores the significant food safety implications of the human-swine interface across Asia. By analyzing 348 complete genomes of Hepatitis E virus (HEV), they identified 34 recombination events, predominantly localized within the RdRp region, including 14 cross-species exchanges between swine and human strains (Liu et al., 2026). The high frequency of HEV-4 recombination in China appears to be driven by the intensive nature of the swine industry and high-density interaction hubs, reinforcing the need for stricter quarantine and molecular monitoring across the entire food production chain to mitigate the risk of zoonotic foodborne transmission (Liu et al., 2026;Zhao et al., 2026).Wildlife reservoirs serve as the primary origin of future pandemics, yet their internal viral landscapes often remain opaque. Huangfu and associates address this gap through an extensive metagenomic survey of the Asian house shrew (Suncus murinus) in tropical Hainan, China. By performing RNA sequencing on multiple tissues, the researchers identified a staggering 192 RNA viruses belonging to 32 known families, including significant human pathogens such as Henipavirus, Langat virus (LGTV), Amur virus (AMRV), and Influenza A (H1N1) (Huangfu et al., 2026). Their findings challenge the traditional perception of shrews as low-diversity reservoirs and identify them as a critical "ecological bridge" facilitating viral sharing between otherwise distantly related hosts, such as rodents and bats (Figure 1). This diversity is likely enhanced by the tropical ecosystem of Hainan, which supports stable host and vector populations year-round. In the avian sector, Tan and collaborator's provide critical insights into the taxonomy and distribution of pigeon gammacoronavirus (PgCoV). Utilizing high-throughput sequencing, they assembled a complete 27,538 nt genome, identifying unique accessory protein profiles, including a novel ORFx2 and the absence of proteins 3a and 3b (Tan et al., 2025). Comparative analysis of five conserved domains (including 3CLpro and RdRp) revealed amino acid differences exceeding 8.3% compared to known species, providing robust evidence for classifying PgCoV as a novel species within the genus Gammacoronavirus. Furthermore, their epidemiological survey documented a remarkably high flock-level prevalence of 97.73%, with RT-PCR evidence of viral replication in intestinal and kidney tissues, signaling the virus's potential pathogenicity in the digestive and urinary systems of pigeons. Collectively, these studies underscore the necessity of integrating understudied synanthropic and avian species into global virological surveillance to monitor emerging biosecurity threats (Tan et al., 2025;Huangfu et al., 2026).The complexity of viral dynamics is further elucidated by combining long-term field surveillance with human-centric molecular network analyses. Weinberg and associates provide a significant regional perspective from Israel, where 27 years of surveillance encompassing over 42,000 animals demonstrated a minimal risk of Rabies lyssavirus spillover from bats to terrestrial mammals (Weinberg et al., 2025). Despite the presence of 32 bat species, including the synanthropic Egyptian fruit bat which frequently forages in urban environments, zero positive cases were identified among the 294 bats tested using the historical gold-standard TAF method. This absence of infection reinforces the epidemiological distinction between Old and New World bat populations and highlights that reservoir roles are often region-specific, suggesting that bats in this region remain largely isolated from terrestrial Lyssavirus transmission cycles. In contrast, in the context of direct human-to-human transmission, Wu and colleagues utilize molecular transmission networks to track HIV-1 among men who have sex with men (MSM) in Ningxia, China. Their analysis of 296 pol gene sequences revealed an overall clustering rate of 43.6%, with the CRF07_BC subtype (57.1%) emerging as a dominant driver of localized spread (Wu et al., 2026). Notably, the study identified an alarming 38.2% prevalence of acquired drug resistance among sequenced individuals, demanding targeted public health interventions based on urban spatial connectivity and a "network-based approach" to effectively manage the spread of the epidemic. These contrasting studies emphasize that while some natural reservoirs pose low immediate risks, human social networks remain high-velocity conduits for viral dissemination and evolution (Weinberg et al., 2025;Wu et al., 2026).In conclusion, the studies presented in this Research Topic reaffirm that genomic surveillance is a proactive sentinel for global health security rather than merely a descriptive tool.By integrating data across the human-animal-environment interface, researchers have mapped recombination hotspots in Hepatitis E and swIAV, identified cross-species transmission potential in felid coronaviruses, and defined novel species of avian gammacoronaviruses. These contributions provide a robust scientific foundation for evidence-based biosecurity and public health policies, highlighting that discrete molecular adaptations can significantly compromise therapeutic efficacy or alter pathogenicity. The adoption of a multidisciplinary "One Health" framework is essential to stay ahead of viral adaptation, particularly as anthropogenic activities and ecological shifts continue to blur the boundaries between domestic and wild habitats.Ultimately, this collection underscores that protecting global biodiversity, ensuring food safety, and monitoring high-risk human networks are intrinsically linked strategies for the mitigation of future pandemic threats in an ever-changing world. Authors: Mingzhe Zhang, Zeliang Chen, Yu Jiang, Yue Cui, Feng Jiang, Xiaohu Han
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Complete reference genomes are essential for molecular diagnosis, typing, and source-tracing; yet their availability for the zoonoses that cause the greatest disease burden is seldom assessed. Along China's land border, where a heterogeneous set of priority zoonoses circulates, we investigated whether complete reference-genome resources correspond to disease burden. For 15 border-priority zoonoses, we compiled disease burden (using notifiable-disease cases from China CDC and animal events from WAHIS), complete-genome availability (from NCBI), and research output (from PubMed) as a secondary metric. We evaluated the relationship between burden and genome availability and verified its robustness using different genome metrics, a leave-one-out jackknife procedure, an exact permutation test, and alternative burden definitions, while also revealing a genome-metric paradox. Among the six diseases with official Chinese human-incidence data, complete reference genomes exhibited a strong inverse correlation with burden (Spearman ρ = −0.94; exact permutation p = 0.001): brucellosis, the highest-burden zoonosis (67 418 cases in 2024), had only 4 complete genomes, whereas low-burden rabies (170 cases) had 2 740. Importantly, the association depended on the use of complete genomes: total assembly counts (which include numerous draft genomes) yielded an opposite correlation ρ = +0.71. Thus, the finding pertains specifically to finished-quality reference genomes rather than total sequencing efforts, and we explicitly report this paradox. Research output (uncapped PubMed totals) showed no significant relationship with burden (ρ = −0.26, p = 0.6). Nine of the 15 border zoonoses lacked any official Chinese human-incidence data. Conclusions: Along China's border, complete reference genomes for priority zoonoses reflect pathogen notoriety rather than disease burden. Investment in finished reference genomes for high-burden, genome-poor zoonoses such as brucellosis would enhance their molecular diagnosis, typing, and source-tracing capabilities.

Organic waste mismanagement, free-roaming dog ecology, and rabies risk in endemic countries: a one health narrative review

Authors: Muhammad Hassnain, Waqas Ahmad, Qamer Mahmood, Hafiz Manzoor Ahmad, Fiaz Ahmad, Muhammad Rafi Ullah, Saba Khalid
Original abstract (English)
Background Rabies remains a neglected tropical disease in many endemic regions of Asia and Africa, where dogs are the principal source of human rabies transmission. Poorly managed organic, household, food, and municipal solid waste can provide predictable food resources that support the survival, aggregation, and persistence of free-roaming dogs in human-dominated environments. Objective This narrative review aimed to synthesize evidence on how organic waste mismanagement supports free-roaming dog populations in rabies-endemic countries and how this environmental pathway may indirectly influence dog-bite risk, rabies exposure, and progress toward eliminating dog-mediated human rabies deaths by 2030. Methodology A structured search for peer-reviewed articles published in English between 2010 and 2026 was conducted using PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science. Eligible studies addressed organic waste, free-roaming dog ecology, human–dog interactions, rabies, dog bite risk, surveillance, vaccination, dog population management, and One Health interventions. The evidence was organized into analytical themes and integrated using thematic narrative synthesis, with country-specific findings used to illustrate common patterns and contextual differences. Findings Household waste, market waste, restaurant waste, butcher shop waste, and open dumps were associated with dog presence, scavenging, aggregation, movement, and population persistence by providing accessible food resources. Human practices, including open waste disposal, intentional feeding, dog abandonment, and unrestricted dog ownership, further maintained dogs’ access to these resources. Waste-associated dog aggregation may increase opportunities for dog–dog and human–dog contact and complicate vaccination and humane population management programs. Rabies prevention was further constrained by incomplete vaccination coverage, inadequate dog population estimates, underreporting, limited or unequal access to post-exposure prophylaxis, weak surveillance, and insufficient intersectoral coordination. However, direct causal evidence that reducing accessible organic waste leads to reductions in dog density, dog-bite incidence, or rabies transmission remains limited. Conclusion Organic waste mismanagement is a neglected environmental pathway that may indirectly contribute to the risk of dog-mediated rabies. Waste management should complement mass dog vaccination, humane population management, responsible ownership, community education, surveillance, and timely postexposure prophylaxis within an integrated One Health framework.

The lower limb was the most frequently affected anatomical site (38.2%), followed by the hand and fingers (29.2%). WHO Category III exposure predominated (59.6%), followed by Category II (36.0%) and Category I (4.5%). Stray dogs accounted for 76.4% of bites. Tetanus prophylaxis was administered to 91.0% of patients, ARV to all patients, and RIG to 94.3% of Category III patients. Overall, 97.8% were managed as outpatients and 2.2% required hospital admission.
Controversies and gaps
The study noted that instances of ARV administration in Category I and RIG administration in Category II exposures warrant evaluation for guideline concordance. Long-term clinical outcomes were not assessed, highlighting a potential evidence gap.
Research and practice implications
Characterising the demographic profile, injury patterns, exposure severity, and post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) practices may help improve emergency management and rabies prevention. The findings suggest the need for further research on long-term clinical outcomes and the adherence to guidelines for PEP administration across different WHO exposure categories.

First Published: Saturday, 22 Aug 2026 4:23 PM MYT × Subscribe to Newsletter Subscribe KOTA SAMARAHAN, Aug 22 — Three cats were among 26 animals confirmed positive for rabies in Sarawak so far this year, with the remaining 23 cases involving dogs. Deputy Premier Datuk Amar Dr Sim Kui Hian said the figures, based on data from the Department of Veterinary Services Sarawak (DVSS), were verified as of Aug 20. “As a medical doctor and your Minister for Public Health, I must remind everyone of the sobering reality. Since July 2017, Sarawak has recorded 94 human rabies cases with 87 deaths, data verified with JKNS on Aug 20, 2026. That is a fatality rate near 93 per cent. Once clinical symptoms appear, rabies is almost universally fatal. Yet, it is 100 per cent preventable. “From the animal health side, for 2026, to date, a total of 23 dogs and three cats were confirmed as positive rabies cases, data from DVSS, verified on Aug 20, 2026,” he said in his speech read by Kuching South City Council (MBKS) Mayor Datuk Wee Hong Seng at the launch of the Stop Rabies 2026: Rabies Education Carnival at Universiti Malaysia Sarawak (Unimas), here on Saturday. Dr Sim, who is also minister for public health, housing and local government, reminded the public that every bite, nip or scratch from an animal must be treated as an immediate health emergency. “Ensure your community knows two simple rules: vaccinate your pets annually, and practise responsible pet ownership. Regular vaccination creates an important protective buffer for the entire neighbourhood. “Wash and run: immediately wash any animal bite or scratch under running water with soap for at least 15 minutes, then go straight to the nearest health clinic. Do not wait for symptoms to appear,” he said. Dr Sim said achieving the target of eliminating human rabies in Sarawak required every citizen to play an active role. “By taking ownership of rabies prevention across all sectors of society, we will fulfil our state target and contribute to the global mission of Zero Human Rabies Deaths by 2030,” he added. The Stop Rabies 2026: Rabies Education Carnival was organised with the Malaysia One Health University Network (MyOHUN), with support from Boehringer Ingelheim Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia, and involved agencies including the Sarawak Health Department (JKNS), DVSS and the Sarawak Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (SSPCA).
