Genetic characterization of rabies virus circulating in crab-eating fox (Cerdocyon thous) in the State of Paraiba, Northeastern Brazil

Authors: Camila de Sousa Bezerra, Albério Antônio de Barros Gomes, Antônio Flávio Medeiros Dantas, Sergio Santos de Azevedo, Clebert José Alves, João Pessoa Araújo Júnior, Camila Dantas Malossi, Letícia da Silva Ferreira Ribeiro Mathias, Leila Sabrina Ullmann, Tiago Jose da Silva Lopes
Original abstract (English)
Rabies is a fatal viral zoonosis that remains endemic in more than two-thirds of all countries worldwide, causing approximately 70,000 human deaths annually-equivalent to one death every ten minutes. The rabies virus (RABV), a member of the genus Lyssavirus (family Rhabdoviridae), has bats and canids as its primary reservoirs, although infections have also been reported in other wild species, including Cerdocyon thous (crab-eating fox) and Pseudalopex vetulus (hoary fox). In this study, we performed a comprehensive genomic and biological characterization of a RABV variant circulating in wild foxes from the Northeastern region of Brazil. Using Next Generation Sequencing (NGS), phylogenetic analysis, and in vitro and in vivo assays, we identified genetic similarities between these isolates and previously described RABV strains from wild and domestic canids in Brazil. However, the presence of escape mutants indicates a heterogeneous viral population, suggesting ongoing adaptive evolution in wildlife reservoirs. These findings reinforce the importance of genomic surveillance to detect emerging variants that may compromise current vaccination or control strategies. Overall, our results provide new insights into the molecular diversity, pathogenicity, and ecological dynamics of RABV circulating among wild canids in Brazil, contributing to improved understanding and prevention of rabies in endemic regions.

Post-exposure prophylaxis and possible breakthrough infections among human rabies case in China, 2019–2024: a nationwide retrospective surveillance study

Authors: Shouming Lyu, Xi Chen, Jinhui Zhang, Xiao Yuan, Danjian Zheng, Yanping Zhang, Di Mu, Canjun Zheng, 原孝
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Rabies remains a uniformly fatal yet preventable zoonotic disease that continues to pose a public health threat in China, especially in rural populations. Although post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is highly effective when delivered promptly and correctly, deaths still occur, including rare infections that develop despite prophylaxis. This study characterized exposure patterns, PEP use, and possible breakthrough infections among human rabies deaths in China from 2019 to 2024. Methods We conducted a retrospective cross-sectional analysis of data from China's national rabies surveillance system. Clinically diagnosed and laboratory-confirmed human rabies cases reported between 1 January 2019 and 31 December 2024 were included. We analyzed demographic characteristics, exposure information, animal sources, wound sites, PEP measures, and incubation periods. Multivariable unconditional logistic regression identified factors associated with PEP receipt, while Cox proportional hazards models, Mann–Whitney U tests, and Kruskal–Wallis H tests were used to examine factors associated with the incubation period. Chi-square or Fisher's exact tests compared standard and non-standard PEP cases. Results Among 930 human rabies deaths, most patients were male, rural farmers, and middle-aged or older adults. Dogs caused 86.88% of exposures, yet only 1.83% of owned biting animals had documented rabies vaccination. PEP uptake was very low: 852 cases (91.62%) received no prophylaxis whatsoever, and only 51 cases (5.48%) received rabies vaccine. Non-category III exposure, wounds at lower-risk sites, injury by household animals, and single-victim incidents were associated with lower likelihood of receiving wound care, vaccination, or rabies immunoglobulin. Among 697 cases with a documented incubation period, the median was 70 days (IQR: 38–134.5). Head, face, or neck exposure and multiple-victim incidents were independently associated with shorter incubation periods. Seventy-eight patients developed rabies despite receiving at least one prophylaxis measure; 60 (76.92%) had clearly non-standard PEP, whereas 18 (23.08%) had no identifiable procedural failure. These possible breakthrough infections accounted for 1.94% of all cases and were characterized by category III exposure, head, face, or neck injuries, stray-dog bites, and a short median incubation period of 16 days. Conclusion Most human rabies deaths in China resulted from a failure to initiate or complete PEP, reflecting preventable gaps in risk perception, access, affordability, and follow-up. Possible breakthrough infections were rare but were concentrated in extremely high-risk exposures. Targeted health education, improved prophylaxis adherence, integrated bite case management, and strengthened dog vaccination under a One Health framework are essential to eliminate dog-mediated human rabies deaths.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background: Zoonotic diseases are a major public health concern in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), where diseases such as rabies, Ebola, and Mpox pose ongoing health risks. Household knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) play an important role in preventing zoonotic disease transmission. This study assessed household knowledge, attitudes, and practices regarding priority zoonotic diseases in Bukavu, South Kivu Province, DRC, and identified factors associated with satisfactory overall KAP. Methods: A cross-sectional study was conducted from September 1 to 15, 2025, in the Bagira and Kadutu health zones of Bukavu. A total of 634 households were selected using two-stage cluster sampling. Data were collected using a structured questionnaire administered through KoboCollect and analyzed using Stata version 17. The questionnaire assessed knowledge, attitudes, and practices concerning Ebola, Mpox, and rabies. Knowledge was measured using 17 items, attitudes using five-point Likert-scale items, and practices using five behavioral items. Scores of at least 70% were classified as satisfactory. Descriptive statistics, group comparisons, and bivariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses were used. Nonparametric tests were used when appropriate. Results: Overall, 42.9% of households demonstrated satisfactory knowledge of zoonotic diseases, 63.6% had favorable attitudes, and 11.7% demonstrated adequate preventive practices. A coordinated One Health approach involving human, animal, and environmental health stakeholders may strengthen household-level prevention of priority zoonotic diseases. The findings can help inform locally tailored prevention strategies and identify priorities for future community-based research. Background Rabies virus (RABV) is a neurotropic zoonotic pathogen responsible for an estimated 60,000 human deaths annually. Conclusions This pseudotype-based microneutralization assay represents a safe, flexible, and scalable alternative to conventional live-virus neutralization tests. Authors: Ayunda Putri, Bernadhita Herindri Samodera Utami, Riza Sawitri, Dwi Herinanto
Original abstract (English)
Rabies is a fatal zoonotic disease that is generally transmitted through the bite of an infected dog. This research aims to predict the spread of rabies in dog and human populations in Muara Enim Regency using a combined SI–SEIR mathematical model. The system of nonlinear differential equations was solved using the fourth-order Runge–Kutta (RK4) method. Rabies data on dogs and humans were obtained from the Lampung Veterinary Center for the period 2021–2024. The simulation results show that the number of infected dogs increased significantly at the beginning of the period, peaked in the third year, and then declined as the population of susceptible dogs decreased. In the human population, the increase in exposed and infected individuals occurred after the surge in the dog population, indicating cross-species transmission. This model provides a quantitative overview of rabies dynamics and can be used as a basis for rabies control strategies in Muara Enim Regency.

Authors: Louis-Marie Bloyet, Sean P. J. Whelan
Original abstract (English)
The large protein (L) of nonsegmented, negative-strand RNA viruses, a viral order that includes rabies, Ebola, Nipah, and respiratory syncytial viruses, catalyze RNA-dependent RNA polymerization on nucleocapsid-sheathed RNA templates. L coordinates two distinct processes: messenger RNA (mRNA) transcription and genome replication. During transcription, L catalyzes cotranscriptional mRNA capping, methylation, and polyadenylation. The additional activities are not required during genome replication; instead, RNA synthesis is coupled to encapsidation by soluble nucleoprotein (N 0 ) recruited by the phosphoprotein (P). We determined the structure of a vesicular stomatitis virus polymerase complex comprising L-P and N 0 . P directly engages the carboxyl-terminal domain of L (L CTD ) to anchor N 0 in a continuum of conformations including a more stable configuration with N 0 positioned at the RNA-product exit channel, poised for encapsidation. This study reveals a function for L CTD in encapsidation of nascent RNAs and provides the structure of a polymerase complex required for genomic RNA replication.

Authors: Jeffrey A Gray, Jessica M Robinson, Jamie Swift, Noah Wren, David Kirschke, Nathan Hale, David Cluck
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Completion of rabies post-exposure prophylaxis (rPEP) remains suboptimal due to vaccine access variables and barriers during follow-up. This study evaluated whether community pharmacy referral improves rPEP series completion as compared to emergency department availability alone. METHODS: A hospital improvement plan was implemented across a 16-emergency department (ED) health system. CONCLUSIONS: Community pharmacy is a viable resource to expand rPEP vaccine access and improve vaccine series completion as compared to emergency department visits alone.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract Objective To evaluate immunogenicity, persistence of antibodies, survival after challenge, and anamnestic response following extralabel parenteral rabies vaccination across mammalian species compared with benchmarks from on-label canine vaccination. Methods Mammals described in published studies of extralabel parenteral rabies vaccination. A structured literature review (1980 to 2025) identified studies reporting serologic or challenge outcomes after extralabel parenteral rabies vaccination. Extracted data included taxonomic family; vaccine type and schedule, timing of serum collection, antibody titers, survival after experimental challenge, and response to booster vaccination. Random-effects meta-analyses were conducted by family and overall. Pooled proportions were compared with the World Organisation for Animal Health benchmark of 88% survival and with canine benchmarks for primary seroconversion (88.1%), antibody levels 6 to 12 months after primary vaccination (72.9%), and booster response (96.8%). Results 32 articles describing 41 studies met inclusion criteria, representing 2,630 animals from 28 species in 17 families. After primary vaccination, 84.4 ± 4.1% achieved titers ≥ 0.5 IU/mL, with 14 of 16 families noninferior to the canine benchmark. At ≥ 6 months, 63.7 ± 10.6% maintained titers ≥ 0.5 IU/mL. Among 79 vaccinated animals challenged with rabies virus, 89.0 ± 9.8% survived. Booster vaccination produced adequate titers in 89.1 ± 8.0% of 275 animals, with reduced responses in the families Elephantidae and Ursidae. Conclusions Modern-day rabies vaccines generate strong antibody responses across diverse mammalian families, with nondomestic canids responding similarly to domestic dogs. Clinical Relevance These findings support recognizing extralabel vaccination as biologically protective and can inform public health and animal management policies.

A new study now reports that a gene-silencing approach delivered by a viral vector can sharply suppress rabies virus replication in laboratory cells and substantially improve survival in infected mice, both when administered before infection and when given after the virus has already entered the body. The research, published as a short communication in the journal Animal Diseases, focused on recombinant adeno-associated virus serotype 2, or rAAV2, as a delivery vehicle for short interfering RNAs, commonly called siRNAs. These small RNA molecules can silence specific genes by binding to their messenger RNA transcripts and triggering their degradation. The team designed siRNAs targeting two rabies virus genes: the G gene, which encodes the glycoprotein responsible for neuronal entry and transport, and the L gene, which encodes the RNA polymerase catalytic subunit essential for viral transcription and replication. The researchers first screened six candidate siRNAs, three against each target gene, in mouse neuroblastoma cells and baby hamster kidney cells infected with the rabies virus CVS-11 strain. Direct immunofluorescence assays showed that four of the candidates, si-G193, si-G672, si-G997, and si-L5055, significantly inhibited viral replication, with a marked reduction in fluorescent spots per field of view. Viral titer measurements confirmed that si-G997 and si-L5055 produced significantly lower titers than the negative control. Quantitative reverse transcription PCR revealed that all siRNAs targeting the G gene effectively reduced G messenger RNA levels in both cell types, while si-L5055 significantly decreased L messenger RNA levels. To verify that these effects reflected true gene knockdown rather than indirect antiviral activity, the team cotransfected human embryonic kidney cells with either si-G997 or si-L5055 together with plasmids expressing tagged rabies virus G or L proteins. Both siRNAs significantly reduced the expression of their respective target proteins compared with a scrambled negative control. Under infection conditions, the protein levels of the viral N and G proteins were reduced by both siRNAs, and the G protein was suppressed more markedly than the N protein. The authors attributed the poor performance of the other four siRNAs to differences in the thermodynamic properties of the target sequences and target site accessibility. Chemically synthesized siRNAs, although rapid in silencing target genes, are prone to degradation in the body and act only briefly. Viral vectors such as adenovirus, retrovirus, and lentivirus can deliver nucleic acids but often provoke strong host immune responses that generate neutralizing antibodies and weaken therapeutic effects. The researchers therefore turned to adeno-associated virus, which they describe as a relatively safe delivery system that supports long-term episomal expression in most tissues. Serotype 2 was chosen because most AAV vectors show predominant tropism for liver and muscle, whereas serotype 2 specifically targets the central nervous system, a property that fits the requirements of rabies virus research. The team encapsulated siRNA expression cassettes, each containing a CMV enhancer, a U6 promoter, and a shRNA coding sequence, into the rAAV2 vector, producing the constructs rAAV-G997 and rAAV-L5055. In HEK293T cells, these vectors significantly reduced the expression of the G protein and L protein, respectively. A CCK-8 assay showed that neither vector affected the viability of BHK-21 cells. When BHK-21 cells were transduced with the vectors 24 hours before rabies virus infection, both N and G protein levels decreased compared with a negative control vector, with the reduction in G expression more pronounced. In neuroblastoma cells transduced with rAAV-G997, G messenger RNA levels and viral titers remained consistently lower than in the control group at all time points examined, and rAAV-L5055 similarly reduced L messenger RNA levels and viral titers at every time point. The in vivo experiments used six-week-old Kunming mice divided into groups of ten. Three groups received intracerebral injections of rAAV2 at a dose of 2 × 1011 vector genomes per kilogram, followed 48 hours later by an intramuscular challenge with 1 × 105 focus-forming units of the CVS-11 strain. Among mice injected with rAAV-G997, rAAV-L5055, or a negative control vector before infection, the rAAV-G997 group maintained relatively stable body weight throughout a 21-day observation period, while survival rates reached 90 percent for rAAV-G997, 90 percent for rAAV-L5055, and 50 percent for the control. In a postexposure setting, mice received the same vectors 24 hours after viral challenge. The rAAV-G997 and rAAV-L5055 groups again showed relatively stable body weight changes compared with the control group, and survival rates were 88.9 percent for both treatment groups versus 55.6 percent for the control. A separate confirmatory experiment using 18 additional mice, six per group, measured viral loads in brain tissue on day 7 after infection. Both pretreatment groups exhibited significantly decreased viral loads in brain tissue compared with the negative control. The authors note that the lack of significant difference in efficacy between the two vectors suggests the strategy has a broad therapeutic window. The protective effect is attributed to two factors. Rabies virus primarily infects the central nervous system, and AAV2 exhibits natural tropism for neurons, enabling efficient transduction of the primary sites of viral replication when delivered directly to the brain. Intracerebral injection also bypasses the blood-brain barrier, delivering the therapeutic vector straight to the brain parenchyma. Consequently, even 24 hours after viral challenge, the vector-delivered siRNAs effectively suppressed viral replication within the central nervous system, an intervention that directly targets the intracellular viral life cycle and offers a prolonged therapeutic window that the authors say surpasses that of conventional biologics. The translational path faces considerable hurdles. The evaluations indicated that a relatively high viral dose is required for efficacy, which increases production costs. Owing to time constraints, the study was unable to assess the purity of the packaged rAAV2, and product-related impurities such as empty capsids may cause batch-to-batch variability and potentially exacerbate host immune responses. The authors conclude that rAAV2-mediated delivery of siRNAs targeting the rabies virus G and L genes can inhibit viral replication in vitro and provide significant protection in vivo, highlighting the potential of this platform as a therapeutic approach for rabies while acknowledging that addressing manufacturing and purity issues remains critical for any future clinical application.
