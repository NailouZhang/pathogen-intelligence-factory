Nanopore Sequencing for Chikungunya Virus: Principles and Application.

Authors: Adelino TÉR, Xavier J, Fonseca V, Giovanetti M
Original abstract (English)
Nanopore sequencing is transforming viral genomics through real-time, portable, long-read analysis of RNA and DNA. Unlike traditional short-read platforms, it detects nucleotide sequences by measuring ionic current changes as nucleic acids pass through nanoscale pores, enabling direct single-molecule sequencing and base modification detection. Its simplicity, flexibility, and capacity for ultra-long reads make it ideal for resolving complex genomic regions, structural variants, and full viral genomes. These advantages have accelerated its use in pathogen surveillance and outbreak response, especially in resource-limited settings. For chikungunya virus (CHIKV), nanopore sequencing allows rapid, culture-independent recovery of complete genomes from clinical and vector samples, enabling real-time tracking of viral diversity, evolution, and spread. Experiences from Ebola, Zika, and COVID-19 have demonstrated the power of portable sequencing, now applied to CHIKV monitoring. Advances in tools such as Guppy, Dorado, Minimap2, and Medaka enhance read quality, consensus accuracy, and downstream analyses. Despite challenges in basecalling and error correction, robust quality control pipelines ensure reliable results. Ongoing improvements in chemistry, flow cell design, and machine learning will further enhance fidelity and throughput, establishing nanopore sequencing as a cornerstone of CHIKV genomic surveillance and epidemic preparedness.

Overview of Chikungunya Virus Epidemiology, Biology, and Pathogenesis.

Authors: Castro EF, Filomatori C
Original abstract (English)
Chikungunya virus (CHIKV), an arthropod-borne alphavirus within the Togaviridae family, is transmitted primarily by Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus. The virus causes an acute febrile illness characterized by severe, often bilateral polyarthralgia, with potential progression to chronic musculoskeletal pain and rare systemic complications involving cardiovascular and neurological systems. CHIKV exhibits a spherical, enveloped virion (~70 nm) with T = 4 icosahedral symmetry, incorporating E1/E2 glycoprotein heterodimers that mediate receptor binding and membrane fusion. Its positive-sense RNA genome (~11.8 kb) encodes nonstructural proteins for replication and structural proteins for virion assembly. Replication occurs in cytoplasmic spherules, involving synthesis of genomic and subgenomic RNAs, followed by glycoprotein maturation and budding at the plasma membrane. Epidemiologically, CHIKV has expanded beyond Africa and Asia, with major outbreaks driven by adaptive mutations enhancing transmission via A. Since introduction to the Americas in 2013, the global incidence remains high, with >180,000 confirmed cases reported in 2025. Preventive strategies rely on vector control and vaccination; VLP-based vaccines (e.g., Vimkunya) show promise, while live-attenuated formulations face safety concerns. No licensed antivirals exist; current management is supportive, though investigational therapies targeting viral replication and immune modulation are under development.

Original abstract (English)
Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) is a positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus, which poses challenges for its study and genetic manipulation. Because direct mutagenesis of viral RNA genomes is technically impractical, reverse genetics systems are essential tools for investigating viral biology. To enable such approaches, infectious clones containing a full-length cDNA copy of the viral genome are constructed. The cDNA is positioned under the control of a bacteriophage RNA polymerase promoter, allowing commercial RNA polymerases to use the linearized plasmid as a template for the in vitro transcription of full-length viral genomic RNA (gRNA). Importantly, positive-sense viral genomes serve as mRNAs for the translation of viral proteins in a cellular environment, meaning that these transcripts contain all the information required to initiate viral replication. Following transfection into permissive cultured cells, viral proteins are expressed, enabling genome replication and, ultimately, the recovery of infectious particles from the cell supernatant. Here, we describe a detailed procedure for generating CHIKV particles through plasmid linearization, in vitro transcription, and subsequent RNA transfection.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract The live-attenuated vaccine IXCHIQ against chikungunya virus (CHIKV) was approved in 2023, with its efficacy evaluated using human neutralizing antibody titers as surrogate effectiveness criterion. Previous in vivo studies assessed its efficacy at high CHIKV-specific antibody titers, whereas its efficacy and antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) risks at suboptimal levels mimicking long-term immunity remain poorly characterized. Here, we assessed antibody-mediated protection following vaccination or natural infection by passively immunizing transiently immunocompromised mice with graded doses of pooled immune sera before challenge with two CHIKV genotypes or the related Mayaro virus (MAYV). Protection was evaluated using viral loads at 3 days post-infection and clinical parameters. Vaccine- and infection-induced antibodies conferred strong protection against both CHIKV genotypes, even at antibody concentrations nearing ELISA detection limits. In contrast, protection against MAYV required higher titers. Our findings align with previous studies supporting durable protection against CHIKV and partial cross-protection against MAYV.

Original abstract (English)
Alphaviruses are arthropod-borne pathogens that cause significant human disease, including severe arthritis and encephalitis. Among the four nonstructural proteins (nsP1-nsP4) that form the viral replication complex (RC), nsP3 is the least functionally characterized. Recent studies have shown that CHIKV-nsP3 forms a right-handed helical scaffold, while Venezuelan equine encephalitis virus (VEEV) nsP3 forms gel-like biomolecular condensates mediated by its Alphavirus Unique Domain (AUD). In this study, we used cryo-electron microscopy to reveal a closed annular stacking assembly of VEEV-nsP3, composed of C15-symmetric rings that stack axially without helical offset-fundamentally differing from the helical arrangement of CHIKV. Furthermore, we propose that the proteolytic cleavage at the nsP2/nsP3 junction regulates the annular to tubular transition. Our work provides insights into the structural diversity and dynamic regulation of alphavirus RC crown formation, with implications for understanding replication complex assembly across this viral lineage.

Importantly, the isolated compounds retained antiviral activity against ZIKV and CHIKV, indicating cross-arboviral efficacy. Together, these findings identify spirostane saponins as promising antiviral scaffolds and support their further development as broad-spectrum antiviral agents.

Question and background
This study aimed to investigate how simultaneous coinfection with DENV-1, DENV-2, and CHIKV affects the vector competence of Aedes aegypti mosquitoes, as vector-borne diseases caused by arboviruses represent a major public health threat in tropical regions.
Design and population
The study involved experimental infections of three to five-day-old female Aedes aegypti mosquitoes, divided into five groups (n = 200 per group) for simultaneous coinfection and four groups (n = 300 per group) for sequential coinfection, using membrane feeding as the infection method.
Core methods
Mosquitoes were infected via membrane feeding, and viral RNA quantification was conducted using RT-qPCR. Saliva samples were collected and stored at −70 °C, and transmission efficiency was assessed using BALB/c mice. Statistical analyses included the Kruskal–Wallis test and Shapiro–Wilk test for normality.
Main results
At 7 dpi, DENV-1 infection rates and viral loads were significantly reduced in the CD1 group compared with monoinfections, while DENV-2 showed increased viral loads in saliva. No significant differences in viral loads were detected at any time point for simultaneous coinfections, and CHIKV infection rates were significantly lower at 14 dpi compared with monoinfections.
Interpretation and novelty
The authors interpreted that DENV-1 and DENV-2 exhibit different transmission dynamics when coinfected with CHIKV, with DENV-1 showing reduced infection rates and viral loads, and DENV-2 showing increased viral loads in saliva, suggesting potential interactions affecting vector competence.

These findings inform the understanding of how multiple arboviruses may interact within mosquitoes, which is crucial for developing targeted surveillance and control strategies in regions where co-infections are common.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study had three independent biological experiments, and no significant inter-experiment variability was detected, indicating a moderate level of confidence in the results.

The review focuses on entomological investigations into yellow fever (YF) outbreaks in northern Ghana. The central question is to understand the vector dynamics and transmission risks associated with YF in the region.

The review is based on field data collected from entomological surveys in northern Ghana.
