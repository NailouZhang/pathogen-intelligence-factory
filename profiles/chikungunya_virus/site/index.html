Molecular characterization of chikungunya viruses associated with outbreaks in Kenya from 2017 to 2020.

Authors: Derrick Amon, Bonventure Juma, Joseph Muriuki, Caroline Ochieng, Naomi Chepkirui Koech, Gilbert Kikwai, Lydia Mwasi, Melvin Ochieng, Caroline Ngugi, Emily H. Davis
Original abstract (English)
Emerging outbreaks of the arbovirus chikungunya virus affect millions of individuals globally, causing debilitating arthralgia and fever. A study on the burden and etiologies of acute febrile illnesses (AFI) in Kenya reported an increase in CHIKV cases in Mombasa between December 2017 and December 2019. In January and February 2020, another outbreak of CHIKV occurred in Dadaab-Hagadera. Using next-generation sequencing on the Illumina MiSeq platform, we established molecular characteristics and phylogenetic differences of the CHIKVs collected from the two epidemiologically distinct settings. Sequenced viruses belonged to the Indian Ocean Lineage and clustered according to geographic region, with E1:K211E and E2:V264A mutations present in both settings. Additional mutations previously reported during Kenyan CHIKV outbreaks and suggested to influence CHIKV adaptation to Aedes albopictus, including E1:T82I and E1:V84D, were also identified. These molecular markers provide useful information on CHIKV spread and can help strengthen outbreak preparedness and response. Clinical features were described only for laboratory-confirmed CHIKV cases from the AFI surveillance cohort and compared with AFI cases testing negative for CHIKV. Sore muscles, headache, and convulsions were more frequently reported among CHIKV-positive participants, whereas diarrhea was less common. No differences were observed in the frequency of cough, skin rash, conjunctivitis, or vomiting. Given the limited number of confirmed CHIKV cases and the predominance of young children in the cohort, the reported clinical symptom associations should only be interpreted as exploratory and warrant confirmation in larger and more representative populations.

Chikungunya waves in 2025 signal risk of endemicity in China.

Authors: Kok Keng Tee, Dongkui Mu, Xueshan Xia
Original abstract (English)
Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) transmission in Guangdong, southern China, intensified markedly in 2025, with successive outbreak waves in Foshan and Jiangmen followed by wider dissemination across all 21 prefectures during a peak domestic travel period. By late 2025, more than 25,000 cases had been reported, suggesting a departure from the historically import-driven and geographically limited outbreaks previously observed in China. These observations raise the possibility of an early transition towards more sustained transmission, although repeated introductions followed by local amplification remain an alternative explanation. Key contributing factors include mobility-associated amplification, climate variability, flood-related expansion of mosquito breeding habitats, favourable urban ecology, and incomplete interruption of transmission during cooler months. Although winter temperatures are suboptimal for sustained transmission, vector persistence and possible vertical viral transmission may permit low-level circulation between epidemic waves. In this context, distinguishing repeated importation from local persistence becomes critical. Preventing endemic establishment will require sustained inter-epidemic surveillance, anticipatory vector control, and clearer policy pathways for deployable vaccination strategies.

Question and background
The study aimed to assess the risk of Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) becoming endemic in China, given the marked intensification of transmission in Guangdong during 2025 and the potential for sustained local spread.
Design and population
The study analyzed CHIKV transmission patterns in Guangdong, southern China, during 2025, focusing on outbreak waves in Foshan and Jiangmen, and their subsequent spread across all 21 prefectures during a peak domestic travel period.
Core methods
The study examined the factors contributing to the intensification of CHIKV transmission, including mobility-associated amplification, climate variability, flood-related expansion of mosquito breeding habitats, and urban ecology, while considering the possibility of repeated introductions and local amplification.
Main results
By late 2025, more than 25,000 cases had been reported, indicating a shift from historically import-driven and geographically limited outbreaks to a more widespread pattern of transmission.
Interpretation and novelty
The findings suggest a potential early transition towards sustained CHIKV transmission in China, highlighting the need to distinguish between repeated importation and local persistence to understand the epidemiological dynamics.
Scientific and public-health significance
The results underscore the importance of sustained inter-epidemic surveillance, anticipatory vector control, and the development of deployable vaccination strategies to prevent the establishment of endemic CHIKV transmission.
Limitations and evidence strength
The study relies on reported case numbers and environmental factors, which may not fully account for all transmission dynamics, and the evidence is moderate due to the absence of direct experimental confirmation of local persistence.

Original abstract (English)
Abstract The chikungunya virus (CHIKV) virus-like particle (VLP) vaccine, VIMKUNYA TM , has demonstrated favorable safety and immunogenicity throughout its clinical development program. Here, we present aggregated safety outcomes from 5 phase 2/3 trials and immunogenicity outcomes from 4 trials conducted at multiple US sites in healthy participants aged 12 to 95 years. Participants received a single intramuscular 40 μg dose of CHIKV VLP vaccine or placebo. Immunogenicity was assessed using serum neutralizing antibody (SNA) titers (NT 80 ), geometric mean titers (GMTs), and seroresponse rates at predefined timepoints. Preclinical nonhuman primate (NHP) data indicated that the regulatory clinical seroresponse threshold (NT 80 ≥100) is more conservative, approximately 4-fold higher than titers associated with sterilizing immunity, prompting exploratory analyses at additional thresholds: NT 80 ≥25.7 (associated with sterilizing immunity in NHPs) and NT 80 ≥15 (assay lower level of quantitation). This exploration of other relevant thresholds may provide valuable context for healthcare professionals and policy makers when evaluating immunization strategies. The CHIKV VLP vaccine elicited rapid and robust neutralizing antibody responses: 47% of recipients achieved NT 80 ≥100 at 7 days postvaccination (Day 8), rising to 96% at 2 weeks and 97% by 3 weeks postvaccination, with 85% maintaining seroresponse at 6 months (Day 183). At Day 8, 92% of participants achieved the NT 80 ≥15 assay threshold and 86% achieved the NHP NT 80 ≥25.7 biological threshold; at Day 183 these responses were maintained by 99% and 98%, respectively. Younger participants and females exhibited more rapid early responses and more sustained seroresponse at later timepoints, reflecting age- and sex- associated immunologic gradients. The vaccine was well-tolerated; most adverse events were mild or moderate, predominantly injection site pain, headache, fatigue, and myalgia. No vaccine-related safety signals or cases of CHIKV-like illness were identified. Older adults reported fewer solicited and unsolicited adverse events than younger age groups, and no treatment‐related serious adverse events observed, supporting a favorable safety profile across all age groups. Together, these findings demonstrate that a single dose of CHIKV VLP vaccine induces rapid, high-magnitude, and durable neutralizing immunity with a favorable safety profile, supporting its use in individuals aged ≥ 12 years and its suitability for broad public health implementation.

Authors: Fengling Chen, Jinsen He, Huihui Liu, Zhilin Wu, Jiwen Wu, Kunlu He, Hongjuan Wen, Xiaohua Li, Hongwei Wu, Lang Lang
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Using the 2025 Chikungunya fever (CHIK) outbreak in Chancheng District, Foshan City, this study applied a random forest (RF) regression model combined with SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) to explore predictive associations, nonlinear relationships and potential thresholds between environmental-social factors and village/community-level cumulative incidence, to inform stratified control of mosquito-borne diseases. METHODOLOGY/PRINCIPAL FINDINGS: In this cross-sectional ecological study of 143 villages/communities, the outcome was cumulative incidence, and nine candidate covariates were assessed, including the hospitalization isolation rate, construction-site density, and population density. Multicollinearity was checked using the variance inflation factor. Model fit was evaluated by the out-of-bag (OOB) R2, RMSE, and MAE, and variable importance by %IncMSE. Robustness was tested with 100 repeated runs, bootstrap thresholds from SHAP dependence plots, and a sensitivity analysis excluding the endogenous isolation rate. On the log(1 + incidence) scale, the OOB R2 was 0.206, with underestimation of high-incidence areas. The hospitalization isolation rate had the highest importance (%IncMSE = 18.43) and was negatively correlated with predictions (ρ = -0.749), but this likely reflects reverse causation and is predictive only. Construction-site density was strongly positive (ρ = 0.855), with a stable threshold near 12.8 sites/km2; it remained the most robust predictor after removing the isolation rate (%IncMSE = 8.54). CONCLUSIONS/SIGNIFICANCE: Construction-site density was the most robust environmental predictor, whereas population density contributed little. These exploratory, predictive associations-not causal effects-should guide risk stratification and require prospective validation with time-matched longitudinal data.

Original abstract (English)
Chikungunya virus (CHIKV), once confined to enzootic cycles in tropical forests of Africa and Southeast Asia, has re-emerged as a widespread human pathogen. Its resurgence is fueled by climate change, globalization, urbanization, and the expansion of competent Aedes mosquito vectors. In India, annual cases exceed 1 million, contributing to 70% of the regional burden. Epidemics in 2005–2006 (1.4 million cases) and recurrent outbreaks in 2016, 2019, and 2023–2025 have strained public health infrastructure. Traditional vector control strategies—larvicides, fogging, and hygiene campaigns—often fail at scale due to high costs, insecticide resistance, and socioeconomic disparities. Viral evolution, including E1-A226V mutations enhancing Aedes albopictus transmission, complicates vaccine and antiviral development. Chronic post-viral arthralgia affects 30–60% of patients, lasting years in some cases. Host-targeted antivirals (e.g., favipiravir) and emerging vaccines like IXCHIQ (approved 2023, 90% efficacy) and PXVX0317 (phase 3) are critical. The continued global spread of CHIKV underscores its potential to cause pandemics, posing challenges to international public health systems and necessitating coordinated surveillance, vector management, and vaccine deployment. India-specific trials addressing genetic diversity and prevalent comorbidities are essential. Sustained research, surveillance, and funding are needed to curb CHIKV infections and prevent future pandemics.

It also notes that superinfection exclusion and Wolbachia infection are important extrinsic factors that shape vector competence to CHIKV.
Controversies and gaps
The review identifies that the mechanisms of antiviral defense against alphaviruses in Aedes mosquitoes are not fully understood, particularly in comparison to the well-characterized responses to orthoflavivirus infections. There is limited evidence on the long-term effects of piRNAs and the role of specific immune pathways in persistent CHIKV infections. The review also notes that the evidence on immune evasion strategies by alphaviruses is still emerging and requires further investigation.
Research and practice implications
The review suggests that future research should focus on elucidating the molecular mechanisms of RNAi and canonical immune signaling pathways in Aedes mosquitoes against CHIKV. It also emphasizes the need for studies on the role of piRNAs and extrinsic factors such as Wolbachia infection in shaping vector competence. These findings could inform the development of more effective and sustainable arbovirus control strategies, including the use of RNAi-based interventions and the manipulation of microbial communities in mosquitoes.

Original abstract (English)
Arboviral infections, including West Nile virus (WNV), dengue virus (DENV), chikungunya virus (CHIKV), Zika virus (ZIKV), and, to a lesser extent, Oropouche virus (OROV), pose a heterogeneous and evolving challenge for pediatric health in Europe. The increasing presence of competent vectors and international travel has resulted in both imported infections and, for selected arboviruses, documented autochthonous transmission events. Although pediatric cases are often underrecognized, children may develop severe manifestations, including neuroinvasive disease and congenital infection. Available data on pediatric arboviral infections in Europe remain limited and fragmented, complicating accurate assessment of disease burden and clinical risk. This review summarizes current evidence on the epidemiology, clinical presentation, diagnostic challenges, and prevention of selected arboviral infections in children within the European context, highlighting persistent gaps in pediatric-specific data and implications for preparedness and public health.
