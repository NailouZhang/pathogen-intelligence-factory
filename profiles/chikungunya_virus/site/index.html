Developing a clinical case definition for Chikungunya fever in the Democratic Republic of the Congo
作者： Florian Vogt, Antoine Nkuba-Ndaye, Anja De Weggheleire, Sheila Makiala-Mandanda, Placide Mbala-Kingebeni, Benoit Mputu-Ngoyi, Eddy Kinganda-Lusamaki, Elisabeth Pukuta, Lorenzo Subissi, Birgit De Smet

Developing a clinical case definition for Chikungunya fever in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

Authors: Florian Vogt, Antoine Nkuba-Ndaye, Anja De Weggheleire, Sheila Makiala-Mandanda, Placide Mbala-Kingebeni, Benoit Mputu-Ngoyi, Eddy Kinganda-Lusamaki, Elisabeth Pukuta, Lorenzo Subissi, Birgit De Smet
Original abstract (English)
INTRODUCTION: Chikungunya fever presents a diagnostic challenge in Sub-Saharan Africa due to the absence of a region-specific case definition, especially in settings with high disease burden and diverse aetiologies of acute febrile illnesses. Our study aimed to develop an evidence-based clinical case definition for acute Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) infection suitable for use at resource-limited primary health care level. METHODS: We conducted a cross-sectional study in Kinshasa, Democratic Republic of the Congo, between June and November 2019 during an ongoing CHIKV outbreak. Patients aged ≥3 years with symptoms suggestive of acute CHIKV infection were enrolled. Clinical examination data and blood samples were collected for CHIKV diagnosis via PCR and ELISA. Malaria rapid diagnostic tests were also performed. We used Classification and Regression Tree (CART) analysis with 10-fold cross-validation and predictive logistic regression models with lasso penalty, with performance assessed via the Area under the Receiver Operating Characteristics Curve (AUC). RESULTS: Of 132 analysed patients, 40.2% had acute CHIKV infection. CART analysis identified knee and shoulder pain as potential predictors, however classifying only 67.4% of patients correctly while missing 71.7% of cases (cross-validated AUC 53%; 95%CI 43%-63%). Logistic regression achieved 61.1% accuracy, with a corresponding cross-validated AUC of 62% (95%CI 52%-72%). Exploratory analysis excluding malaria co-infections yielded similar CART performance, while logistic regression failed to identify any reliable predictors. CONCLUSIONS: While our study provides valuable insights into the clinical presentation of CHIKV in Sub-Saharan Africa, it highlights the challenges of developing a simple symptom-based diagnostic algorithm suitable for resource-limited primary health care settings. Certain joint pain patterns showed predictive value, however no model achieved sufficient accuracy for clinical use. Improved access to serological testing and integration of epidemiological context are essential for CHIKV diagnosis in such contexts. Further research is needed to refine clinical criteria and support outbreak response in resource-limited regions.

Dengue fever and chikungunya: prevalence, hematological parameters, and associated factors among febrile patients in eastern Ethiopia: A cross-sectional study

Authors: Getachew Kabew Mekonnen, Desalegn Admassu Ayana, Ahmed Mohammed, Addisu Alemu, Tadesse Shumie, Mesay Arkew, Haftu Asmerom, Delenasaw Yewhalaw
Original abstract (English)
Dengue fever and chikungunya are emerging arboviral diseases and are becoming major public health threats in tropical and subtropical regions. However, the actual burden and contributing factors are not well studied in Ethiopia. Thus, this study aimed to determine the prevalence of dengue fever and chikungunya and assess the hematological parameters and associated factors among febrile patients in eastern Ethiopia. An institution-based cross-sectional study was conducted in Dire Dawa city administration from October to December 2023. Sociodemographic and clinical information of patients were collected using a structured questionnaire and checklist. Hematological parameters were determined using the Mindray BC-3000 Plus automated hematology analyzer. The Kruskal-Wallis test, Spearman correlation test, and logistic regression were used during data analysis. A P-value of < .05 with a 95% confidence interval was considered statistically significant. A total of 576 febrile patients were investigated. The majority of the study participants, 312 (54.2%), were male, and 524 (91%) were rural residents. Nearly 327 (57%) cases were diagnosed with dengue fever, while 73 (13%) cases were for chikungunya. The type of visited healthcare institution (Sabian Comprehensive Hospital, AOR: 8.2 [95% CI: 4.02, 17.3] and Dilchora Referral Hospital, AOR: 2.3 [95% CI: 1.5, 3.7]), urban residence (AOR: 2.3, 95% CI: 1.37-7.7), government employment (AOR: 0.34 [0.12-0.99]), and patients' previous history of a similar AFI (acute febrile illness) AOR: 3.2 (95% CI: 1.02, 10.3) were significantly associated with empiric arboviral diseases. White blood cell, red blood cell, and platelet counts were significantly decreased among patients with DENV infection compared to other febrile patients (P < .001). In addition, a statistically significant weak negative correlation was noted between the duration of fever and white blood cell count, red blood cell count, and platelet count among dengue patients. High rates of clinical dengue fever and chikungunya cases were observed. Some socio-demographic and institutional factors were more likely to be significantly associated with clinically diagnosed arboviral diseases. Furthermore, significant differences were observed in some hematological parameters between cases with dengue/chikungunya diseases and those without. The findings highlighted the need for early diagnosis and urgent action to downplay the undesirable outcomes of arboviral diseases.

MoS2/carbon nanotubes nanocomposite thin films as platform for electrochemical dengue detection.

Authors: Patricia S Araújo, Gustavo Martins, Luiz H Marcolino-Jr, Márcio F Bergamini, Aldo J. G. Zarbin
Original abstract (English)
Sensitive electrochemical immunosensors are important for addressing global health challenges, particularly for the rapid and affordable diagnosis of infectious diseases such as dengue fever. This work reports the preparation and characterization of molybdenum disulphide/carbon nanotubes (MoS2/CNT) thin films, obtained through the liquid-liquid interfacial route, and their application as active materials in an electrochemical immunosensor for the detection of dengue virus nonstructural protein 1 (NS1), a key biomarker for early diagnosis. The nanocomposites were prepared starting from two different MoS2 samples (laboratory-synthesized or commercial) and characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), X-ray diffractometry (XRD), Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), Raman spectroscopy, cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). The film based on laboratory-synthesized MoS2 exhibited smaller flakes, higher surface area and superior electrochemical performance compared to the commercial ones. For the immunosensor assembly, a stencil-printed, lab-made electrode was coated with the MoS2/CNT film, followed by antibody/BSA immobilization. A label-free detection mode was adopted, and under EIS conditions, a log-linear dynamic range of 4-1000 ng mL-1 and a low detection limit of 8 ng mL-1 were achieved. High selectivity was confirmed against other arboviruses (Chikungunya and Yellow Fever), and reliable performance was achieved in diluted human serum. The proposed MoS2/CNT thin-film is a promising platform for the future development of low-cost, miniaturized, and sensitive point-of-care diagnostic devices for dengue and related infectious diseases.

Authors: Ikeoluwa Feyisayo Aina, Oluyinka Oladele Opaleye, Dorcas Ayomide Atilade, Foluke Julianah Adetutu
Original abstract (English)
Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) causes an acute febrile illness with debilitating arthralgia, and serological assays remain central to its diagnosis and surveillance. However, antibody-based results are complicated by cross-reactivity in settings where multiple arboviruses co-circulate. This review distinguishes three related but distinct phenomena affecting CHIKV serology: biological cross-reactivity, arising from genuine antigenic overlap between related viruses; assay-associated non-target reactivity, in which a diagnostic platform generates a positive signal without an established biological relationship between the viruses; and diagnostic misclassification, the downstream epidemiological consequence of conflating the two. Applying this framework to five virus pairs, we find that CHIKV–O'nyong-nyong virus (ONNV), CHIKV–Mayaro virus (MAYV) and CHIKV–Ross River virus (RRV) relationships are supported by shared alphavirus antigenic architecture and, in several studies, by functional cross-neutralisation. By contrast, CHIKV reactivity reported in dengue virus (DENV)- and Zika virus (ZIKV)-associated sera lacks an established biological basis and is better classified as assay-associated non-target reactivity of unresolved mechanism. These distinctions matter: unadjusted seropositivity can inflate or obscure CHIKV transmission estimates, and no single diagnostic platform resolves the problem for every virus pair. We argue that CHIKV diagnosis in co-endemic settings requires a layered strategy: molecular detection during the acute phase, antigenically informed and multiplex serology thereafter, and neutralisation-based confirmation where attribution is critical, combined with cross-reactivity-aware statistical modelling for surveillance. Strengthening assay validation and confirmatory testing, particularly in African settings such as Nigeria where CHIKV, DENV and related alphaviruses circulate together, is essential for improving diagnostic accuracy and epidemiological inference.

Original abstract (English)
Background: Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) infection is an emerging disease of growing concern to international travelers due to the expanding geographic distribution of competent vectors, climate change, and frequent outbreaks in endemic and previously unaffected regions. Several additional vaccine platforms remain under investigation, although some candidates have been discontinued. Conclusions: The recent approval of chikungunya vaccines marks a significant milestone in the field of travel medicine. However, uncertainties regarding long-term protection, safety in specific populations, and optimal targeting of travelers remain. Individualized risk–benefit assessment is essential, and further data are needed to refine vaccination strategies for travelers and populations at risk.

Cross-Family Mechanistic Analysis of Plant Alkaloids Against Neglected Arboviruses and Related RNA Viruses

Authors: Márcia Régis, Mario Fernando Sanchez Moreno, Hugo Germain, Natacha Mérindol, Isabel Desgagné‐Penix, I. Desgagné-Penix
Original abstract (English)
Neglected arboviruses dengue (DENV), Zika (ZIKV), yellow fever (YFV), Japanese encephalitis (JEV), and chikungunya collectively affect hundreds of millions of people annually, yet no specific antiviral drug has been approved for any of them. Alkaloids, nitrogen-containing specialized metabolites produced by diverse plant families, have emerged as a promising source of broad-spectrum antiviral scaffolds. This review compiles and critically analyzes over 100 alkaloid-virus pairs across several RNA virus families, providing a comparative mechanistic analysis. Lycorine, narciclasine, emetine, and berbamine, among others, exhibit potent activity against phylogenetically distant viruses, with the most potent activities reported against flaviviruses (narciclasine: EC50 0.02 μM against DENV, ZIKV, YFV, and JEV; pancratistatine: 0.0063 μM against ZIKV). Structure-activity analysis of multiple alkaloid classes identifies key pharmacophoric features, including the phenanthridine nucleus (lycorine derivatives) and the bis-benzylisoquinoline scaffold (tetrandrine, berbamine), as determinants of antiviral potency, selectivity, and broad-spectrum activity. Genetic resistance data in West Nile virus challenge the widely accepted model of lycorine as a direct nucleoside inhibitor, instead pointing toward the involvement of the membrane-associated NS4A-2K-NS4B replication complex, though direct validation remains limited for other flaviviruses. Converging structural, biochemical, and transcriptomic evidence suggests that ribosome-mediated translational stress may represent an additional host-directed antiviral mechanism for isoquinoline-type alkaloids, though the causal chain from ribosome binding to activation of the integrated stress response and to antiviral effect has not been established. The present analysis highlights that in vivo validation remains limited to a few alkaloid-virus pairs. Unbiased target deconvolution and formal testing of the ribosome/integrated stress response hypothesis stand out as essential research priorities.

Original abstract (English)
This scoping review systematically maps the molecular (PCR-based), serological, and antigen-detection targets used across published studies for the laboratory diagnosis of acute febrile illness (AFI)-causing pathogens, including dengue virus, chikungunya virus, scrub typhus (Orientia tsutsugamushi), leptospirosis, and typhoid fever (Salmonella Typhi). Despite widespread use of PCR, ELISA, immunofluorescence, agglutination, and rapid antigen-detection assays for AFI diagnosis, no prior review has comprehensively catalogued the specific gene targets, antigens, and epitopes used across these platforms and pathogens. This review addresses that gap by systematically searching PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane CENTRAL, WHO Global Index Medicus, and Europe PMC, followed by structured data extraction and narrative/tabular synthesis of diagnostic targets by pathogen and assay type, following PRISMA-ScR guidance. Findings are expected to identify commonly used viral biomarkers, variations in target selection across platforms, and gaps in target standardisation relevant to the development of next-generation multiplex and point-of-care diagnostics, particularly in endemic settings such as India.

Infectious Diseases Kerala has experienced chikungunya outbreaks in the past, making continued monitoring important during periods of heavy rainfall. What is chikungunya? Chikungunya is a viral infection caused by the chikungunya virus. It is transmitted when a person is bitten by an infected Aedes mosquito. The infection is not generally spread directly from one person to another through casual contact, coughing or sneezing. Instead, a mosquito becomes infected after biting a person carrying the virus and can subsequently transmit the infection when it bites another person. The mosquitoes responsible for transmission are primarily active during the daytime, including the early morning and late afternoon. Symptoms can include severe joint pain Chikungunya commonly begins suddenly, with fever and intense joint pain among its most characteristic symptoms. Other symptoms can include headache, muscle pain, fatigue, skin rashes and swelling around the joints. Pain can particularly affect the hands, wrists, ankles and feet. While the fever often improves within several days, joint pain may persist for weeks or months in some patients. In certain cases, prolonged joint inflammation and stiffness can interfere with routine activities and mobility. Weather This prolonged joint pain is one of the major reasons chikungunya can cause significant illness even though it is rarely fatal. How does chikungunya spread? The virus spreads primarily through infected Aedes mosquitoes. These mosquitoes commonly breed in small collections of stagnant water around homes and communities. Potential breeding sites include water-filled buckets, flower pots, discarded containers, old tyres, coconut shells, coolers and uncovered water-storage vessels. During the monsoon, rainfall can quickly create additional breeding sites. Even small quantities of stagnant water can provide suitable conditions for mosquito larvae. Health officials therefore stress the importance of regularly inspecting homes and surrounding areas and removing standing water. Diagnosis and treatment Chikungunya is generally diagnosed by healthcare professionals based on symptoms, exposure history and laboratory testing where required. There is currently no specific antiviral treatment routinely used to cure chikungunya. Treatment mainly focuses on managing symptoms and supporting recovery. Adequate rest and fluid intake are important, while medicines such as paracetamol may be used for fever and pain when advised by a healthcare professional. Insects & Entomology Because chikungunya and dengue can have overlapping symptoms, people with fever and other suspected symptoms should seek medical advice rather than self-medicate. Medical evaluation can help determine the appropriate tests and treatment. Complications require attention Although most chikungunya infections are not fatal, some patients can experience prolonged or severe symptoms. Persistent joint pain, swelling and stiffness can continue after the acute infection has resolved. In some cases, prolonged inflammation may affect mobility and daily activities. Rare complications can involve the nervous system, heart or eyes. Severe illness is more likely to be a concern among older adults, newborns, pregnant women around the time of delivery and people with certain underlying health conditions. Patients experiencing severe symptoms, persistent fever, significant weakness, dehydration or other concerning signs should seek prompt medical attention. Residents can help by removing stagnant water from their homes and surroundings at least once a week and keeping water-storage containers properly covered. Discarded containers, tyres and other objects that can collect rainwater should also be cleared or stored in a way that prevents water accumulation. Health Conditions People can further reduce mosquito bites by wearing full-sleeved clothing, using mosquito repellents and ensuring that windows and doors are appropriately screened. Community-level action is equally important because mosquito breeding sites can exist outside individual homes. Kerala Reports 105 Chikungunya Cases Amid Monsoon Surge

News Brief
Kerala, India, has reported 105 confirmed and 62 probable chikungunya cases as of August 4, 2026, with the majority concentrated in Thiruvananthapuram district. Health officials attribute the surge to monsoon-driven mosquito breeding, particularly of Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus, which also transmit dengue. Authorities have intensified vector-control measures, surveillance, and public awareness campaigns to curb transmission. While chikungunya is rarely fatal, prolonged joint pain can significantly impact patients, especially older adults and those with underlying conditions. There is no specific antiviral treatment; management focuses on symptom relief and hydration. Experts warn that eliminating stagnant water and preventing mosquito breeding remain critical to reducing spread during the ongoing rainy season.

Why Monsoon Conditions Raise The Risk The rise in cases has been linked by health experts to favorable mosquito-breeding conditions during the rainy season. Rainwater can accumulate in discarded containers, tyres, flower pots, coconut shells, drains and uncovered water-storage vessels. TS Anish, Professor of Community Medicine at the School of Public Health, Kerala University of Health Sciences, noted that monsoon conditions favor the breeding of both Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus. Higher mosquito populations can allow chikungunya transmission to continue efficiently. This makes eliminating breeding sites an important part of the state's response. Regularly emptying stagnant water and preventing containers from collecting rainwater can reduce the number of places where mosquitoes reproduce. What Is Chikungunya? Chikungunya (1✔ ✔Trusted Source About Chikungunya Virus Go to source) is a viral disease caused by the chikungunya virus and is primarily transmitted through the bite of an infected Aedes mosquito. These mosquitoes are generally active during the daytime, including the early morning and late afternoon. Unlike illnesses that spread through respiratory droplets, chikungunya is primarily transmitted through infected mosquitoes. A mosquito can acquire the virus when it bites an infected person and subsequently transmit it to another person. Mosquito Diseases Mosquito-borne diseases, like malaria, filaria, dengue, etc are common in places conducive of mosquito breeding. Swamps, ponds and stagnated drainage provide optimal breeding ground for mosquitoes. Severe Joint Pain Is A Key Symptom Chikungunya begins with a sudden fever accompanied by intense joint pain. Other symptoms can include headache, muscle pain, fatigue, joint swelling and skin rashes. While the acute fever often improves within several days, joint pain can persist for weeks or months in some patients. Prolonged inflammation and stiffness can affect mobility and interfere with everyday activities. The illness is generally not considered highly fatal, but its ability to cause prolonged joint-related symptoms makes it a significant public health concern. Chikungunya And Dengue Can Be Confused One challenge during mosquito-borne disease outbreaks is that chikungunya and dengue can initially produce similar symptoms, particularly fever, headache, muscle aches and fatigue. Because the two diseases require different clinical considerations, people experiencing persistent or sudden fever should seek medical advice rather than assuming the infection is chikungunya. Healthcare professionals can determine whether laboratory testing is required and recommend appropriate treatment. No Specific Antiviral Treatment There is currently no routinely used specific antiviral treatment that eliminates chikungunya infection. Treatment generally focuses on relieving symptoms, maintaining adequate hydration and allowing the body to recover. Because dengue can produce similar symptoms, people should consult healthcare professionals before taking medicines for fever or pain. Medical guidance is particularly important when symptoms are severe, persistent or worsening. Who May Need Extra Care? Although most chikungunya infections are not fatal, severe or prolonged illness can be particularly concerning for vulnerable groups. Older adults, newborns and people with certain underlying health conditions may be at greater risk of complications. Persistent joint pain can also continue after the initial infection has resolved, sometimes affecting a person's ability to work or carry out normal activities. How To Prevent Chikungunya With Kerala's monsoon continuing, preventing mosquito breeding remains one of the most important ways to reduce transmission. Residents should regularly check their homes and surroundings for stagnant water. Water containers should be kept covered, while discarded tyres, bottles, coconut shells, flower pots and other objects capable of collecting rainwater should be emptied, removed or stored appropriately. People can also reduce mosquito bites by wearing long-sleeved clothing, using mosquito repellents and keeping doors and windows screened where possible. Community participation is particularly important because mosquitoes can breed outside individual homes as well. Regular cleaning of neighborhoods, drains and public spaces can therefore complement household-level prevention. Thiruvananthapuram At The Centre Of Concern The concentration of cases in Thiruvananthapuram has made the district a particular focus of attention. The reported increase has prompted authorities to strengthen surveillance and mosquito-control measures while increasing public awareness about prevention. The situation also highlights the broader challenge Kerala faces during the monsoon, when mosquito populations can increase rapidly. Since Aedes mosquitoes transmit both chikungunya and dengue, reducing their breeding sites can help lower the risk of both diseases. What Happens Next? With 105 confirmed and 62 probable chikungunya cases recorded as of August 4, continued surveillance will be important as Kerala moves through the monsoon season. Health officials are focusing on monitoring affected areas, controlling mosquito populations and encouraging residents to remove stagnant water. The effectiveness of these measures will depend not only on government action but also on regular participation from households and communities. For residents, the message remains straightforward: remove stagnant water, prevent mosquito bites and seek medical advice if fever and severe joint pain develop.
