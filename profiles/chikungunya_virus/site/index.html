HLA polymorphisms potentially associated with susceptibility to chronic post-chikungunya inflammatory joint disease: an exploratory study.

Authors: Barbara Barreto Oliveira, Camila Alves Maia da Silva, Izabella Brito de Souza, Milena Gomes Cabral, Ester Cerdeira Sabino, Maria Lucia Carnevale Marin, Jorge Kalil, Cristina Dos Santos Ferreira, Mariana Severo Ramundo
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: A substantial proportion of Chikungunya virus (CHIKV)-infected patients progress to post-Chikungunya chronic inflammatory joint disease (pCHIKV-CIJD), yet host genetic determinants of this outcome remain poorly understood. HLA polymorphisms are established risk factors for inflammatory arthropathies, but their role in CHIKV chronification has not been investigated. OBJECTIVES: To explore associations between HLA alleles and progression to pCHIKV-CIJD in a Brazilian cohort. STUDY DESIGN: We performed in silico HLA typing from whole-blood RNA-seq data of 59 CHIKV-infected patients (32 pCHIKV-CIJD and 27 non-pCHIKV-CIJD) using HLAminer. We compared allelic frequencies at two-digit resolution using Fisher's exact test with Bonferroni correction. Additional analyses included supertype classification, heterozygosity scoring, KIR ligand grouping, and logistic regression adjusted for sex, age, and race. RESULTS: HLA-A02 was enriched among pCHIKV-CIJD patients (35.9% vs. 14.8%; OR = 3.19, 95% CI: 1.21-9.21, p = 0.012), remaining nominally significant after covariate adjustment (adjusted OR = 3.57, 95% CI: 1.09-11.73, p = 0.036). The B62 supertype was also more frequent in the chronic group (15.6% vs. 3.7%; OR = 4.76, 95% CI: 0.95-46.73, p = 0.037). Chronic patients showed lower HLA heterozygosity (p = 0.043). However, no association survived the Bonferroni correction. CONCLUSIONS: Our data support a potential association between HLA-A02 carriage and an increased risk of pCHIKV-CIJD. These findings are hypothesis-generating and require validation in larger, independent cohorts.

Targeting conserved interaction surfaces of alphavirus nsP4 supports the rational design of live-attenuated vaccine candidates.

Authors: Xiao-Yao Yang, Evelin Väljaots, Kai-Yu Li, Krystyna Naumenko, Sandra Koit, Yaw Bia Tan, Xiang Liu, Victor Rodrigues de Melo Costa, Suresh Mahalingam, Da-Hai Luo
Original abstract (English)
UNLABELLED: Alphaviruses are emerging arboviruses that pose a growing threat to global public health; however, effective interventions remain limited. Recent structural studies of the chikungunya virus (CHIKV) replicase core revealed that the viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase nsP4 interacts with both nsP1 and nsP2 and is predicted to engage viral RNA. The conservation and multifunctionality of nsP4 make it a challenging but promising target for rational attenuation. Here, we evaluated 58 structure-guided mutations in nsP4 predicted to disrupt interactions with nsP1, nsP2, or viral RNA using a trans-replicase system. Sixteen selected mutations were further analyzed in a CHIKV-o'nyong-nyong virus chimera and Semliki Forest virus (SFV). Most mutants were viable but showed varying degrees of attenuation in mammalian and mosquito cells. Viruses carrying mutations in predicted RNA-interaction sites frequently acquired pseudoreversions and/or second-site compensatory changes, whereas those harboring mutations affecting nsP1 or nsP2 interfaces were more genetically stable. Three substitutions representing defects in nsP1 interaction (F164A), predicted RNA interaction (K174S), and nsP2 interaction (D393A) were further characterized in authentic CHIKV and displayed attenuation consistent with that observed in SFV. In vivo, all three variants showed reduced pathogenicity in the SFV mouse model, with F164A being avirulent, immunogenic, and conferring complete protection against lethal challenge. In a CHIKV mouse model, these mutations attenuated disease and reduced serum virus titers and inflammatory cell infiltration. Together, these findings provide a molecular framework for the rational design of live-attenuated alphavirus vaccine candidates and support broader evaluation of nsP4-based attenuation. IMPORTANCE: Alphaviruses are an increasing global health concern, with hundreds of thousands of chikungunya cases reported worldwide in 2025; however, vaccine options remain limited, and no specific antiviral treatment is available. Recent structural studies have revealed conserved functional interfaces in the viral RNA polymerase nsP4, but their value for attenuation had not been systematically explored. Here, using structure-guided mutagenesis, validation in multiple viral systems, and in vivo analysis, we show that selected nsP4 mutations can strongly attenuate replication and pathogenicity while preserving immunogenicity. These findings identify conserved nsP4 interfaces as a shared vulnerability of alphaviruses and provide a molecular framework for the rational design of broadly applicable live-attenuated vaccines while also highlighting nsP4 as a functionally constrained target relevant to antiviral development.

A machine learning approach for differential classification of chikungunya virus: an explainable AI study based on surveillance data.

Authors: Natacha Usanase, Berna Uzun, Dilber Uzun Ozsahin
Original abstract (English)
PURPOSE: Mosquito-borne viral diseases such as chikungunya usually present similar clinical symptoms, which makes them hard to differentiate without laboratory confirmatory tests. Therefore, this work retrospectively developed interpretable machine learning models to improve the classification of chikungunya using routinely collected data. METHODS: Brazilian surveillance data, with 18,127 records, were analyzed, and eight different machine learning algorithms were trained and five-fold cross-validated. In addition, SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) and permutation feature importance described model behavior. RESULTS: In univariable analysis, arthralgia (OR = 5.84, 95% CI 5.39-6.33) and fever (OR = 5.85, 95% CI 5.29-6.46) were most positively associated with Chikungunya. The macro-AUC values ranged from 0.89 to 0.90 for all models in the multiclass analysis. The Chikungunya-versus-Non-Chikungunya task demonstrated moderate discrimination. The Chikungunya-versus-Dengue scenario produced the highest performance, with an AUC of 0.97 in most models. CONCLUSION: The explainability mechanism characterized how fitted models utilized available clinical and demographic predictors, with acute symptom variables contributing prominently to predictions. The results demonstrate the potential of using an interpretable model-assisted approach for arboviral classification. Importantly, prospective, temporal, and external geographic validation, with harmonized data, are required before clinical decision-support or point-of-care implementation can be considered.

Original abstract (English)
Alphaviruses are positive-sense single-stranded RNA viruses belonging to the family Togaviridae; they are also part of the non-taxonomic group of arboviruses and constitute a group of arboviruses of major which are of great clinical and epidemiological relevance worldwide. These viruses can be broadly classified into encephalitic and arthritogenic alphaviruses. Important examples include Chikungunya virus (CHIKV), Mayaro virus (MAYV), Venezuelan equine encephalitis virus (VEEV), Ross River virus (RRV), Sindbis virus (SINV), and Semliki Forest virus (SFV), among others. Despite their substantial impact on global public health, characterized by the risk of new outbreaks, high transmission rates, and frequently debilitating or potentially fatal clinical manifestations, effective and specific therapeutic options, including antivirals and vaccines, remain limited for most alphavirus infections. This represents an important public health vulnerability, particularly in tropical and subtropical regions. To address this concerning scenario, a comprehensive understanding of the complex interactions between alphaviruses and their hosts is essential for identifying determinants of viral pathogenicity and, consequently, factors associated with disease severity. In this context, transcriptomic and gene expression profiling approaches have become valuable tools for investigating host responses to viral infection. These approaches provide high-resolution information on changes in host gene expression during Alphavirus infection, contributing to the elucidation of molecular mechanisms underlying disease pathogenesis. They can also provide important insights into the identification of biomarkers and molecular signatures, the upregulation or downregulation of specific genes, and the modulation of inflammatory, oxidative, immune, and other biological pathways. Accordingly, this review provides a comprehensive systematic-narrative synthesis of studies investigating alphavirus–host interactions through gene expression profiling. Studies assessing host gene expression in response to alphavirus infection using approaches such as RNA sequencing (RNA-seq), single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), microarrays, RT-qPCR, and other relevant gene expression technologies will be considered across different host species, tissues, experimental models, and infection conditions. Particular emphasis will be placed on studies investigating how alphaviruses modulate key pathogenic pathways, immune responses, inflammation, tissue-specific damage, and other biological processes associated with infection. By integrating evidence from host gene expression profiles, this review aims to highlight the major transcriptional alterations and biological pathways associated with alphavirus pathogenesis. Such integration may provide insights into virus–host interactions and support the identification of candidate molecular targets for therapeutic development, early diagnostic tools, and effective preventive strategies. Therefore, the general objective of this study is to integrate the available evidence on the modulation of host gene expression during alphavirus infection, seeking to elucidate virus–host interactions, molecular mechanisms associated with pathogenesis, and potential key factors involved in these processes. The specific objectives are to: Describe the state of the art regarding alphavirus infections, providing a clinical and epidemiological context for these viruses; Characterize host transcriptional responses to alphavirus infection, identifying similarities and differences in the molecular mechanisms activated or suppressed across different alphaviruses and host systems; Describe molecular mechanisms associated with alphavirus pathogenesis and other biological processes through the integration of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and enriched biological pathways; Map relevant genes and biological pathways associated with different stages and processes of alphavirus infection, across different experimental systems, including in vitro, in vivo, and publicly available transcriptomic datasets; Identify key genes and molecular factors involved in alphavirus–host interactions and explore their potential as therapeutic targets; Identify knowledge gaps, recurrent patterns, and sources of heterogeneity in the existing literature on alphavirus-associated host gene expression. Through this integrative approach, the review is expected to generate sufficient evidence to support hypotheses and future investigations into therapeutic interventions, including the manipulation, editing, or silencing of specific host genes whose modulation or inhibition may impair viral replication while preserving host-cell or host-organism viability. Original abstract (English)
Chikungunya (CHIK) is a viral disease transmitted to humans through the bites of Aedes mosquitoes infected with the chikungunya virus (CHIKV). CHIKV has been imported annually to Florida in the last decade due to Miami’s crucial location as a hub for international travel, particularly from Central and South America, including Brazil, where CHIK is endemic. This work addresses the geographic spread of CHIK, incorporating factors such as human movement, temperature dependency, as well as vertical transmission, and incubation periods, for different patches. Central to the model is the integration of a multipatch framework and the inclusion of human movement between endemic Brazilian states and Florida in the numerical analysis. We establish crucial correlations between the mosquito reproduction number [Formula: see text] and the disease reproduction number [Formula: see text] with the disease dynamics in a multipatch environment, encompassing not only from a theoretical perspective but also from numerical simulations. More specifically, after establishing the well-posedness of the multipatch model problem, we present conditions to ensure the uniform persistence of the disease. Next, using real population and temperature data we are able to determine all parameters that are temperature-dependent for both Brazil and Miami-Dade County and apply a two-patch model to calibrate the CHIK data from Brazil and Miami-Dade County. Finally, via sensitivity analysis we discuss the disease dynamics under different scenarios and make future projections, offering insights for potential effective control strategies, as well as addressing the timing for these strategies to be adopted.

The model is analyzed for well-posedness and uniform persistence of the disease, with numerical simulations used to explore disease dynamics and future projections.
Consensus and conclusions
The study establishes a crucial correlation between the mosquito reproduction number and the disease reproduction number in a multipatch environment, providing insights into the spread of CHIKV between Brazil and Florida. It also highlights the importance of human movement and environmental factors in shaping disease transmission patterns.
Controversies and gaps
The review acknowledges the need for more detailed data on human movement patterns and temperature variations to improve the accuracy of the model. Additionally, the study notes that the model's predictions depend on the assumptions made about parameter values and environmental conditions.
Research and practice implications
The findings suggest the importance of implementing targeted control strategies in regions with high human mobility and temperature suitability for CHIKV transmission. The study also emphasizes the need for further research on the environmental and demographic factors influencing the spread of CHIKV.

Federal surveillance data current through August 11 show 89 travel-associated cases reported to the national arbovirus system from US states, plus one in a US territory, and zero locally acquired cases anywhere in the country. That combination is the whole story. The virus keeps arriving in the United States inside returning travelers, and so far this year it has not established itself in local mosquito populations the way dengue has in parts of Florida. The pattern shifts the risk from a neighborhood question to a trip planning question. For families, the practical consequence is narrow but real. Anyone booking travel to the Caribbean, Latin America, South Asia, Southeast Asia, parts of Africa, or several Indian Ocean islands is making a decision about mosquito precautions and, in some cases, vaccination. Anyone returning with fever and severe joint pain needs to tell a clinician where they have been. A Mosquito Illness Arriving by Airplane, Not by Backyard Chikungunya became a nationally notifiable condition in 2015, and the counts since then have swung with international outbreak cycles. Federal figures show 33 travel associated cases in US states in 2020, 152 in 2023, 241 in 2024 and 628 in 2025. Against that trend line, the current figures are preliminary and incomplete rather than reassuring. Arbovirus reporting lags because cases move from clinician to laboratory to state health department to the federal system, and the published national total is a snapshot that trails what states are seeing. The agency's areas at risk page, updated in early September, lists active outbreak notices for Bolivia, specifically the Santa Cruz and Cochabamba departments, along with northwest Costa Rica, French Guiana, Mauritius, Nicaragua and Suriname. A separate list flags ten countries where US travelers face elevated exposure even without a declared outbreak: Brazil, Colombia, India, Indonesia, Mexico, Nigeria, Pakistan, Peru, the Philippines and Thailand. Countries reach that list by producing a median of at least one case a year in US travelers over five years. Two locally acquired cases were reported in US states in 2025, in Florida and New York, the first since a single Texas case in 2015. None have been reported so far this year. Florida's Own Count Shows Where the Cases Land State surveillance offers a sharper local picture than the national total, though the two should not be added together. Federal tallies count cases by the patient's state of residence, while Florida maps them by county of exposure, and the two systems use different reporting cutoffs. Florida's weekly arbovirus surveillance report for the week ending August 29 counted 55 travel associated chikungunya cases with onset this year. Miami-Dade County accounted for 29 of them and Hillsborough for eight, with the rest scattered across eleven other counties. The same report recorded no locally acquired chikungunya cases in the state, alongside 73 locally acquired dengue cases in five counties. The countries of origin are the most useful detail in that report, and they do not match the federal risk lists. Forty of Florida's 55 cases were acquired in Cuba, which carries neither an outbreak notice nor a place on the elevated risk list. Nicaragua accounted for eight. That gap is a reminder that outbreak notices reflect what agencies have confirmed and prioritized, not a complete map of where the virus is circulating. The geography is not accidental. Florida combines heavy travel volume to the Caribbean and Latin America with a resident population that has family ties across the region, and it runs one of the most detailed mosquito surveillance programs in the country. Both species capable of transmitting the virus, Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus, are established across the southern United States, and a returning traveler bitten during the viremic phase is how a travel case becomes a local one. Joint Pain That Can Outlast the Fever Most people infected with chikungunya develop symptoms, which distinguishes it from several other mosquito-borne viruses. Illness typically begins with sudden fever and severe joint pain, often in the hands and feet, and can include headache, muscle pain, joint swelling, and rash. Those typical symptoms overlap heavily with dengue, which is why travel history matters at the point of diagnosis. The acute phase usually lasts about a week. The joint pain does not always follow that schedule. A meaningful share of patients report arthritis like pain persisting for months, and older adults and people with existing joint disease tend to have the harder recoveries. Deaths are rare. Newborns infected around the time of delivery, adults aged 65 and older, and people with heart disease, diabetes or high blood pressure face the highest risk of severe illness. There is no antiviral treatment. Care is supportive, focused on fluids, rest and pain control, and clinicians generally rule out dengue before recommending certain pain medicines because the two illnesses look similar early on. Anyone who develops fever within two weeks of returning from an affected area should contact a clinician and mention the travel history, since testing is not routine without it. Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, confusion, or difficulty breathing warrant urgent evaluation. Vaccination "is recommended for travelers visiting an area with an outbreak." For countries with elevated risk but no declared outbreak, vaccination may be considered for travelers planning an extended stay. That framing means a two week beach trip and a six month work assignment are different decisions, and both belong in a pre travel consultation rather than a self assessment. Travel clinics can advise on eligibility, since vaccine recommendations differ by age and health status, and pregnancy is handled separately. Current travel health notices are worth checking close to departure, since the outbreak list changes. Mosquito precautions apply regardless of vaccination status. "The cases we share here aren't typical misuse, but rather examples of the most notable and novel threat activity we've identified to date," it stressed.
