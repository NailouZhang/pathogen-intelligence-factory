Autochthonous chikungunya virus outbreak in Verona province, Italy, 2025.

Authors: Concetta Castilletti, Leonardo Ancillotti, Antonio Mori, Silvia Accordini, Michela Deiana, Samuele Cheri, Natasha Gianesini, Lorena Maria Chesini, Simone Malagò, Andrea Matucci
Original abstract (English)
Abstract Background Chikungunya virus (CHIKV), primarily transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes, has increasingly caused autochthonous outbreaks in temperate regions, including Italy. In 2025, a local CHIKV outbreak occurred in Verona province, Veneto Region. We report the findings of integrated epidemiological, virological, and entomological investigations. Methods In 2025, a dedicated surveillance protocol was implemented to assess local transmission of arboviral infections in Verona province, enabling early detection of a local CHIKV circulation. Following notification of the index case, investigations were conducted in accordance with the national and regional arbovirus surveillance plans, including comprehensive epidemiological, virological, and entomological analyses. Results The index case was identified on 6 August 2025, with retrospective detection of the first case with symptom onset on 31 July; the primary case was not identified. As of 15 October, 69 cases were reported (62 confirmed, 7 probable), with a mean age of 55 years; 91.3% had identifiable epidemiological links, mainly clustered in two municipalities of the Verona province. Most cases (76.8%) occurred between mid-August and mid-September. The outbreak lasted approximately fifteen weeks, from the end of July to the mid of October. CHIKV-RNA was detectable in whole blood and, less frequently, urine only within the first five days after symptom onset, with higher viral loads in blood. CHIKV-specific IgM antibodies were detectable from days 4–5 and IgG from days 7–10. A total of 225 Aedes albopictus specimens were collected, all testing negative for CHIKV. Phylogenetic analysis revealed a highly homogeneous, monophyletic cluster within the East-Central-South African (ECSA) genotype, closely related to strains from the 2024–2025 Réunion outbreak. Conclusions This outbreak underscores the value of dedicated surveillance protocols for early detection of febrile/suspected arboviral cases, while also highlighting key vulnerabilities, namely, an undetected primary case and negative entomological findings, pointing to the need for lower testing thresholds and earlier, risk-based vector sampling.

Mosquito diversity and arbovirus detection in Paraguay: first evidence of dengue and chikungunya virus infection in field populations.
作者： Darian Cáceres Roche, Luis Ferreira, Cynthia Bernal, Lívia Sacchetto, Sofía Echeverria, Patricia Ayala, Adriana Valenzuela, Laura Patricia Mendoza, Ana K Ibarrola-Vannucci, José Montiel
摘要中文翻译虫媒病毒是蚊媒病毒,对公共卫生具有重要意义,特别是在热带和亚热带地区。在巴拉圭,登革热 (DENV) 和基孔肯雅 (CHIKV) 病毒属于流行病学最相关的病毒;然而,整合蚊子和虫媒病毒 监测 的研究仍然很少。本研究旨在表征不同生态环境下的蚊子组合组成,并研究现场收集的蚊子中的虫媒病毒循环。2013 年至 2024 年间,从巴拉圭的城市、农村和大西洋森林环境中总共收集了 4,595 只蚊子(609 个池)。已鉴定出 26 种蚊子,其中致倦库蚊 (55.3%) 数量最多,其次是肩胛伊蚊 (Ochlerotatus) (13.3%)。埃及伊蚊(7.3%)主要与城市地区有关。多样性分析显示,大西洋森林的α多样性和伽玛多样性较高,而农村地区的值较低,而高的β多样性表明不同地点之间存在大量物种更替。通过实时 RT-PCR 进行的分子筛查未发现西尼罗河病毒、圣路易斯脑炎、委内瑞拉马脑炎、马亚罗病毒、黄热病、寨卡病毒或奥罗普切病毒的证据。埃及伊蚊池检测出 DENV(n=3;2021 年和 2023 年)和 CHIKV(n=3;2023 年)呈阳性。系统发育重建证实了DENV-4基因型II(谱系B.1.1)、DENV-2基因型II(F.1.1.2)和CHIKV东/中/南非(ECSA)谱系在巴拉圭的循环,所有这些谱系都与人类暴发相关序列密切相关。这些发现提供了巴拉圭现场采集的蚊子中虫媒病毒感染的第一个分子证据,并强调了综合昆虫学和病毒 监测 的重要性。

Mosquito diversity and arbovirus detection in Paraguay: first evidence of dengue and chikungunya virus infection in field populations.

Authors: Darian Cáceres Roche, Luis Ferreira, Cynthia Bernal, Lívia Sacchetto, Sofía Echeverria, Patricia Ayala, Adriana Valenzuela, Laura Patricia Mendoza, Ana K Ibarrola-Vannucci, José Montiel
Original abstract (English)
Arboviruses are mosquito-borne viruses of major public health importance, particularly in tropical and subtropical regions. In Paraguay, dengue (DENV) and chikungunya (CHIKV) viruses are among the most epidemiologically relevant; however, studies integrating mosquito and arbovirus surveillance remain scarce. This study aimed to characterize mosquito assemblage composition across different ecological settings and investigate arbovirus circulation in field-collected mosquitoes. A total of 4,595 mosquitoes (609 pools) were collected between 2013 and 2024 from urban, rural, and Atlantic Forest environments in Paraguay. Twenty-six mosquito species were identified, with Culex quinquefasciatus (55.3%) as the most abundant, followed by Aedes (Ochlerotatus) scapularis (13.3%). Aedes aegypti (7.3%) was mainly associated with urban areas. Diversity analyses showed higher alpha diversity and greater gamma diversity in the Atlantic Forest and lower values in rural areas, whereas high beta diversity indicated substantial species turnover among locations. Molecular screening by real-time RT-PCR detected no evidence of West Nile, Saint Louis encephalitis, Venezuelan equine encephalitis, Mayaro, yellow fever, Zika, or Oropouche viruses. aegypti pools tested positive for DENV (n=3; 2021 and 2023) and CHIKV (n=3; 2023). Phylogenetic reconstruction confirmed the circulation of DENV-4 Genotype II (lineage B.1.1), DENV-2 Genotype II (F.1.1.2), and CHIKV East/Central/South African (ECSA) lineages in Paraguay, all closely related to human outbreak-associated sequences. These findings provide the first molecular evidence of arbovirus infection in field-collected mosquitoes in Paraguay and emphasize the importance of integrated entomological and viral surveillance.

Question and background
Arboviruses transmitted by mosquitoes pose significant public health threats in tropical and subtropical regions, with dengue and chikungunya viruses being particularly relevant in Paraguay. However, integrated studies combining mosquito diversity and arbovirus surveillance have been limited in the country.
Design and population
A cross-sectional entomological survey collected 4,595 mosquitoes (609 pools) between 2013 and 2024 from urban, rural, and Atlantic Forest environments in Paraguay. Twenty-six mosquito species were identified, with Culex quinquefasciatus as the most abundant.

Mosquitoes were screened for arboviruses using real-time RT-PCR assays targeting West Nile, Saint Louis encephalitis, Venezuelan equine encephalitis, Mayaro, yellow fever, Zika, Oropouche, dengue (DENV), and chikungunya (CHIKV) viruses.

Interpretation and novelty
This study provides the first molecular evidence of CHIKV infection in field-collected mosquitoes in Paraguay, highlighting the need for integrated entomological and viral surveillance to monitor arbovirus circulation.
Scientific and public-health significance

Neglected arboviruses in South America: clinical manifestations and diagnostic challenges.

Authors: Milagros Saavedra-Samillán, Sharon Díaz Velásquez, Marcos A. Castillo Zuta, Julio Sandoval-Bances, Rafael Tapia-Limonchi, Stella M. Chenet
Original abstract (English)
In South America, the arboviruses that are most noted are those that lead to dengue, Zika, and chikungunya. Nevertheless, there are additional arboviruses that trigger outbreaks, yet do not receive adequate attention, including Oropouche virus (OROV), Mayaro virus (MAYV), Saint Louis Encephalitis virus, Madariaga virus (MADV), and Rocío virus (ROCV). A rapid review was conducted using the databases PubMed, LILACS, Scopus, and Google Scholar. This analysis focused on studies that outline the clinical signs and diagnostic methods of neglected arboviruses impacting human populations in South America. A total of 38 studies met the inclusion criteria. Most reports focused on OROV and MAYV, whereas only a few studies described MADV and ROCV. Brazil accounted for the largest proportion of studies, followed by Peru. Across the reviewed literature, these arboviruses were associated with a broad clinical spectrum, ranging from non-specific febrile illness to severe neurological disease and congenital complications. Diagnostic approaches included serological assays, viral isolation or neutralization tests, and molecular methods such as RT-PCR. The increasing spread of these under-reported arboviruses in South America requires strengthening monitoring systems, optimizing access to specific diagnostics, and training healthcare personnel to identify these infections early and manage them appropriately.

不同X射线照射剂量对转染和未感染沃尔巴克氏体的埃及伊蚊的影响

作者： Jiraporn Yongyai, Parinda Thayanukul, Pattamaporn Kittayapong
摘要中文翻译背景:埃及伊蚊是多种虫媒病毒疾病的主要 媒介,包括登革热、基孔肯雅热、寨卡病毒和黄热病。埃及伊蚊可以破坏病毒发病率周期,从而减少这些疾病的发病率。释放无菌Ae。已使用三种方法来抑制埃及雄性埃及伊蚊的自然种群,即电离辐射诱导的绝育(不育昆虫技术,SIT)、沃尔巴克氏体诱导的细胞质不相容性(不相容昆虫技术,IIT)以及两种方法的组合(SIT/IIT)。方法/主要发现:本研究调查了不同 X 射线辐射剂量对泰国野生型和沃尔巴克氏体反式感染伊蚊生态适应性的影响。野生型和 wAlbB 均转染伊蚊。埃及伊蚊在蛹期接受不同剂量的X射线照射,即30、50和70 Gy。我们的结果表明,两种 Ae 的适应度参数均有所降低。当辐照剂量增加时,埃及菌株受到影响,在沃尔巴克氏体感染组中观察到更大的不良反应。有趣的是,30 Gy 照射似乎可以延长 wAlbB 反式感染的伊蚊的寿命。在 50 Gy 和 70 Gy 照射下,野生型和 wAlbB 反式感染蚊子的雄性生育力几乎为零。沃尔巴克氏体密度通常随着辐射的增加而下降,而较高的剂量与交配竞争力的降低有关。在 30 Gy 时,野生型雄性在交配竞争力方面优于 wAlbB 反式感染的雄性;但在 50 Gy 和 70 Gy 时则相反。结论/意义:根据实验室条件下不同 X 射线剂量的生态适应性参数和交配竞争力评估,建议使用 30 Gy 对泰国野生型和 wAlbB 反式感染的伊蚊进行灭菌。我们的研究结果旨在提高蚊子无菌释放计划的效率,从而抑制伊蚊。埃及伊蚊 媒介 种群并降低虫媒病毒病 传播 的风险。

Effects of different X-ray irradiation dosages on Wolbachia trans-infected and uninfected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes.

Authors: Jiraporn Yongyai, Parinda Thayanukul, Pattamaporn Kittayapong
Original abstract (English)
BACKGROUND: Aedes aegypti is a primary vector of several arboviral diseases, including dengue, chikungunya, Zika, and yellow fever. aegypti can disrupt virus transmission cycles, thereby reducing the incidence of these diseases. Releasing sterile Ae. aegypti males has been implemented to suppress their natural populations, using three approaches, i.e., ionizing radiation-induced sterilization (Sterile Insect Technique, SIT), Wolbachia-induced cytoplasmic incompatibility (Incompatible Insect Technique, IIT), and a combination of both approaches (SIT/IIT). METHODOLOGY/PRINCIPAL FINDINGS: This study investigated effects of different X-ray radiation doses on the ecological fitness of the Thai wild-type and Wolbachia trans-infected Ae. Both wild-type and wAlbB trans-infected Ae. aegypti were irradiated with different X-ray radiation dosages, i.e., 30, 50, and 70 Gy, at the pupal stage. Our results demonstrated reduced fitness parameters in both Ae. aegypti strains when irradiation dosages increased, with greater adverse effects observed in the Wolbachia-infected group. Interestingly, 30 Gy irradiation appeared to prolong the lifespan of wAlbB trans-infected Ae. Male fertility in both wild-type and wAlbB trans-infected mosquitoes irradiated at 50 Gy and 70 Gy was nearly zero. Wolbachia density generally declined with increasing radiation, and higher doses were associated with reduced mating competitiveness. At 30 Gy, wild-type males outperformed wAlbB trans-infected males in mating competitiveness; but it was reversed at 50 Gy and 70 Gy. CONCLUSIONS/SIGNIFICANCE: Based on the evaluation of ecological fitness parameters and mating competitiveness across various X-ray dosages under laboratory conditions, 30 Gy is recommended for sterilizing both Thai wild-type and wAlbB trans-infected Ae. Our findings aim to improve the efficiency of sterile mosquito release programs, thereby suppressing Ae. aegypti vector populations and mitigating the risk of arboviral disease transmission.

Etiological Spectrum of Acute Febrile Illness in a Tertiary Care Hospital in Assam, India: A Cross-Sectional Study

Authors: Dhritiman Misra, P. Das, J. S. Roy, P. Deka, Saurav Sarma, S. S. Sen, Gitasree Borah
Original abstract (English)
Introduction: Acute Febrile Illness (AFI) encompasses a spectrum of conditions marked by fever, often caused by various infectious agents. The aetiological diversity poses challenges in diagnosis and treatment, particularly in regions with limited diagnostic resources. Aim: To evaluate the spectrum of pathogens causing AFI with or without rash in patients. Materials and Methods: A hospital-based cross-sectional study was conducted from January 2024 to December 2024 on 1,544 patients who had attended Lakhimpur Medical College and Hospital, North Lakhimpur, Assam, India. Patients with fever (≥ 37.5°C) with or without rash were enrolled. Serological investigations for Measles, Rubella, Dengue, Varicella-zoster, Chikungunya viruses, Japanese Encephalitis (JE), Orientia tsutsugamushi, Leptospira, and Hepatitis viruses were performed using Enzyme-linked Immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and Rapid Detection Testing (RDT) kits. Data analysis was performed using both descriptive and inferential methods. A p-value of <0.05 was considered significant. Results: Fever was prevalent in all 1,544 (100%) cases, followed by 856 (55.44%) cases of headache, 636 (41.19%) with cough, 476 (30.83%) with vomiting, 347 (22.47%) with abdominal pain, 59 (3.82%) cases with dysentery, 52 (3.37%) with diarrhoea, 46 (2.98%) cases with unconsciousness, and 21 (1.36%) cases presented with rash. Fever was observed in combination with headache in 856 (55.44%), with cough in 636 (41.19%), with bodyache in 481 (31.15%), and with vomiting in 476 (30.83%) cases. The study identified seasonal variations in infection rates, with peaks during the monsoon months. The majority of positive cases were attributed to Scrub Typhus 133/817 (16.28%), followed by Leptospirosis 129/473 (27.27%), Dengue 84/1273 (6.59%), and JE 20/87 (22.99%), while some had co-infection. Conclusion: The study underscores the importance of integrated clinical and laboratory approaches in diagnosing AFI. Scrub typhus, leptospirosis, and dengue were identified as major causes, emphasising the need for enhanced surveillance and diagnostic capabilities.

Original abstract (English)
Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) causes an acute febrile illness with debilitating arthralgia, and serological assays remain central to its diagnosis and surveillance. However, antibody-based results are complicated by cross-reactivity in settings where multiple arboviruses co-circulate. This review distinguishes three related but distinct phenomena affecting CHIKV serology: biological cross-reactivity, arising from genuine antigenic overlap between related viruses; assay-associated non-target reactivity, in which a diagnostic platform generates a positive signal without an established biological relationship between the viruses; and diagnostic misclassification, the downstream epidemiological consequence of conflating the two. A new vaccine strategy developed by researchers from Brazil and Germany has shown promising results in protecting mice against chikungunya. The study, published in npj Vaccines, also found that the vaccine produced antibodies capable of neutralising Mayaro virus, a related mosquito-borne virus.
